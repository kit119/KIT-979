Jinacaritaya [O 0.0001]

The Conduct of the Conqueror

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

1.

1.

Uttamaṃ uttamaṅgena namassitvā mahesino;

Nibbāṇamadhudaṃ pādapaṅkajaṃ sajjanālinaṃ.

Having paid homage with the highest limb, the head, to the lotus-feet of the Great Sage, which yield the nectar of Nibbāna and to which the virtuous are drawn like bees;

2.

2.

Mahāmohatamaṃ loke dhaṃsentaṃ dhammabhākaraṃ;

Pātubhūtaṃ mahātejaṃ dhammarājodayācale.

To the sun of the Dhamma, which, with great radiance, has arisen on the mountain of the Dhamma King's dawn, dispelling the great darkness of delusion in the world;

3.

3.

Jantucittasare jātaṃ pasādakumudaṃ sadā;

Bodhentaṃ saṅghavandañca silorukiraṇujjalaṃ.

Ever awakening the water lily of faith born in the lake of beings' minds, venerated by the Sangha, and radiant with the great rays of virtue.

4.

4.

Tahiṃ, tahiṃ suvitthiṇṇaṃ jinassa caritaṃ hitaṃ;

Pavakkhāmi samāsena sadā’nussaraṇatthiko.

Though the Victor’s beneficial conduct is widely proclaimed here and there, I shall recount it briefly for the purpose of constant remembrance.

5.

5.

Paṇitaṃ taṃ sarantānaṃ dullabhampi sivaṃpadaṃ;

Adullabhaṃ bhave bhogapaṭilābhamhi kā kathā?

For those who remember that sublime conduct, even the difficult-to-obtain state of bliss becomes accessible; what then of the acquisition of worldly enjoyments?

6.

6.

Tasmā taṃ bhaññamānaṃ me cittavuttapadakkamaṃ;

Sundaraṃ madhuraṃ suddhaṃ sotusotarasāyanaṃ.

Therefore, as it is being related by me, this flow of thought, expression, and verse—beautiful, sweet, and pure—is a nectar for the ear of the listener.

7.

7.

Sotahatthapuṭā sammā gahetvāna nirantaraṃ;

Ajarāmarami’cchantā sādhavo paribhuñjatha.

O virtuous ones, desiring the Undecaying and Deathless, rightly and constantly grasp it with the cupped hands of your ears and partake of it.

8.

8.

Kappasatasahassassa catunnaṃ cā’pi matthake;

Asaṅkheyyānamā’vāsaṃ sabbadā puññakāminaṃ.

Four incalculable periods and a hundred thousand aeons ago, he dwelt, ever desiring merit.

9. [O 0.0002]

9.

Nānāratanasampannaṃ nānājanasamākulaṃ;

Vicittāpaṇa saṃkiṇṇaṃ toraṇa gghika bhūsitaṃ.

Endowed with various jewels, thronged with diverse people, crowded with varied shops, and embellished with arches and festoons.

10.

10.

Yuttaṃ dasahi saddehi devindapurasannibhaṃ;

Puraṃ amarasaṅkhātaṃ ahosī ruciraṃ varaṃ.

Resounding with the tenfold sounds and resembling the city of Indra, there existed the delightful and excellent city known as Amarā.

11.

11.

Tahiṃ brāhmanvaye jāto sabbalokābhipūjito;

Mahādayo mahāpañño abhirūpo manoramo.

There, born into a brahmin family, was one revered by all the world, of great compassion and great wisdom, handsome and pleasing to the mind.

12.

12.

Sumedho nāma nāmena vedasāgarapāragū;

Kumāro’si garūnaṃ so avasāne jinaṃkuro.

A youth named Sumedha, he had mastered the ocean of the Vedas; in the end, this pupil of his teachers became a nascent Conqueror.

13.

13.

Rāsivaḍḍhakamaccena dassitaṃ amitaṃ dhanaṃ;

Anekasatagabbhesu nicitaṃ taṃ udikkhiya.

Having surveyed the immeasurable wealth stored in many hundreds of chambers, which had been shown to him by the treasurer,

14.

14.

Dhanasannicayaṃ katvā aho mayhaṃ pitādayo;

Gatā māsakame’kampi nevā’dāya divaṃ iti.

And seeing the accumulation of wealth, he thought: "Alas, my forefathers have passed away without taking even a single coin to the heavens."

15.

15.

Saṃvegamu’payāto’vacīmantesī’ti guṇākaro;

Dhanasāraṃ ihaṃ gayha gantuṃ yuttanti me pana.

Moved by spiritual urgency, that mine of virtues deliberated thus: "It is fitting for me to grasp the essence of this wealth and then depart."

16.

16.

Rahogato nisīditvā sundare nijamandire;

Dehe doso udikkhanto ovadanto’pi attano.

Having gone to a secluded place and sat down in his lovely mansion, observing the defects in the body, he admonished himself thus:

17.

17.

Bhedanaṃ tanunodukkhaṃ dukkho tasso’dayo’pi ca;

Jātidhammo jarādhammo vyādhidhammo ahaṃ iti.

"The breaking up of the body is suffering, and its arising is also painful. I am subject to birth, subject to aging, and subject to disease."

18.

18.

Evamā’dīhi dehasmiṃ disvā dose anekadhā;

Pure bheriṃ carāpetvā ārocetvāna rājino.

Having thus seen the manifold faults in the body, he first had a drum beaten and then announced his intention to the king.

19.

19.

Bherinādasugandhena yācakālisamāgate;

Dānakiñjakkhaoghena sattāhaṃ pīnayī tato.

Then, as a swarm of beggars gathered, drawn by the welcome sound of the drum, for seven days he satisfied them with a flood of gifts like pollen.

20.

20.

Dānaggahimabindūnaṃ nipātenā’pi dhaṃsanaṃ;

Ayātaṃ taṃ viloketvā ratanambujakānanaṃ.

He saw that forest of jewel-lotuses, his wealth, which was not diminished even by the falling of the dewdrops of gifts being taken.

21.

21.

Rudato ñātisaṅghassa jalitānalakānanā;

Gajindo viya gehamhā nikkhamitvā manoramā.

While his host of relatives wept, he departed from his delightful home, like a lordly elephant from a blazing forest.

22. [O 0.0003]

22.

Mahantaṃ so mahāvīro upagañchi himālayaṃ;

Haricandanakappūrāgarugandhehi vāsitaṃ.

That great hero approached the vast Himālaya, perfumed with the fragrances of yellow sandalwood, camphor, and aloe-wood.

23.

23.

Suphallacampakāsokapāṭalītilakehi ca;

Pūgapunnāganāgādipādapehi ca maṇḍitaṃ.

It was adorned with well-blossoming campaka, asoka, pāṭalī, and tilaka trees, and with areca, punnāga, nāga, and other trees.

24.

24.

Sīhavyagghataracchehi ibhadīpikapīhi ca;

Turaṅgamādinekehi migehi ca samākulaṃ.

It was filled with lions, tigers, and hyenas; with elephants, leopards, and monkeys; and with horses and many other kinds of deer.

25.

25.

Sāḷikāravihaṃsehi haṃsakoñcasuvehi ca;

Kapotakaravīkādisakuntehi ca kūjitaṃ.

It resounded with the calls of mynas, crows, and swans; with cranes and parrots; and with pigeons, cuckoos, and other birds.

26.

26.

Yakkharakkhasagandhabbadevadānavakehi ca;

Siddhavijjādharādīhi bhūtehi ca nisevitaṃ.

It was frequented by yakkhas, rakkhasas, gandhabbas, devas, and dānavas; and by siddhas, vijjādharas, and other such beings.

27.

27.

Manosīlindanīlorucārupabbatapantihi;

Sajjhuhemādinekehi bhūdharehi ca bhāsuraṃ.

It was radiant with great, beautiful mountain ranges of red arsenic and sapphire, and glorious with numerous mountains of silver, gold, and other minerals.

28.

28.

Suvaṇṇamaṇisopānanekatitthasarehi ca;

Sobhitaṃ tattha kīḷantanekadevaṅganāhi ca.

It was beautified by lakes with golden and jeweled stairways and many bathing places, and by the many celestial maidens playing there.

29.

29.

Sītasīkarasañchannanijjharānaṃ satehi ca;

Kiṇṇaroragaraṅgehi rammehi virājitaṃ.

Beautified by hundreds of waterfalls covered with cool spray, and by the delightful haunts of kinnaras and serpents.

30.

30.

Sikhaṇḍisaṇḍanaccehi latānaṃ maṇḍapehi ca;

Setavālukasañchannamālakehi ca maṇḍitaṃ.

Adorned with the dances of peacock flocks, with bowers of vines, and with terraces covered with white sand.

31.

31.

Suvaṇṇamaṇimuttādi anekaratanākaraṃ;

Icchantānaṃ janālīnaṃ puññakiñjakkhamā’layaṃ.

A mine of gold, gems, pearls, and various other jewels; an abode for multitudes of people desiring the pollen of merit.

32.

32.

Tama’jjhogayha so dhīro sahassakkhena māpite;

Disvā isiparikkhāre paṇṇasālavare tahiṃ.

Having entered it, that wise one saw there the excellent leaf-hut and the requisites of a hermit, created by the thousand-eyed one.

33.

33.

Isivesaṃ gahetvāna viharanto samāhito;

Sattāha’bbhantare pañca abhiñña’ṭṭhavidhā’pi ca.

Taking on the hermit's guise, he dwelt composed; and within seven days, he attained the five superknowledges and the eight attainments.

34.

34.

Uppādetvā samāpattisukhene’ca tapodhano;

Nabhasā divase’kasmiṃ gacchanto janataṃ isi.

Having produced the bliss of absorption, that ascetic, rich in austerity, was traveling through the sky on a certain day when he saw the people.

35. [O 0.0004]

35.

Sodhentama’ñjasaṃ disvā otaritvā nabhā tahiṃ;

Iti taṃ janataṃ pucchi kasmā sodhetha añjasaṃ.

Seeing them clearing the road, he descended from the sky and asked that crowd: "Why do you clear the road?"

36.

36.

Sumedha tvaṃ najānāsi dīpaṅkaratathāgato;

Sambodhimu’ttamaṃ patvā dhammacakkama’nuttaraṃ.

"Sumedha, do you not know? The Tathāgata Dīpaṅkara, having attained supreme enlightenment, has set in motion the unsurpassed Wheel of the Dhamma."

37.

37.

Pavattetvāna lokassa karonto dhammasaṅgahaṃ;

Rammaṃ rammapuraṃ patvā vasatī’ha sudassane.

Having set it in motion, bringing the benefit of the Dhamma to the world, he has reached the delightful city of Ramma and dwells here in the Sudassana monastery.

38.

38.

Bhikkhusatasahassehi catūhi vimalehi taṃ;

Nimantayimha dānena mayaṃ lokekanāyaka.

We invited him, the unique leader of the world, together with four hundred thousand stainless bhikkhus, with an offering.

39.

39.

Tassa āgamanatthāya maggaṃ sodhema cakkhuma;

Iti sotassa so tassa sukhaṃ dento jano’braviṃ.

"For the arrival of that Seer, we are clearing the path." Thus spoke the people, bringing happiness to him as he listened.

40.

40.

Buddho’ti vacanaṃ sutvā pītiyo’daggamānaso;

Sakabhāvena saṇṭhātuṃ nevasakkhi guṇākaro.

Hearing the word "Buddha," his mind was uplifted with rapture; he, the mine of virtues, could not maintain his composure.

41.

41.

Tenā’raddhañjasā dhīro yācitvāna padesakaṃ;

Labhitvā visamaṃ ṭhānaṃ samaṃ kātuṃ samārabhi.

Therefore, that resolute wise one, having requested a portion of the path and received an uneven spot, began to make it level.

42.

42.

Nā’laṅkateyeva tahiṃ padese,

Lokekanātho sanarāmarehi;

Sampūjito lokahito mahesi,

Vasīhi saddhiṃ paṭipajji maggaṃ.

While that very spot was not yet adorned, the unique protector of the world, honored by humans and gods, the great seer and benefactor of the world, proceeded along the path together with the self-controlled ones.

43.

43.

Chabbaṇṇaraṃsijālehi pajjalantaṃ tathāgataṃ;

Āgacchantaṃ tahi disvā modamāno vicintayi.

Seeing the Tathāgata approaching there, blazing with a net of six-colored rays, he rejoiced and thought thus:

44.

44.

Yannūni’massa dhīrassa setuṃ katvāna kaddame;

Sakattānaṃ nipajjaye sasaṅghassa mahesino.

"Would that I might make a bridge in the mud and lie down myself for this resolute one, the great sage, together with his Sangha."

45.

45.

Dīgharattāma’laṃ taṃ me hitāya ca sukhāya ca;

Icce’vaṃ cintayitvāna nipanno’so jinaṅkuro.

"For a long time, that will be for my benefit and happiness." Having reflected thus, that scion of the Conqueror lay down.

46.

46.

Pabodhetvāna disvāna cārulocanapaṅkaje;

Puna’pe’vaṃ vicintesi nipanno dhitimā tahiṃ.

Arousing himself and seeing the lovely lotus-like eyes, resolute, he again pondered thus as he lay there:

47. [O 0.0005]

47.

Iccheyyaṃ ce’hama’jje’va hantvā’nantaraṇe bhave;

Saṅghassa navako hutvā paviseyyaṃ puraṃ varaṃ.

"If I wished, I could this very day destroy the defilements in this life without interval and enter the excellent city as a newcomer to the Sangha."

48.

48.

Kima’ññātakavesena klesanibbāpaṇena me;

Ayaṃ buddho’va’haṃ buddho hutvā loke anuttaro.

"What use to me is extinguishing the defilements in an unknown guise? Just as this one is a Buddha, so I too, having become a Buddha, shall be unsurpassed in the world."

49.

49.

Janataṃ dhammanāvāya tāretvāna bhavaṇṇavāva;

Nibbāṇapuramā’netvā seyyaṃ me parinibbutaṃ.

"Having ferried the people across the ocean of existence with the boat of the Dhamma and led them to the city of Nibbāna, it is better for me to attain final Nibbāna myself."

50.

50.

Icce’vaṃ cintayitvāna nipanno kaddame tahiṃ;

Suvaṇṇakadalikkhandhasannibho so’ti sobhati.

Having thought thus, he lay there in the mud; he shone resplendently, like a golden banana trunk.

51.

51.

Chabbaṇṇaraṃsīhi virājamānaṃ,

Disvā manuññaṃ sugatattabhāvaṃ;

Sañjātapītīhi udaggacitto,

Sambodhiyā chandama’kāsi dhīro.

Seeing the delightful form of the Well-Gone One, radiant with six-colored rays, his mind uplifted with arisen joy, the wise one made an aspiration for enlightenment.

52.

52.

Āgantvāna tahiṃ ṭhānaṃ isiṃ paṅke nipannakaṃ;

Lokassa setubhūto’pi setubhūtaṃ tama’ttano.

Having arrived at that place, he who was a bridge for the world saw the sage lying in the mud, who had become a bridge for himself.

53.

53.

Disvā ussīsake tassa ṭhatvā lokekasetuno;

Lokekalocano dhīro dīpaṅkaratathāgato.

Having seen him, the unique bridge for the world, and standing at his head, the unique eye of the world, the resolute Tathāgata Dīpaṅkara declared:

54.

54.

Gotamo nāma nāmena sambuddho’yaṃ anāgate;

Bhavissatīti vyākāsi sāvake ca purādike.

"In the future, this one will be a Perfectly Enlightened One named Gotama." Thus he prophesied to the disciples and the foremost ones.

55.

55.

Idaṃ vatvāna katvāna sasaṅgho taṃ padakkhiṇaṃ;

Pūjesi aṭṭhamuṭṭhihi kusumehi guṇappiyo.

Having said this, he who is beloved for his virtues, together with the Sangha, circumambulated him to the right and honored him with eight handfuls of flowers.

56.

56.

Iti kātūna pāyāsi sasaṅgho lokanāyakova;

Rammakaṃ nāma nagaraṃ rammārāmālayālayaṃ

Having done so, the Leader of the World, together with the Sangha, departed for the city named Rammaka, an abode of abodes with delightful parks.

57.

57.

Jinassa vacanaṃ sutvā uṭṭhahitvāna paṅkato;

Mudito devasaṅghehi kusumādīhi pūjito.

Hearing the Victor's words and rising from the mud, joyful, he was honored by the assembly of gods with flowers and other offerings.

58.

58.

Pallaṅkamā’bhujitvāna nisīdi kusumāsane;

Mahātapo mahāpañño sumedho damitindriyo.

Having folded his legs into the cross-legged posture, he sat upon the seat of flowers—Sumedha, of great austerity, of great wisdom, with senses tamed.

59. [O 0.0006]

59.

Devā dasasahassesu cakkavāḷesu moditā;

Abhitvaviṃsu taṃ dhīraṃ nisinnaṃ kusumāsane.

The gods in the ten thousand world-systems rejoiced; they extolled that wise one seated on the flower seat.

60.

60.

Nisinno upadhāresi dhamme buddhakare tadā;

Kimuddhaṃ vā adho vā’pi disāsu vidisāsu ca.

Then, while sitting, he pondered the qualities that make one a Buddha: what is upward, or downward, or in the directions and intermediate directions.

61.

61.

Icce’vaṃ vicinanto so sakalaṃ dhammadhātukaṃ;

Addakkhi sakasantāne paṭhamaṃ dānapāramiṃ.

Thus, examining thoroughly the entire Dhamma-realm, he first saw in his own continuum the perfection of giving.

62.

62.

Evame’vaṃ gavesanto uttariṃ pāramī vidū;

Sabbā pāramiyo disvā attano ñāṇacakkhunā.

Even so, seeking further perfections, the wise one saw all the perfections with his own eye of knowledge.

63.

63.

Saṃsāre saṃsaranto so bahuṃ dukkhaṃ titikkhiya;

Gavesanto’mataṃ santo pūretvā dānapāramiṃ.

Wandering in saṃsāra, he endured much suffering; seeking the deathless, the peaceful one fulfilled the perfection of giving.

64.

64.

Sattanaṃ kapparukkho’va cintāmaṇi’va kāmado;

Icchiticchitamannādiṃ dadanto dadataṃ varo.

Like a wish-fulfilling tree to beings, like a wish-fulfilling gem, a granter of desires; giving whatever food and so forth was wished for, he is the best among givers.

65.

65.

Tārakāhi bahuṃ katvā nabhe cāruvilocane;

Uppāṭetvā dadaṃ dhīro yācakānaṃ pamodito.

More numerous than the stars in the sky, the wise one, delighted, plucked out his beautiful eyes and gave them to those who asked.

66.

66.

Mahiyā paṃsuto cā’pi samuddodakato’dhikaṃ;

Dadaṃ sarīramaṃsañca lohitampi ca attano.

More than the dust of the earth and the water of the ocean, he gave his own flesh and blood.

67.

67.

Molinā’laṅkate sīse’dhikaṃ katvā sineruto;

Kampayitvā mahiṃ dento sute cā’pi sakaṅgatāva.

Giving his head adorned with a diadem, an offering greater than Mount Sineru, shaking the earth, he also gave his children and his own limbs.

68.

68.

Sīlanekkammapaññādī pūretvā sabbapāramī;

Vessantarattabhāve’ vampatvā tambhā cuto pana.

Having fulfilled all the perfections—virtue, renunciation, wisdom, and so forth—in his existence as Vessantara, he then passed away from there.

69.

69.

Uppajjitvā surāvāse sundare tusite pure;

Vasanto suciraṃ kālaṃ bhutvānā’tantasampadaṃ.

Having been reborn in the divine abode, the beautiful city of Tusita, he dwelt there for a very long time, enjoying endless prosperity.

70.

70.

Katañjalīhi devehi yācito dipaduttamo;

Sambodhāya mahāvīra kālo tuyhantiādinā.

The Supreme One among bipeds was entreated by the devas with folded hands, saying: 'O Great Hero, it is time for your enlightenment!' and so forth.

71.

71.

Viloketvāna kālādiṃ ñatvā kālanti bodhiyā;

Paṭiññaṃ devasaṅghassa datvā nandanakānanaṃ.

Having surveyed the time and so forth, and knowing it was the time for Awakening, he gave his pledge to the assembly of devas and departed for the Nandana Grove.

72. [O 0.0007]

72.

Gantvāna devasaṅghehi sugatiṃ gacchi’to cuto;

Abhitthuto mahāpañño cavitvāna tato idha.

Having gone forth with the assembly of devas, he who had reached a happy state passed away from there; the greatly wise one, extolled, having deceased from there, came to be here.

73.

73.

Susajjitaṅgoruturaṅgamākule,

Vicittanānāpaṇapaṇyasampade;

Manoramuttuṅgajindarājite,

Vibhūsite toraṇaketurāsihi.

In a city crowded with well-equipped horses and chariots, rich with diverse goods from various shops, charming and lofty, resplendent with victorious kings, adorned with arches and heaps of banners.

74.

74.

Alaṅkataṭṭālavisālamālaye,

Sugopure sundarasundarālaye;

Sudassanīye kapiḷavhaye pure,

Purindadassā’pi purassa hāsake.

In wide courtyards adorned with watchtowers, with fine gates and beautiful, lovely abodes; in the city named Kapila, pleasing to behold, which surpassed even the city of Purindada.

75.

75.

Bhūpālamoḷiratanālinisevitaṅghi,

Paṅkeruhaṃ vimalanekaguṇādhivāsaṃ;

Okkākarājakulaketumanāthanāthaṃ,

Suddhodanaṃ narapatiṃ pavaraṃ paṭicca.

Relying on the eminent King Suddhodana, whose lotus feet were served by rows of jewels from the crowns of other kings, who was an abode of many pure virtues, the banner of the royal Okkāka dynasty, and a protector of the protectorless.

76.

76.

So sajjhudāmadhavalāmaladassanīya,

Soṇḍāya saṃgahitasetavarāracindaṃ;

Candāvadātavaravāraṇarājavaṇṇaṃ,

Sandassayitva supinena visālapañño.

The one of vast wisdom, in the guise of a noble elephant king, white as the moon, lovely to behold, dazzlingly and spotlessly white, holding a supreme white lotus in his trunk, showed himself in a dream.

77.

77.

Bimbādharāya vikacuppalalocanāya,

Devindacāparaticaḍḍhanabhūlatāya;

Sampuṇṇasommavimalinduvarānanāya,

Sovaṇṇahaṃsayugacārupayodharāya.

With lips like bimba fruit, eyes like blooming lotuses, with brows arched like Indra's bow, with a face like the full, pure, and gentle moon, and with beautiful breasts like a pair of golden swans.

78.

78.

Pādāravindakarapallavasundarāya,

Sovaṇṇavaṇṇatanuvaṇṇavirājitāya;

Sīlādineka guṇabhūsanabhūsitāya,

Māyāya rājavanitāyu’pagañchi kucchiṃ.

He entered the womb of the royal lady Māyā, who was beautiful with lotus-like feet and sprout-like hands, radiant with the complexion of a golden body, and adorned with the ornament of many virtues such as morality.

79.

79.

Paṭisandhikkhaṇe tassa jātā’nekavidhabbhutā;

Athā’yaṃ gahitārakkho narehi amarehi ca.

At the moment of his conception, many and various wonders occurred; then he was taken under protection and guarded by both humans and gods.

80. [O 0.0008]

80.

Manuññarattambujakaṇṇikāya,

Mā’sīnasiṅgīpaṭimā’va rammā;

Suvaṇṇavaṇṇo dipadānamindo,

Pallaṅkamā’bhuñchiya mātugabbhe.

In his mother's womb, the golden-hued lord of bipeds sat cross-legged, as lovely as a golden statue seated within the pericarp of a delightful red lotus.

81.

81.

Maṇimhi vippasannamhi rattasuttami’vā’vutaṃ;

Mātucittambujaṃ dhīro bodhayanto padissati.

Like a red thread strung through a perfectly clear gem, so the wise one was visible, awakening the lotus of his mother's heart.

82.

82.

Dasamāsāvasānamhi devī rañño kathesi’daṃ;

Mayhaṃ ñātigharaṃ deva gantumi’cchāma’haṃ iti.

At the end of ten months, the queen said this to the king: 'Your Majesty, I wish to go to my relatives’ home.'
