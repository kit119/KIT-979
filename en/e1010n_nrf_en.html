Homage and Proclamation

1.

1.

Cakkavāḷa [M 0.0001] nahutā ga, devāli gaṇa cumbito;

Buddha pādambujo ṭhātu, sīse dayā tigandhajo.

May the Buddha's lotus feet, kissed by the hosts of gods from hundreds of thousands of world-systems, remain upon my head, O Compassionate One, born of the three fragrances.

2.

2.

Nanta [M 0.0002] cakkavāḷa bbhugga, guṇa sanniccitaṃ jinaṃ;

Vande tappūjitaṃ dhammaṃ, tajjaṃ saṅghañca nimmalaṃ.

I worship the Conqueror, whose virtues are amassed, transcending endless world-systems; I worship the Dhamma, revered by him, and the Sangha, born of him, immaculate.

3.

3.

Vakkhāmi [M 0.0003] caturārakkhaṃ, sambuddha vacana nvayaṃ;

Appamādāvahaṃ etaṃ, sottabbaṃ bhavabhīruhi.

I will declare the four protections, according to the words of the Perfectly Enlightened One. This brings about heedfulness; it should be heard by those who fear existence.

4.

4.

Buddhānussati [M 0.0004] maraṇā, bhubhā mettāca bhāvanā;

Appamādāya ārakkhā, catasso mānitā sataṃ.

Recollection of the Buddha, mindfulness of death, meditation on repulsiveness, and the cultivation of loving-kindness; these four protections for heedfulness are honored by the good.

Buddhovādaṃ [M 0.0005] saritvāva, maccubbiggā sukhesino;

Sītā sītataraṃ yanti, subhamettambusi-ṭṭhitā.

Having recollected the Buddha's admonition, those who fear death and seek happiness go to a cool, even cooler state, established in the water of purity and loving-kindness.

6.

6.

Maraṇaggi vāraṇambu, sambuddhavacanaṃ yidaṃ;

Bahū tadaggi santattā, sītāvāsuṃ tadambunā.

This word of the Perfectly Enlightened One is the water that wards off the fire of death. Many, scorched by that fire, found a cool dwelling with that water.

7.

7.

Saddhaṃ [M 0.0006] buddhena tejetvā, mānaṃ maraṇacintayā;

Asubhāya hane rāgaṃ, dosaṃ mettāya paññavā.

A wise person should kindle faith through the Buddha, destroy pride with contemplation of death, eradicate lust through the repulsive, and eliminate hatred with loving-kindness.

1. Buddhānussati niddesa

1. The Exposition of the Recollection of the Buddha

1.

1.

Arahaṃ [M 0.0007] sammāsambuddho, vijjakkhi caraṇappado;

Sugato sugado satthā, sabbaññū bhagavādamo.

The Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One, endowed with knowledge and conduct, the Well-farer, the Well-spoken, the Teacher, the All-knower, the Blessed One, the Tamer.

2.

2.

Ārakattārihantattā[M 0.0009], pāpākārakatoraho;

Hata cakkārato pūjā, rahattā cārahaṃ name.

Because he is far from defilements, because he has destroyed the enemies, because he does no evil even in secret, because he has destroyed the spokes of the wheel, and because he is worthy of offerings, he is an Arahant; to him I pay homage.

3.

3.

Samutte [M 0.0011] jiya gihīnaṃ, anupubbikatho jino;

Adāsi paramaṃ tuṭṭhiṃ, saccāni dassayaṃ divā.

Having encouraged the householders, the Conqueror gave the gradual discourse; he bestowed supreme satisfaction, revealing the truths by day.

4.

4.

Bhikkhūnaṃ [M 0.0012] paṭhame yāme, pāyesi amatāgadaṃ;

Jātikhettāga devānaṃ, kaṅkhacchedo sa majjhime;

In the first watch of the night, he gave the deathless nectar to the monks; for the gods come from the realm of birth, he cut off doubt in the middle watch.

5.

5.

Ādo phalasukhaṃ vedi, majjhe seyya makā jino;

Veneyyo lokanaṃ ante, pacchimepi tidhā kate.

In the beginning, he experienced the happiness of fruition; in the middle, the Conqueror made his bed; in the end, he surveyed the world for trainable beings, thus dividing the last watch also into three.

6.

6.

Khedaṃ [M 0.0014] agaṇayaṃ nātho, pañca buddhakataṃ vahaṃ;

Satthasiddho paratthaṃva, byāvaṭo sumahādayo.

The Lord, disregarding weariness, bearing the fivefold Buddha-duty; like a perfect Teacher, he was engaged for the welfare of others, being greatly compassionate.

7.

7.

Caṅkamitvā nisīditvā, rattiṃdivañca jhāyituṃ;

Supituṃ majjhayāmeva, buddho bhikkhūna movadi.

Having walked and sat, to meditate night and day, and to sleep only in the middle watch—thus the Buddha advised the monks.

8.

8.

Nāla [M 0.0015] mālasituṃ tassa, mahāvīrassa sāsane;

Pamādāya munindassa, kataññū sādhu sammato.

It is not proper to be lazy in the teaching of that Great Hero, nor to be heedless towards the Lord of Sages; the grateful one is well-esteemed by the virtuous.

9.

9.

Anaññātassa ñātāya, apattassaca pattiyā;

Ārabhetuṃva no yutto, appamatto rahogato.

For the unknown to be known, and for the unreached to be reached, it is fitting for one to strive, heedful and gone to solitude.

10.

10.

Ārabbhatha [M 0.0016] nikkamatha, yuñjatha buddhasāsane;

Dhunātha maccuno senaṃ, naḷāgāraṃva kuñjaro.

Strive, go forth, apply yourselves to the Buddha's teaching! Demolish the army of Death, as an elephant demolishes a reed house.

11.

11.

Yo [M 0.0017] imasmiṃ dhammavinaye, appamatto vihissati;

Pahāya jāti saṃsāraṃ, dukkhassantaṃ karissati;

Whoever dwells heedfully in this Dhamma and Vinaya, having abandoned the cycle of birth, will make an end of suffering.

12.

12.

Iti [M 0.0018] uyyojanaṃ mhākaṃ, muttassa muttiyā saraṃ;

Nayuttova pamādāya, mahādayassa satthuno.

Thus, this is our exhortation: remembering the liberation of the Liberated One, it is not proper to be heedless towards the Teacher of great compassion.

13.

13.

Suttena dubbitakkena, akicca karaṇenavā;

Mogha kālakkhayo mando, dukkhassantaṃ kathaṃkare.

Through wrongly conceived doctrine or by doing what should not be done, the fool wastes time in vain; how can he make an end of suffering?

14.

14.

Ākāsaṃ [M 0.0019] cakkavāḷañca, sattā buddhaguṇā pica;

Anantānāma cattāro, paricchedo navijjati.

Space, world-systems, beings, and the virtues of the Buddha—these four are indeed infinite; no limit is found.

15.

15.

Yathāpi nabha mākāsaṃ, aṅgularajjuyaṭṭhibhi;

Minetuṃ neva sakkoti, evaṃ kenaci tagguṇaṃ.

Just as the sky, space, cannot be measured with a finger, a rope, or a rod, so too, no one can measure his virtues.

2. Maraṇassatiniddesa

2. The Exposition on Mindfulness of Death

1.

1.

Maraṇassati [M 0.0023] micchanto, tāva buddhavaco suṇa;

Avikkhittena cittena, sambuddha vacanaṃ hidaṃ.

Desiring mindfulness of death, one should first listen to the Buddha's word; with an undistracted mind, for this is the word of the Perfectly Enlightened One.

2.

2.

Animitta [M 0.0024] manaññātaṃ, maccānaṃ idha jīvitaṃ;

Kasirañca parittañca, tañca dukkhena saṃyutaṃ.

The life of mortals here is without sign and unknown; it is difficult and brief, and conjoined with suffering.

3.

3.

Na hi so pakkamo atthi, yena jātā namiyyare;

Jarampi patvā maraṇaṃ, evaṃdhammā hi pāṇino.

For there is no means by which those who are born will not die; even having reached old age, there is death—such indeed is the nature of living beings.

4.

4.

Phalāna [M 0.0025] miva pakkānaṃ, pāto patanato bhayaṃ;

Evaṃ jātāna maccānaṃ, niccaṃ maraṇa to bhayaṃ.

Just as for ripe fruits, there is fear of falling early in the day, so for mortals once born, there is constant fear of death.

5.

5.

Yathāpi kumbhakārassa, katā mattikabhājanā;

Sabbe bhedapariyantā, evaṃ maccāna jīvitaṃ.

Just as all earthenware vessels made by a potter have breaking as their end, so is the life of mortals.

6.

6.

Daharāca [M 0.0026] mahantāca, yebālā yeca paṇḍitā;

Sabbe maccuvasaṃ yanti, sabbe maccuparāyanā.

Both the young and the old, both the foolish and the wise, all go under the sway of death, all have death as their destination.

7.

7.

Tesaṃ maccuparetānaṃ, gacchataṃ paralokato;

Napitā tāyate puttaṃ, ñātivāpana ñātake;

For those overcome by death, going to the next world, a father cannot protect his son, nor relatives their kinsmen.

8.

8.

Pekkhataññeva [M 0.0027] ñātīnaṃ, passa lālappataṃ puthu;

Ekamekova maccānaṃ, go vajjhoviya niyyati.

While their kinsmen are looking on and lamenting profusely, one by one mortals are led away, like an ox to the slaughter.

9.

9.

Eva mabbhāhato loko, maccunāca jarāyaca;

Tasmā dhīrā nasocanti, viditvā loka pariyāyaṃ.

Thus the world is afflicted by death and old age; therefore, the wise do not grieve, knowing the way of the world.

10.

10.

Aññepi [M 0.0028] passa gamane, yathā kammupage nare;

Maccuno vasamāgamma, phandantevidha pāṇino.

See also other people passing on according to their kamma; having come under the sway of death, beings tremble here.

11.

11.

Yena yenahi maññanti,

Tatotassa hi aññathā;

Etādiso vinābhāvo,

Passa lokassa pariyāyanti.

In whatever way they imagine it, it turns out other than that for them. Such is this separation; see the way of the world.

[Sulla sutte vuttaṃ.]

[As stated in the Salla Sutta.]

12.

12.

Yathāpi [M 0.0029] selā vipulā,

Nabhaṃ āhacca paccatā;

Samantā anupari yeyyuṃ,

Nippothentā catuddisā.

Just as massive mountains of rock, reaching to the sky, might advance from all sides, crushing the four quarters;

13.

13.

Evaṃ jarāca maccuca,

Adhivattanti pāṇine;

Khattiyebrāhmaṇe vesse,

Sudde caṇḍāla pakkuse;

Nakiñci parivajjeti,

Sabbamevā bhimaddati.

So too, old age and death overwhelm living beings—warriors, brahmins, merchants, workers, outcasts, and scavengers; they spare nothing, but crush all.

14.

14.

Na [M 0.0030] tattha hatthi naṃ bhummi, na rathānaṃ napattiyā;

Na cāpi mantayuddhena, sakkā jetuṃ dhanenavā.

There, there is no place for elephant troops, nor for chariots, nor for infantry; nor is it possible to conquer by stratagem or by wealth.

15.

15.

Tasmāhi [M 0.0031] paṇḍito poso,

Sampassaṃ attha mattano;

Buddhe dhamme ca saṅgheca,

Dhīro saddaṃ nivesaye.

Therefore, a wise person, seeing their own welfare, steadfast, should place their faith in the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Sangha.

16.

16.

Yo dhammacārī kāyena, vācāya uda cetasā;

Idhevanaṃ pasaṃsanti, paccasagge pamodatīti.

Whoever lives righteously by body, speech, or mind, is praised in this very life, and after death rejoices in heaven.

[Pabbatū mama sutte vuttaṃ.]

[As stated in the Pabbatūpama Sutta.]

17.

17.

Yathā [M 0.0032] vārivaho pūro, vahe rukkhe pakūlaje;

Evaṃ jarāmaraṇena, vuyhante sabba pāṇino.

Just as a full river sweeps away the trees on its bank, so are all beings swept away by aging and death.

18.

18.

Daharāpi hi miyyanti,

Narāca athanāriyo;

Tattha ko visāseposo,

Daharo mhītijīvite.

For even the young die, both men and women. Who then, in this life, can be confident, thinking, 'I am young'?

19.

19.

Sāya [M 0.0033] meke nidissanti, pāto diṭṭhā bahujjanā;

Pāto eke nadissanti, sāyaṃ diṭṭhā bahujjanā.

Of the many people seen in the morning, some are not seen by evening; of the many people seen in the evening, some are not seen by morning.

20.

20.

Ajjeva kiccaṃ ātappaṃ, ko jaññā maraṇaṃ suve;

Nahi no saṅkaraṃ tena, mahāsenana maccunāti.

Today itself the ardent effort should be made; who knows if death will come tomorrow? For we have no agreement with Death and his great army.

[Jātakesuvuttaṃ.]

[As stated in the Jātaka.]

21.

21.

Natthetthañño [M 0.0034] nusāsanto, sayaṃvattāna movada;

Jineritā nusārena, bhikkhu saṃsāra bhīruko.

There is no other instructor here; one should admonish oneself. Following the instruction proclaimed by the Conqueror, the monk, fearful of saṃsāra,

22.

22.

Ahivāpi [M 0.0035] maṃ ḍaṃseyya, aññepi visadhārino;

Apiyāpica ghāteyyuṃ, uppajjeyyuṃ rujāpime.

Even a snake might bite me, or other poison-bearing creatures; even enemies might kill me, and pains might arise for me.

23.

23.

Maccusenā vudhāsaṅkhyā, bāhirajjhattu paddavā;

Tehāyu pīḷitaṃchejjaṃ, marissa majjavā suve.

The weapons of Death’s army are countless, as are the external and internal dangers; oppressed by them, life is cut off; I shall die today or tomorrow.

24.

24.

Aḷakkā [M 0.0036] hi gavādīhi, corādīhi arīhipi;

Abhiṇha sannipātehi, rujā chanavutīhipi.

Indeed, afflicted by cattle and so on, by thieves and so on, and by enemies; by frequent calamities, and by the ninety-six diseases.

25.

25.

Bahūna mupakārehi, annodakādikehipi;

Marissaṃ pīḷito niccaṃ, nirujjheyyāyu ajjavā.

Even with the support of many things, such as food and water, being constantly oppressed, I shall die; life might cease today or tomorrow.

26.

26.

Bahvāvudhe [M 0.0037] visajjeti, nilleṇaṃ maccuniddayo;

Vasantaṃ bhavasaṅgāme, namutto koci āvudhā;

Pitiless Death relentlessly hurls many weapons; dwelling in the battle of existence, no one is freed from his weapon.

27.

27.

Mahabbalā mahāpaññā, mahiddhikā mahaddhanā;

Namuttā sammāsambuddho, sabbalokādhipo api;

Great in strength, great in wisdom, great in psychic power, great in wealth—none are freed; not even the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One, the sovereign of all the world.

28.

28.

Mayā [M 0.0038] samā navā vuddhā, tadāvudhehi te matā;

Tathā hampi marissāmi, leṇaṃ puññaṃva me kataṃ.

The young and the old, just like me, died by those weapons. So too shall I die; merit indeed is the refuge I have made.

29.

29.

Pathabyāpādayo dhātthū,

Āhārā bhojanādayo;

Sītuṇha mutunāmetaṃ,

Dosā pittasemhānilā.

The elements such as earth and water; nutriments such as food; cold, heat, and the seasons; the humors: bile, phlegm, and wind.

30.

30.

Dhātvāhāru [M 0.0039] tudosānaṃ, samatte vāyu tiṭṭhati;

Visame taṅkhaṇaññeva, chejja mappaṃ parādhinaṃ.

When the elements, nutriments, seasons, and humors are in balance, life endures; when they are unbalanced, at that very moment it is cut off—short and dependent on others.

31.

31.

Dhātvā hārutu dosānaṃ, visamā svepyakallako;

Ussāhe kallakāleva, kiṃkareyyaakallako;

When the elements, nutriments, seasons, and humors are unbalanced, one is unwell even tomorrow. One should strive while the time is suitable; what can one who is unwell do?

32.

32.

Uppajjeyyuṃ [M 0.0040] rujā svepi, asāto dukkhamā kharā;

Puretaraṃva ārabbhe, māpacchā anutāpanaṃ.

Even if pains should arise tomorrow, unpleasant, painful, and harsh, one should strive beforehand, so there is no regret afterward.

33.

33.

Sattānaṃ [M 0.0041] nīcakammānaṃ, saraṇopi bhayaṅkaro;

Nīcānīcaṃ najānāmi, maraṇāsannatampica.

For beings of lowly kamma, even refuge is terrifying; I do not know my future state, whether low or high, nor the nearness of death.

34.

34.

Kamma pīḷita sattānaṃ, taṅkhaṇampi bhayubbhavo;

Mareyya majjavā svevā, nayuttova pamajjituṃ.

For beings oppressed by kamma, fear arises at that very moment; I might die today or tomorrow, so it is not at all proper to be heedless.

35.

35.

Puññakkhīṇā pajā khippaṃ, ahetunāpi nassaye;

Suddhacitto marissāmi, na kiliṭṭhena cetasā.

People whose merit is exhausted might perish quickly, even without a cause; I shall die with a pure mind, not with a defiled mind.

36.

36.

Upacchedāpi [M 0.0042] me santi, bhavābhavacitā bahū;

Chejjaṃ tehāyu ajjāpi, sādhvāsumārabhe mataṃ;

Causes of life's interruption also exist for me, many accumulated from existence to existence; by them life can be cut off even today; therefore, one should strive well and quickly for the end of death.

37.

37.

Kammā parādha sattānaṃ, vināse paccupaṭṭhite;

Anayo nayarūpena, buddhimākamya tiṭṭhati.

For beings whose kamma is faulty, when ruin is at hand, misfortune in the guise of good fortune stands, having overcome their intelligence.

38.

38.

Maraṇāsanna [M 0.0043] taññeva, cinteyya paññavā sato;

Evaṃ cintayanto santo, napāpaṃ kattu mussahe.

The wise and mindful one should contemplate that very nearness of death. Contemplating thus, the peaceful one would not venture to commit evil.

39.

39.

Maraṇāsanna taññeva, cinteyya buddhasāvako;

Evaṃ cintayanto santo, kadācipi anuṇṇato.

The Buddha's disciple should contemplate that very nearness of death. Contemplating thus, the peaceful one would never be arrogant.

40.

40.

Addhāga [M 0.0044] maraṇaṃ pañño, puretaraṃva cintaye;

Kare kātabba kammañca, evaṃ so nānusocati.

Surely, the wise one should contemplate death beforehand. And he should do what ought to be done; thus he does not grieve.

41.

41.

Maraṇāsanna saññī so, appamatto vicakkhaṇo;

Pattepi maraṇe kāle, na sammuḷho nasokavā.

He who is conscious of the nearness of death, heedful and discerning, is not bewildered nor sorrowful, even when the time of death arrives.

42.

42.

Maraṇā [M 0.0045] sannasaññī so, sodheti attano malaṃ;

Nimmalena cuto bhikkhu, natvevā pāya gāmiko.

He who is conscious of the nearness of death purifies his own defilements; the monk, passing away stainless, is indeed not bound for a state of woe.

43.

43.

Sati āsanna maraṇe,

Dūrasaññī pamāda vā;

Yo karoti akātabbaṃ,

Tadā so atisocati.

When death is near, whoever, perceiving it as distant and being heedless, does what should not be done, then grieves exceedingly.

44.

44.

Sati [M 0.0046] āsannamaraṇe, dūrasaññī pamādavā;

Ākiṇṇo pāpadhammehi, pajjhāyi dummano tadā.

When death is near, one who perceives it as distant and is heedless, being overwhelmed by evil qualities, then broods with a sorrowful mind.

45.

45.

Sati āsanna maraṇe, duṭṭho dosoti thaddhavā;

Abhinandati sādeti, tadāso atidukkhito.

When death is near, the wicked and stubborn one who, out of hatred, rejoices and delights, is then exceedingly afflicted.

46.

46.

Sati [M 0.0047] āsannamaraṇe, tuvaṭaṃ na cikicchati;

Sañciccāpatti māpanno, tadā so paridevati.

When death is near, one who does not quickly seek a cure, having committed a deliberate offense, then laments.

47.

47.

Sati āsanna maraṇe, gihīhi navakehica;

Saṃsaṭṭho na nulomehi, svativassu mukho tadā.

When death is near, one who has associated with laypeople and novices, but not with suitable companions, then has a face full of tears.

48.

48.

Sati [M 0.0048] āsanna maraṇe, kuhako kuladūsako;

Micchājīva samāpanno, dunnimittova so cuto.

When death is near, the deceiver, the corrupter of families, the one engaged in wrong livelihood, passes away like a bad omen.

49.

49.

Sasokī [M 0.0049] sahanandīca, dukkhe dukkho sukhe sukho;

Gihikammesu ussukko, passaṃ gijjhakūṭaṃ cuto.

With sorrow and with joy, suffering when there is suffering, happy when there is happiness; zealous in household duties, seeing this, he has fallen from Vulture Peak.

50.

50.

Marissanti anāvajja, kilesātura pīḷito;

Kimikhajjavaṇo sāva, bhanto kiṃ sugatiṃ vaje.

One will die unreflecting, afflicted and oppressed by defilements. Like a festering, oozing wound, bewildered—what happy destiny would he reach?

51.

51.

Marissanti [M 0.0050] anāvajja, dhuradvayaṃ na pūrati;

Ganthaṃ vipassanaṃ tandī, kāruññoyeva so cuto.

They will die unreflecting, not fulfilling the dual burden. Slothful in study and insight, that compassionate one has indeed fallen.

52.

52.

Marissanti anāvajja, kilesānaṃ vasānugo;

Sambuddhā-ṇaṃ vītikkanto, kāruñño natthi tassamo.

They will die unreflecting, under the sway of defilements. Having transgressed against the Buddhas, there is no compassion for such a one.

53.

53.

Marissanti [M 0.0051] anāvajjaṃ, dhanamesī adhammato;

Puññe cintāpi nuppajji, nirayaṃ so manaṃ gato.

They will die unreflecting, seeking wealth unrighteously. Not even a thought of merit arises; his mind has gone to hell.

54.

54.

Mareyyanti anāvajjaṃ, dhanaṃ cini adhammato;

Citaṃ citaṃ ihevetaṃ, tasmiṃgiddho sa pettiko.

They will die unreflecting, having accumulated wealth unrighteously. Heap upon heap, it remains right here. Greedy for that, he belongs to the realm of ghosts.

55.

55.

Atthā [M 0.0052] gehe nivattante, susāne mittabandhavā;

Sukataṃ dukkataṃ kammaṃ, gacchanta manugacchati.

Possessions remain at home, friends and relatives at the charnel ground. Well-done and ill-done kamma follows the one who is going.

56.

56.

Mareyyanti anāvajjaṃ, ihatthaṃ vā nuyuñjati;

Samparāya manapekkho, sujuṃ vā pāyagāmiko.

They will die unreflecting, or they apply themselves to the purpose of this world, indifferent to the next world; or they are one who goes straight to the beyond.

57.

57.

Mareyyanti [M 0.0053] samāvajja, dhammato dhana mesati;

Puññakārī suladdhena, maraṇepi sa modati.

Reflecting 'I will die,' one seeks wealth righteously. The doer of merit rejoices, even at death, in what has been well-gained.

58.

58.

Pañcasīla sadārakkho, yathābalañca dāyako;

Kāle uposathāvāso, so niccaṃ sugatiṃ vajje.

One who always guards the five precepts, gives according to one's ability, and observes the Uposatha at the proper time—such a one would ever go to a happy destiny.

59.

59.

Dhovāpattimalaṃ [M 0.0054] khippaṃ, maccu addhā gamissati;

Micchāvitakka mucchijja, kara kātabbabhāvanaṃ.

Wash away the stain of offenses quickly, for death will surely come. Having cut off wrong thoughts, undertake the cultivation that should be done.

60.

60.

Atikkantā bahū ratyo, khepetvā mama jīvitaṃ;

Mandāyunā pamādena, yutto viharituṃ kathaṃ.

Many nights have passed, having spent my life. With little life remaining, how can I live endowed with heedlessness?

61.

61.

Hāsantaṃ [M 0.0055] nandi mattānaṃ, maccusandhīhi tacchaye;

Kucchi meyyacuto ajja, ko hāsananditabbako.

Laughing, intoxicated with delight, one is hewn down by the joints of death. My belly is like a gourd today; what is there to laugh and delight in?

62.

62.

Maccusenāvudhā saṅkhyā, maraṇābhimukho ahaṃ;

Accāyitabba kālo yaṃ, ikkhitabba mudikkhatu.

Death's army with its weapons is arrayed; I am facing death. This is the time for urgent action; let one examine what should be examined.

63.

63.

Puremarāmi [M 0.0056] daṭṭhabbaṃ,

Dakkheyyaṃ maccu essati;

Accāyitabba kālo yaṃ,

Nokāso hāsatuṭṭhiyā.

Before I die, I should see what must be seen: that death will come. This is the time for urgent action; there is no opportunity for laughter and delight.

64.

64.

Ākiṇṇamaccusenānaṃ[M 0.0057], ajja svevā vināsinaṃ;

Addhā pahāya gāmīnaṃ, kiṃ pamāda vihārinā.

For one surrounded by the army of death, who will perish today or tomorrow, who will surely depart, having abandoned all—what is the use of living heedlessly?

65.

65.

Khaṇamattova paccakkho, ajja svevā atissati;

Samparāyo atidīgho, paramparo anantiko.

This life is directly perceived as but a moment; it will pass away today or tomorrow. The next world is very long, an endless succession.

66.

66.

Khaṇamattova [M 0.0058] paccakkho, maccunā taṃ jahissati;

Pahāya gamanīye-smiṃ, mahussāho niratthako.

This life is directly perceived as but a moment; it will be forsaken through death. In this state of having to abandon all and depart, great striving is useless.

67.

67.

Samparāyo atidīgho, apātheyye suduttaro;

Mahussāhena kātabbo, tadattho dīghadassinā.

The next world is very long, very difficult to cross without provisions for the journey. One with foresight should accomplish that purpose with great energy.

68.

68.

Saddhā [M 0.0059] bandhatu pātheyyaṃ, tadesanaṃ iheva hi;

Bhavantare nalabbheyya, apātheyya tidukkhito.

Let faith bind up the provisions for the journey, for the seeking of it is right here. In another existence it cannot be obtained; without provisions, one is greatly distressed.

69.

69.

Saṃsāra taraṇatthāya, maholumpāni bandhatha;

Bhāvanā dāna sīlehi, tivittiṇṇo bhavaṇṇavo.

For the sake of crossing saṃsāra, construct great rafts. By means of cultivation, generosity, and virtue, the ocean of becoming is crossed over.

70.

70.

Bhuñjaṃ [M 0.0060] bhuñjaṃ janaṃ kāme,

Kālākālā-budhontako;

Kantekanteti maṃsāso,

Pivaṃpivaṃva kaṃ migaṃ.

While people repeatedly indulge in sensual pleasures, the End-maker, unaware of the right or wrong time, a flesh-eater ever desiring, is like one drinking and drinking from a deer.

71.

71.

Kāme kāmesanāyeyya,

Kālokālo mateṭṭhiyā;

Pūre pūretabbaṃ dhammaṃ,

Addhā addhāna saṃsaraṃ.

One might seek pleasures in sensual objects, considering the right and wrong time for what is desired. One should fulfill the Dhamma that is to be fulfilled; surely, the journey of saṃsāra is long.

72.

72.

Mareyyanti [M 0.0061] anubbiggo, pāpakaṃ kattumussahe;

Kareyya hāsanandiñca, cāpallañca pamāda vā.

Unagitated by the thought 'I will die,' one dares to do evil. One would indulge in laughter and delight, and fickleness or heedlessness.

73.

73.

Mareyyamiti [M 0.0062] saṃviggo, leṇameva gavesati;

Na hāsi nevanandīca, na cāpallo kadācipi.

Stirred by the thought 'I will die,' one seeks only a refuge. One does not laugh, nor does one delight, nor is one ever fickle.

74.

74.

Accuṭṭhita rujaggīhi, accāyāse bhayānake;

Nosadhe maraṇāsanne, katapuññaṃva sāta-daṃ.

When the fires of illness have fiercely arisen, in overwhelming and dreadful danger, with no medicine when death is near, merit that has been done brings comfort.

75.

75.

Ñātisaṅghā [M 0.0063] viyojentā, maraṇanta bhusāturā;

Sabbaṃ pahāya gantāpi, nanditabbāni puññino.

Separated from the assembly of relatives, severely afflicted until death, having gone and abandoned everything—even so, the meritorious have things in which to rejoice.

76.

76.

Passantā sunimittāni, pākaṭāni sakammunā;

Sukhanti maraṇe kāle, numodantā katānica.

Seeing auspicious signs, made manifest by their own kamma, they are happy at the time of death, rejoicing in what they have done.

77.

77.

Sāta-dātāni [M 0.0064] puññāni, evaṃ mahabbhaye api;

Sugatiṃ lahunetāni, kātabbāni puretaraṃ.

Meritorious deeds bring happiness, even in such great danger. These lead swiftly to a happy destiny; they should be done beforehand.

78.

78.

Devadūte [M 0.0065] pakāsetvā, yamapuṭṭho sayaṃkataṃ;

Puññaṃ sarati ce satto, tadeva sugatiṃ vaje.

When the divine messengers have appeared, and one is questioned by Yama about what was done by oneself, if the being remembers their merit, that alone would lead to a happy destiny.

79.

79.

Pāpa [M 0.0066] kaḍḍhampi niraye, manaṃ dukkhagataṃ pajaṃ;

Dukkhā moceti yaṃpuññaṃ, sadā kātabbameva taṃ.

Evil drags a being, whose mind has gone to suffering, even into hell. That merit which frees from suffering—it should always be done.

80.

80.

Pahāyakaṃva puññañhi, pahātabbaṃva pāpakaṃ;

Taṃ padīpandhakāraṃva, dvayaṃ otvā khukaṃ viya.

Merit is to be undertaken, and evil is to be abandoned. Like a lamp and darkness, both are to be discarded like vomit.

81.

81.

Ñātisaṅghā [M 0.0067] viyojentā, maraṇanta bhusāturā;

Sabbaṃ pahāya gantāro, bhayānakāni pāpino.

Separated from kinsmen and communities, intensely afflicted until death, all must depart, having abandoned everything; the wicked experience dreadful things.

82.

82.

Passantā dunnimittāni, pākaṭāni sakammunā;

Maraṇe atidukkhanti, nutāpentā katānica.

Seeing ill omens made manifest by their own deeds, and lamenting what they have done, they suffer extremely at the time of death.

83.

83.

Paṭipīḷāni [M 0.0068] pāpāni, evaṃ mahabbhaye sati;

Duggatiṃ lahunetāni, yuttova parivajjituṃ.

Evil deeds are oppressive. When such great fear is present, they swiftly lead to a woeful state; it is indeed proper to avoid them.

84.

84.

Devadūte [M 0.0069] pakāsetvā, yamarājena pucchito;

Pamādassanti cikkhanto, mahaggimhi turaṃ pati.

When King Yama has pointed out the divine messengers and questioned him, he, declaring his negligence, quickly falls into a great fire.

85.

85.

Puññaṃ akarivā māvā, yamarājinda pucchito;

Pamādassanti cikkhanto, mahādukkhaṃ turaṃ gami.

Questioned by Yama, the king, 'Did you not perform merit?', and declaring, 'It was negligence,' one will quickly go to great suffering.

86.

86.

Pāpaṃ [M 0.0070] akarivā māvā, pucchito yamasāminā;

Pamādassanti cikkhanto, tattaṃ guḷaṃ turaṃ gili.

Questioned by the lord Yama, 'Did you commit evil?', and declaring, 'It was negligence,' one will quickly swallow a molten iron ball.

87.

87.

Jātamattā tijiṇṇāca, āturāca matā vudhā;

Devadūte ime pañca, disvā saṃviggataṃ vaje.

A newborn, an aged person, a sick person, a dead person, and one punished for a crime—seeing these five divine messengers, one should be stirred to spiritual urgency.

88.

88.

Coditā [M 0.0071] devadūtehi, ye pamajjanti mānavā;

Te dīgharattaṃ socanti, hina kāyū pagānarā.

Those human beings who are heedless, though spurred by the divine messengers, grieve for a long time, having attained a lowly existence.

89.

89.

Dhuradvaya [M 0.0072] manārabbha, gihikammādike rato;

Kathaṃ gijjhakūṭaṃ sesaṃ, petāvāsaṃ atissati.

Not undertaking the twofold yoke, delighting in household duties and the like, how will he cross over Vulture Peak and the abode of ghosts?

90.

90.

Pariyatti masikkhanto,

Nāraddho paṭipattiyaṃ;

Alaso dubbitakko so,

Kiṃ taṃselaṃ atissati.

Not learning the scriptures, not having commenced the practice, being lazy and of wrong thought, how will he cross over that flood?

92.

92.

Mocanatthāya [M 0.0073] pabbajja, saṃkiliṭṭhā pamādino;

Sugatyāpica te bhaṭṭhā, atidūrāva muttito.

Ordination is for the sake of liberation, but the negligent are defiled. Fallen even from a good destiny, they are very far indeed from freedom.

93.

93.

Sīdanteva [M 0.0074] jale khittā, silā mahāva khuddakā;

Patanti khuddakenāpi, apāyaṃ pāpa kammunā.

Just as large and small stones, when thrown into water, sink, so too one falls into a state of loss by an evil deed, even a small one.

94.

94.

Patantā khuddakeneva, bahūhi puna pīḷitā;

Mokkhokāsaṃ navindanti, pāpaṃ khuddampi nācare.

Falling due to even a small evil deed, then oppressed by many, they find no opportunity for release; therefore, one should not commit any evil, however small.

95.

95.

Jegucchitthūdarāgamma[M 0.0075], punāpi tattha niccagū;

Dukkhāti dukkha saṃkiṇṇo, haṭṭhuṃ tuṭṭhuṃ nasakkuṇe.

Having come from a loathsome woman's womb, one constantly goes there again. Filled with suffering upon suffering, one is unable to be joyful or glad.

96.

96.

Atibyāpiguṇo [M 0.0076] puñño,

Mahāyaso sirindharo;

Kucchiyaṃ retasi vāso,

Atīva lajjitabbako.

Merit has exceedingly pervasive qualities; it brings great fame and holds splendor. Yet dwelling in the womb, in semen, is a thing of extreme shame.

97.

97.

Maccudukkhaṃ khaṇaṃyeva, atidukkhaṃ taduttari;

Mātugāmudare sandhi, patiṭṭhānaṃ bhayānakaṃ.

The suffering of death is but for a moment; the suffering beyond it is extreme. Conception and establishment in a mother's womb are dreadful.

98.

98.

Maccudukkhaṃ [M 0.0077] khaṇaṃyeva, atidukkhaṃ cirattanaṃ;

Āma pakkantare sandhi, patiṭṭhānaṃ bhayānakaṃ.

The suffering of death is but for a moment; the extreme suffering is long-lasting. Conception and establishment between the raw and the digested is a dreadful thing.

99.

99.

Duggatyaṃṭhātu taṃdukkhaṃ, suṇa uccakuleapi;

Kucchiyaṃ atisambādhe, jalābumhi jigucchite.

Let that suffering be in a woeful state, or even in a high family; it is in the exceedingly cramped womb, in the loathsome amniotic sac.

100.

100.

Miḷha semhādi saṃkiṇṇe, ati duggandha vāsite;

Gūthakūpe kimīviya, tamejā mūlakammato.

In a place mixed with excrement, phlegm, and the like, pervaded by an exceedingly foul stench, like worms in a cesspool, one is born from the root-deed of craving.

101.

101.

Paramāṇukāyo ṭhāti, dukkhī nerayiko viya;

Dhuvāturo sukhāmisso, āma pakkāsayantare;

The body, at first a mere particle, exists suffering like a hell-being; constantly diseased, mingled with pleasure, within the receptacle of the raw and the digested.

102.

102.

Vedanaṭṭova saṃvaḍḍho, acittoviya niccalo;

Dasamāsantare kacce, bahū maranti pāṇino.

Developing for the sake of feeling, as if mindless and motionless; within ten months in the womb, many beings die.

103.

103.

Paripakko [M 0.0079] pamuñcho so,

Atisambādha yonito;

Malākiṇṇena gattena,

Accāyāso vijāyati.

When fully developed, he is released from the exceedingly narrow birth canal. With a body smeared with filth, he is born through extreme exertion.

104.

104.

Evaṃ [M 0.0080] maccuñca sandhiñca, vijāyanañca bheravaṃ;

Passaṃ nibbindanto santo, virajjeyya bhavanduke.

Thus seeing death, conception, and birth as terrifying, a peaceful person, becoming disenchanted, should grow dispassionate toward the bond of existence.

105.

105.

Evaṃ maccuñca sandiñca, anussara mabhiṇhaso;

Rājaseṭṭhi bhavādimpi, naiccheyya tadanvitaṃ.

Thus, constantly recollecting death and conception, one should not desire even the existence of a king or a great merchant, since it is endowed with these things.

106.

106.

Bhave [M 0.0081] dukkha macintetvā, bhavāsāya pavattitaṃ;

Puññaṃ punappunaṃ deti, sandhiṃ na nibbutiṃ varaṃ.

Merit performed without contemplating the suffering in existence, and set in motion by the desire for existence, yields rebirth again and again, not the supreme quenching of Nibbāna.

107.

107.

Bhave dukkhaṃ vibhāyitvā, nibbindena pavattitaṃ;

Puññaṃ bhava matikkamma, nibbānaṃ deti nibbutiṃ.

Having clearly seen the suffering in existence, merit set in motion by disenchantment, transcending existence, yields Nibbāna, the quenching.

108.

108.

Bhave [M 0.0082] dukkhaṃ saritvāna, maccusandhi sayādikaṃ;

Tibhavesu virajjeyyā, ditta geheva sāmiko.

Remembering the suffering in existence—death, conception, and so forth—one should become dispassionate toward the three realms, like the master of a burning house.

109.

109.

Santo puññāni karonto, sandhidukkha manussaraṃ;

Nibbinda yutta cittena, vajjeyya bhavasāta to.

A peaceful person, while performing meritorious deeds and recollecting the suffering of rebirth-linking, should, with a mind joined with disenchantment, avoid delight in existence.

110.

110.

Puñña [M 0.0083] nibbatta ṭhānepi, jegucche sandhisambhavo;

Bhava sāta vasā tasmā, dhīro taṃ lagganaṃ caje.

Even in a place produced by merit, the arising of conception is loathsome; therefore, the wise one abandons that clinging which is due to delight in existence.

3. Asubhabhāvanā niddesa

3. Exposition of the Meditation on the Unattractive

1.

1.

Sirimaṃ [M 0.0084] gaṇikaṃ disvā, dametuṃ rattacetasaṃ;

Dassetvā matasārīraṃ, tassā jino idaṃ bravi.

Having seen the courtesan Sirimā, the Victor, in order to tame an impassioned mind, showed her dead body and spoke this.

2.

2.

Caraṃvā [M 0.0089] yadivā tiṭṭhaṃ, nisinno udavā sayaṃ;

Samañcheti pasāreti, esā kāyassa iñjanā.

Whether walking or standing, sitting or lying down, contracting or extending—this is the body’s movement.

3.

3.

Aṭṭhi nhārūhi saṃyutto, taca maṃsāva lepano;

Chaviyā kāyo paṭicchanno, yathābhūtaṃ nadissati.

Bound with bones and sinews, plastered with flesh and skin, the body, covered by the outer skin, is not seen as it truly is.

4.

4.

Antapūro [M 0.0090] darapūro, yakana peḷassa vatthino;

Hadayassa papphāsassa, vakkassa pihakassaca.

It is filled with intestines, with the contents of the stomach, with the liver, bladder, heart, lungs, kidneys, and spleen.

5.

5.

Siṅghānikāya kheḷassa, sedassaca medassaca;

Lohitassa lasikāya, pittassaca vasāyaca.

With mucus, saliva, sweat, and fat; with blood, synovial fluid, bile, and grease.

6.

6.

Athassa [M 0.0091] navahi sotehi, asuci savati sabbadā;

Akkhimhā akkhigūthako, kaṇṇamhā kaṇṇagūthako.

From its nine openings, impurity constantly flows: from the eyes, eye-excrement; from the ears, ear-excrement.

7.

7.

Siṅghānikāca nāsato, mukhato vamati ekadā;

Pittaṃ semhañca vamati, kāyamhā sedajallikā.

Mucus from the nose, and at times one vomits from the mouth; one vomits bile and phlegm, and from the body come sweat and grime.

8.

8.

Athassa [M 0.0092] susiraṃ sīsaṃ,

Matthaluṅgassa pūritaṃ;

Subhato naṃ maññati bālo,

Avijjāya purakkhato.

And its hollow head is filled with brain-matter; the fool, led on by ignorance, thinks it beautiful.

9.

9.

Yadāca so mato seti,

Uddhumāto vinīlako;

Apaviddho susānasmiṃ,

Anapekkhā honti ñātayo.

And when it lies dead, bloated and discolored, cast aside in a charnel ground, relatives have no regard for it.

10.

10.

Khādanti [M 0.0093] naṃ suvānāca, siṅgālakāca kimiyo;

Kākā gijjhāca khādanti, yecaññe santi pāṇakā.

Dogs, jackals, and worms devour it; crows and vultures feed on it, and whatever other creatures there may be.

11.

11.

Sutvāna [M 0.0094] buddhavacanaṃ, bhikkhu paññāṇavā idha;

Sokho naṃ parijānāti, yathābhūtañhi passati.

Having heard the Buddha’s word, a wise monk here fully understands it, for he sees it as it truly is.

12.

12.

Yathāidaṃ tathāetaṃ, yathāetaṃ tathāidaṃ;

Ajjhattañca bahiddhāca, kāye chandaṃ virājaye.

As this is, so is that; as that is, so is this. Both internally and externally, he should become dispassionate towards desire for the body.

13.

13.

Chanda [M 0.0095] rāga viratto so, bhikkhu paññāṇavā idha;

Ajjhagā amataṃ santiṃ, nibbānaṃ pada maccutaṃ.

Dispassionate towards desire and lust, that wise monk here has attained the deathless peace, Nibbāna, the imperishable state.

14.

14.

Dvipādako yaṃ asuci, duggandho parihārati;

Nānākuṇapa paripūro, vissavanto tatotato.

This two-legged thing, which is impure and foul-smelling, one carries around; it is full of various corpses, oozing from here and there.

15.

15.

Etādisena [M 0.0096] kāyena, yo maññe unnametave;

Paraṃvā avajāneyya, kimaññatra adassanāti;

Kāya vicchandanīyasuttaṃ, vijayasuttantipi vattabbaṃ.

With such a body, who would think to exalt himself or to despise another? What is this but a lack of vision? This is the Discourse on the Repulsiveness of the Body, which should also be called the Discourse on Victory.

16.

16.

Ra-akkharo [M 0.0097] siyāggimhi, rova aggiva āgato;

Tasmā rāgoti vattabbo, taṇhāva niccatāpikā.

The letter 'ra' is said to be in fire, or comes as if from fire; therefore, it is called 'rāga' (passion), for craving is a constant tormentor.

17.

17.

Abhiṇhameva rāgaggi, dayhate subhasaññinaṃ;

Kimikhajjavaṇo sāva, dukkhī rāgī sa sabbadā.

Those with a perception of beauty are constantly burned by the fire of passion. Like a woodpecker pecking a tree, the passionate person is always in distress.

18.

18.

Dukkhī [M 0.0098] piya maladdhāna, laddhāpya paripuṇṇato;

Natthi rāgaggikhandhassa, piyindhena hi puṇṇatā.

One suffers when not obtaining what is beloved, and is not fulfilled even after obtaining it. For the mass of fire that is passion, there is no satisfaction with the fuel of affection.

19.

19.

Natthi [M 0.0099] rāgasamo aggi,

Iti vuttaṃ mahesinā;

Tena rāgagginā daḍḍho,

Sabbo loko tidukkhito.

“There is no fire like passion,” so said the Great Seer. Burned by that fire of passion, the whole world is afflicted with threefold suffering.

20.

20.

Ekassa pivitaṃ khīraṃ, catū dadhi jalā bahu;

Rāgahetu bhave sandhi, ṭṭhānaṃ anamataggikaṃ.

The milk drunk by one person is more than the water of the four oceans. Because of passion, there is a linking in existence, a state without a known beginning.

21.

21.

Catūdami [M 0.0100] jalā bhiyyo, sīsacchedana lohitaṃ;

Rāgahetu bhave macca, bhayaṃ anamataggikaṃ.

More than the water of the four oceans is the blood from beheadings. Because of passion, mortals in existence experience fear without a known beginning.

22.

22.

Ekassa rudato assu, catū dadhi jalā bahu;

Dukkhaṃ anamataggaṃva, taṃhetu paridevanaṃ.

The tears wept by one person are more than the water of the four oceans. Suffering is indeed without a known beginning, and for that reason there is lamentation.

23.

23.

Tattāyo [M 0.0101] guḷa gilita, vadhaggi dayhanā dikaṃ;

Asaṅkhyeyyaṃ mahādukkhaṃ, taṃhetu niraye labhi.

Swallowing molten iron balls, burning in the executioner's fire, and so on—for that reason, one obtains immeasurable great suffering in hell.

24.

24.

Eka dvitti catu pañca, buddhuppādepya mocitaṃ;

Khuppipāsita nijjhāmaṃ, labhi taṃhetu pettikaṃ.

Not freed during one, two, three, four, or five Buddha-arisings, for that reason one obtains the hungry ghost state, parched by hunger and thirst.

25.

25.

Tiracchāne [M 0.0102] asūreca, dukkhaṃ nānāvidhaṃ labhi;

Namataggika saṃsāre, sabbantaṃ rāgahetukaṃ.

In the animal realm and among the asuras, one obtains manifold suffering. In the cycle of existence without a known beginning, all this is caused by passion.

26.

26.

Ekassekena kappena, puggalassaṭṭhi sañcayo;

Sace saṃhārito assa, vepulla pabbatādhiko.

The accumulation of a single person’s bones in one eon, if it were gathered together, would be greater than Mount Vepulla.

27.

27.

Eka kappe idaṃ dukkhaṃ, nādikappesu kākathā;

Rāgo nanu mahāverī, bālo jano ta micchati.

If this is the suffering in a single eon, what can be said of beginningless eons? Passion is indeed a great enemy, yet the foolish person desires it.

28.

28.

Rāgasuddhi [M 0.0103] asokoca, niddukkho ñāyapattica;

Nibbānaṃ pañca paccakkhā, asubhāya phalā matā.

Purity from passion, sorrowlessness, freedom from suffering, and attainment of the path—with Nibbāna as the fifth—are considered the fruits of the meditation on the unattractive.

29.

29.

Asubhaggahaṇaṃ [M 0.0104] jhāyī, mitā sindriya saṃvaro;

Somitya mukkaṭṭhāvācā, chaḷime rāga suddhiyā.

Grasping the unattractive, being a meditator, moderation in eating, restraint of the senses, amiability, and excellent speech—these six are for the purification from passion.

30.

30.

Niccuggarāga rogīnaṃ, asubhā vātulosadhā;

Rāgayakkhābhi gayhānaṃ, asubhā manta muttaraṃ.

For those sick with constant, fierce passion, the unattractive is a soothing remedy; for those possessed by the demon of passion, the unattractive is the supreme incantation.

31.

31.

Sajīvakāca [M 0.0105] nijjīvā, asubhā duvidhā matā;

Sajīvā kesalomādi, dasevime ajīvakā.

The unattractive is considered to be of two kinds: the living and the lifeless. The living are hair of the head, hair of the body, and so on; these ten are the lifeless.

32.

32.

Uddhumātaka [M 0.0106] vīnīlaṃ, vipubbakaṃ vichiddakaṃ;

Vikkhāyitaka vikkhittaṃ, hativikkhitta lohitaṃ.

The bloated, the livid, the festering, the fissured, the gnawed, the scattered, the hacked and scattered, and the bleeding.

33.

33.

Puḷuva ṭṭhika miccesu, laddhā aññataraṃ sato;

Ratana vānapasseyya, yathā cetasi pākaṭaṃ.

The worm-infested, the skeleton—having obtained any one of these, a mindful one should contemplate it as a treasure, as it appears clearly in the mind.

34.

34.

Mataṃ [M 0.0107] khajjaṃ sa maṃsañca, nilohitaṃ nimaṃsakaṃ;

Vikkhittaṃ seta puñjaṭṭhiṃ, navadhā putimikkhaye.

The dead, the eaten, the fleshy, the bloodless, the fleshless, the scattered, and the white heap of bones—thus should one observe the ninefold putrescence.

35.

35.

Maccuto [M 0.0108] parimuccāmi, paṭivatti yimā yiti;

Payojana samāvajja, moditabbaṃ jigucchake.

“I am freed from death; this is the turning back.” Thus reflecting on the purpose, one should rejoice in the repulsive.

36.

36.

Sajīvake jigucchatthaṃ, nijjīvā subha mīritaṃ;

Tathūpamo ayaṃkāyo, evameva bhavissati.

For the sake of disgust with the living, the lifeless is spoken of as unattractive. This body is of the same nature; just so will it become.

37.

37.

Evaṃdhammo [M 0.0109] ayaṃkāyo, evaṃbhāvī natikkamo;

Iccupa saṃhare disvā, ekadviha matādikaṃ.

Such is the nature of this body, such is its future state, which cannot be escaped. Having seen a corpse one or two days old and so on, one should thus apply this to oneself.

38.

38.

Yathā [M 0.0110] idaṃ tathāetaṃ, yathāetaṃ tathā idaṃ;

Jegucchaṃ paṭikūlyañca, kāye iccupa saṃhare.

As that is, so is this; as this is, so is that. Thus should one apply the perception of the disgusting and repulsive to this body.

39.

39.

Uddhumāta vinīlādi,

Paṭikūlyo jigucchito;

Tathevāyampi me kāyo,

Viseso nāyu-sāyuva.

The bloated, the livid, and so forth are repulsive and disgusting. Just so is this body of mine; there is no difference between that with life and that without.

40.

40.

Uddhumāta [M 0.0111] vinīlādo, sobhaṇaṃ natthi kiñcipi;

Imasmiṃpi me kāye, gavesantopi sabbaso.

In the bloated, the livid, and so forth, there is nothing beautiful whatsoever. So too in this body of mine, even when searching thoroughly.

41.

41.

Paṭikūlavasā dhātu, vasāca dvippakārato;

Paccavekkheyyimaṃ kāyaṃ, icchaṃ virāga mattani.

One should contemplate this body by way of its repulsive elements and by way of fat in its two forms, desiring dispassion for oneself.

42.

42.

Vaṇṇa [M 0.0112] saṇṭhāna gandhehi, āsayo kāsatopica;

Jeguccha paṭikūlyāca, kesā na tuṭṭhamānitā.

By their color, shape, smell, location, and origin, and because they are disgusting and repulsive, the hairs of the head are no cause for delight to the conceited.

43.

43.

Iti kesesu ikkheyya,

Lomā dīsupyayaṃ nayo;

Dvattiṃsevañhi koṭṭhāse,

Paccavekkhe visuṃvisuṃ.

Thus should one contemplate the hairs of the head; this same method applies to the hairs of the body and so on. Indeed, one should contemplate the thirty-two parts, each one separately.

44.

44.

Kāyato [M 0.0113] bahinikkhantaṃ, paṭikūlyaṃ jigucchitaṃ;

Anikkhantampi jegucchaṃ, paṭikūlyaṃva tassamaṃ.

That which is repulsive and disgusting when it has left the body is, even when it has not left, just as disgusting, just as repulsive, and the same.

45.

45.

Saṅkhatampi yathā vaccaṃ, manuññataṃ na pāpuṇe;

Upakkama sahassehi, evaṃ kesādikampica.

Just as excrement, even if well-adorned with a thousand efforts, does not attain pleasantness, so too is it with the hairs of the head and the other parts.

46.

46.

Sabhāva [M 0.0114] paṭikūlyaṃva, ekampi vacca puñjakaṃ;

Nanu jegucchitā bhiyyo, dvattiṃsa vaccapuñjakā.

If even a single heap of excrement is repulsive by nature, are not thirty-two heaps of excrement surely more disgusting?

47.

47.

Paccekampi [M 0.0115] paṭikūlyaṃ, kesādikaṃ sabhāvato;

Kesādidvattiṃsa puñjo, bhiyyo jegucchito nanu.

Each part—the hair and so on—is repulsive by its own nature; is not the heap of thirty-two such things surely more disgusting?

48.

48.

Puñjitesveva kantesu, kantohoti sa puñjako;

Puñjitesu akantesa, akantova sa puñjako.

When lovely things are heaped together, that heap is lovely. When unlovely things are heaped together, that heap is simply unlovely.

49.

49.

Paccekaṃ [M 0.0116] vinibhuttesa, kesa loma nakhādisu;

Natthi taññā kumārīvā, mukhahatthādikānivā.

When hair, body hair, nails, and so forth are analyzed individually, there is no craving for them, as there is for a young girl, or for her face, hands, and the like.

50.

50.

Sampiṇḍi tesu tesveva,

Kuto tā tāni āgatā;

Paññatti matta mevesā,

Jigucchaññā na kācipi.

When these parts are combined, from where has that [being] come? It is merely a concept; in reality, there is nothing other than the disgusting.

51.

51.

Santaṃ [M 0.0117] cinteyya nāsantaṃ, santa cintayato sukhaṃ;

Asantaṃ parikappento, nānādukkhehi tappati.

One should contemplate the real, not the unreal. For one who contemplates the real, there is happiness. By imagining the unreal, one is tormented by various sufferings.

52.

52.

Nāvajja santajegucchaṃ, saññaṃ asati kātuna;

Subhā itthīti gāraggi, uppajji subhasaññino.

One should not neglect what is truly repulsive, nor form a perception in what is not real. The thought, “A woman is beautiful,” is a fire of passion that arises for one with the perception of beauty.

53.

53.

Asantaṃva [M 0.0118] abhūtaṃva, passe rāgaggijotiyā;

Tāya santañca bhūtañca, na passati kadācipi.

By the light of the fire of passion, one sees only the unreal and the untrue. By that, one never sees the real and the true.

54.

54.

Ekassa [M 0.0119] pivitaṃ khīraṃ, sīsacchedana lohitaṃ;

Rudato assu taṃhetu, catūdadhi jalā bahu.

The milk one has drunk, the blood from one's beheadings, the tears from one's weeping: each is more abundant than the water in the four oceans.

55.

55.

Āyatimpi atīteva, saṃsarantassa hessati;

Rāgaṃ hantu manīhoce, khīraṃ assuca lohitaṃ.

For one wandering in saṃsāra, the future will be just like the past. If one wishes to destroy passion, one should contemplate milk, tears, and blood.

56.

56.

Subhasaññāya [M 0.0120] so vaḍḍho,

Tadabhāve sa nassati;

Thiraṃ hantuṃ na taṃsaññaṃ,

Sakkā sithila vīriyo.

Passion grows through the perception of beauty; in the absence of that perception, it perishes. That perception cannot be firmly destroyed by one of slackened energy.

57.

57.

Ussoḷhi [M 0.0121] vīriyo hutvā, brūheyyāsubha bhāvanaṃ;

Subhasaññāppa hānāya, pariccajjāpi jīvitaṃ.

Having become zealous in energy, one should develop the meditation on the unattractive. For the abandoning of the perception of beauty, one should relinquish even life itself.

58.

58.

Aññakicca mupekkhāya, brūheyyāsubha bhāvanaṃ;

Mandi hutveha rāgaggi, nibbāyissati āyatiṃ.

Disregarding other duties, one should develop the meditation on the unattractive. When the fire of passion has become weak here, it will be extinguished in the future.

59.

59.

Kiccaṃ me idameveti, brūheyyāsubha bhāvanaṃ;

Dāni mandaggi hutvāna, pāmojjaṃ ve labhissati.

'This is my task,' one should declare, and cultivate the meditation on unattractiveness. Now, having become weak in passion, he will obtain delight.

60.

60.

Kāye [M 0.0122] daṭṭhabba jegucchaṃ, apassanto pamādavā;

Aladdhā kiñci pāmojjaṃ, pabbajjampi na modati.

One who, being heedless, does not see the repulsiveness to be seen in the body, finds no joy at all, nor delights in the monastic life.

61.

61.

Pure marāmi kāye smiṃ, passā mi passitabbakaṃ;

Iccā raddho vītiṃladdhā, pabbajjaṃ atimodati.

'Before I die, let me see what should be seen.' Thus resolved, having attained peace, one greatly rejoices in the monastic life.

62.

62.

Kāye [M 0.0123] daṭṭhabba jegucchaṃ, apassanto pamāda vā;

Moghaṃva dullabhātīto, mahājānīyataṃ gato.

One who, being heedless, does not see the repulsiveness to be seen in the body, has wasted a rare opportunity and gone to great ignorance.

63.

63.

Santaṃ bhūtañca jegucchaṃ, rāgagginā apassiyaṃ;

Paññāpadīpajotena, samikkheyya abhiṇhaso.

Not seeing the real and existing repulsiveness due to the fire of passion, one should constantly examine it with the light of the lamp of wisdom.

64.

64.

Santaṃ [M 0.0124] bhūtañca kāye smiṃ, daṭṭhukāmo sadāsato;

Paññāpadīpakeneva, dakkhe na rāgīsīkhinā.

Ever mindful and desiring to see the real and existing nature in the body, one should observe with the lamp of wisdom, not with the flame of passion.

65.

65.

Jegucchitena kāyena, nikkhantena jigucchato;

Ajja svevā vinaṭṭhena, nāla munnamituṃ sato.

Having gone forth out of disgust with this repulsive body, it is not proper for a mindful person to be puffed up over that which is destroyed today or tomorrow.

66.

66.

Kīdisaṃ [M 0.0125] maṃ tuvaṃ maññi, ahaṃ sabba jegucchako;

Jegucchatoca nikkhanto, icceva vattu marahati.

'What do you think I am? I am disgusted by everything. Having gone forth from disgust, thus should I speak.'

67.

67.

Kāye jegucchasaññaṃva, kare sabbiriyā pathe;

Tasmiṃ tuṭṭhabbakaṃ natthi, piyāyitaṃ mamāyitaṃ.

One should cultivate the perception of repulsiveness in the body in all postures. In this, there is nothing to be delighted in, no affection, no possessiveness.

68.

68.

Subhāya [M 0.0126] nava mattānaṃ, asubhā paripācaye;

Santo pakkassa saṃsāro, nanto navassa rāgino.

For the immature, the beautiful is ever new; for the mature, the unbeautiful ripens. For the mature, there is an end to saṃsāra; for the immature, inflamed with lust, there is no end.

69.

69.

Kāye asubha saññāya, paripakka sabhāvino;

Ālambesu acāpallā, thirā sambuddha sāsane.

With the perception of the unbeautiful in the body, their nature fully developed, they are unfickle in their objects, steadfast in the teaching of the Perfectly Enlightened One.

70.

70.

Anto [M 0.0127] gocarikā pakkā, bahi gocarikā navā;

Pakkā nāsāya uccā te, nīcāyeva navā sino.

The mature have their range inwardly, the immature outwardly. The mature are exalted for the sake of destruction; the immature are bent down low.

71.

71.

Navānavā [M 0.0128] subhābhogī, nīcānīcā bhigāmino;

Pakkā pakkāva dhījhāyī, santāsantā virāgino.

The immature enjoy ever new pleasures, going after what is low and degraded. The mature, indeed, are wise contemplatives; the peaceful are dispassionate.

72.

72.

Sakkā sakkā na dassetuṃ, subhaṃsubhaṃ sataṃsataṃ;

Dhīrādhīrāga mujjhanti, kāye kāye kriyekriye.

It is hard to show the beautiful as beautiful to the mindful. The wise and the unwise are deluded by lust concerning the body's every action.

73.

73.

Sakkā [M 0.0129] sakkāpi taṃ kātuṃ, subhaṃsubhaṃ na dhīmayaṃ;

Santosantojigucchaññū, na vānavā subhesako.

Even a Sakka might do that, but not the wise who perceive the beautiful as foul. The contented, the disgusted, and those who know revulsion do not seek the beautiful.

74.

74.

Dukāyaṃ [M 0.0130] suti cintetvā, mamāyantā mahātapā;

Tapaṃ nibbāyituṃ icchaṃ, dukāyaṃ duti cintaye.

Having contemplated the perception of suffering in the body, one sees that possessiveness is a great torment. Desiring to extinguish that torment, one should contemplate the suffering in the body again.

75.

75.

Yvāsubhaṃ subhato maññi, konubālotaduttari;

Andho ummattakovā so, nattānaṃ maññate tathā.

Whoever perceives the unbeautiful as beautiful, what fool is worse than that? Blind or deranged is he who does not recognize himself as such.

76.

76.

Saritabbaka [M 0.0131] mevetaṃ, kāye jeguccha puñjataṃ;

Mandarāgo manosītaṃ, labheyya tamanussanaṃ.

This should be remembered: this body, a disgusting collection. Through such recollection, one with fading lust may obtain the cooling of the mind.

77.

77.

Gihibhāve apāyeca, rāgayakkhandha ninnitā;

Maṃpi nessati soyakkho, sādemice tadāgataṃ.

In the household life and in states of woe, people are cast down, bound by the demon of lust. That demon will lead even me away, should I relish it when it arises.

78.

78.

Abhiṇha [M 0.0132] gāhinaṃ rāga, yakkhaṃ ananta dukkha daṃ;

Asubhā tula mantena, vārehi taṃ sa bhāyati.

The demon of lust, ever grasping, giver of endless suffering; with the incomparable charm of contemplating the unbeautiful, drive it away; thus, it is terrified.

79.

79.

Rāgayakkho bahumāyo, saddhāmettā divesavā;

Rāgampi kusalaṃ maññi, jano teneva vañcito.

The demon of lust is full of deceit; it destroys faith and loving-kindness. People consider lust wholesome, and thus are deceived by it.

80.

80.

Āturaṃ [M 0.0133] asuciṃ putiṃ, passa nande samussayaṃ;

Uggharantaṃ paggharantaṃ, bālānaṃ abhipatthitaṃ.

See this diseased, impure, foul heap, O Nanda, belching forth and oozing—desired by fools.

81.

81.

Subhato naṃ maññati bālo, avijjāya purakkhato;

Iccāha bhagavā nindi, bāloti subhasaññinaṃ.

The fool, led on by ignorance, imagines it is beautiful. Thus the Blessed One rebuked one who perceived the beautiful, calling him a fool.

82.

82.

Nhāruṭṭhi [M 0.0134] taca maṃsāni, saraṃ sataṃ na nindito;

Buddhanindāya mocetuṃ, tānārabbha anussare.

Remembering the sinews, bones, skin, and flesh, a mindful person is not rebuked. To be freed from the Buddha's rebuke, one should recollect these, taking them as object.

83.

83.

Maññitvā attano bālyaṃ, asubhe subhadassino;

Vāyāmeyya abālāya, kāyaṃ asubhato saraṃ.

Recognizing his own foolishness in seeing the unbeautiful as beautiful, one should strive for non-foolishness, remembering the body as unbeautiful.

84.

84.

Attānaṃ [M 0.0135] garahitvāna, bālaṃ viparidassinaṃ;

Subhasaññaṃ pahinneyya, kareyyāsubha saññitaṃ.

Condemning oneself as a fool for seeing perversely, one should abandon the perception of the beautiful and develop the perception of the unbeautiful.

85.

85.

Visa bhesajjarukkhaṭṭho, ahi ḍaṃseyyasosato;

Yathā tasseva paṇṇādiṃ, khādetvā visa mujjahe.

Just as a man standing near a tree that is both poison and medicine, should he be bitten by a snake, might eat its very leaves and be freed from the poison;

86.

86.

Evaṃ rāgo samuppajje, kāye gandhādi vāsite;

Anto tasseva jegucchaṃ, cintetvā rāga mujjahe.

Even so, should lust arise in a body perfumed with scents, having contemplated its inner repulsiveness, one should dispel that lust.

87.

87.

Jigucchitena [M 0.0136] kāyena, apassanto jigucchataṃ;

Unnameti avaññāti, avijjāya purakkhato.

Concerning this disgusting body, one who does not see its disgusting nature becomes conceited and contemptuous, being led on by ignorance.

88.

88.

Āyatiṃ [M 0.0137] maggalābhāya, bījaṃ kareyya bhāvanaṃ;

Bījā bhāve kuto maggo, maggabījā hi bhāvanā.

For the future attainment of the path, one should make development the seed. In the absence of the seed, whence the path? Development, indeed, is the seed of the path.

89.

89.

Maggabījo apāyepi, nimmuggo samaye gate;

Ummujjitvāva buddhānaṃ, maggaṃ labheyya santike.

Even in states of woe, the seed of the path, though submerged, when the time arrives, emerges and one may obtain the path in the presence of the Buddhas.

90.

90.

Ajījassa [M 0.0138] tu saṃsāro, dīghoyeva anantiko;

Tasmāhi bhāvanābījaṃ, kareyya mocanatthiko.

For one without the seed, saṃsāra is indeed long and without end; therefore, one who seeks liberation should create the seed of mental development.

91.

91.

Abhiṇha pīḷitaṃ rāgaṃ, asubhāya nivāraye;

Mandīhutvā pahīyeyya, rāgo asubha bhīruko.

When repeatedly afflicted by lust, one should restrain it with the unbeautiful. Having become weak, lust is abandoned, being fearful of the unbeautiful.

92.

92.

Mājegucchaṃ [M 0.0139] mamāyetha, sāva jegucchamāmako;

Ananta dukkha māpādi, jegucchita mamāyanā.

Do not cling to the disgusting, lest you become one who clings to the disgusting. From clinging to the disgusting, one attains endless suffering.

93.

93.

Maṃsalaggo tacacchanno,

Nhārubandho ṭṭhipuñjako;

Moheti chaviyā lokaṃ,

Mahādukkho sa mohito.

A lump of flesh, covered with skin, bound by sinews, a heap of bones; it deludes the world by its complexion. Great is the suffering of that deluded one.

94.

94.

Nhāruṭṭhi [M 0.0140] taca maṃsehi, rāgavaḍḍhaki saṅkhate;

Gehe rogā putī pāpā, vasanti kucchitā sadā.

In this house constructed with sinews, bones, skin, and flesh by the carpenter of lust, diseases, putrid things, and evils, being disgusting, always dwell.

95.

95.

Luṅgantā vīsa bhūdhātū, pittādī dvādasambuva;

Tāpaṃ jiraṃ dahaṃ pakkaṃ, caturaggi chavāyukā.

Twenty solid elements, twelve fluid elements; that which heats, that which ages, that which burns, that which digests—the four fires; and the six winds.

96.

96.

Adhoddhaṃ kucchi koṭṭhāsā,

Aṅgacārīca pāṇakā;

Dhātuyoyeva kāyesmiṃ,

Dvitālīsa anaññakā.

The parts of the belly, below and above, and the creatures moving in the limbs; in this body there are only forty-two elements, and no other.

97.

97.

Yathā [M 0.0142] bahi tathā ajjhattaṃ, dhātū bhvāpā nalānilā;

Name nāhaṃ naattāti, saṃmaseyya punappunanti.

As without, so within, are the elements: earth, water, fire, and air. "This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self," thus should one repeatedly reflect.

4. Mettābhāvanāniddesa

4. The Exposition on the Development of Loving-Kindness

1.

1.

Mettā [M 0.0145] bhāvana micchampi, suṇa buddhavaco yidaṃ;

Dosa niggahaṇatthāya, doso mettāyaverihi.

If you desire the development of loving-kindness, hear this word of the Buddha for the sake of suppressing hatred; for hatred is indeed an enemy to loving-kindness.

2.

2.

Akkocchimaṃ avadhimaṃ, ajinimaṃ ahāsime;

Yeca taṃ upanayhanti, veraṃ tesaṃ nasammati.

“He abused me, he struck me, he overcame me, he robbed me”—for those who harbor such thoughts, hatred is never appeased.

3.

3.

Akkocchimaṃ [M 0.0146] avadhimaṃ, ajinimaṃ ahāsime;

Yecataṃ nupanayhanti, veraṃ tesaṃ upasammati.

“He abused me, he struck me, he overcame me, he robbed me”—for those who do not harbor such thoughts, hatred is appeased.

4.

4.

Nahiverena verāni, sammantidha kudācana;

Averenaca sammanti, esadhammo sanantano.

For not by hatred are hatreds ever appeased here; but by non-hatred are they appeased. This is an eternal law.

5.

5.

Pareca [M 0.0147] navijānanti, maya mettha yamāmase;

Yeca tattha vijānanti, tato sammanti medhagā.

Others do not realize that here we must all perish; but those who do realize this, their quarrels subside.

6.

6.

Kuddho atthaṃ najānāti, kuddho dhammaṃ napassati;

Sadā andhatamaṃ hoti, yaṃkodho sahatenaraṃ.

An angry person does not know what is beneficial, an angry person does not see the Dhamma; there is always blinding darkness when anger overcomes a person.

7.

7.

Ubhinna [M 0.0148] matthaṃ carati, attanoca parassaca;

Paraṃ saṃkuppitaṃ ñatvā, yo sato upasammati.

He acts for the good of both himself and others who, being mindful, calms down upon knowing that the other is enraged.

8.

8.

Tasseva tena pāpiyyo, yo kuddhaṃ paṭikujjhati;

Kuddhaṃ apaṭikujjhanto, saṅgāmaṃ jeti dujjayaṃ.

He who retaliates with anger against one who is angry is thereby the worse of the two; he who does not retaliate against the angry one wins a battle hard to win.

9.

9.

Khantī [M 0.0149] paramaṃ tapo titikkhā,

Nibbānaṃ paramaṃ vadanti buddhā;

Nahi pabbajito parūpaghātī,

Nasamaṇo hoti paraṃ viheṭhayanto.

Forbearance and patience are the highest austerity. Nibbāna is supreme, say the Buddhas. He is not a renunciant who harms another, nor is he a recluse who oppresses another.

Conquer anger with non-anger, conquer the wicked with goodness; conquer the miserly with giving, and the liar with truth.

He who checks his rising anger as one would a swerving chariot, him I call a charioteer; the other person is merely a rein-holder.

For a person who is born, an axe is born in his mouth, with which the fool, speaking ill-spoken words, cuts himself.

As a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, so the wise are not moved by blame or praise.

Let one be impartial towards being reviled or honored in the village; let one guard the mind from defilement, and, being peaceful, one should not be elated.

Just as a mother would protect with her life her own son, her only son, even so, towards all beings, one should develop a boundless mind.

Having heard much offensive speech, whether from recluses or worldlings, one should not retort harshly, for the peaceful do not retaliate.

One should speak the truth, not be angry, and give, even if a little, when asked; by these three means, one may go into the presence of the gods.

Associating with such a person, one becomes better, not worse; so said the Lord of Sages, the Teacher supreme in the three worlds.

The wise one, pervading with the fragrance of loving-kindness, keeps hatred far away; towards all, both far and near, even towards his enemies.

One should crush anger and animosity, for they indeed cause harm; when all of them are stilled, loving-kindness becomes perfectly pure.

The mind of the good is not agitated by the words of the wicked; for it is not possible to heat the river Ganges with a torch of grass.

By blame and praise, the minds of the good are not disturbed; just as a rocky mountain never wavers in the winds.

Small boats in the river are tossed high and low by the waves; large ships do not tremble, even with great waves.

In the world, by praise and blame, the wicked are elated and depressed; but the wise and good do not waver, even by great blame and praise.

Lābhālābhā sukhādukkhā,

He is unshaken by gain and loss, pleasure and pain, fame and disgrace, praise and blame, for these are impermanent; he stands firm like a solid rock.

What can the wicked do to one who cultivates the highest forbearance? Like fire falling on ground without grass, it is extinguished of itself.

Let one fashion oneself out of fear of death; let one not censure another out of hatred. What is the purpose of censuring another?

Let one not censure another out of hatred, for censuring others is not superior. Let one not fear another as one would a snake or a tiger; rather, let one fear one's own mental suffering.

Relying on one worthy of respect, who frees many from evil; with composure and serenity, one attains worthiness of respect.

Relying on one worthy of respect, who increases merit for many; one attains worthiness of respect through wisdom and the guarding of virtue.

Dujjanaṃ domanassena, ahibyaggheva bhāyati.

The peaceful, virtuous, and wise person is restrained by moral shame and dread; the wicked person, however, fears with mental suffering, as one fears a snake or a tiger.

A wise person should not tame others with harsh words; having tamed oneself, one should advise others gently.

Whether the mind is gentle or harsh, let speech never be coarse; therefore, in giving advice and so forth, one should guard against a stubborn mind.

For the sake of one's own admonition, one should learn what the Buddha has spoken; and out of compassion for others, wishing to guide, one should instruct.

One should not censure another for anything concerning their learning or practice; one's own self-restraint is better. To this, O offspring of the Conqueror, one should indeed be devoted.

One should not reveal one's own hatred, greed, conceit, or defilement. Let not others think, upon seeing it, 'Has this person truly been ordained for a long time?'

Even if enemies were to sever you with a saw, you should not bear them ill will. O offspring of the Conqueror, accept this advice of the Lord of Sages.

Even an extremely resentful enemy causes suffering only externally; therefore, do not create suffering in your mind regarding that matter.

Bhavantaraṃ naanveti, sakammunā gato hiso.

Even an enemy, strongly bound by enmity, is a maker of suffering in this life only. He does not follow one to another existence; indeed, he has gone according to his own kamma.

Dukkhāvaho bhavebhave;

Mahānattha karaṃ dosaṃ,

Kasmā vaḍḍheti cetasi.

Hatred, having oppressed one here, brings suffering in existence after existence. Why does one increase in one's mind this hatred, which causes great harm?

Mahā nattha karaṃ dosaṃ, nibbāyatu sa cetasi.

With the sprinkling of the cool water of loving-kindness, remembering the Conqueror's advice, may that anger, which causes great harm, be extinguished in the mind.

Dhammapālo khami tātaṃ, kapindo kandaro pataṃ.

The elephant Chaddanta endured the evil hunter; Bhūridatta, the snake charmer; Dhammapāla forgave his father; the monkey king, the fall into the ravine.

Nāthassa pāramiṃ khantiṃ, saraṃ dhīro titikkhatu.

Remembering the Protector's perfection of patience, who for innumerable existences endured the faults of others, let the wise one likewise endure.

Tuvaṃ paṭicca maññantu, sāsane sappayojanaṃ.

May they think, on account of you, 'By dwelling long in the Dispensation, he has thus become faultless and is of purpose within the Dispensation.'

Let them not think, on your account, 'Though they have dwelt long in the Dispensation, they are just like us,' and, having approached you, consider you useless in the Dispensation.

That hatred, which burns two—oneself and others—should be abandoned in all cases by one who desires the welfare of others.

Paravajjaṃ khametabbaṃ, nasādhu aññavirodhito.

Life stands not by what is given by others, nor is it under one's own control. The fault of another should be endured; opposing another is not good.

Khamantotu nagaṇhāti, jānaṃ jegucchitāniti.

The fool, being intolerant, seizes upon disgusting things, abuse, and blame; but the patient one does not seize them, knowing them to be disgusting.

Words given by another, no one accepts as a gift. Likewise, one should not grasp harsh words; not grasping them, the mindful one should endure.

Sabbe uppatitā dosā, khantipatvā samanti te.

Like the waves and ripples of a river, having reached the bank, subside here; so too all arisen angers, having met with patience, subside.

What will one who is not mad do with the speech of one maddened by anger? Indeed, by speaking such words, he himself would become mad.

Gāmamajjhe aḷakkova, tathā māhohi taṃ jaha.

The angry, intolerant one, perceiving others as corrupt, and fearful, is like a rabid dog in the middle of a village. Do not be like that; abandon it.

Parattha sattha micchanto,

Khanti mettañca bhāvaye.

Abounding in loving-kindness, of patient conduct, fearless in all situations, desiring the welfare of others, one should cultivate patience and loving-kindness.

Abuse and blame from others ripen for them alone, not going to another, like dust thrown against the wind. What heavy thing can patience not endure?

Idha peccaca nīceyyo, naṃnayaṃ gaṇhi akkhamo.

The one who abuses and blames, afflicted by his own kamma, is of a low state both here and hereafter; being intolerant, he has not grasped this method.

Iti ñatvāva sappañño,

Akkosaṃ na garuṃ kare.

The abuse does not come to me; it brings harm to him alone. Knowing this, the wise one does not take abuse seriously.

61.

Māmittavasa manvehi, nibbikāro tuvaṃbhava.

Desiring to see you fall into a distorted state, the enemy attacks you greatly. Do not follow the sway of the enemy; you, be undistorted.

With the water of loving-kindness, both one's own anger and others' anger subside; with the sprinkling of loving-kindness towards all, one should extinguish the fire from everywhere.

Mettā toyena vāreyya, siyā nibbuti sabbadhi.

The fire of one's own anger and others' anger, standing in every direction, one should ward off with the water of loving-kindness; may there be coolness everywhere.

Mettambunāva nibbātu, saparaggi dvayaṃ bhusaṃ.

One should not let one's own fire spread, nor let another's fire approach. May the two fires, of oneself and of others, be thoroughly extinguished by the water of loving-kindness alone.

Nindaṃnindaṃ nakuppeyya, nasādiye thutiṃ thutiṃ.

The virtuous one is blamed, the virtuous one is praised. At blame, one should not be angered; in praise, one should not delight.

Vaḍḍhetuṃvā pasaṃsāya, garuṃkare na taṃdvayaṃ.

No skilled person can destroy virtue by blame, nor increase it by praise; one should not take those two seriously.

Taṃmuttassa tu etassa, atissaya pabhāvato.

Covered by the defilement of the cloud of anger, the moon of loving-kindness does not shine; but for one freed from that, its radiance is exceedingly great.

May one send forth loving-kindness as a gift to all, far and near; may one teach the Dhamma to those who are receptive; be a moon in every path.

Wash away the defilements of those one encounters, always bringing coolness with compassion. Making no distinction between high and low, be like water in every path.

When touched by the eight worldly conditions, be you established and unchanging, like the earth.

A rock never quarrels and is always unmoved by anyone. Likewise, cultivating loving-kindness and patience, one should hold great virtue in high regard.

Like a rock, may one be virtuous; may one endure harsh words. By retorting, anger increases; when not uttered, it indeed subsides.

Knowing that all beings desire happiness, just as I do, one should cultivate loving-kindness gradually towards the dear, the neutral, and the hostile.

All tremble at violence, all fear death; having made oneself the example, one should neither strike nor kill.

Pecca so nalabhesukhaṃ.

Whoever harms with violence those beings who desire happiness, while seeking his own happiness, will not obtain happiness hereafter.

May I be free from enmity, ill-will, and distress, and may I be happy. Likewise, may those who wish me well, the neutral, and the hostile also be so.

Sukhīhontūti bhāveyya, caje tesuca lagganaṃ.

Mothers, brothers, relatives, donors, and lay followers—one should cultivate the thought, 'May they be happy,' and abandon clinging to them.

Hatred is the distant enemy of loving-kindness; craving is the enemy close at hand. One should restrain craving regarding dear ones, and the one who is loving should restrain hatred regarding enemies.

May students and teachers in the same place be happy; may fellow practitioners also be happy, and may they bring welfare to one another.

May kings and ministers be happy, and the rulers in the village; may the deities also be happy, and through them may one be well-protected and happy.

By whom we, the inhabitants of the realm, are well-guarded and happy, may that righteous king be happy, radiant, and long-lived.

May these be free from enmity, free from ill-will, free from distress, and happy. May they maintain themselves. Thus should one cultivate in four ways.

In the eastern direction, may all beings be free from enmity, free from ill-will, happy, and free from distress. Thus should one cultivate.

Dvādasakkhattuṃ bhāveyya, sesāsupi ayaṃnayo.

In the eastern direction, with 'May all living beings...' and so forth, one should cultivate twelve times. This is the method for the remaining directions as well.

The four directions, the intermediate directions, below, and above—beings and living beings; creatures, individuals, and those with an individual existence; all women and men.

Noble ones and ignoble ones, deities, humans, and those in states of woe—may they be free from enmity, free from ill-will, free from distress, and happy in themselves.

The four directions, the intermediate directions, below, and above—thus the ten directions. Having delimited those twelve categories of beings, the wise one should cultivate towards those individuals.

There are five boundless and seven delimited applications, making twelve types of individuals. Having cultivated these in four ways, they become forty-eight.

The Miscellaneous Exposition on What Leads to Heedfulness

Saṃvijjanti [M 0.0200] dha lokasmiṃ,

Bahū jīvitakappanā;

Gahetvā patta muñcho yo,

Jīvikānaṃ sa lāmako.

In this world are found many ways of making a living. But he who, having taken up the bowl, becomes deluded, is the lowest of all who seek a livelihood.

Sukulāca tadupagā, kāmabhogā napekkhino;

Na bhayaṭṭā na iṇaṭṭā, neva ājīva kāraṇā.

They are from good families and have approached this life accordingly, not longing for sensual pleasures. They have not gone forth out of fear, nor due to debt, nor for the sake of a livelihood.

3.

Nālaṃva [M 0.0201] gihinā brahma, cariyāya akhaṇḍitaṃ;

Gharāvāso tisambādho, pabbajjāva nirālayā.

It is not possible for a householder to live the holy life completely unbroken. The household life is constricting, whereas the going forth is free from attachments.

4.

4.

Bhavapaṅkā [M 0.0202] pamuccāma, tivittiṇṇā bhayānakā;

Paṭipatti yimāyāti, katvā tadupagā ime.

Thinking, 'By this practice we shall be freed from the swamp of existence, having crossed the terrifying floods,' these practitioners have undertaken it.

5.

5.

Uttiṭṭhe nappamajjeyya, dhammaṃ caritaṃ sucare;

Dhammacārī sukhaṃseti, asmiṃloke paramhica.

One should arise and not be heedless; one should practice the Dhamma well. A practitioner of the Dhamma dwells happily in this world and in the next.

6.

Svāgatā [M 0.0203] vata tebhikkhū, pattā sambuddhaputta taṃ;

Gihi bandhana pucchijja, sukhitā sāsane ratā.

Welcome indeed, O bhikkhus! You have attained the state of being sons of the Perfectly Enlightened One. Having severed the householder's bonds, you are happy, delighting in the Dispensation.

7.

Katapuñña visesāva, ete suladdha dullabhā;

Chaṭṭetvāpi mahārajjaṃ, nedisaṃ laddha maññadā.

These are indeed ones who have made special merit, having well-obtained that which is difficult to obtain. Even having renounced a great kingdom, such a thing is not obtained otherwise.

8.

Svāgatā [M 0.0204] sugatī hontu, māduggatī pamādino;

Dussīlā ce gamissanti, apāyaṃ tibhayānakaṃ.

May those who have gone forth well reach a happy destiny; may the heedless not reach a woeful state. For if the immoral go forth, they will go to the threefold terrifying plane of misery.

9.

Gihibhogā [M 0.0205] parīhinno, sāmaññattañca dūbhato;

Paridhaṃsamāno pakireti, chavālātaṃva nassati.

Fallen from the pleasures of a householder and corrupt in his asceticism, he falls into ruin and scatters dust, perishing like a charnel-ground firebrand.

10.

Kuso yathā duggahito, hatthamevā nukantati;

Sāmaññaṃ dupparāmaṭṭhaṃ, nirayā yupa kaḍḍhati.

Just as a blade of kusa grass, wrongly grasped, cuts the hand, so the ascetic life, wrongly handled, drags one to hell.

11.

Yaṃkiñci [M 0.0206] sithilaṃ kammaṃ, saṃkiliṭṭhañca yaṃkataṃ;

Saṅkassaraṃ brahmacariyaṃ, nataṃhoti mahapphalaṃ.

Whatever action is slack, whatever deed is defiled, and a holy life lived with reluctance—none of this bears great fruit.

12.

12.

Kariyāce kariyā venaṃ, daḷhamenaṃ parakkame;

Sithilohi paribbajo, bhiyyo ākirate rajaṃ.

If something is to be done, one should do it. One should strive at it firmly. For a lax ascetic stirs up dust all the more.

13.

Iti [M 0.0207] vuttaṃ munindena, nussaraṃ ani vattito;

Sadā alina cittena, careyya buddhasāvako.

Thus was it spoken by the Lord of Sages; remembering this, unwavering, a disciple of the Buddha should always live with an unslothful mind.

14.

14.

Rāgaṃ asubhacintāya, dosaṃ mettāya vāraye;

Maraṇena dhajaṃmānaṃ, sambuddhe tikkha saddhiko.

One should ward off lust by contemplating unattractiveness, hatred by loving-kindness, and conceit by contemplating death, while being of sharp faith in the Perfectly Enlightened One.

15.

Asubhā kāmavitakkaṃ, mettā byāpāda takkitaṃ;

Vihiṃsaṃ karuṇāyeva, nivāreyya sadāsato.

Thoughts of sensuality should be warded off by unattractiveness, thoughts of ill-will by loving-kindness, and cruelty by compassion itself—thus should one always be mindful.

16.

Buddhāṇatti [M 0.0208] sadātīto, micchāvitakka pīḷito;

Pāpadhammehi saṃkiṇṇo, soniccāpāya gāmiko.

One who has always transgressed the Buddha's command, oppressed by wrong thoughts, and filled with evil qualities, constantly goes to a woeful destination.

17.

Dhovitvā pattimalāni, punātikkama saṃvuto;

Micchāvitakka sañchedī, dūro apāya gāmito.

Having washed away the stains of transgression, again restrained from transgression, cutting off wrong thoughts, one is far from the states of loss.

18.

Khīṇāsavatta [M 0.0209] buddhattaṃ, niyyānika ntarā yikaṃ;

Sīhanādaṃ catuṭṭhāne, vesārajjo jino nadi.

The Buddha, with taints destroyed, realized Buddhahood, which leads to liberation and is without obstacles; the Victor, confident, roared his lion’s roar in the four grounds of confidence.

19.

Sīlaṃ [M 0.0210] niyyānikaṃ nāma, āpatti antarāyikaṃ;

Antarāya manāpajja, niyyāneva patiṭṭhatu.

Virtue is called the way out, an offense is an obstruction; not incurring an obstruction, may one be established in the way out itself.

20.

20.

Niyyānikāca [M 0.0211] asubhā, subhasaññā ntarāyikā;

Antarāya manāpajja, niyyāneva patiṭṭhatu.

Contemplation of unattractiveness leads to liberation, while perceptions of beauty are an obstruction. Not incurring an obstruction, may one be established in the way out itself.

21.

Nānāpatti [M 0.0212] pakiṇṇopi, pārājikā vasesako;

So mityatta paṇīdhīhi, lajjīyeva visodhako.

Though stained by various offenses, with even a Pārājika offense remaining, one who is conscientious and has firm resolves is indeed a purifier.

22.

Alajjīkamma kiṇṇopi,

Saṃvejetvā sumittiko;

Lajjīyeva visodhento,

Matakova asodhako.

Even if filled with shameless deeds, having felt compunction, one becomes endowed with good friends. The conscientious one indeed purifies himself; the shameless one, like a corpse, is unpurified.

23.

Yo [M 0.0213] pubbeva pamajjitvā, pacchāso nappamajjati;

Somaṃ lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhāmuttovacandimā.

He who was formerly heedless but is later not heedless, illuminates this world like the moon freed from a cloud.

24.

Dhuraṃkatvā [M 0.0214] dhipatayo, yo puññesu parakkame;

Tassa niyyānikaṃ kammaṃ, kiṃnāmakaṃ nasijjhate.

For those leaders who, having taken up the burden, strive in meritorious deeds, how could their action that leads to liberation not succeed?

25.

Paccate [M 0.0215] munino bhattaṃ, thokaṃthokaṃ ghareghare;

Piṇḍikāyeva jīvantu, māpajjantu anesanaṃ.

The sage's food is prepared little by little, from house to house. Let them live on alms-gathering itself, and not fall into improper seeking.

Dhoveyyā pattimagāni, vuṭṭhāna desanambuhi;

Saṃvarissanti cittena, sīlaṃ dhotassa nimmalaṃ.

They should wash away the stains of transgression with the water of rising from an offense and confessing it. They will restrain themselves with their mind, and the virtue of one so cleansed will be stainless.

27.

Buddhāṇātikkamāpatti[M 0.0216], niggahe rāgadosake;

Natthi sañcicca āpatti, lajīva so pavuccati.

Transgression of the Buddha's command is an offense. Yet, in the suppression of greed and hatred, there is no intentional offense; such a one is called conscientious.

Vāṇijja kasikādīhi, nāhāreṭṭhi dhasāsane;

Dhura dvayaṃva kiccaṃ taṃ, nāññakiccehi hāpaye.

In this Dispensation, one should not seek sustenance through trade, farming, and the like. The twofold burden is the true duty; one should not neglect it for other tasks.

29.

Niggaṇheyya [M 0.0217] sakaṃcittaṃ, kiṭṭhādiṃ viya duppasuṃ;

Satimā sampajānoca, care sabbiriyāpathe.

One should control one's own mind, as a goad controls a poorly trained animal; mindful and clearly comprehending, one should conduct oneself in all postures.

Yathā [M 0.0218] thambhe nibandheyya, vacchaṃ damaṃ naro idha;

Bandheyyevaṃ sakaṃcittaṃ, satiyā rammaṇe daḷhaṃ.

Just as a man here would tether a calf to a post to be tamed, even so should one firmly bind one's own mind with mindfulness to an object of meditation.

31.

Adhisīlādhicittānaṃ, adhipaññāya sikkhanaṃ;

Bhikkhu kiccattayaṃ etaṃ, karontova subhikkhuko.

The training in higher virtue, higher mind, and higher wisdom—these are the threefold duties of a monk. One who fulfills them is truly a good monk.

32.

Pañcāṭṭha [M 0.0219] dasa sīlāni, nādhisīlaṃ taduttari;

Pātimokkhaṃ adhisīlaṃ, pabbatā dhika meruva.

The five, eight, or ten precepts are not the higher virtue, nor what is superior to it; the Pātimokkha is the higher virtue, as Mount Meru is higher than other mountains.

33.

Pātimokkhaṃ [M 0.0220] visodhento, appeva jīvitaṃ jahe;

Paññattaṃ lokanāthena, nabhinde sīlasaṃvaraṃ.

While purifying the Pātimokkha, one might even give up one's life; one should not break the restraint of virtue laid down by the Protector of the World.

34.

Sīlenā tikkamaṃ thullaṃ, pariyuṭṭhaṃ samādhinā;

Paññāyā nusayaṃ saṇhaṃ, kilesaṃ bhikkhu bhindati.

A monk shatters defilement: the gross transgression through virtue, obsession through concentration, and the subtle latent tendency through wisdom.

35.

35.

Sāsanassādi [M 0.0221] sīlaṃva, majjhe tassa samādhiva;

Paññāva pariyosānaṃ, kalyāṇāva imetayo.

Virtue is indeed the beginning of the Dispensation, concentration is its middle, and wisdom is its culmination—these three are truly excellent.

36.

Mahāpuññe [M 0.0222] ṭhitaṃ sīlaṃ, samādhi appanā gataṃ;

Catumagga yutā paññā, etaṃ sikkhattayaṃ mataṃ.

Virtue established in great merit, concentration that has reached absorption, and wisdom endowed with the four paths—this is considered the threefold training.

37.

37.

Sīlanalakkhaṇaṃ sīlaṃ, dussīlya dhaṃsanaṃ rasaṃ;

Hirottappa padaṭṭhānaṃ, suci paccupaṭṭhānakaṃ.

The characteristic of virtue is moral conduct, its function is destroying bad conduct, its proximate cause is shame and fear of wrongdoing, and its manifestation is purity.

38.

38.

Sasīlagutti [M 0.0223] nāthoca, dunnigaho visārado;

Dhammaṭṭhītīti pañcete, guṇā venayike matā.

Guarded by virtue and a protector, hard to censure yet skilled, and firm in the Dhamma—these five qualities are considered to belong to one trained in the Vinaya.

39.

39.

Ādi [M 0.0224] kalrāṇa saṃvedī, sīlamattaṭṭha bhikkhavo;

Uddhaṃ kalyāṇa lābhāya, alino anivattiko.

Knowing the good from the beginning, monks established for the sake of virtue are unslothful and unretreating for the sake of gaining the higher good.

40.

40.

Dhovitvā pattimalāni, vuṭṭhāna desana mbuhi;

Suddhasīle ṭhitoyeva, evaṃ cinteyya paññavā.

Having washed away the stains of transgression with the water of rising from an offense and confessing it, established in pure virtue, thus should the wise one reflect.

41.

Sampuddhorasa [M 0.0225] puttāva, buddhurojā nussāvanā;

Sambhūtā pitu dāyādā, puttānāma sabhāvato.

Like true sons born from the breast of the Enlightened One, nourished by the essence of the Buddha's teaching, born as heirs to the Father, they are called sons by their very nature.

42.

Khīraṃ pitvāva jīvanti, jātāpi idha puttakā;

Pariyatti jinakkhīraṃ, pitvāva jinaputtakā.

Just as children born here live by drinking milk, so the sons of the Victor live by drinking the milk of the Victor's teaching.

43.

Dāyoca [M 0.0226] nāma buddhassa, dhammāmisa vasādvidhā;

Maggañāṇā dayo dhammo, cattāro paccayāmisā.

The Buddha's heirs are indeed of two kinds: by way of Dhamma and material things. The Dhamma is path-knowledge and so forth, and the four requisites are the material things.

44.

Cira māmisa dāyādā, rājapūjādi gāhino;

Dāyā missaggahaṃ nicchi, saddhamma garuko jino.

For a long time there have been heirs of material things, receivers of honors from kings and so forth. The Victor, who esteems the true Dhamma, determined the non-acceptance of the material inheritance.

45.

45.

Lakkha [M 0.0227] kappa catussaṅkhya, kālaṃ vicita niccitaṃ;

Dhammadāyaṃ navindamhā, buddhaputtāpi yemayaṃ.

For four asaṅkhyas and a hundred thousand aeons of time, it was pondered and accumulated; yet we have not obtained the inheritance of the Dhamma, even though we are sons of the Buddha.

46.

46.

Buddhavārita dāyādā, saddhammadāya bāhirā;

Puttāpi satthudāsābhā, bhuttamattā hi dāsakā.

Heirs forbidden by the Buddha are outside the inheritance of the true Dhamma. Even though they are sons, they are like servants of the Teacher, for servants are merely those who have eaten their fill.

47.

47.

Bhaddanta [M 0.0228] rāhulasseva, dāyaṃ nopi adā jino;

Nādiyimhā pamādāya, taṃ dāyaṃ kusalantakaṃ.

The Victor did not give the material inheritance even to Venerable Rāhula; yet we, due to heedlessness, have not accepted that inheritance which leads to the wholesome.

48.

48.

Dhammadāyādā mebhikkhave tumhebhavatha,

Māāmisa dāyādā;

Iti vuttaṃ munindena,

Sāvakesu dayāvatā.

"O bhikkhus, be my heirs of the Dhamma, not my heirs of material things." Thus was it spoken by the Lord of Sages, who is compassionate toward his disciples.

49.

49.

Imāya [M 0.0229] buddhavācāya, buddhasantaka taṃ sare;

Dvinnaṃ āmisaa dāyāda, bhāvassaca nivāraṇaṃ.

By this word of the Buddha, one should remember that which belongs to the Buddha: the prevention of the state of being an heir of material things for both.

50.

50.

Rajje caṇḍālaputtāva, saddhammacakka vattino;

Puttā hontāpi dāyesmiṃ, nirāsā tiva ninditā.

Like the sons of an outcaste in a kingdom, though they are sons in the inheritance, they are despised and without hope; in contrast are the turners of the wheel of the true Dhamma.

51.

51.

Micchājīva [M 0.0230] samāpannā, accāsā paccayāmise;

Mahājānīya sampattā, moghakatvā tidullabhaṃ.

Engaged in wrong livelihood, with excessive desire for material requisites, having come to great loss, they make vain that which is exceedingly rare to obtain.

52.

52.

Gihikāme [M 0.0231] pahāyāgo, paravantosu laggito;

Gaṅgātiṇṇo taḷākamhi, nimuggovā tinindito.

Having abandoned household sensual pleasures, one is yet attached to the possessions of others; like one who has crossed the Ganges only to drown in a pond, one is greatly despised.

53.

53.

Cīvare [M 0.0232] piṇḍapāteca, paccaye sayanāsane;

Etesu taṇha mākāsi, mālokaṃ punarāgami.

In robes, almsfood, requisites, lodging, and seats—in these do not create craving, lest you return again to this world.

54.

54.

Itivuttā nusārena, paccavekkhaṇa suddhiyā;

Āmisesu hane āsaṃ, puttamaṃsu pamaṃ saraṃ.

In accordance with what has been said, through the purity of reflection, one should destroy desire for material things, remembering the simile of the son's flesh.

55.

55.

Seyyo [M 0.0233] ayoguḷo bhutto,

Tatto aggisikhūpamo;

Yañce bhuñjeyya dussīlo,

Raṭṭhapiṇḍaṃ asaññato.

Better to have eaten a ball of iron, heated and like a flame of fire, than that one who is immoral and unrestrained should eat the alms-food of the land.

56.

56.

Itivuttaṃ [M 0.0234] nucintento, vajje dussīla bhāvato;

Sīle ṭhitova bhuñjeyya, māditta guḷakaṃ gili.

Reflecting thus on what has been said, one should avoid the state of immorality. Established in virtue, one should eat as though swallowing a blazing ball.

57.

57.

Annāna matho pānānaṃ,

Khādanīyāna mathopi vatthānaṃ;

Laddhāna sannidhiṃ kariyā,

Naca parittase tāni alabhamāno.

Of foods and drinks, of edibles and also of clothes—having obtained them, one should not make a store, nor should one be anxious when not obtaining them.

58.

58.

Aññāhi [M 0.0235] lābhupanisā, aññā nibbāna gāminī;

Sakkāraṃ nābhinandeyya, viveka manubrūhaye.

Indeed, one path is for worldly gain, another leads to Nibbāna; one should not delight in honor, but should cultivate seclusion.

59.

59.

Akatvā [M 0.0236] āmise āsaṃ,

Saddhammeyeva āsiko;

Appamatto samāraddho,

Dhammadāyaṃ labhissati.

Having no desire for material things, devoted solely to the true Dhamma, heedful and resolute, one will obtain the inheritance of the Dhamma.

60.

60.

Pariyattiṃ vinā seyyaṃ, nalabhanti budhāapi;

Seyyatthikova sikkheyya, neva pūjādi kāraṇā.

Without scriptural learning, even the wise do not obtain what is better. One who desires what is better should train, and not for the sake of offerings and so forth.

61.

61.

Bhavanissaraṇatthaṃva[M 0.0237], sikkhe nā laggadūpamo;

Tathūpamāya sikkhanto, apāyesu patissati.

One should train for the sake of escaping from existence, not like a decoy. One who trains in such a manner will fall into the woeful states.

62.

62.

Sikkhitena amānatthaṃ, nasādhu mānathaddhiko;

Mudubhāvāya sikkhitvā, damento muduko bhave.

Training is for the sake of non-conceit; it is not good to be stiff with conceit. Having trained for gentleness, by taming oneself one should become gentle.

63.

63.

Rāgaṃ [M 0.0238] dosaṃ dhajaṃmānaṃ, sikkhantopi vivajjaye;

Daharāpi hi miyyanti, natthi vassaggato mataṃ.

Lust, hatred, and the banner of conceit—one should abandon them even while training. For even the young die; death is not determined by the number of one's years.

64.

64.

Saddhaṃtikkheyya buddhena, rāgaṃ asubha cintayā;

Maraṇena dhajaṃmānaṃ, dosaṃ mettāya vāraye.

One should sharpen faith through the Buddha, counter lust with contemplation of the unattractive, subdue the banner of conceit with mindfulness of death, and ward off hatred with loving-kindness.

65.

65.

Etehi [M 0.0239] caturakkhehi, ganthaṃ sikkheyya saṃ vuto;

Sikkhantassehi rakkhehi, nakoci saṃkilesiko.

Guarded by these four safeguards, one who is restrained should study the texts. For one who trains with these safeguards, there is no defiler.

66.

66.

Buddhavācampi [M 0.0240] sajjhāya, etepi manasīkara;

Vutto dhammavihārīti, ediso sāsane varo.

Recite the word of the Buddha, and bear these things in mind. Such a one is called a 'Dweller in the Dhamma' and is excellent in the Dispensation.

67.

67.

Garūna mupadesena,

Caturakkho susīlavā;

Appassutopi pāsaṃso,

Bhiyyoyeva bahussuto.

By the instruction of teachers, one is guarded in four ways and virtuous. Even one of little learning is then praiseworthy; how much more so one who is greatly learned.

68.

68.

Sātaṃ [M 0.0241] sevakkhaṇevappaṃ, taṃhetvā nantādukkhanti;

Dhīro āsaṃ hane kāme, khuradhāramadhūpame.

There is little pleasure in the moment of indulgence, and because of that, endless suffering. The wise person should destroy desire for sensual pleasures, which are like honey on a razor's edge.

69.

69.

Yodha kāme sukhaṃmaññi,

Na so dukkhā vimuccati;

Mātāhi byaggha manvento,

Vacho mutto kathaṃbhayā.

Whoever here considers sensual pleasures to be happiness is not released from suffering. Like a calf, released from its mother, that follows a tigress—how can it be free from fear?

70.

70.

Tiracchā [M 0.0242] peta laddhabbe, nāsaṃ kāmasukhe kare;

Bhāyitabba sukhaṃ tañhi, tasmiṃ laggā mahātapā.

In that which is obtainable even by animals and spirits, one should have no desire for sensual pleasure. Indeed, that pleasure is to be feared, for great torment is attached to it.

Sambuddhassa virodhiko.

May I not be the same as the world; being the same as it, what is there beyond? This world has become blind, hostile to the Perfectly Enlightened One.

151.

151.

Sambuddhassa vasaṃ nvetu, sanjhādi bhayatajjito;

Tabbasaṃyeva anvento, bhavatiṇṇo bhavissati.

Terrified by the fear of decay and so on, may one come under the sway of the Perfectly Enlightened One. By following his sway alone, one will cross over becoming.

152.

152.

Anattāti [M 0.0288] girā saccā, attāti vacanaṃ musā;

Musāya vivade loko, buddhena sacca vādinā.

'Not-self' is a true utterance; 'self' is a false statement. The world disputes with falsehood against the Buddha, the speaker of truth.

153.

153.

Turaṅgavajagāmamhā, purime camakya kānane;

Vasatā aggadhammena, therena racito ayaṃ.

This was composed by the elder dwelling in the foremost Dhamma, who was from the village of Turaṅgavaja and resided in the eastern Camakya forest.

154.

154.

Arimatteyya buddhassa,

Dhamma ssutakkhaṇe bhave;

Khīṇāsavo mahāpañño,

Puññena tena sāvakoti.

By this merit, may I be a disciple of the Buddha Ariya Metteyya, becoming one of great wisdom with taints destroyed at the very moment of hearing the Dhamma.

Kāyapaccavekkhaṇā

Contemplation of the Body

1.

1.

Nādikāla [M 0.0291] viparita, jana bhūtattha dassino;

Dātume kantikassādaṃ, satthu pādo timānito.

Highly honored are the feet of the Teacher, who, unperverted from beginningless time and seeing the true state of beings, gives the ultimate satisfaction.

2.

2.

Sīse [M 0.0292] pilandiyā modī, sambuddha caraṇa mbujaṃ;

Sādhu tuṭṭhikaraṃ brūmi, sukāya paccavekkhaṇaṃ.

Having joyfully placed on my head the lotus feet of the Perfectly Enlightened One, I shall speak on the Contemplation of the Body, a good and satisfying teaching.

3.

3.

Yoniso manasīkatvā, nādā viruddha maññitaṃ;

Sādhavo ta mudikkhantu, mayāpi mandabuddhino.

Let the virtuous, having attended wisely and without adopting contradictory views, examine this which has been composed even by me, one of dull intellect.

4.

4.

Icchitabbāna [M 0.0293] māyattā, tesañca cayabhāvato;

Tadākārena vattitā, kāyo jeguccha puñjako.

Because it is dependent on desirable things, because of their nature of accumulation and decay, and because it behaves accordingly, the body is a repulsive heap.

5.

5.

Punappunaṃva okkama, ñāṇa manto pavesiya;

Bāhiraṃva anālamba, ikkhaṇā paccavekkhaṇā.

The wise one penetrates by causing the mind to enter again and again; this seeing, without external support, is contemplation.

6.

6.

Maṃsacchanna [M 0.0294] ṭṭhirūpeva, manoja vāyu cālite;

Nārī gatāti yācintā, neva sāpaccavekkhaṇā.

Regarding that which is covered with flesh, seemingly firm, and moved by the mind-born wind—the thought, 'a woman has come,' is not contemplation.

7.

7.

Saññādiṭṭhica cittañca, vipallāsā imetayo;

Ta dākārena vattanti, avijjo ttharitā bhusaṃ.

Perception, view, and consciousness—these three are perversions. They proceed in that manner, being thoroughly overwhelmed by ignorance.

8.

8.

Āsā [M 0.0295] viparite yesaṃ,

Vipallāsāti tematā;

Āsā āsisanā vuttā,

Taṇhāyeva sabhāvato.

For whom hopes are perverted, they are therefore considered perversions. Hope is said to be aspiration, which by its very nature is craving.

9.

9.

Asubheva subhamiti, anicceeva niccato;

Dukkheyeva sukhaṃvāti, anattaniva attato.

In the impure, [the perception] 'it is pure'; in the impermanent, as permanent; in suffering, as pleasant; and in what is not-self, as self.

10.

10.

Saññāṇaṃ dassanaṃ cintā,

Dvādasā kārato tayo;

Diṭṭhisā cādi maggena,

Sesā sesehi vajjhitā.

Perception, view, and thought—these three have twelve aspects. The perversion of view is abandoned by the first path; the remaining ones are abandoned by the remaining paths.

11.

11.

Taṇhā [M 0.0297] tassi mametanti, māno maññi ahantica;

Yassi diṭṭhica attāti, papañcā nāmime tayo.

Craving is the thirst 'this is mine'; conceit is the conceiving 'I am'; and the view 'this is my self'—these three are named proliferations.

12.

12.

Papañcanti saṃsāraṃ, tasmā papañcanāmakā;

Bhavayante payojentā, mokkhaṃ nādaṃsu te ciraṃ.

They proliferate saṃsāra; therefore, they are named proliferations. Developing and engaging in them, they did not attain liberation for a long time.

13.

13.

Bhavapaṅke ni mujjantā, papañcānaṃ vasānugā;

Cirassaṃ dukkhitā honti, ārā nibbānato tiva.

Sinking in the mire of becoming, following the sway of proliferations, for a long time they are afflicted with suffering, very far indeed from Nibbāna.

14.

14.

Name [M 0.0298] nāhaṃ naattāti, etehi vivadaṃ kare;

Bhaṇḍantā vivadantā te, nibbānato adūrino.

With [the phrases] 'Not mine, not I, not self,' one should engage in dispute. Those who thus quarrel and dispute are not far from Nibbāna.

15.

15.

Vipallāse papañceca, dvepiete pahātave;

Sopaccavekkhitabbe vaṃ, kāyo jegucchapuñjako.

Perversions and proliferations, both these must be abandoned. Thus should this body be contemplated: a repulsive heap.

16.

16.

Kesā [M 0.0299] lomā nakhādantā,

Taṇhāyāpica gocarā;

Tasmā te daṭṭhukāmena,

Taṇhā nivāritā sadā.

Hair of the head, hair of the body, nails, and teeth are also the domain of craving. Therefore, by one who desires to see their true nature, craving should always be restrained.

17.

17.

Laggikā chaviyaṃyeva, taṇhā bāhiragocarā;

Tasmā ettha tacovāha, sambuddho na bahicchaviṃ.

Clinging to the skin, craving ranges externally. Therefore, here, the skin is a vehicle; the Perfectly Enlightened One is not the outer skin.

18.

18.

Esā [M 0.0300] tacapariyanta, padenāpi nivāritā;

Ato chavi manālamba, tacasīva manekare.

This [craving] is bounded by the skin and is restrained even by a word. Therefore, do not make the outer skin a support, nor make it like the hide.

19.

19.

Jigucchitāni chādeti, aṭṭhi maṃsa tacādini;

Ñāṇena chindi tabbāca, tasmā chavīti vuccati.

It covers disgusting things—bones, flesh, hide, and so on; and because it should be cut with wisdom, it is therefore called 'chavi' (skin).

20.

20.

Chaviṃ [M 0.0301] chetvā tacaṃ passe,

Taṃ chetvā maṃsakādayo;

Gabbhevatthūni dīpena,

Yathā paññāpadīpiko.

Having cut through the outer skin, one should see the hide; having cut through that, the flesh and so on—just as one with the lamp of wisdom sees the things within a chamber with a lamp.

21.

21.

Jeguccho chaviyā kāyo,

Asubhova subhāyate;

Nicchavā tacamattena,

Kathaṃ subhāyate ayaṃ.

The body, with its skin, is repulsive, yet being impure, it appears beautiful. When stripped of its outer skin, with only the hide, how can this appear beautiful?

22.

22.

Nhārubandho [M 0.0302] ṭṭhisaṅghāto, maṃsalohi ta limpito;

Chaviyāva vimoheti, tacacchanno imaṃ pajaṃ.

A framework of bones bound by tendons, smeared with flesh and blood; covered by the hide, it deludes this populace by its outer skin alone.

23.

23.

Vaṇṇa saṇṭhāna toceva,

Gandho kāsā sayehica;

Jegucchā paṭikulyāca,

Kesānāma na mepiyā.

In color, shape, texture, scent, their location and contents, they are disgusting and repulsive. Hairs, indeed, are not dear to me.

24.

24.

Ekekaṃ [M 0.0303] manasīkatvā, naye nicceva mādinā;

Bhāvetabbā samārambha, yathāpaññāyate tathā.

Having attended to each one individually according to the method of impermanence and so on, this undertaking should be developed just as it is understood by wisdom.

25.

25.

Pūritaṃ matthaluṅgassa, sīsaṭṭhipi jigucchitaṃ;

Mukha nāsakkhi kaṇṇādi, chiddā vachidda duddasaṃ.

Filled with brain matter, the skull too is repulsive; mouth, nose, eyes, ears, and so on—holes and gaps, foul to see.

26.

26.

Pūti [M 0.0304] vāyu vicarita, kucchiṭṭhantāni lohitaṃ;

Pittaṃ semhañca papphāsaṃ, hadayaṃ yakanampi dhī.

Behold: the contents of the belly, the intestines, blood, bile, and phlegm, the lungs, heart, and liver, through which foul winds move.

27.

27.

Anna pānaṃ manuññampi, kheḷa tinta madhopari;

Dantehi pisitaṃ svāna, vamathūva jigucchitaṃ.

Though food and drink are pleasant, moistened with spittle and with honey on top, when ground by the teeth and chewed, it is repulsive, like vomit.

28.

28.

Yāvatāyu [M 0.0305] adhotevā, māsaye gilitaṃ ṭhitaṃ;

Kimikūla samākiṇṇe, tahimevā sitāsitaṃ.

For as long as it remains unexpelled, the swallowed food stays in the stomach, which is infested with swarms of worms, both white and black.

29.

29.

Etaṃ udariyaṃ nāma, tamhā pakkāsayaṃ gataṃ;

Dinaccaye karīsantaṃ, sā sayaṃ taṃdvayampi dhī.

This is called the contents of the stomach; from there it goes to the intestine, at day's end becoming excrement. Behold, that person himself is attached to both.

30.

30.

Pakāsetvā [M 0.0306] paveseti, annapānaṃ mahārahaṃ;

Paṭicchanno niharati, tameva nto ṭhitaṃ jano.

A person brings in costly food and drink openly, but secretly expels that very thing that was within.

31.

31.

Pavese taṃ parivato,

Nihareko raho lino;

Manuññaṃva pavīsante,

Nikkhamante jigucchitaṃ.

Entering, it is surrounded; exiting, it is alone, hidden. Pleasant as it enters, disgusting as it exits.

32.

32.

Jeguccha [M 0.0307] paṭikulyāni, maṃsanhāru tacaṭṭhini;

Napiyāni na tuṭṭhāni, nevaitthī napūpiso.

Disgusting, repulsive things: flesh, tendons, hide, and bones; they are neither dear nor delightful, neither woman nor man.

33.

33.

Hattha pāda mukhādīni,

Natthaññāni jigucchitā;

Tatthā kumārikā kaññā,

Mohena atthisaññitā.

Hands, feet, mouth, and so on—there is nothing other than these repulsive things. Yet therein, through delusion, a 'maiden' or 'young woman' is perceived to exist.

34.

34.

Paccekaṃ [M 0.0308] vinibhuttesa, kesa loma nakhādisu;

Natthikaññā kumārīvā, sampiṇḍitesu sā kuto.

When separated individually, in hair, body hair, nails, and so on, no maiden or young woman exists. How then when they are aggregated?

35.

35.

Ākāsoyeva [M 0.0309] kāyāṅkhyo,

Tacādi parivārito;

Tathāsīsaṃ mukhaṃhattho,

Pādoru kaṭiādayo.

The so-called body is merely space, surrounded by skin and so on; likewise head, face, hand, foot, thigh, waist, and other parts.

36.

36.

Thambhādīsviva gehoti,

Piṇḍite svesu sammuti;

Kāyoti itthiposoti,

Saṃmuḷho tāyarajjati.

Just as there is a 'house' in relation to pillars and so on, a convention arises for these aggregated parts as 'body,' 'woman,' or 'man'; the deluded one is attached to that.

37.

37.

Santaṃ [M 0.0310] cinteyya nāsantaṃ, santaṃ cintayato sukhaṃ;

Asantaṃ anucintento, nānādukkhehi tappati.

One should contemplate the real, not the unreal; for one contemplating the real, there is happiness. Contemplating the unreal, one is tormented by various sufferings.

38.

38.

Javatyā vijjamāneva, nāvijjā vijjamānake;

Tasmātaṃnāmako moho, taṇhāpica tadanvitā.

When swiftness is present, ignorance is not; when ignorance is present, swiftness is not. Therefore, that is named delusion, and craving is associated with it.

39.

39.

Puṃkāyovāthīkāyovā[M 0.0311], malāsucijigucchito;

Tassamaṃ natthigārayhaṃ, yvāmalampi malaṃkare.

Whether a male body or a female body, it is disgusting, filthy, and impure. There is nothing as blameworthy as that which makes even filth more filthy.

40.

40.

Natthi [M 0.0312] kāyasamoverī,

Mahānatthakaro cīraṃ;

Natthi kāyasamo vañco,

Asubhova subhāyate.

There is no enemy like the body, causing great harm for a long time. There is no deceiver like the body, for the impure appears beautiful.

41.

41.

Thīpuṃ saparakāyoti, passatipi napassati;

Jeguccha paṭikulyoti, sammā passati passati.

Seeing it as a woman's body, a man's body, one's own or another's body, one sees and yet does not see; but seeing it as disgusting and repulsive, rightly seeing, one truly sees.

42.

42.

Subhosubhoti [M 0.0313] maññantā,

Dhīti dhīti jinerite;

Lokālokā nadhīyesaṃ,

Bhavā bhavā vacārino.

Imagining beauty in the impure, and thinking 'wise, wise' regarding the Conqueror's teaching, they do not comprehend the world and what is not the world, wandering from existence to existence.

43.

43.

Bhiyyobhiyyova rāgaggi,

Subhosubhotipassato;

Mandomandova soaggi,

Dhīvadhīvavipassato.

For one who sees the impure as beautiful, the fire of lust grows more and more. For one who sees the impure as impure, that fire becomes weaker and weaker.

44.

44.

Bahussutopi [M 0.0314] bālova, asubhe subhamaññako;

Asubhoti vipassanto, appassutopipaṇḍito.

Even if very learned, one who imagines beauty in the impure is a fool. One who sees it as impure, even if unlearned, is wise.

45.

45.

Yoca sippāni jāneyya, satāni sahassānipi;

Kāyekajānanaṃ seyyo, yañce añña vijānaṃnaṃ.

Though one may know hundreds, even thousands, of crafts, better is the single knowledge of the body than any other knowledge.

46.

46.

Kāyamekampi [M 0.0315] naññāmi, buddhāladdhanayo api;

Sutāca paṇḍitātyamhā, yuttoyevā tilajjituṃ.

I do not know even this one body, though I have obtained the Buddha's method. Since I am called learned and wise, it is indeed fitting to be greatly ashamed.

47.

47.

Subhatoyeva maññāmi, evaṃ jigucchitampinaṃ;

Mañca ñe paṇḍito tyāhu, alamevātilajjituṃ.

I imagine as beautiful this thing that is so repulsive. And they call me wise; it is indeed enough to be greatly ashamed.

48.

48.

Kāye [M 0.0316] asubhasaññaṃyo, nalabhāmi kadācipi;

Suladdha sugato vādo, svārahovātilajjituṃ.

I never obtain the perception of impurity regarding the body. The Sugata's teaching has been well-gained, yet it is fitting for me to be greatly ashamed.

49.

49.

Kāyena saṃsarantopi, tadākāraṃ yathātathaṃ;

Bhavebhave ajānanto, mamāyitvāva taṃ cajiṃ.

Though wandering through saṃsāra with this body, I did not know its nature as it truly is. In existence after existence, not knowing, I regarded it as 'mine' and then abandoned it.

50.

50.

Kāyena [M 0.0317] saṃsarantopi, naññā kāya jiguccataṃ;

Niccupādā mamāyanto, piyāyitvāva taṃ cajiṃ.

Though wandering through saṃsāra with this body, I did not know the body's repulsiveness. Always clinging, regarding it as 'mine', and cherishing it, I then abandoned it.

51.

51.

Kubhāraṃ sārasaññāya, piyāyitvāva hiṃsakaṃ;

Anantadukkha māpādiṃ, vipallāso bhavebhave.

Perceiving essence in a foul burden, cherishing what is harmful, I have come upon endless suffering. Such is the perversion in existence after existence.

52.

52.

Mahājānīya [M 0.0318] pattoti, saṃvejetvā sakaṃmanaṃ;

Dirokata jinovādo, anivattita vīriyo.

Having realized my great loss, having stirred my own mind, let the Conqueror's teaching be realized with unremitting energy.

53.

53.

Adiṭṭhapubba metassa, tathākāraṃva passatu;

Kicca mañña mupekkhāya, saṃsāra bhaya bhīruko.

Let one who is fearful of the danger of saṃsāra see its nature in such a way as it has not been seen before; let him not neglect what must be done.

54.

54.

Yañhikiccaṃ [M 0.0319] apaviṭṭhaṃ, akiccaṃ pana kayirā;

Unnaḷānaṃ pamattānaṃ, tesaṃ vaḍḍhanti āsavā.

For those who neglect what should be done and do what should not be done, for those arrogant and heedless ones, the taints increase.

55.

55.

Yesañca susamāraddhā, niccaṃ kāyagatā sati;

Akiccaṃ te nasevanti, kicce sātata kārino;

Satānaṃ sampajānānaṃ, atthaṃ gacchanti āsavāti.

But for those whose mindfulness of the body is constantly well-undertaken, they do not pursue what should not be done and are ever-persistent in what should be done. For such mindful and clearly comprehending ones, the taints come to an end.

56.

56.

Thomentā [M 0.0320] soṇṇaṃ kāyora,

Mukhakkhi tyādinā imaṃ;

Ratte muṭṭhe karontete,

Aññejane sayaṃviya.

Praising this body as golden, citing its 'mouth,' 'eyes,' and so on, they, being impassioned, create confusion in other people, just as in themselves.

57.

57.

Kāyasobhya pakāsetā,

Vācā ve māradesanā;

Tadasobhya pakāsetā,

Vācā sammuddha desanā.

Speech that proclaims the body's beauty is indeed the teaching of Māra; speech that proclaims its ugliness is the teaching of the Perfectly Enlightened One.

58.

58.

Asubhoti [M 0.0321] jinuddiṭṭhaṃ, kāyaṃ subhoti gāhino;

Saṃmuḷhāte na muccanti, bhavā buddha virodhino.

Those who grasp the body as beautiful, which was declared by the Conqueror to be impure—those deluded ones, opponents of the Buddha, are not freed from becoming.

59.

59.

Asubhoti jinuddiṭṭhaṃ, kāyaṃ tatheva gāhino;

Paṇḍitā teva muccanti, bhavā buddhamatānugā.

Those who grasp the body just as it was declared by the Conqueror to be impure—those wise ones, followers of the Buddha's teaching, are indeed freed from becoming.

60.

60.

Sodhentelaṅkaronteva[M 0.0322], malāsavantikāyato;

Alaṃ kāyavisodhena, bālova taṃ garuṃ karo.

Cleansing and adorning this body from which filth oozes—enough with such bodily purification! The fool holds it in high esteem.

61.

61.

Gopenteva arogāya,

Kāyo rogenasaṃvase;

Gāyaguttaṃ mudhāyeva,

Cittaguttaṃva sātthakaṃ.

Though one guards it for the sake of health, the body dwells with disease. Guarding the body is indeed in vain; guarding the mind is truly meaningful.

62.

62.

Candanādi [M 0.0323] vilittopi,

Muttomaṇi vibhūsito;

Taṃsabhāvova sokāyo,

Vissavanto tatotato.

Though anointed with sandalwood and the like, and adorned with pearls and gems, that body, by its very nature, oozes filth from here and there.

63.

63.

Patiteca [M 0.0324] apatite,

Viseso natthi kiñcipi;

Kāyo cemanuñño tamhā,

Patitopi tathāsiyā.

Between the fallen and the unfalled body, there is no difference whatsoever. Therefore, if this body is pleasing, then when fallen it should be likewise.

64.

64.

Kāyo manussajātīnaṃ, tiracchāna ttabhāvato;

Jegucchita tarohoti, dubbisodhoca dubbharo.

The body of human beings, compared to that of animals, is more repulsive, difficult to cleanse, and difficult to bear.

65.

65.

Yathājātena [M 0.0325] kāyena, sakkā viharituṃ naca;

Paccahaṃ sodhanīyoca, dhovana majjanādibhi.

It is not possible to live with this body just as it is born; it must be cleansed daily by washing, bathing, and other such means.

66.

66.

Rattaṃ pātuṃ chaviṃ chetvā, sakkā ḍaṃsādayopinaṃ;

Chetvā maṃsa ṭṭhikādīni, dhīro nālambituṃ kathaṃ.

Even gadflies and the like are able to pierce the skin to drink its blood. Seeing it as something to be cut through—flesh, bones, and so on—how could a wise person cling to it?

67.

67.

Lagganti [M 0.0326] chavimatte ye, makkhikā sedapā yathā;

Thīpuṃ mukhādi saññāya, te pamuḷhā mahātapā.

Those who cling to mere skin by means of perceptions such as 'woman's face' or 'man's face'—like flies that drink sweat—are deluded and greatly tormented.

68.

68.

Cārī agocare kāme, laggālepe kapīriva;

Bahūhi pīḷitā rīhi, maranti atidukkhino.

Wandering in an improper pasture of sensual pleasures, like monkeys stuck in birdlime, afflicted by many sufferings, they die in great misery.

69.

69.

Rāgāriṃ [M 0.0327] dujjayaṃ jeyyuṃ, jayabhummāsubhe carā;

Sītānissita laṭukī, senakaṃva mahabbalaṃ.

One should conquer lust, a foe hard to defeat, by dwelling on the unattractive, on the ground of victory; like a quail freed from a snare, and like the mighty Senaka.

70.

70.

Kāyadhi [M 0.0328] ggocaro veso,

Jayabhūbuddha duttiyā;

Ettheva gocarā hontu,

Mābho kāme jayatthikā.

The body is indeed the proper range, the second domain of the Victorious Buddha. Let this alone be your range; do not, sirs, be desirous of victory in sensual pleasures.

71.

71.

Kāyā [M 0.0329] subhaṃ vipassantu, dibba kkhināpya passiyaṃ;

Āyatiṃ maggalābhāya, taṃ dassanaṃ bhavissati.

Let one discern the body as unattractive, as something unfit to be seen even with the divine eye; for the future attainment of the path, that vision will be.

72.

72.

Dhīcakkhunāva dhikkāyaṃ, passe na maṃsacakkhunā;

Ummilitvāva dhīcakkhuṃ, vivekaṭṭho udikkhatu.

Let one see this contemptible body with the eye of wisdom, not with the eye of flesh. Having opened the eye of wisdom, let one who abides in seclusion look on.

73.

73.

Pañcaṅgāni [M 0.0330] yathā kummo, cakkhādīni nigūhaye;

Verī labhatu mokāsaṃ, pañcadvārā arakkhitā.

Just as a turtle withdraws its five limbs, so one should conceal the eyes and so on. An enemy finds an opportunity when the five doors are unguarded.

74.

74.

Cakkhurūpena [M 0.0331] saṃvāsā, rāgaputtaṃ vijāyati;

Mahānatthakaro soca, saṃvāsaṃ tena vāraye.

From association with eye and form, the offspring of lust is born; and it causes great harm, so one should avoid that association.

75.

75.

Rūpādīsusañjantīti, sattā itthyādi saññāya;

Natveva khandhasaññāya, taṃsaññihi virāgino.

Beings are attached to forms and so on through the perception of 'woman' and so on, but not indeed through the perception of the aggregates; those with the latter perception are dispassionate.

Āsaṃ chetvā sukhaṃseti,

Āsāya dukkhitā pajā.

A wise person should cut off longing, both for one's own body and for another's. Having cut off longing, one dwells happily; people suffer because of longing.

Nirāso sukhito hoti,

Anirāsotidukkhito.

In seeing, hearing, bodily contact, and in sexual intercourse, one without longing is happy; one with longing suffers greatly.

Whose outside is cleansed, but who has not vomited the repulsive heap within; knowing that body as it is, one's own attachment would become slight.

Desiring dispassion for the body, one should indeed see its interior; seeing the inner, unstirred, one obtains release from saṃsāra.

Santosanto vipassanto, navānavāyatiṃbhave.

Beings are attached to what is external, taking essence as essence and claiming it as 'mine'; but the good person, being content and discerning, is not renewed in future existence.

Enough, enough with this body, from which impurity upon impurity oozes! Let the mind be led to a beautiful, beautiful state, a mind also well-adorned.

From association, affections are born; following on affection, this suffering arises. Seeing the danger born of affection, one should wander alone like a rhinoceros's horn.

Piyavippayogaṃ vijigucchamāno,

Eko care khaggavisāṇa kappo.

There is play and delight in the midst of companions, and for children there is great love; detesting separation from the beloved, one should wander alone like a rhinoceros's horn.

Vaṃsakaḷirova asajjamāno,

Eko care khaggavisāṇa kappo.

Like a spreading bamboo entangled, so is the longing for sons and wives; like a bamboo shoot unattached, one should wander alone like a rhinoceros's horn.

For one desiring a sensual pleasure, if that should succeed for him, surely that mortal becomes joyful-minded, having obtained what he wishes.

But if for that desiring person, for that being in whom craving has arisen, those desires diminish, he is afflicted as if pierced by a dart.

He who avoids sensual pleasures, as one would a snake's head with the foot, being mindful, overcomes this entanglement in the world.

Fields, property, ponds, cattle and horses, slaves and servants, women, relatives, and various sensual pleasures—the person who is greedy for these...

Weaknesses overpower him, dangers crush him; then suffering follows him, like water a broken boat.

Therefore, a being, ever mindful, should avoid sensual pleasures. Having abandoned them, one should cross the flood, like one who, having bailed out the boat, has reached the far shore.

Kāmato vippamuttassa,

Natthi soko kuto bhayaṃ.

From craving, sorrow is born; from craving, fear is born. For one who is completely freed from craving, there is no sorrow, whence fear?

One who dwells contemplating the attractive, unrestrained in the senses, immoderate in food, lazy, and of low energy—Māra indeed overthrows him, as the wind a weak tree.

One who dwells contemplating the unattractive, well-restrained in the senses, moderate in food, faithful, and with aroused energy—Māra indeed cannot overthrow him, as the wind a rocky mountain.

Just as rain penetrates a poorly thatched house, so lust penetrates an undeveloped mind.

Those who are weakened by the fire of lust reflect in this way; having gained a footing in the Teaching, they are without longing, not indolent, and find delight.

Even having obtained the Buddha's teaching, one should not say, 'I am not capable, I am new.' Not dispelling hatred and craving, not performing meritorious deeds, not forgiving the faults of others—when will one become ripe?

Craving, which conditions the body, is low, and it makes one lower and lower; faith, which conditions the mind, is high, and it makes one higher and higher.

Craving delights in what is seen, while faith is devoted to good conduct; craving wanders scattered, while faith moves clearly.

Craving, defiling the mind, brings endless suffering that follows; faith, gladdening the mind, brings happiness that follows oneself.

Craving and ignorance are the two roots of the poisonous tree of saṃsāra; with all devotion, the hearing of the True Dhamma bears two sweet fruits.
