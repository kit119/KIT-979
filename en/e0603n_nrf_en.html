Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Lakkhaṇāto

By Way of Characteristic

Buddhathomanāgāthā

Verses in Praise of the Buddha

1.

1.

Bhavābhavesu [M 0.0019] nekesu,

Pūresi tiṃsapāramī;

Cariyāyoca sambuddho,

Pariccāgejahaṃsadā.

In many states of existence, He fulfilled the thirty perfections; and the Perfectly Enlightened One, through his conduct, was always abandoning in relinquishment.

2.

2.

Vessantarattabhāvamhi[M 0.0020],

Ṭhitomaddiṃ pisoadā;

Nijaṃkaṇhājinaṃjāliṃ,

Viyaṃvaṅkatapabbate.

In his existence as Vessantara, he gave away Maddī; he also gave his own children, Kaṇhājinā and Jāli, as if discarded on Mount Vaṅkata.

3.

3.

Tatocavitvātusite,

Pureuppajjipuññavā;

Rūpādidasaṭhānehi[M 0.0021],

Aññedeveatikkami.

Having passed away from there, the meritorious one was reborn in the Tusita realm; in ten respects beginning with form, he surpassed the other devas.

4.

4.

Cakkavāḷasahassehi,

Dasahāgammasabbaso;

Yācitodevabrahmūhi,

Buddhabhāvamanonato.

Entreated by devas and brahmās who had come from ten thousand world-systems, he inclined his mind toward the state of Buddhahood.

5.

5.

Vilokanānisopañca[M 0.0022],

Viloketvāsirindharo;

Tatocavitvāmāyāya,

Uppajjisākiyekule.

The glorious one, having made the five great surveys, passed away from there and was reborn in the Sakyan clan from Māyā.

6.

6.

Visākhapuṇṇameseṭṭhe[M 0.0023],

Vijāyilumbinīvane;

Sīhanādaṃpanādento,

Sattapādaṃgatotadā.

On the excellent full-moon day of Vesākha, he was born in the Lumbinī grove; uttering a lion's roar, he then took seven steps.

8.

8.

Virociṃsutadākāse[M 0.0024],

Tārocandadivākarā;

Ratanānicabhūmaṭṭhā,

Nānāpupphācapupphare.

Then in the sky shone the stars, the moon, and the sun; and on the ground were jewels, and various flowers blossomed.

8.

8.

Sītāyanti avīcaggī[M 0.0025],

Khuppipāsācasohitā;

Lokantaremahāpabhā,

Andhāpassantitāvade.

The fires of Avīci grew cool, and hunger and thirst were allayed; a great light appeared in the Lokantarika hells, and then the blind were able to see.

9.

9.

Aghaṭṭitāvāditāni[M 0.0026],

Tūriyānicaambare;

Madhurenapanādiṃsu,

Yasaṃsarenatāvade.

And in the sky, musical instruments played without being struck; they resounded then with a sweet sound, proclaiming his glory.

10.

10.

Thutigītānigāyantā,

Devācakamalāsanā;

Sammodentāvaaṭṭhaṃsu[M 0.0027],

Buddhaṅkurassasantike.

Singing songs of praise, devas and those on lotus seats, rejoicing, stood near the Buddha-to-be.

11.

11.

Kumārocārudhātīhi,

Tositārājamandire;

Vuddhosisusukhaṃkhemaṃ,

Sakyakaññānamantare.

The prince, attended by charming nurses, grew up in the royal palace, very happy and secure among the Sakyan maidens.

12.

12.

Soḷasavassikosakya[M 0.0028],

Nandanopitarāsatā;

Rājatteabhisittosi,

Modesituvanattaye.

At sixteen years of age, the delight of the Sakyans, dear to his father, was consecrated in kingship, bringing delight to the triple world.

13.

13.

Nandanānaṃsovaṇṇa[M 0.0029],

Vaṇṇasobhaṃyasodharaṃ;

Sokāresimahesittaṃ,

Lokanettarasāyanaṃ.

He made Yasodharā—who was radiant with a complexion like gold from the Nandana grove, an elixir for the world's eyes—his chief queen.

14.

14.

Ekūnatiṃsamevasse,

Visākhapuṇṇamevare;

Sahasenāyauyyāsi,

Yānenuyyānamaṇḍalaṃ.

In his twenty-ninth year, on the excellent full-moon day of Vesākha, he went out with his retinue by chariot to the pleasure grove.

15.

15.

Nimittecaturodisvā[M 0.0030],

Puttassacavijāyanaṃ;

Sutvāubbiggacittena,

Naramitthapuretahiṃ.

Having seen the four signs, and having heard of the birth of his son, with an agitated mind he went forth from the city.

16.

16.

Paccāgammapuraṃtamhā[M 0.0031],

Pattopāsādamaṇḍalaṃ;

Nippajjidevarājāva,

Sayanesomahārahe.

Returning to the city from there, he reached the palace grounds; like the king of the devas, he lay down on a magnificent couch.

17.

17.

Sundarītaṃpurakkhitvā[M 0.0032],

Naccagītenatosayuṃ;

Evaṃpisonaramitvā,

Āhunikkhamamānaso.

Beautiful women, placing him before them, tried to please him with dance and song; yet even so, not taking delight, his mind was set on renunciation.

18.

18.

Pathamaṃrāhulaṃputtaṃ,

Passitvānikkhamāmahaṃ;

Iticintiyamāneso,

Agājāyānivesanaṃ;

“First I will see my son Rāhula, and then I will go forth.” Thinking thus, he went to his wife's chamber.

19.

19.

Thapetvāpādadummāre[M 0.0033],

Gīvamantopavesayaṃ;

Olokayaṃsapassittha,

Nippannaṃtaṃsamātaraṃ.

Placing his foot on the threshold and stretching his neck inside, he looked and saw the child lying with his mother.

20.

20.

Gaṇheyyaṃtaṃapanetvā[M 0.0034],

Bāhuṃcedeviyāahaṃ;

Nadadenikkhamokāsaṃ,

Pabujjhitvāyasodharā.

“If I were to take the child, having moved aside the queen's arm, Yasodharā would awaken and not give me the opportunity to go forth.”

21.

21.

Buddhapattovapasseyyaṃ,

Rāhulaṃiticintiya;

Kaṇḍakaṃāruhitvāna,

Tamhāchannenanikkhami.

Thinking, “I will see Rāhula only after I have become a Buddha,” he mounted Kaṇṭhaka and departed from there with Channa.

22.

22.

Dvārāsayāvivariṃsu[M 0.0035],

Devatādvāramaṇḍalaṃ;

Ratanukkāsahassāni,

Jālayiṃsumarūtahiṃ.

The deities themselves opened the city gates; there the Maruts lit thousands of jeweled torches.

23.

23.

Mānikkhamassusiddhattha,

Itotvaṃsattamedine;

Bhavissasecakkavattī[M 0.0036],

Itinīvāripāpimā.

“Do not go forth, Siddhattha! In seven days from now you will become a wheel-turning monarch.” Thus the Evil One tried to prevent him.

24.

24.

Tagghajānāmitaṃmāra,

Idhamātiṭṭhapakkama;

Itimāraṃ palāpetvā,

Vissaṭṭhosoabhikkami.

“Indeed, Māra, I know that. Do not stand here! Go away!” Thus, having driven Māra away, he resolutely went forward.

25.

25.

Patvānomanadītīraṃ[M 0.0037],

Punāgamesituragaṃ;

Channañcanagaraṃ bimbā;

Devilāpaṭivedituṃ.

Having reached the bank of the Anomā River, he sent his horse and Channa back to the city to inform Queen Bimbā of the sorrowful news.

26.

26.

Bandhitaṃyasodharāya,

Moḷiṃsugandhavāsitaṃ;

Chetvākhaggenatiṇhena,

Ukkhipitthatamambare.

His top-knot, tied by Yasodharā and made fragrant, he cut off with a sharp sword and threw it into the sky.

27.

27.

Aṭṭhaseṭṭhaparikkhāre[M 0.0038],

Dhāretvābrahmunābhate;

Hitvārājārahaṃdussaṃ,

Samaṇatthamupāgami.

Donning the eight excellent requisites brought by a brahmā, and having discarded the robes fit for a king, he undertook the life of a recluse.

28.

28.

Karontodukkaraṃkammaṃ[M 0.0039],

Chabbassāniparakkamo;

Oḷārikannapānehi;

Dehaṃyāpesiṭhītiyā.

The striving one performed difficult deeds for six years; for the maintenance of his body, he sustained it with coarse food and drink.

29.

29.

Visākhapuṇṇamehema,

Pātiṃpāyasapūritaṃ;

Sujātāyābhataṃgayha,

Nerañcaramupāgami.

On the full-moon day of Vesākha, having accepted the golden bowl filled with milk-rice brought by Sujātā, he approached the Nerañjarā River.

30.

30.

Nerañcarāyatīramhi[M 0.0040],

Sutvāpāyāsamuttamaṃ;

Paṭiyatthavaramaggena,

Bodhimaṇḍamupāgami.

On the bank of the Nerañjarā, having eaten the excellent milk-rice, he approached the Terrace of Awakening by the excellent, prepared path.

31.

31.

Jayāsanepallaṅkamhi[M 0.0041],

Ṭhitodhiṭṭhesidhītimā;

Navīriyaṃjahissāmi,

Apattoitibuddhataṃ.

Seated cross-legged on the seat of victory, the resolute one made this determination: “I will not give up my energy until I have attained Buddhahood.”

32.

32.

Devindodhamayaṃsaṅkhaṃ[M 0.0042],

Setacchattaṃpitāmaho;

Dhārayaṃvādentovīṇaṃ,

Pañcasīkhoṭhitotahiṃ.

The lord of devas blew his conch, the Great Father held the white parasol, and Pañcasikha stood there playing his lute.

33.

33.

Thutigītānigāyantī,

Aṭṭhāsuṃdevasundarī;

Gahetvāhemamañjūsā,

Surapupphehipūritā.

Celestial nymphs stood singing songs of praise, holding golden caskets filled with divine flowers.

34.

34.

Anatthamentesūriye[M 0.0043],

Mārasenaṃpalāpayī;

Aruṇuggamanekāle,

Pattosibuddhataṃvaraṃ.

Before the sun had set, he put Māra's army to flight; at the time of dawn's rising, he attained supreme Buddhahood.

35.

35.

Paṭṭhānaṃsammasantassa,

Nāthassadehatosubhā;

Chabbaṇṇabhānicchadhanti[M 0.0044],

Vidhāvanti tahiṃ tahiṃ.

As the Protector was contemplating the Conditional Relations, beautiful six-colored rays issued from his body, spreading in all directions.

36.

36.

Nīlānīlāpitāpītā,

Rattārattācanicchare;

Setāsetācamañciṭṭhā,

Tamhātamhāpabhassarā.

Blue rays, yellow rays, and red rays issued forth; white rays, crimson rays, and a brilliant, radiant mixture.

37.

37.

Buddhapattopibodheyya[M 0.0045],

Dhammagambhīrataṃjino;

Nisāmetvānadesetuṃ,

Nirussāhamupāgato.

Although the Conqueror had attained Buddhahood and understood the profundity of the Dhamma, having considered it, he became disinclined to teach.

38.

38.

Sahampatitadābrahmā[M 0.0046],

Desanāyābhiyācati;

Tenasopathamaṃseṭṭhaṃ,

Dhammacakkamadesayi.

Then the Brahmā Sahampati requested him to teach. Therefore, he taught for the first time the excellent Wheel of the Dhamma.

39.

39.

Ekavassovasambuddho[M 0.0047],

Varephagguṇapuṇṇame;

Sakyānaṃnagaraṃgañchī,

Sāvakehipurakkhato.

One year after his perfect enlightenment, on the excellent full-moon day of Phagguṇa, the Perfectly Enlightened One went to the city of the Sakyans, escorted by his disciples.

40.

40.

Samāgamamhiñātīnaṃ,

Vassāpesisusītalaṃ;

Mahāmeghañcadesesi[M 0.0048],

Vessantarassajātakaṃ.

At the gathering of his kinsmen, he caused a great, cool rain to fall and taught the Vessantara Jātaka.

41.

41.

Gantvāsāvakayugena,

Yasodharāyamandiraṃ;

Taṃtosetvānadesesi,

Candakinnarajātakaṃ.

Going with a pair of disciples to Yasodharā's dwelling, he pleased her and then taught the Candakinnara Jātaka.

42.

42.

Vasitvāsattamevasse[M 0.0049],

Tāvatiṃse silāsane;

Abhidhammena tosesi,

Devabrahmagaṇaṃbahuṃ.

Having spent the seventh rains-residence in Tāvatiṃsa, seated on the stone throne, he delighted the many hosts of devas and brahmās with the Abhidhamma.

43.

43.

Pattharaṃñāṇajālena,

Lokaṃlokesisabbadā;

Bodhetuṃbodhiyābhāya;

Bodheyyajanapaṅkajaṃ.

Always spreading the net of his knowledge, he surveyed the world in order to awaken, with the light of his enlightenment, the lotus-like people fit for awakening.

44.

44.

Bodhaneyyaṃjanaṃdisvā[M 0.0050],

Cakkavāḷaparaṃparā;

Gantvāpivibodhesi,

Buddhaseṭṭhosubodhimā.

Seeing people fit to be awakened, even across a succession of world-systems, the supreme Buddha, the greatly enlightened one, would go and awaken them.

45.

45.

Bakādikecabrahmāno,

Yakkhecāḷavakādike;

Purindadādikedeve,

Sūcilomādirakkhase.

Brahmās such as Baka, yakkhas such as Āḷavaka, devas such as Purindada, and rākkhasas such as Sūciloma—

46.

46.

Jāṇusoṇādikeneke[M 0.0051],

Brāhmaṇecāvikakkhaḷe;

Cūḷodarādikenāge,

Dhanapālādikegaje.

many harsh brahmins such as Jāṇussoṇi, nāgas such as Cūḷodara, and elephants such as Dhanapāla—

47.

47.

Buddhāmayaṃtimaññante,

Titthiyenekaladdhike;

Damesidamataṃseṭṭho,

Paravādappamaddano.

and many sectarians with their followings who thought, “We are the Buddhas”—all these the supreme one, the crusher of opposing doctrines, the Deathless One, tamed.

48.

48.

Anubyañjanasampanno[M 0.0052],

Esabāttiṃsalakkhaṇo;

Byāmappabhāparikkhitto,

Ketumālāsilaṅkhato.

He is endowed with the minor characteristics and the thirty-two major marks, surrounded by a fathom-wide radiance, and adorned with the glorious cranial ray.

49.

49.

Caturāsītisahassa[M 0.0053],

Dhammakkhandhesadesayi;

Pañcatāthīsavassāni,

Tilokassahitāyave.

He taught the eighty-four thousand aggregates of the Dhamma; for forty-five years he acted for the welfare of the three worlds.

50.

50.

Buddhakiccaṃkarontoso,

Vahaṃlokahitaṃvaraṃ;

Mocayaṃbandhanāsatte[M 0.0054],

Kālaṃkhepetisabbadā.

Performing the Buddha's duty, bringing supreme welfare to the world, he always spent his time releasing beings from their bonds.

51.

51.

Yahiṃ āsītikonātho,

Nibbutovaramañjake;

Mahāsālavaneramme,

Mallānaṃnigametahiṃ.

There, in the delightful great Sāla grove in the town of the Mallas, the Protector of the Three Worlds lay on the excellent couch, fully extinguished.

52.

52.

Aggikkhandhovalokasmiṃ[M 0.0055],

Jāletvānasasāvako;

Nibbāyitthasaṅkhatānaṃ,

Pakāsentoaniccataṃti.

Like a mass of fire that has blazed up in the world, he, together with his disciples, was extinguished, thus showing the impermanence of all conditioned things.

“Namotebhagavānātha[M 0.0056],

Namotejayataṃjaya;

Namotesirisampanna,

Namotelokanandana.”

Homage to you, Blessed One, Protector! Homage to you, Victor over victors! Homage to you, endowed with glory! Homage to you, delight of the world!

Buddhavandanā

Homage to the Buddha

1.

1.

Devalokā cavitvāna[M 0.0065],

Mahāmāyāya kucchiyaṃ;

Uppajji guruvāramhi,

Vandetaṃ sakyapuṅgavaṃ.

Having departed from the deva world, he arose in the womb of Mahāmāyā on a Thursday. I venerate that bull among the Sakyans.

2.

2.

Dasamā saccaye neso[M 0.0066],

Vijāyi mātukucchito;

Sukkavāre lumbiniyaṃ,

Vande taṃ lokapūjitaṃ.

After ten months had passed, he emerged victorious from his mother’s womb on a Friday in Lumbinī. I venerate him, the one honored by the world.

3.

3.

Cakkavattisiriṃ hitvā,

Mahāsinikkhamaṃ sudhī;

Nikkhamī candavāramhi,

Name taṃ munikuñjaraṃ.

Having abandoned the glory of a universal monarch, the wise one undertook the Great Renunciation; he went forth on a Monday. I pay homage to that elephant among sages.

4.

4.

Assatthabodhimūlamhi[M 0.0067],

Pallaṅke aparājite;

Patto sabbaññutaṃ nātho,

Name taṃ budhavāsare.

At the foot of the Assattha, the Bodhi tree, upon the unconquered throne, the Lord attained omniscience. We pay homage to him on Wednesday.

5.

5.

Pañcavaggiyamukhānaṃ[M 0.0068],

Devānaṃ migadāvane;

Sorivāre dhammacakkaṃ,

Vattesi taṃ jinaṃ name.

In the Deer Park, for the group of five ascetics and the devas, he set in motion the Wheel of Dhamma on a Saturday. I pay homage to that Victor.

6.

6.

Sabbasaṅkhatadhammānaṃ,

Pakāsiyaaniccataṃ;

Nibbutaṃaṅgavāramhi,

Namādhiamatandadaṃ.

Having proclaimed the impermanence of all conditioned phenomena, he attained final Nibbāna on a Tuesday. I pay homage to him, the giver of the Deathless.

7.

7.

Sūriyavāre soṇṇāya[M 0.0069],

Doṇiyā jinaviggaho;

Agginujjalito tassa,

Name dhātusarīrakaṃ.

On a Sunday, the Victor's body in a golden urn was set ablaze by fire. I pay homage to his bodily relics.

Uṇṇālomikanātha vandanā

Homage to the Lord with the Brow-Hair Tuft

Uṇṇālodhikanāthassa[M 0.0071],

Uṇṇāya bhamukantare;

Vajirāviya sobhanti,

Nikkhanti yo supaṇḍarā.

From the uṇṇā between the eyebrows of the Lord with the uṇṇā tuft, supremely white rays issue forth, shining like a diamond.

Vajiragghanakāyassa[M 0.0072],

Nāthassa dehato subhā;

Vajireyyā niccharanti;

Vijjūva gagaṇantare.

From the body of the Lord, a body solid as diamond, issue forth beautiful, diamond-like rays, like lightning in the sky.

Rinindamandahāsena,

Dāṭhāya niccharā pabhā;

Vipphūrisā disāsabbā,

Vajirāyanti paṇḍarā.

With the gentle smile of the Lord of Victors, radiance issues from his canine tooth, spreading to all directions, white and sparkling like diamonds.
