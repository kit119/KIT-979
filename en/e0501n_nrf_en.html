Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Paṭṭhānuddesa dīpanīpāṭha

An Exposition of the Outline of the Paṭṭhāna

1. Hetupaccayo

1. The Root Condition

Katamo [M 0.0465] hetupaccayo. Lobho hetupaccayo. Doso, moho, alobho, adoso, amoho hetu paccayo.

What is the root condition? Greed is a root condition. Hatred, delusion, non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion are root conditions.

Katame dhammā hetupaccayassa paccayuppannā. Lobha sahajātā cittacetasikā dhammā ca rūpakalāpādhammā ca dosasaha jātā mohasahajātā alobhasahajātā adosasaha jātā amohasahajātā cittacetasikā dhammā ca rūpakalāpā dhammā ca hetupaccayato uppannā hetupaccayuppannā dhammā.

Which phenomena are conditionally arisen by the root condition? Consciousness, mental factors, and groups of matter co-arisen with greed; consciousness and mental factors co-arisen with hatred; consciousness and mental factors co-arisen with delusion; and consciousness, mental factors, and groups of matter co-arisen with non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion—these are the phenomena conditionally arisen by the root condition.

Sahajātarūpakalāpā nāma sahetukapaṭisandhikkhaṇe kammajarūpāni ca pavattikāle sahetukacittajarūpāni ca. Tattha paṭisandhikkhaṇo nāma paṭisandhicittassa uppādakkhaṇo. Pavattikālo nāma paṭisandhicittassa ṭhitikkhaṇato paṭṭhāya yāva cutikālaṃ vuccati.

Co-arisen groups of matter refers to matter produced by kamma at the moment of rebirth-linking with roots, and also matter produced by consciousness with roots during the course of existence. Herein, the moment of rebirth-linking is the arising moment of the rebirth-linking consciousness. The course of existence is said to be the period from the static moment of the rebirth-linking consciousness up to the time of death.

Kenaṭṭhena hetu, kenaṭṭhena paccayoti. Mūlaṭṭhena hetu, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayoti. Tattha mūlayamake vuttānaṃ lobhādīnaṃ mūladhammānaṃ mūlabhāvo mūlaṭṭho nāma. So mūlaṭṭho mūlayamakadīpaniyaṃ amhehi rukkhopamāya dīpitoyeva.

In what sense is it a root (hetu), and in what sense a condition (paccayo)? It is a root in the sense of being a foundation, and a condition in the sense of being supportive. Herein, the root-nature of the root-phenomena, such as greed, mentioned in the Root-Pair, is what is called the 'root-sense'. That root-sense has already been explained by us in the Exposition of the Root-Pair with the simile of a tree.

Api ca eko puriso ekissaṃ itthiyaṃ paṭibaddha citto hoti. So yāva taṃ cittaṃ na jahati, tāva taṃ itthiṃ ārabbha tassa purisassa lobhasahajātāni kāyavacīmanokammāni ca lobhasamuṭṭhitāni cittajarūpāni ca cirakālaṃpi pavattanti. Sabbāni ca tāni cittacetasikarūpāni tassaṃ itthiyaṃ rajjanalobhamūlakāni honti. So lobho tesaṃ mūlaṭṭhena hetu ca, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayo ca. Tasmā hetupaccayo. Esa nayo sesāni rajjanīyavatthūni ārabbharajjanavasena uppannesu lobhesu, dussanīyavatthūni ārabbha dussanavasena uppannesu dosesu, muyhanīyavatthūni ārabbha muyhanavasena uppannesu mohesu ca.

Furthermore, suppose a man's mind is attached to a certain woman. As long as he does not abandon that mind-state, with respect to that woman, the man's bodily, verbal, and mental actions co-arisen with greed, and the mind-originated matter arisen from greed, continue to occur for a long time. And all those states of consciousness, mental factors, and matter are rooted in the greed of attachment for that woman. That greed is their root (hetu) in the sense of being a foundation and their condition (paccayo) in the sense of being supportive. Therefore, it is a root-condition. This same method applies to instances of greed that arise through attachment with regard to desirable objects, to instances of hatred that arise through aversion with regard to repulsive objects, and to instances of delusion that arise through bewilderment with regard to bewildering objects.

Tattha [M 0.0466] yathā rukkhassa mūlāni sayaṃ antopathaviyaṃ suṭṭhu patiṭṭhahitvā pathavirasañca āporasañca gahetvā taṃ rukkhaṃ yāva aggā abhiharanti, tena rukkho cirakālaṃ vaḍḍhamāno tiṭṭhati. Tathā lobho ca tasmiṃ tasmiṃ vatthumhi rajjanavasena suṭṭhu patiṭṭhahitvā tassa tassa vatthussa piyarūparasañca sātarūparasañca gahetvā sampayuttadhamme yāva kāyavacīvītikkamā abhiharati, kāya vītikkamaṃ vā vacīvītikkamaṃ vā pāpeti. Tathā doso ca dussana vasena appiyarūparasañca asātarūparasañca gahetvā, moho ca muyhanavasena nānārammaṇesu niratthakacittācārarasaṃ vaḍḍhetvāti vattabbaṃ. Evaṃ abhiharantā tayo dhammā rajjanīyādīsu vatthūsu sampayuttadhamme modamāne pamodamāne karonto viya cira kālaṃ pavattenti. Sampayuttadhammā ca tathā pavattanti. Sampayutta dhammesu ca tathā pavattamānesu sahajātarūpakalāpāpi tathā pavattantiyeva. Tattha sampayuttadhamme abhiharatīti dve piyarūpa sātarūparase sampayuttadhammānaṃ santikaṃ pāpetīti attho.

Therein, just as the roots of a tree, having established themselves firmly within the earth, draw up the nutritive essence of earth and water and convey it to the tree up to its very top, whereby the tree stands firm, growing for a long time; so too, greed, having established itself firmly in this or that object through attachment, draws up the flavour of what is pleasing and agreeable in that object and conveys it to its associated states up to the point of bodily and verbal transgression, or it leads to bodily or verbal transgression. Likewise, it should be said, hatred, through aversion, draws up the flavour of what is displeasing and disagreeable; and delusion, through confusion, enhances the flavour of pointless mental activity regarding various objects. Thus conveying, these three states cause their associated states to continue for a long time regarding desirable objects and so on, as if making them delight and rejoice. The associated states also continue in that way. And while the associated states continue in that way, the co-arisen groups of matter also continue in just that way. Here, the meaning of 'conveys to the associated states' is that it brings the two flavours—of the pleasing and the agreeable—to the associated states.

Sukkapakkhe so puriso yadākāmesu ādīnavaṃ passati, tadā so taṃ cittaṃjahati, taṃ itthiṃ ārabbha alobho sañjā yati. Pubbe yasmiṃ kāle taṃ itthiṃ ārabbha lobhamūlakāni asuddhāni kāyavacīmanokammāni vattanti. Idāni tasmiṃ kālepi alobhamūlakāni suddhāni kāyavacīmanokammāni vattanti. Pabbajita sīlasaṃvarāni vā jhānaparikammāni vā appanājhānāni vā vattanti. So alobho tesaṃ mūlaṭṭhena hetu ca hoti, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayoca. Tasmā hetupaccayo. Esa nayo sesesu lobha paṭipakkhesu alobhesu, dosapaṭipakkhesu adosesu, moha paṭipakkhesu amohesu ca.

On the bright side, when that man sees the danger in sensual pleasures, he abandons that mind-state; with regard to that woman, non-greed arises. Whereas previously, at the time when impure bodily, verbal, and mental actions rooted in greed occurred with regard to that woman, now at this time pure bodily, verbal, and mental actions rooted in non-greed occur. For one who has gone forth, there occur moral restraints, or preliminary work for jhāna, or absorption jhāna. That non-greed is for them a root in the sense of being a foundation and a condition in the sense of being supportive. Therefore, it is a root-condition. This is the method for the other cases of non-greed as the opposite of greed, non-hatred as the opposite of hatred, and non-delusion as the opposite of delusion.

Tattha rukkhamūlāni viya alobho lobhaneyyavatthūsu lobhaṃ pahāya lobhavivekasukharasaṃ vaḍḍhetvā tena sukhena sampayuttadhamme modamāne pamodamāne karonto viya yāva jhānasamāpattisukhā vā yāva maggaphalasukhā vā vaḍḍhāpeti. Tathā adoso ca dosaneyyavatthūsu dosavivekasukharasaṃ vaḍḍhetvā, amoho ca mohaneyyavatthūsu mohavivekasukharasaṃ vaḍḍhetvāti vattabbaṃ. Evaṃ vaḍḍhāpentā tayo dhammā kusalesu dhammesu sampayuttadhamme modamāne pamodamāne karonto viya cira kālaṃpi pavattenti. Sampayuttadhammā ca tathā pavattanti. Sampayutta dhammesu ca tathā pavattamānesu sahajātarūpakalāpāpi tathā pavattantiyeva.

Herein, like the roots of a tree, non-greed, having abandoned greed towards objects that are grounds for greed, enhances the flavour of the happiness of seclusion from greed. By means of that happiness, as if making its associated states delight and rejoice, it develops them up to the happiness of the attainment of jhāna or the happiness of the path and fruition. Similarly, it should be said, non-hatred enhances the flavour of the happiness of seclusion from hatred towards objects that are grounds for hatred; and non-delusion enhances the flavour of the happiness of seclusion from delusion towards objects that are grounds for delusion. Thus developing them, these three states cause their associated states among wholesome states to continue for a long time, as if making them delight and rejoice. The associated states also continue in that way. And while the associated states continue in that way, the co-arisen groups of matter also continue in just that way.

Tattha [M 0.0467] lobhavivekasukharasanti viviccanaṃ vigamanaṃ viveko. Lobhassa viveko lobhaviveko. Lobhaviveke sukhaṃ lobhavivekasukhaṃ. Lobhavivekaṃ paṭicca uppannasukhanti vuttaṃ hoti. Tadeva raso lobhavivekasukharasoti samāso. Ayaṃ abhidhamme paṭṭhānanayo.

Herein, regarding 'the flavour of the happiness of seclusion from greed' (lobhavivekasukharasa): Seclusion (viveka) means separation, departure, detachment. 'Lobhaviveka' is seclusion from greed. 'Lobhavivekasukha' is the happiness in seclusion from greed, which is to say, 'the happiness that has arisen in dependence on seclusion from greed.' That very happiness as a flavour is the compound 'lobhavivekasukharasa'. This is the Paṭṭhāna method in the Abhidhamma.

Hattantanayo pana avijjāsaṅkhāto moho ca taṇhāsaṅkhāto lobho cāti dve dhammā sabbesaṃpi vaṭṭadukkhadhammānaṃ mūlāni honti. Doso pana lobhassa nissandabhūtaṃ pāpamūlaṃ hoti. Vijjāsaṅkhāto amoho ca nikkhamadhātusaṅkhāto alobho cāti dve dhammā vivaṭṭadhammānaṃ mūlāni honti. Adoso pana alobhassa nissandabhūtaṃ kalyāṇamūlaṃ hoti. Evaṃ chabbidhāni mūlāni sahajātānaṃpi asahajātānaṃpi nāmarūpadhammānaṃ paccayā hontīti. Ayaṃ suttantesu nayo. Hetupaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

However, according to the Suttanta method, two states—delusion reckoned as ignorance and greed reckoned as craving—are the roots of all phenomena of the suffering of the round. Hatred, in turn, being a consequence of greed, is an evil root. Two states—non-delusion reckoned as true knowledge and non-greed reckoned as the element of renunciation—are the roots of the phenomena that escape the round. Non-hatred, in turn, being a consequence of non-greed, is a wholesome root. Thus these six kinds of roots are conditions for both co-arisen and non-co-arisen mental and material phenomena. This is the method in the Suttas. The Exposition of the Root-Condition is concluded.

2. Ārammaṇapaccayo

2. The Object Condition

Katamo ārammaṇapaccayo. Sabbepi cittacetasikā dhammā sabbepi rūpadhammā sabbaṃpi nibbānaṃ sabbāpi paññattiyo ārammaṇa paccayo. Na hi so nāma ekopi dhammo atthi, yo citta cetasikānaṃ ārammaṇaṃ na hoti. Saṅkhepato pana ārammaṇaṃ chabbidhaṃ hoti rūpārammaṇaṃ saddārammaṇaṃ gandhārammaṇaṃ rasārammaṇaṃ phoṭṭhabbārammaṇaṃ dhammārammaṇanti.

What is the object condition? All consciousness and mental factors, all material phenomena, Nibbāna, and all concepts are the object condition. For there is not a single phenomenon that does not serve as an object for consciousness and mental factors. In brief, an object is of six kinds: visible form, sound, odor, taste, tangible object, and mental object.

Katame dhammā ārammaṇapaccayassa paccayuppannā. Sabbepi citta cetasikādhammā ārammaṇapaccayassa paccayuppannā. Na hi kiñci cittaṃ nāma atthi, yaṃ cittaṃ bhūtena vā abhūtena vā ārammaṇena vinā pavattati.

Which phenomena are conditionally arisen by the object condition? All consciousness and mental factors are conditionally arisen by the object condition. For there is no consciousness whatsoever that can proceed without an object, whether that object is real or conceptual.

Tattha paccuppannaṃ rūpārammaṇaṃ duvidhassa cakkhuviññāṇacittassa ārammaṇapaccayo. Paccuppannaṃ saddārammaṇaṃ duvidhassa sotaviññāṇa cittassa. Paccuppannaṃ gandhārammaṇaṃ duvidhassa ghānaviññāṇacittassa. Paccuppannaṃ rasārammaṇaṃ duvidhassa jivhāviññāṇacittassa. Paccuppannaṃ tividhaṃ phoṭṭhabbārammaṇaṃ duvidhassa kāyaviññāṇacittassa. Paccuppannāni tāni pañcārammaṇāni tividhassa manodhātucittassa ārammaṇapaccayo. Sabbāni tāni atītānāgatapaccuppannāni pañcārammaṇāni vā sabbāni tekālikāni kālavimuttāni dhammārammaṇāni vā cha sattatividhānaṃ manoviññāṇacittānaṃ yathārahaṃ ārammaṇapaccayo.

Therein, a present visible-form object is the object condition for the two types of eye-consciousness. A present sound object is for the two types of ear-consciousness. A present odor object is for the two types of nose-consciousness. A present taste object is for the two types of tongue-consciousness. A present threefold tangible object is for the two types of body-consciousness. Those five present objects are the object condition for the three types of mind-element. All those five objects belonging to the past, future, and present, or all mental objects—which may be of the three times or timeless—are, as appropriate, the object condition for the seventy-six types of mind-consciousness.

Kenaṭṭhena ārammaṇaṃ, kenaṭṭhena paccayoti. Citta cetasikehi ālambitabbaṭṭhena ārammaṇaṃ, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayoti.

In what sense is it an object, and in what sense a condition? It is an object in the sense of being that which is to be apprehended by consciousness and mental factors; it is a condition in the sense of being a support.

Ālambitabbaṭṭhenāti [M 0.0468] cettha ālambaṇakiriyā nāma citta cetasikānaṃ ārammaṇaggahaṇakiriyā, ārammaṇupādāna kiriyā.

Herein, by 'in the sense of being that which is to be apprehended,' is meant the act of apprehending. This is the act whereby consciousness and mental factors grasp an object, the act of taking it up.

Yathā hi loke ayodhātuṃ kāmeti icchatīti atthena ayokantako nāma lohadhātuviseso atthi. So ayokhandhasamīpaṃ sampatto taṃ ayokhandhaṃ kāmento viya icchanto viya ayokhandhābhimukho cañcalati. Sayaṃ vā taṃ ayokhandhaṃ upagacchati. Ayokhandhaṃ vā attābhimukhaṃ ākaḍḍhati, ayokhandho tadabhimukho cañcalati, taṃ vā upagacchati. Ayaṃ ayokantakassa ālambaṇakiriyā nāma. Evameva cittacetasikānaṃ ārammaṇesu ālambaṇakiriyā daṭṭhabbā. Na kevalaṃ ārammaṇesu ālambaṇa mattaṃ hoti. Atha kho cittacetasikā dhammā sattasantāne uppajjamānā chasu dvāresu ārammaṇānaṃ āpātāgamane eva khaṇe khaṇe uppajjanti. Uppajjitvā ca khaṇe khaṇe nirujjhanti.

For just as in the world there is a special kind of mineral called a magnet, so called because it ‘desires’ or ‘wishes for’ iron, so when it comes near a piece of iron, it trembles towards the iron as if desiring or wishing for it. It either goes towards the piece of iron itself, or it draws the piece of iron towards itself, and the piece of iron trembles towards it or goes towards it. This is called the magnet’s act of apprehending. In the very same way, the act of apprehending on the part of consciousness and mental factors in relation to their objects should be understood. It is not just a mere apprehending of objects. Rather, consciousness and mental factors, arising in the continuity of a being, arise moment after moment at the six doors upon the impact and arrival of objects. And having arisen, they cease moment after moment.

Yathā taṃ bheritale bherisaddā uppajjamānā tattha tattha hatthena paharaṇakāle eva khaṇe khaṇe uppajjanti, uppajjitvā ca khaṇe khaṇe nirujjhanti. Vīṇāsaddā uppajjamānā vīṇātantīsu tattha tattha vīṇādantakena paharaṇakāle eva khaṇe khaṇe uppajjanti, uppajjitvā ca khaṇe khaṇe nirujjhantīti. Niddāyantassa bhavaṅgacittappavatti kālepi pubbabhave maraṇāsannakāle chasu dvāresu āpāta māgatāni kamma kammanimitta gatinimittāni eva bhavaṅgacittānaṃ ārammaṇapaccayoti. Ārammaṇapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Just as sounds arising on the surface of a drum arise moment after moment only at the time and place it is struck by the hand, and having arisen, cease moment after moment; and just as sounds arising on the strings of a lute arise moment after moment only at the time and place they are struck by the plectrum, and having arisen, cease moment after moment. So too, even in the case of a sleeping person, during the flow of the life-continuum consciousness, the object condition for the life-continuum consciousness consists of the kamma, kamma-sign, or destination-sign that came into range at the six doors at the time near death in the previous existence. The exposition of the Object Condition is concluded.

3. Adhipatipaccayo

3. Dominance Condition

Duvidho adhipatipaccayo ārammaṇādhipatipaccayo saha jātādhipatipaccayo ca. Tattha katamo ārammaṇādhipatipaccayo. Ārammaṇapaccaye vuttesu ārammaṇesu yāni ārammaṇāni atiiṭṭhāni honti atikantāni atimanāpāni garukatāni. Tāni ārammaṇāni ārammaṇādhipatipaccayo. Tattha atiiṭṭhānīti sabhāvato iṭṭhāni vā hontu aniṭṭhāni vā, tena tena puggalena atiicchitāni ārammaṇāni idha atiiṭṭhāni nāma.

The dominance condition is twofold: the object-dominance condition and the conascence-dominance condition. Herein, what is the object-dominance condition? Among the objects mentioned under the object condition, those that are exceedingly desirable, exceedingly pleasing, exceedingly agreeable, and taken as important—these objects constitute the object-dominance condition. Herein, by 'exceedingly desirable' is meant objects that are exceedingly desired by a particular individual, whether they are desirable or undesirable in their own nature; these are here called 'exceedingly desirable'.

Tāni pana dhammato dve dosamūlacittuppāde ca dve momūha cittuppāde ca dukkhasahagatakāyaviññāṇacittuppāde ca ṭhapetvā avasesāni sabbāni kāmāvacaracittacetasikāni ca rūpārūpa lokuttaracittacetasikāni ca sabbāni atiiṭṭharūpāni ca honti.

By way of phenomena, these are all exceedingly desirable material phenomena, and all states of sense-sphere consciousness and mental factors, fine-material and immaterial-sphere consciousness and mental factors, and supramundane consciousness and mental factors, with the exception of the two consciousnesses rooted in hatred, the two rooted in delusion, and the consciousness accompanied by bodily pain.

Tesupi [M 0.0469] kāmārammaṇāni garuṃ karontasseva ārammaṇā dhipatipaccayo. Garuṃ akarontassa ārammaṇādhipatipaccayo na hoti. Jhānalābhino pana attanā paṭiladdhāni mahaggatajhānāni ariyasāvakā ca attanā paṭiladdhe lokuttaradhamme garuṃ akarontā nāma natthi.

Among these, sense-sphere objects serve as an object-dominance condition only for one who regards them as important; for one who does not regard them as important, they do not serve as an object-dominance condition. However, there are no jhāna-attainers who fail to regard as important the sublime jhānas they have personally attained, and no noble disciples who fail to regard as important the supramundane states they have personally attained.

Katame dhammā tassa paccayassa paccayuppannā. Aṭṭha lobha mūlacittāni aṭṭha kāmāvacarakusalacittāni cattāri kāmāvacara ñāṇasampayuttakiriyacittāni aṭṭha lokuttaracittāni tassa paccayassa paccayuppannā.

Which phenomena are conditionally arisen from that condition? The eight consciousnesses rooted in greed, the eight wholesome sense-sphere consciousnesses, the four sense-sphere functional consciousnesses associated with knowledge, and the eight supramundane consciousnesses are conditionally arisen from that condition.

Tattha lokiyāni chaḷārammaṇāni lobhamūlacittānaṃ paccayo. Sattarasa lokiyakusalāni catunnaṃ ñāṇavippayutta kusalānaṃ. Tāni kusalāniceva heṭṭhimamaggaphalāni ca nibbānañca catunnaṃ ñāṇasampayuttakusalānaṃ. Arahattamaggaphalāni ca nibbānañca catunnaṃ ñāṇasampayuttakiriyānaṃ. Nibbānaṃ aṭṭhannaṃ lokuttara cittānanti.

Therein, the six worldly objects are the condition for the consciousnesses rooted in greed. Seventeen worldly wholesome states are the condition for the four wholesome consciousnesses dissociated from knowledge. Those wholesome states, the lower paths and fruitions, and Nibbāna are the condition for the four wholesome consciousnesses associated with knowledge. The path and fruition of Arahantship and Nibbāna are the condition for the four functional consciousnesses associated with knowledge. Nibbāna is the condition for the eight supramundane consciousnesses.

Kenaṭṭhena ārammaṇaṃ, kenaṭṭhena adhipati. Ālambi tabbaṭṭhena ārammaṇaṃ, ādhipaccaṭṭhena adhipati. Ko ādhipaccaṭṭho. Attānaṃ garuṃ katvā pavattesu cittacetasikesu issarabhāvo ādhipaccaṭṭho. Loke sāmikā viya ārammaṇādhipatipaccaya dhammā daṭṭhabbā, dāsā viya paccayuppannadhammā daṭṭhabbā.

In what sense is it an object, and in what sense a dominant factor? It is an object in the sense of being that which is to be apprehended. It is a dominant factor in the sense of exercising dominance. What is the meaning of dominance? The meaning of dominance is the state of mastery with regard to the consciousness and mental factors that arise by taking that object as important. The phenomena that are the object-dominance condition should be seen as being like masters in the world; the conditionally arisen phenomena should be seen as being like servants.

Sutasomajātake rājā porisādo manussamaṃsaṃ garuṃ karonto manussamaṃsahetu rajjaṃ pahāya araññe vicarati. Tattha manussamaṃse gandharasa dhammā ārammaṇādhipatipaccayo. Rañño porisādassa lobhamūlacittaṃ paccayuppannadhammo. Rājā suta somo saccadhammaṃ garuṃ katvā saccadhammahetu rajjasampattiñca ñāti saṅghañca attano jīvitañca chaṭṭetvā puna rañño porisādassa hatthaṃ upagato. Tattha saccadhammo ārammaṇādhipatipaccayo. Rañño sutasomassa kusalacittaṃ paccayuppannadhammo. Esanayo sabbesu garukatesu ārammaṇesu.

In the Sutasoma Jātaka, King Porisāda, taking human flesh as important, abandoned his kingdom and wandered in the forest for the sake of human flesh. There, the odor and taste in the human flesh were the object-dominance condition. The greed-rooted consciousness of King Porisāda was the conditionally arisen phenomenon. King Sutasoma, taking the true Dhamma as important, for the sake of the true Dhamma relinquished his royal fortune, his host of relatives, and his own life, and returned to the hands of King Porisāda. There, the true Dhamma was the object-dominance condition. The wholesome consciousness of King Sutasoma was the conditionally arisen phenomenon. This is the principle for all objects that are taken as important.

Katamo sahajātādhipatipaccayo. Adhipatibhāvaṃ pattā cattāro dhammā adhipatipaccayo, chando cittaṃ vīriyaṃ vīmaṃsā.

What is the conascence-dominance condition? The four phenomena that have attained a state of dominance are the dominance condition: intention, consciousness, energy, and investigation.

Katame dhammā tassa paccayassa paccayuppannā. Adhipati sampayuttā cittacetasikā ca adhipatisamuṭṭhitā cittajarūpadhammā ca tassa paccayassa paccayuppannā.

Which phenomena are conditionally arisen from that condition? The consciousness and mental factors associated with a dominant factor, and the material phenomena originated by a consciousness possessing a dominant factor, are conditionally arisen from that condition.

Kenaṭṭhena sahajāto, kenaṭṭhena adhipati.

In what sense is it conascent? In what sense is it dominant?

Sahuppādanaṭṭhena sahajāto, sahajātānaṃ dhammānaṃ abhibhavanaṭṭhena adhipati. Tattha sahuppādanaṭṭhenāti [M 0.0470] yo dhammo sayaṃ uppajjamāno attanā sahajātadhamme ca attanā saheva uppādeti, tassa attanā sahajātadhammānaṃ sahuppādanaṭṭhena.

It is conascent in the sense of causing co-arising; it is dominant in the sense of overpowering conascent phenomena. Herein, 'in the sense of causing co-arising' means that a phenomenon, while arising itself, causes the phenomena conascent with it to arise together with itself; this is its meaning of causing co-arising with its conascent phenomena.

Abhibhavanaṭṭhenāti ajjhottharaṇaṭṭhena. Yathā rājā cakkavatti attano puññiddhiyā sakaladīpavāsino abhibhavanto ajjhottharanto attano vase vattāpeti, sakaladīpavāsino ca tassa vase vattanti. Tathā adhipatiṭṭhānapattā ime cattāro dhammā attano attano visaye sahajātadhamme abhibhavantā ajjhottharantā attano vase vattāpenti, sahajātadhammā ca tesaṃ vase vattanti. Yathā vā silāthambhe pathavidhātu udakakkhandhe āpodhātu aggikkhandhe tejodhātu vātakkhandhe vāyodhātu attanā sahajātā tisso dhātuyo abhibhavantā ajjhottharantā attano gatiṃ gamāpenti, sahajātadhātuyo ca tāsaṃ gatiṃ gacchanti, evameva adhipatiṭṭhānapattā ime cattāro dhammā attano balena sahajātadhamme attano gatiṃ gamāpenti, sahajātadhammā ca tesaṃ gatiṃ gacchanti, evaṃ sahajātadhammānaṃ abhibhavanaṭṭhena.

'In the sense of overpowering' means in the sense of overwhelming. Just as a wheel-turning monarch, by his meritorious power, overpowers and overwhelms all the inhabitants of the island, making them live under his sway, and all the inhabitants of the island live under his sway; so too these four phenomena, having attained the state of dominance, overpower and overwhelm their conascent phenomena within their respective domains, making them live under their sway, and the conascent phenomena live under their sway. Or, just as in a stone pillar the earth element, in a mass of water the water element, in a mass of fire the fire element, and in a mass of air the air element, overpowers and overwhelms the three other elements conascent with itself, making them follow its own course, and the conascent elements follow its course; in the very same way, these four phenomena, having attained the state of dominance, by their own power make their conascent phenomena follow their own course, and the conascent phenomena follow their course. Thus it is in the sense of overpowering conascent phenomena.

Ettha vadeyyuṃ, yadi sahajātadhammānaṃ abhibhavanaṭṭhena adhipatināma siyā. Evaṃ sati tiṭṭhatu chando, lobho eva adhipatināma siyā, so hi chandatopi balavataro hutvā saha jātadhamme abhibhavanto pavattatīti. Vuccate, bālaputhujjanesu eva lobho chandato balavataro hoti, paṇḍitesu pana chando eva lobhato balavataro hutvā sahajātadhamme abhi bhavanto pavattati. Sace hi lobho eva chandato balavataro siyā, kathaṃ ime sattā lobhassa hatthagatā bhavasampatti bhogasammattiyo chaṭṭetvā nekkhammadhamme pūretvā vaṭṭadukkhato nissareyyuṃ. Yasmā pana chando eva lobhato balavataro hoti, tasmā ime sattā lobhassa hatthagatā bhavasampatti bhogasampattiyo chaṭṭetvā nekkhammadhamme pūretvā vaṭṭadukkhato nissaranti. Tasmā chando eva lobhato balavataro hoti, chando eva adhipati, na lobhoti. Esa nayo dosādīsupīti.

Here they might say: 'If the name ‘dominance’ is given because of overpowering co-arisen states, then, this being so, let zeal stand aside; greed alone should be called ‘dominance,’ for it is stronger than zeal and, being stronger, it proceeds by overpowering its co-arisen states.' It is said: It is only in foolish worldlings that greed is stronger than zeal, but in the wise, zeal is stronger than greed and proceeds by overpowering its co-arisen states. For if greed were stronger than zeal, how could these beings, who have fallen into the hands of greed, abandon the good fortune of existence and the abundance of enjoyments, fulfill the factors of renunciation, and escape from the suffering of the round? But because zeal is indeed stronger than greed, these beings, though they have fallen into the hands of greed, abandon the good fortune of existence and the abundance of enjoyments, fulfill the factors of renunciation, and escape from the suffering of the round. Therefore, zeal is stronger than greed; zeal alone is the dominant factor, not greed. This same method applies to hatred and the other defilements as well.

Tattha loke mahantesu sudukkaresu purisakammesu paccupaṭṭhitesu ime cattāro dhammā kammasiddhiyā saṃvattanti. Kathaṃ.

In the world, when great and very difficult human endeavors have arisen, these four factors conduce to the accomplishment of the task. How so?

Hīnacchandā bahujjanā mahantāni sudukkarāni purisakammāni disvā nivattacchandā honti. Kātuṃ na icchanti, amhākaṃ avisayoti nirapekkhā ṭhapenti. Chandādhiko pana tādisāni purisakammāni disvā uggatacchando hoti, ativiya [M 0.0471] kātuṃ icchati, mama visayo esoti adhiṭṭhānaṃ gacchati. So chandena abhikaḍḍhito yāva taṃ kammaṃ na sijjhati, tāva antarā taṃ kammaṃ chaṭṭetuṃ na sakkoti. Evañca sati atimahantaṃpi taṃ kammaṃ ekasmiṃ kāle siddhaṃ bhavissati.

Many people of weak zeal, on seeing great and very difficult human endeavors, find their zeal recedes. They do not wish to undertake them; thinking, 'This is not our domain,' they set them aside with indifference. But one with superior zeal, on seeing such human endeavors, becomes aroused in zeal. He wishes intensely to do it, making the resolution, 'This is my domain.' Drawn on by that zeal, he is unable to abandon the task midway before it is accomplished. This being so, even a very great task will at some time be accomplished.

Hīnavīriyā ca bahujjanā tādisāni kammāni disvā nivatta vīriyā honti, idaṃ me kammaṃ karontassa bahuṃ kāyadukkhaṃ vā cetodukkhaṃ vā bhavissatīti nivattanti. Vīriyādhiko pana tādisāni purisakammāni disvā uggatavīriyo hoti, idāneva uṭṭhahitvā kātuṃ icchati. So cirakālaṃpi taṃ kammaṃ karonto bahuṃ kāyadukkhaṃ vā cetodukkhaṃ vā anubhavantopi tasmiṃ vīriya kamme nanibbindati, mahantena kammavīriyena vinā bhavituṃ na sakkoti, tādisena vīriyena rattidivaṃ khepento cittasukhaṃ vindati. Evañca sati atimahantaṃpi taṃ kammaṃ ekasmiṃ kāle siddhaṃ bhavissati.

And many people of weak energy, on seeing such tasks, find their energy recedes; thinking, 'If I do this task, much bodily pain or mental pain will arise for me,' they turn back. But one with superior energy, on seeing such human endeavors, becomes aroused in energy; he wishes to rise up and do it right away. Even while doing that task for a long time and experiencing much bodily or mental pain, he does not become weary of that energetic task. He cannot be without great energy for the task; passing day and night with such energy, he finds mental happiness. This being so, even a very great task will at some time be accomplished.

Hīnacittā ca bahujjanā tādisāni kammāni disvā nivattacittā honti. Puna ārammaṇaṃpi na karonti. Cittādhiko pana tādisāni kammāni disvā uggatacitto hoti, cittaṃ vinodetuṃpi na sakkoti, niccakālaṃ tattha nibandhacitto hoti. So cittavasiko hutvā cirakālaṃpi taṃ kammaṃ karonto bahuṃ kāyadukkhaṃvāpītiādinā chandādhipatinayena vattabbaṃ.

And many people with an inferior mind, on seeing such tasks, find their mind turns back. They do not even make it an object of attention again. But one with a superior mind, on seeing such tasks, becomes uplifted in mind. He is unable even to divert his mind from it; his mind is constantly bound to it. Being under the control of his mind, even while doing that task for a long time and experiencing much bodily pain... and so on, should be explained according to the method for zeal as the dominant factor.

Mandapaññā ca bahujjanā tādisāni kammāni disvā nivattapaññā honti, kammānaṃ ādimpi na passanti, antapi na passanti, andhakāre pavisantā viya honti, tāni kammāni kātuṃ cittaṃpi na namati. Paññādhiko pana tādisāni kammāni disvā uggatapañño hoti, kammānaṃ ādiṃpi passati, antaṃpi passati, phalaṃpi passati, ānisaṃsaṃpi passati. Sukhena kammasiddhiyā nānāupāyaṃpi passati. So cirakālaṃpi taṃ kammaṃ karontotiādinā vīriyādhipatinayena vattabbaṃ. Idha pana mahatiyā kammavīmaṃsāyāti ca tādisiyā kamma vīmaṃsāyāti ca vattabbaṃ.

And many people of dull wisdom, on seeing such tasks, find their wisdom recedes. They see neither the beginning of the task nor its end; they are as if entering into darkness. Their minds do not even incline to perform those tasks. But one with superior wisdom, on seeing such tasks, becomes uplifted in wisdom. He sees the beginning of the task and its end, its fruit and its benefit. He also sees various means for accomplishing the task with ease. The passage beginning, 'Even while doing that task for a long time...' should be explained according to the method for energy as the dominant factor. Here, however, it should be stated: 'by means of great investigation of the task' and 'by means of such investigation of the task.'

Evaṃ loke mahantesu sudukkaresu purisakammesu paccupaṭṭhitesu ime cattāro dhammā kammasiddhiyā saṃvattanti. Imesañca catunnaṃ adhipatīnaṃ vijjamānattā loke purisavisesā nāma dissanti, sabbaññubuddhā nāma dissanti, sabbaññubodhisattā nāma dissanti, paccekabuddhā nāma dissanti, paccekabodhisattānāma dissanti, aggasāvakānāma mahāsāvakā nāma sāvakabodhisattā nāma dissanti. Lokepi evarūpānaṃ purisavisesānaṃ vasena satta lokassa atthāya hitāya sukhāya paññāsippavisesā ca paribhogavatthuvisesā ca dissantīti. Adhipatipaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Thus, when great and very difficult human endeavors have arisen in the world, these four factors conduce to the accomplishment of the task. And because of the existence of these four dominant factors, outstanding persons are seen in the world, namely: Omniscient Buddhas are seen, Omniscient Bodhisattas are seen, Paccekabuddhas are seen, Paccekabodhisattas are seen, chief disciples, great disciples, and disciple-bodhisattas are seen. And in the world, on account of such outstanding persons, special kinds of wisdom and crafts and special kinds of articles for use are seen, for the good, welfare, and happiness of the world of beings. The exposition of the Dominance Condition is finished.

4. Anantarapaccayo

4. The Immediacy Condition

Katamo [M 0.0472] anantarapaccayo. Anantare khaṇe niruddho citta cetasikadhammasamūho anantarapaccayo.

What is the immediacy condition? The aggregate of consciousness and mental factors that has ceased in the immediately preceding moment is the immediacy condition.

Katamo dhammo anantarapaccayassa paccayuppanno. Pacchime anantare eva khaṇe uppanno cittacetasikadhammasamūho tassa paccayassa paccayuppanno.

What state is dependently arisen through the immediacy condition? The aggregate of consciousness and mental factors arising in the immediately succeeding moment is dependently arisen through that condition.

Ekasmiṃ bhave paṭisandhicittaṃ paṭhamabhavaṅgacittassa anantara paccayo, paṭhama bhavaṅgacittaṃ dutiyabhavaṅgacittassa anantarapaccayotiādinā vattabbo.

In one existence, the rebirth-linking consciousness is the immediacy condition for the first life-continuum consciousness; the first life-continuum consciousness is the immediacy condition for the second life-continuum consciousness, and so on. This is how it is to be understood by the wise.

Yadā pana dhammayamake suddhāvāsānaṃ dutiye akusale citte vattamāneti vuttanayena tassa sattassa attano abhinavaṃ attabhāvaṃ ārabbha etaṃ mama esohamasmi eso me attāti pavattaṃ bhavanikantika taṇhāsahagatacittaṃ uppajjati. Tadā paṭhamaṃ dvikkhattuṃ bhavaṅgaṃ calati. Tato manodvārāvajjanacittaṃ uppajjati. Tato satta bhavanikantikajavanāni uppajjanti. Tato paraṃ bhavaṅgavāro.

When, as stated in the Dhammayamaka concerning the second unwholesome consciousness arising in the Pure Abodes, there arises in that being, with regard to its own new existence, a consciousness accompanied by craving for becoming, proceeding with the thought: 'This is mine, this I am, this is my self.' Then, first, the life-continuum vibrates twice. After that, the mind-door-adverting consciousness arises. Then, seven impulsions of craving for becoming arise. After that, the life-continuum sequence.

Api ca so satto tadā paccuppannabhave kiñci na jānāti, pubbabhave attanā anubhūtaṃ ārammaṇaṃ anussaramāno acchati. Vatthussa pana atidubbalattā tañca ārammaṇaṃ aparibyattameva hoti. Taṃ ārabbha uddhaccasahagatacittameva bahulaṃ pavattati.

Moreover, at that time, that being knows nothing in the present existence, but abides recollecting an object experienced by himself in a past existence. But due to the extreme weakness of the physical basis, that object too remains indistinct. Taking it as object, a consciousness accompanied by restlessness mostly occurs.

Yadā gabbho thokaṃ vaḍḍhamāno hoti atirekadvemāsaṃ gato, tadā cakkhādīni indriyāni paripuṇṇāni honti. Evaṃ santepi mātugabbhe ālokādīnaṃ paccayānaṃ abhāvato cakkhuviññāṇādīni cattāri viññāṇāni nuppajjanti, kāyaviññāṇamanoviññāṇāni eva uppajjanti. So satto mātuyā iriyāpathaparivattanādīsu bahūni dukkhadomanassāni paccanubhoti. Vijāyanakāle pana bhusaṃ dukkhaṃ nigacchatiyeva. Vijāyitvāpi yāva vatthurūpāni mudūni honti, paripākaṃ na gacchanti, tāva so atimandarūpo uttānaseyyako hutvā acchati. Na kiñci paccuppannaṃ ārammaṇaṃ sallakkheti. Yebhuyyena purima bhavānusārī eva tassa viññāṇaṃ hoti. Sace so niraya bhavato āgato hoti, so virūpamukhabahulo hoti. Purimāni nirayārammaṇāni ārabbha khaṇe khaṇe virūpamukhamassa paññāyati. Atha devalokato āgato hoti, dibbāni ārammaṇāni ārabbha vippasannamukhabahulo hoti, khaṇe khaṇe mihitamukhamassa paññāyati.

When the embryo grows a little and is more than two months old, the eye and other faculties become fully developed. Even so, due to the absence of conditions like light in the mother’s womb, the four types of consciousness—eye-consciousness and the others—do not arise; only body-consciousness and mind-consciousness arise. That being experiences much suffering and displeasure due to the mother’s movements and changes in posture. At the time of birth, it undergoes severe pain. Even after birth, as long as the physical bases remain soft and immature, it lies on its back, extremely feeble. It does not perceive any present object. For the most part, its consciousness follows the previous existence. If it has come from a hellish existence, it often has a distorted face; taking former hellish objects as object, from moment to moment its face appears distorted. But if it has come from a heavenly world, taking divine objects as object, it often has a serene face, and from moment to moment a smiling face appears.

Yadā pana vatthurūpāni tikkhāni honti, paripākaṃ gacchanti. Viññāṇāni cassa suvisadāni pavattanti. Tadā amandarūpo hutvā kīḷanto līḷanto modanto [M 0.0473] pamodanto acchati. Paccuppanne ārammaṇāni sallakkheti. Mātubhāsaṃ sallakkheti. Idha lokānusārī viññāṇamassa bahulaṃ pavattati. Purimajātiṃ pamussati.

But when the physical bases become sharp and reach maturity, and its consciousness functions very clearly, then, no longer weak, it abides playing, sporting, delighting, and rejoicing. It perceives present objects. It perceives the mother’s speech. Here, its consciousness for the most part follows this world. It forgets its former birth.

Kiṃ pana sabbopi satto imasmiṃ ṭhāne eva purimaṃ jātiṃ pamussatīti ce. Na sabbopi satto imasmiṃ ṭhāne eva pamussati. Koci atirekataraṃ gambhavāsadukkhena paripīḷito gabbhe eva pamussati. Koci vijāyanakāle, koci imasmiṃ ṭhāne pamussati. Koci itoparampi daharakāle na pamussati. Vuḍḍhakāle eva pamussati. Koci yāvajīvaṃpi na pamussati. Dve tayo bhave anussarantopi atthiyeva. Ime jātissarasattā nāma honti.

But does every being forget its past life in this very state? Not every being forgets in this very state. Some, being excessively afflicted by the suffering of dwelling in the womb, forget even in the womb itself. Some forget at the time of birth, some forget in this state. Some do not forget even later in early childhood, but forget only in old age. Some do not forget even for as long as they live. There are indeed those who remember two or three existences. These are called beings with memory of past lives.

Tattha vijāyanakālato paṭṭhāya chadvārikavīthicittāni pavattanti. Paccuppannārammaṇaṃ sallakkhaṇato paṭṭhāya chadvārikavīthi cittāni paripuṇṇāni pavattanti. Sabbatthapi purimaṃ purimaṃ anantare niruddhaṃ cittaṃ pacchimassa pacchimassa anantare uppannassa cittassa anantara paccayo hoti. Ayañca anantarapaccayo nāma anamatagge saṃsāre ekassa sattassa ekappabandho eva hoti. Yadā satto arahatta maggaṃ labhitvā khandhaparinibbānaṃ pāpuṇāti, tadā eva so pabandho chijjati.

Therein, from the time of birth onward, cognitive processes of the six doors occur. From the time of perceiving a present object, the cognitive processes of the six doors occur in their complete form. In all cases, each preceding consciousness, having ceased immediately before, is the immediacy condition for the succeeding consciousness arising immediately after. This immediacy condition is the single continuum of a being in beginningless saṃsāra. Only when a being attains the path of arahantship and reaches the final parinibbāna of the aggregates does this continuum get cut off.

Kenaṭṭhena anantaro, kenaṭṭhena paccayoti. Attano anantare attasadisassa dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhena anantaro, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayo. Tattha attasadisassāti sārammaṇa bhāvena attanā sadisassa. Sārammaṇabhāvenāti ca yo dhammo ārammaṇena vinā na pavattati, so sārammaṇo nāma, evaṃ sārammaṇabhāvena. Dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhenāti purimasmiṃ citte niruddhepi tassa cintanakiriyāvego na vūpasammati, pacchimaṃ cittaṃ uppādetvā eva vūpasammati, evaṃ pacchimassa dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhena.

In what sense is it 'immediacy' and in what sense a 'condition'? It is 'immediacy' in the sense of producing another state similar to itself immediately after itself; it is a 'condition' in the sense of being supportive. Here, 'similar to itself' means similar by way of having an object. 'By way of having an object' means that a state which does not occur without an object is called 'one with an object'; thus, 'by way of having an object.' 'Producing another state' means that even when the preceding consciousness has ceased, the force of its cognitive activity does not subside; it subsides only after having produced the succeeding consciousness. Thus it is 'in the sense of producing a succeeding other state.'

Tattha purimā purimā mātuparamparā viya anantarapaccayaparamparā daṭṭhabbā. Pacchimā pacchimā dhītuparamparā viya tassa paccayuppannaparamparā daṭṭhabbā. Evaṃ sante arahantānaṃ sabbapacchimaṃ parinibbānacittampi puna paṭisandhicittasaṅkhātaṃ dhammantaraṃ uppādeyyāti. Na uppādeyya. Kasmā, tadā kammakilesavegānaṃ sabbaso paṭippassaddhibhāvena accantasantatarattā tassa cittassāti. Anantarapaccaya dīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Herein, the preceding series of immediacy conditions should be seen like a lineage of mothers, and the subsequent series of dependently arisen phenomena should be seen like a lineage of daughters. If this were so, would even the final parinibbāna-consciousness of arahants give rise to another phenomenon called rebirth-linking consciousness? It would not. Why? Because at that time, that consciousness is utterly tranquil due to the complete subsiding of the force of kamma and defilements. The exposition of the immediacy condition is concluded.

5. Samanantarapaccayo

5. Contiguity Condition

Paccayadhammavibhāgo [M 0.0474] ca paccayuppannadhammavibhāgo ca anantapaccaya sadiso.

The analysis of the conditioning phenomenon and the analysis of the conditioned phenomenon is similar to that for the Immediacy Condition.

Kenaṭṭhena samanantaroti. Suṭṭhu anantaraṭṭhena samanantaro. Yathā silāthambhādīsu rūpakalāpā ekābaddhā samānāpi rūpa dhammabhāvena saṇṭhāna jātikattā majjhe paricchedarūpasahitā eva honti. Dvinnaṃ rūpakalāpānaṃ majjhe antaraṃ nāma vivaraṃ nāma atthiyeva. Na tathā purimapacchimānaṃ dvinnaṃ cittacetasika kalāpānaṃ majjhe. Te pana arūpadhammabhāvena asaṇṭhāna jātikattā majjhe paricchedadhammassa nāma kassaci ākāsavivarassa abhāvato sabbaso antara rahitā eva honti. Lokassapi dvinnaṃ kalāpānaṃ antaraṃ nāma na dissati. Tato ime sattā cittaṃ nāma niccaṃ dhuvaṃ thāvaraṃ avipariṇāmadhammanti evaṃ citte niccasaññino honti. Evaṃ suṭṭhu anantaraṭṭhena samanantaro. Anantaraṭṭhenāti ca attano anantare attasadisassa dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhenāti pubbe vuttameva.

In what sense is it contiguity? It is contiguity in the sense of perfect immediacy. For example, in the case of stone pillars and the like, although material groups are bound together as one, because they are material phenomena by nature and possess a shape, they still have delimiting matter in between. Between two material groups there is indeed a gap, an opening. It is not so between preceding and succeeding groups of consciousness and mental factors. Since they are immaterial phenomena, by nature without shape, due to the absence of any delimiting phenomenon or any spatial gap, they are completely without an interval. For a worldling, too, no interval is seen between two such groups. Therefore, these beings hold the perception of permanence in regard to consciousness, thinking: 'Consciousness is permanent, stable, lasting, and not subject to change.' Thus, it is contiguity in the sense of perfect immediacy. And 'in the sense of immediacy' means, as was stated before, in the sense of causing the arising, immediately after itself, of another phenomenon of a similar nature.

Evaṃ sante nirodhasamāpattikāle purimacittaṃ nāma neva saññānāsaññāyatanacittaṃ, pacchimacittaṃ nāma ariyaphalacittaṃ, dvinnaṃ cittānaṃ antare ekarattidivampi dverattidivānipi.La. Sattarattidivānipi acittako hoti. Asaññasattabhūmiyaṃpi purime kāmabhave cuticittaṃ purimacittaṃ nāma. Pacchime kāmabhave paṭisandhicittaṃ pacchimacittaṃ nāma, dvinnaṃ cittānaṃ antare asaññasattabhave pañcakappasatāni puggalo acittako tiṭṭhati. Tattha dve purimacittāni attano anantare attasadisassa dhammantarassa uppādanapaccayasattirahitāni hontīti. Na honti. Mahantehi pana bhāvanāpaṇidhibalehi paṭibāhitattā purimacittāni ca nirujjhamānāni anantare dhammantarassa uppādana sattisahitāni eva nirujjhanti. Pacchimacittāni ca uppajjamānāni tasmiṃ khaṇe ekābaddhabhāvena anuppajjitvā cirakāle eva uppajjanti. Na ca ettakamattena purimacittānaṃ anantare dhammantarassa uppādana sattināma natthīti ca, te anantarapaccayadhammā nāma na hontīti ca sakkā vattuṃ. Yathā taṃ rañño yodhā nāma atthi, kadāci rājā kālaṃ ñatvā tumhe idāni māyujjhatha, yuddhakālo na hoti, asukasmiṃ kāle eva yujjhathāti vadeyya. Te ca tadā ayujjhamānā vicareyyuṃ. Evaṃ santepi tesaṃ yujjhanasatti nāma natthīti ca, te yodhā nāma na hontīti ca na sakkā vattunti.

This being so, in the case of the attainment of cessation, the preceding consciousness is the consciousness of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and the succeeding consciousness is a noble fruition-consciousness. Between these two consciousnesses, one can be without consciousness for one day and night, two days and nights, and so on, up to seven days and nights. Also, in the plane of non-percipient beings, the death-consciousness in a previous sense-sphere existence is the preceding consciousness, and the relinking-consciousness in a subsequent sense-sphere existence is the succeeding consciousness; between these two consciousnesses, a person remains without consciousness for five hundred eons in the plane of non-percipient beings. In these cases, are the two preceding consciousnesses devoid of the condition-potency for producing, immediately after themselves, another phenomenon of a similar nature? They are not. For, being obstructed by the great power of meditative development and resolution, the preceding consciousnesses, while ceasing, cease possessed of the potency to produce another phenomenon in proximity. And the succeeding consciousnesses, when arising, do not arise at that moment in an uninterrupted sequence but arise only after a long time. Yet it cannot be said on this account alone that the preceding consciousnesses lack the potency to produce another phenomenon in proximity, nor that they are not contiguity-condition phenomena. It is like a king's soldiers. The king, knowing the time, might say: 'Do not fight now, it is not the time for battle. Fight only at such-and-such a time.' And they would then wander about without fighting. Even so, it cannot be said that they lack the capacity to fight or that they are not soldiers.

Ettha vadeyyuṃ, imasmiṃ paccaye te pana arūpadhammabhāvena asaṇṭhāna jātikattā majjheparicchedadhammassa nāma kassaci abhāvato sabbaso antararahitā [M 0.0475] eva hontīti vuttaṃ. Evaṃ sante pubbe ārammaṇa paccaye bherisaddavīṇāsaddopamāhi yo cittānaṃ khaṇe khaṇe uppādo ca nirodho ca vutto, so amhehi kathaṃ paccetabboti. Aññamaññaviruddhānaṃ nānācittānaṃ khaṇamattepi pubbāpara parivattanassa loke paññāyanato. Ayamattho pubbe citta yamakadīpaniyaṃ vitthārato vuttoyevāti. Samanantarapaccaya dīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Here some might say: 'In this condition it was said that because they are immaterial phenomena, by nature without shape, and due to the absence of any delimiting phenomenon, they are completely without an interval.' This being so, how are we to understand what was said previously under the Object Condition, with the similes of the sound of a drum and the sound of a lute, about the momentary arising and ceasing of consciousnesses? Because the succession of different, mutually opposed consciousnesses, preceding and succeeding one another even in a single moment, is apparent to the world. This matter has already been explained in detail before in the Exposition on the Yamaka of Consciousness. The exposition of the Contiguity Condition is finished.

6. Sahajātapaccayo

6. Co-nascence Condition

Paccayadhammavibhāgo ca paccayuppannadhammavibhāgoca vuccati. Ekato uppannā sabbepi cittacetasikā dhammā aññamaññaṃ sahajāta paccayā ca honti sahajātapaccayuppannā ca. Paṭisandhināmakkhandhā ca paṭisandhisahajātaṃ hadayavatthu ca aññamaññaṃ sahajātapaccayā ca honti sahajātapaccayuppannā ca. Sabbāni mahābhūtānipi aññamaññaṃ sahajātapaccayā ca honti paccayuppannadhammā ca. Paṭisandhicittassa uppādakkhaṇe sabbāni kammajarūpāni ca pavattikāle tassa tassa cittassa uppādakkhaṇe tena tena cittena jātāni sabbāni cittajarūpāni ca sahajātacittassa paccayuppannāni nāma. Sabbāni upādārūpāni sahajātamahābhūtānaṃ paccayuppannāni nāma.

The analysis of the conditioning phenomenon and the analysis of the conditioned phenomenon are stated. All states of consciousness and mental factors that arise together are mutually a co-nascence condition for each other and are also phenomena conditioned by co-nascence. The mental aggregates at relinking and the heart-base co-nascent with relinking are mutually a co-nascence condition for each other and are also phenomena conditioned by co-nascence. All the great primary elements are mutually a co-nascence condition for each other and are also conditioned phenomena. At the arising-moment of the relinking-consciousness, all matter born of kamma, and during the course of life, at the arising-moment of each consciousness, all matter born of that consciousness, are called phenomena conditioned by the co-nascent consciousness. All derived matter is called phenomena conditioned by the co-nascent great primary elements.

Kenaṭṭhena sahajāto, kenaṭṭhena paccayo. Saha jānanaṭṭhena sahajāto, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayo. Tattha sahajānanaṭṭhenāti yo dhammo jāyamāno attano paccayuppannehi dhammehi saheva sayañca jāyati uppajjati, attano paccayuppanne ca dhamme attanā saheva janeti uppādeti, tassa so attho sahajānanaṭṭho nāma.

In what sense is it co-nascent? In what sense is it a condition? It is co-nascent in the sense of arising together; it is a condition in the sense of being a support. Herein, 'in the sense of arising together' means this: when a phenomenon, while coming into being, arises and is born together with the phenomena conditioned by it, and generates and produces the phenomena conditioned by it together with itself—this is the meaning called 'the sense of arising together'.

Yathā sūriyo nāma udayanto sūriyātape ca sūriyā loke ca attanā saheva janayanto udeti. Yathā ca padīpo nāma jalanto padīpātape ca padīpāloke ca attanā saheva janayanto jalati. Evamevaṃ ayaṃ paccayadhammo uppajjamāno attano paccayuppannadhamme attanā saheva uppādeti. Tattha sūriyo viya ekameko nāma dhammo, sūriyātapā viya taṃsampayuttadhammā, sūriyālokā viya sahajātarūpadhammā. Tathā sūriyo viya ekameko mahābhūtarūpadhammo, sūriyātapā viya sahajātamahābhūtadhammā, sūriyālokā viya sahajātaupādārūpadhammā, esa nayo padīpupamāyapīti. Sahajātapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Just as the sun, while rising, rises generating the sun's heat and the sun's light together with itself; and just as a lamp, while burning, burns generating the lamp's heat and the lamp's light together with itself; in the very same way, this conditioning phenomenon, while arising, produces its conditioned phenomena together with itself. Herein, a single mental state is like the sun; its associated states are like the sun's heat; the co-nascent material phenomena are like the sun's light. Similarly, a single great primary material phenomenon is like the sun; the co-nascent great primary phenomena are like the sun's heat; the co-nascent derived material phenomena are like the sun's light. This same method applies also to the simile of the lamp. The exposition of the Co-nascence Condition is finished.

7. Aññamaññapaccayo

7. Mutuality Condition

Sahajātapaccaye [M 0.0476] vibhattesu dhammesu yo yo paccaya dhammoti vutto, so so eva idha paccayadhammo ceva paccayuppanna dhammo ca hoti. Sabbepi cittacetasikā dhammā aññamaññassa paccayadhammā ca honti aññamaññassa paccayuppannadhammā. Sahajātā cattāro mahābhūtā aññamaññassa paccayadhammā ca honti aññamaññassa paccayuppannadhammā ca. Paṭisandhināmakkhandhā ca paṭisandhi sahajātaṃ hadayavatthurūpañca aññamaññassa paccayadhammā ca honti aññamaññassa paccayuppannadhammā ca. Attho suviññeyyoyeva.

Among the phenomena analyzed under the co-nascence condition, whatever is called a conditioning phenomenon is here both a conditioning phenomenon and a conditionally arisen phenomenon. All states of mind and mental factors are conditioning phenomena for one another and conditionally arisen phenomena for one another. The four co-nascent great elements are conditioning phenomena for one another and conditionally arisen phenomena for one another. The mental aggregates at rebirth-linking and the heart-base materiality co-nascent with them are conditioning phenomena for one another and conditionally arisen phenomena for one another. The meaning is quite easy to understand.

Yathā aññamaññaṃ nissāya ussāpitā tayo daṇḍā aññamaññassa nissayā ca honti, aññamaññaṃ nissitā ca. Tesu ekamekasmiṃ ussite sabbe ussitā honti, ekamekasmiṃ patante sabbe patanti. Evaṃ aññamaññadhammā ca daṭṭhabbā.

Just as three sticks, set up leaning against one another, are a support for one another and are dependent on one another. When one of them is lifted up, all are lifted up; when one of them falls, all fall. So too should mutually related phenomena be understood.

Ettha vadeyyuṃ, sabbe cetasikā dhammā cittapaccayaṃ alabha mānā uppajjituṃ na sakkontīti yuttaṃ. Kasmā. Phusanādīnaṃ cetasika kiccānaṃ vijānanakiccapubbaṅgamattā, manopubbaṅgamāti hi vuttaṃ. Cittaṃ pana cetasikapaccayaṃ alabhamānaṃ uppajjituṃ na sakkotīti na yujjeyyāti. Vuccate, cetasikadhammā nāma cittassa sahāyaṅgāni honti. Tasmā tehi vinā cittampi uppajjituṃ na sakkotiyeva. Eseva nayo catūsu mahābhūtesupīti. Upādārūpāni pana mahābhūtānaṃ nissandamattattā sahāyaṅgāni na hontīti. Nanu āhārarūpañca jīvitarūpañca paccayavisesattā sahāyaṅgaṃ hotīti. Vuccate, ṭhitiyā eva sahāyaṅgaṃ hoti. Na uppāde. Idha pana uppāde sahāyaṅgaṃ adhippetanti. Aññamaññapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Here some might say: 'It is proper that all mental factors cannot arise without obtaining mind as their condition. Why? Because the functions of the mental factors, such as contact, are preceded by the function of consciousness, for it is said: “Mind is the forerunner.” But it would not be proper that mind cannot arise without obtaining the mental factors as its condition.' It is said in reply: Mental factors are, by nature, associates of the mind. Therefore, without them, the mind too cannot possibly arise. This same principle applies also to the four great elements. Derived materiality, however, being merely a product of the great elements, are not their associates. (An objection:) But are not nutritive essence and the life faculty, being special conditions, associates? (The reply:) It is said: They are associates only for maintenance, not for origination. But here an associate in origination is intended. The exposition of the Mutuality Condition is finished.

8. Nissayapaccayo

8. Dependence Condition

Tividho nissayapaccayo, sahajātanissayo vatthu purejātanissayo vatthārammaṇapurejātanissayo.

The dependence condition is threefold: co-nascence-dependence, basis-prenascence-dependence, and basis-object-prenascence-dependence.

Tattha katamo sahajātanissayo. Sabbo sahajāta paccayo sahajātanissayo. Tasmā tattha paccayavibhāgo ca paccayuppannavibhāgo ca sahajātapaccaye vuttanayena veditabbo.

Herein, what is co-nascence-dependence? Every co-nascence condition is a co-nascence-dependence. Therefore, the analysis of conditioning phenomena and the analysis of conditionally arisen phenomena in that regard should be understood in the way stated for the co-nascence condition.

Katamo pana vatthupurejātanissayo. Cha vatthūni cakkhu vatthu sotavatthu ghānavatthu jivhāvatthu kāyavatthu hadayavatthu. Imāni chavatthūni pavattikāle sattānaṃ viññāṇadhātūnaṃ vatthupure jātanissayo.

But what is basis-prenascence-dependence? There are six bases: the eye-basis, ear-basis, nose-basis, tongue-basis, body-basis, and heart-basis. During the course of existence, these six bases are the basis-prenascence-dependence for the seven consciousness-elements.

Vatthurūpameva [M 0.0477] purejātaṃ hutvā nissayo vatthupurejāta nissayo. Tattha cittacetasikānaṃ nissayaṭṭhānaṭṭhena vatthu nāma. Purejātanti attano attano paccayuppannassa dhammassa uppattikkhaṇato purime khaṇe jātaṃ.

The basis-materiality itself, having arisen previously, is a dependence; this is basis-prenascence-dependence. Herein, it is called 'basis' because it is the support-site for mind and mental factors. 'Prenascent' means born in a moment prior to the moment of origination of its own conditionally arisen phenomenon.

Tattha paṭisandhicittaṃ tadā purejātassa vatthurūpassa abhāvato attanā sahuppannameva hadayavatthurūpaṃ nissāya uppajjati. Paṭhama bhavaṅgacittaṃ pana paṭisandhicittena sahuppannahadaya vatthurūpaṃ nissāya uppajjati. Dutiyabhavaṅgacittaṃ paṭhamabhavaṅgacittena sahuppannaṃ nissāya uppajjati. Evaṃ tatiyabhavaṅgacittaṃ dutiya bhavaṅgacittena sahuppannantiādinā yāvamaraṇāsannakālā dvinnaṃ manodhātumanoviññāṇadhātūnaṃ vatthupurejātanissayo veditabbo.

Herein, the rebirth-linking consciousness, because there is no pre-arisen material basis at that time, arises in dependence on the heart-basis that arises simultaneously with it. The first life-continuum consciousness, however, arises in dependence on the heart-basis that arose simultaneously with the rebirth-linking consciousness. The second life-continuum consciousness arises in dependence on what arose simultaneously with the first life-continuum consciousness. Thus, from the third life-continuum consciousness, which arises in dependence on what arose simultaneously with the second, and so on up to the time approaching death, the basis-prenascence-dependence for the mind-element and the mind-consciousness-element should be understood.

Yathā vīṇāsaddā nāma vīṇātantīsu vīṇādaṇḍakehi paharaṇavegena eva jāyanti, no aññathā. Tathā pañcaviññāṇāni nāma pañcavatthusaṅkhātesu pañcadvāresu pañcannaṃ ārammaṇānaṃ āpātā gamanavegena eva jāyanti, no aññathā.

Just as the sounds of a lute arise only by the force of striking the lute strings with the lute's plectrum, and not otherwise, so too the five consciousnesses arise only by the force of the impingement of the five objects on the five sense doors, which are reckoned as the five bases, and not otherwise.

Āpātāgamanañca tesaṃ dvārārammaṇānaṃ ṭhitipattakāle eva hoti. Āpātāgamanapaccayā ca dvikkhattuṃ bhavaṅgaṃ calati. Bhavaṅgacalana paccayā ca āvajjanaṃ uppajjati. Āvajjanapaccayā ca tāni pañcaviññāṇāni uppajjanti. Tasmā cakkhādīni pañcavatthūni pure atītabhavaṅgacittassa uppādakkhaṇe uppannāni eva pañcaviññāṇadhātūnaṃ vatthupurejātapaccayā honti.

And the impingement of those sense doors and objects occurs precisely at the moment they attain presence. Conditioned by the impingement, the life-continuum vibrates twice. Conditioned by the vibration of the life-continuum, adverting arises. Conditioned by adverting, those five sense-consciousnesses arise. Therefore, the five bases—the eye, etc.—having arisen previously at the arising-moment of the preceding life-continuum consciousness, become the basis-prenascence condition for the five sense-consciousness elements.

Maraṇāsannakāle pana sabbāni cha vatthūni cuticittato pure sattarasamassa bhavaṅgacittassa uppādakkhaṇe eva uppajjanti, tato paraṃ na uppajjanti. Tasmā maraṇāsannakāle bhavaṅgacittāni ca sabbāni chadvārikavīthicittāni ca cuticittañca puretaraṃ uppannāni tāniyeva attano attano vatthūni nissāya uppajjanti. Ayaṃ vatthupurejāta nissayo.

At the time approaching death, however, all six bases arise only at the arising-moment of the seventeenth life-continuum consciousness before the death-consciousness, and they do not arise after that. Therefore, at the time approaching death, the life-continuum cittas, all the cognitive-process cittas of the six doors, and the death-consciousness arise in dependence on their respective bases, those very ones that had arisen even earlier. This is basis-prenascence-dependence.

Katamo pana vatthārammaṇapurejātanissayo. Yadā attano ajjhattaṃ vatthurūpaṃ ārabbha yaṃ rūpaṃ nissāya mama manoviññāṇaṃ vattati, etaṃ mama eso hamasmi eso me attāti evaṃ taṇhāmāna diṭṭhīhi gahaṇavasena vā etaṃ aniccaṃ etaṃ dukkhaṃ etaṃ anattāti evaṃ sammasanavasena vā āvajjanādīni manodvārikavīthi cittāni pavattanti. Tadā taṃ taṃ vatthurūpaṃ paccekaṃ tesaṃ nissayavatthu ca hoti tesaṃ ārammaṇañca. Tasmā taṃ taṃ hadayarūpaṃ tassa tassa cittuppādassa vatthārammaṇapurejātapaccayo hoti. Ayaṃ vatthārammaṇa purejātanissayo. Evaṃ nissayapaccayo tividho hoti.

What, then, is basis-object-prenascence-dependence? When, taking as object one's own internal basis-materiality—that is, the materiality on which one's mind-consciousness depends—the mind-door cognitive process cittas, beginning with adverting, occur, either by way of grasping through craving, conceit, and wrong view, thinking: 'This is mine, this I am, this is my self,' or by way of comprehension, thinking: 'This is impermanent, this is suffering, this is non-self.' Then that particular basis-materiality is for each of them both the support-basis and the object. Therefore, that particular heart-materiality is the basis-object-prenascence condition for the arising of that particular citta. This is basis-object-prenascence-dependence. Thus the dependence condition is threefold.

Idha [M 0.0478] suttantanissayopi vattabbo. Ime manussā vā tiracchānagatā vā rukkhādayo vā mahāpathaviyaṃ patiṭṭhitā, mahā pathavī ca heṭṭhā mahāudakakkhandhe, mahāudakakkhandho ca heṭṭhā mahāvātakkhandhe, mahāvātakkhandho ca heṭṭhā ajaṭākāse, manussā gehesu, bhikkhū vihāresu, devā dibbavimānesūtiādinā sabbaṃ lokappavattiṃ ñatvā nissayapaccayo veditabbo. Nissayapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Here the Suttanta-style dependence should also be recited. Knowing that all these—humans, animals, trees, etc.—are established on the great earth; and the great earth on the great mass of water below; and the great mass of water on the great mass of wind below; and the great mass of wind on the open space; and that humans are in houses, bhikkhus in monasteries, devas in celestial mansions, and so on—having known the entire proceeding of the world in this way, one should understand the dependence condition. The exposition of the dependence condition is finished.

9. Upanissayapaccayo

9. Decisive Support Condition

Tividho upanissayapaccayo, ārammaṇūpanissayo anantarūpanissayo pakatūpanissayo. Tattha ārammaṇūpa nissayo ārammaṇādhipatipaccayasadiso, anantarūpanissayo anantarapaccayasadiso.

The decisive support condition is threefold: object-decisive-support condition, proximity-decisive-support condition, and natural decisive support condition. Therein, the object-decisive-support condition is similar to the object-predominance condition, and the proximity-decisive-support condition is similar to the proximity condition.

Katamo pakatūpanissayo. Sabbepi atītānāgata paccuppannā ajjhattabahiddhābhūtā cittacetasikarūpadhammā ca nibbānañca paññatti ca sabbesaṃ paccuppannānaṃ cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ yathārahaṃ pakatūpanissayo.

What is the natural decisive support condition? All past, future, and present, internal and external phenomena—consciousness, mental factors, and material form, as well as Nibbāna and concepts—are, as is fit, a natural decisive support condition for all present consciousness and mental factors.

Tattha atīto parinibbuto amhākaṃ buddho ca dhammo ca ariyasāvakasaṅgho ca sammutisaṅghaparamparā ca amhākaṃ pacchima janānaṃ kusaluppattiyā pakatūpanissayapaccayo. Tathā loke atītā kālaṅkatā mātāpitaro ca ācariyā ca paṇḍitasamaṇabrāhmaṇā ca pākaṭā nānātitthācariyā ca mahiddhikā mahānubhāvā porāṇakarājāno ca pacchimakānaṃ janānaṃ kusaluppattiyā vā akusaluppattiyā vā sukhadukkhuppattiyā vā pakatūpanissayo. Tathā hi te pacchimakānaṃ janānaṃ atthāya nānāsaddhammapaññattiyo vā nānāasaddhammapaññattiyo vā nānālokūpakaraṇāni vā pure paṭṭhapesuṃ. Pacchimajanā ca tehi paṭṭhapitesu dānasīlādidhammesu vā lokacāritta kulagottacāritta dhammesu vā nānādiṭṭhivādesu vā nānākammāyatanasippāyatana vijjāṭhānesu vā yathāpaṭṭhapitesu gāmanigamanagarakhettavatthu taḷākapokkharaṇiāvāṭādīsu vā geharathasakaṭanāvā sampotasambandhādīsu vā jātarūparajatamaṇimuttādīsu vā dāyajjaṃ paṭipajjantā loke vaḍḍhanti.

Therein, our past Buddha who has attained final Nibbāna, the Dhamma, the Sangha of noble disciples, and the lineage of the conventional Sangha are a natural decisive support condition for the arising of wholesome states in us, the later generation. Similarly, in the world, deceased parents, teachers, wise ascetics and brahmins, prominent teachers of various other persuasions, and ancient kings of great psychic power and great might are a natural decisive support for the arising of wholesome or unwholesome states, or for the arising of happiness or suffering in later generations. For they established in the past for the benefit of later generations various true-Dhamma teachings or various false-Dhamma teachings or various worldly instruments. And later generations, having entered upon as their inheritance what was established by them—whether in matters of giving, virtuous behavior, etc.; or in worldly conduct and the conduct of family and clan; or in various views and doctrines; or in various fields of work, crafts, and sciences; or in established villages, towns, cities, fields, grounds, ponds, lotus ponds, wells, etc.; or in houses, chariots, carts, boats, ships, connections, etc.; or in gold, silver, gems, pearls, etc.—prosper in the world.

Anāgatopi metteyyo nāma buddho ca tassa dhammo ca tassa saṅgho ca etarahi bahujjanānaṃ pāramipuññappavattiyā pakatūpanissayapaccayo. Tathā imasmiṃ bhave ca pacchime kāle paṭilabhissamānā issariyaṭṭhānadhanadhaññasampattiyo purime kāle ṭhitānaṃ mahājanānaṃ nānābhisaṅkhāruppattiyā pakatūpanissaya paccayo. Anāgatabhave ca anubhavissamānā bhavasampatti bhoga sampattiyo [M 0.0479] maggaphalanibbānasampattiyo ca etarahi paccuppannabhave ṭhitānaṃ dānasīlādipuññakiriyuppattiyā pakatūpanissayapaccayo. Yathā hi loke hemante kāle dhaññapphalāni labhissāmāti vassike kāle kassanavappanakammāni ārabhanti, kamme siddhe taṃ taṃ dhanaṃ labhissāmāti pubbabhāge taṃ taṃ vīriyakammaṃ vā taṃ taṃ paññākammaṃ vā ārabhanti. Tattha dhaññapphalappaṭilābho ca taṃ taṃ dhanappaṭi lābhoca taṃ taṃ kammārambhassa anāgatapakatūpanissayapaccayo taṃ taṃ kammārambho ca dhaññapphalappaṭilābhassa ca taṃ taṃ dhanappaṭi lābhassa ca atītapakatūpanissayapaccayo. Evameva pacchime anāgate kāle nānākammapphalāni sampassantā patthayantā mahājanā purime paccuppanne kāle nānāpuññakammāni ārabhanti. Tattha puññapphalāni puññakammānaṃ anāgatapakatūpanissayapaccayo. Puññakammāni puññapphalānaṃ atītapakatūpanissayapaccayo. Tasmā anāgatapakatūpanissayopi atītapakatūpanissayo viya atimahanto paccayo hoti.

The future Buddha named Metteyya, his Dhamma, and his Sangha are, even now, a natural decisive support condition for the performance of meritorious actions for the perfections by many people. Similarly, the attainments of positions of authority, wealth, and grain to be obtained in this life at a later time are a natural decisive support condition for the arising of various formations in the great populace living at an earlier time. And the attainments of existence, attainments of wealth, and attainments of path, fruition, and Nibbāna to be experienced in a future existence are, for those living in the present existence, a natural decisive support condition for the arising of meritorious deeds such as giving and virtuous behavior. Just as in the world people begin the work of ploughing and sowing in the rainy season, thinking, 'We will obtain the harvest of grain in the winter season,' and they undertake such and such work of energy or such and such work of wisdom in the preliminary stage, thinking, 'When the work is accomplished, we will obtain such and such wealth.' Therein, the obtaining of the harvest of grain and the acquisition of such and such wealth is a future natural decisive support condition for undertaking that work; and undertaking that work is a past natural decisive support condition for obtaining the harvest of grain and such and such wealth. In the same way, seeing and desiring the various fruits of kamma in the distant future, the great populace undertakes various meritorious deeds in the earlier, present time. Therein, the fruits of merit are a future natural decisive support condition for the meritorious deeds, and the meritorious deeds are a past natural decisive support condition for the fruits of merit. Therefore, the future natural decisive support condition, like the past natural decisive support condition, is an extremely great condition.

Paccuppannā buddhāyo paccayā paccuppannānaṃ manussadevabrahmānaṃ paccuppannā mātāpitaro paccuppannānaṃ puttadhītādīnaṃ paccuppannapakatūpa nissayo nāma. So supākaṭoyeva.

Present Buddhas are a condition for present humans, devas, and brahmās; present parents are for present sons, daughters, etc. This is called the present natural decisive support condition. It is very clear.

Ajjhattabhūtā pakatūpanissayadhammā nāma buddhādīsu saviññāṇakasantānesu uppannā paccayadhammā. Bahiddhābhūtā pakatūpanissayadhammā nāma sattānaṃ patiṭṭhānabhūtā pathavipabbatanadī samuddādayo tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ bahūpakārā araññavanarukkha tiṇapubbaṇṇāparaṇṇādayo candasūriyagahanakkhattādayo vassodakaaggivātasītauṇhādayo ca sabbepi te sattānaṃ kusaluppattiyā vā akusaluppattiyā vā sukhuppattiyā vā dukkhuppattiyā vā balavapaccayā honti.

Internal natural decisive support phenomena are the conditioning phenomena that have arisen in the conscious continuums of the Buddha and others. External natural decisive support phenomena are: the supporting grounds of beings, such as the earth, mountains, rivers, and oceans; things of great benefit to those beings, such as forests, woods, trees, grass, early grain, and later grain; the moon, sun, planets, and constellations; and rain, water, fire, wind, cold, and heat. All these are powerful conditions for the arising of wholesome or unwholesome states, or for the arising of happiness or suffering in beings.

Ime janā diṭṭheva dhamme parinibbāyissāmāti bodhipakkhiya dhamme vā bhāventi, anāgate buddhakāle parinibbāyissāmāti pāramīdhamme vā paripūrenti. Tattha nibbānaṃ tesaṃ dhammānaṃ uppattiyā balavapaccayo hoti.

These people develop the aids to enlightenment, thinking, 'We will attain final Nibbāna in this very life,' or they fulfill the perfections, thinking, 'We will attain final Nibbāna in the time of a future Buddha.' Therein, Nibbāna is a powerful condition for the arising of those states.

Loke nānāvohārabhūtā nāmapaññattiyo ca buddha sāsane tipiṭakapariyattidhammabhūtā nāmapaññattiyo ca tesaṃ tesaṃ atthānaṃ jānanatthāya balavapaccayo.

The name-concepts that are various conventions in the world, and the name-concepts that are the scriptural teachings of the Tipiṭaka in the Buddha's Dispensation, are a powerful condition for knowing their respective meanings.

Tattha saṅkhatadhammā nāma paccaye sati uppajjanti, asati nuppajjanti. Uppajjitvāpi paccaye sati tiṭṭhanti, asati na tiṭṭhanti. Tasmā tesaṃ uppattiyā vā ṭhitiyā vā paccayo nāma icchitabbo. Nibbānaṃ pana paññatti ca asaṅkhatadhammā honti [M 0.0480] ajātidhammā anuppādadhammā nicca dhammā dhuvadhammā. Tasmā tesaṃ uppādāya vā ṭhitiyā vā paccayo nāma natthīti.

Herein, conditioned phenomena arise when a condition is present and do not arise when it is absent. Having arisen, they persist when a condition is present and do not persist when it is absent. Therefore, a condition for their arising or persistence is to be required. But Nibbāna and concepts are unconditioned phenomena: they are unborn, unproduced, permanent, and stable. Therefore, there is no condition for their arising or persistence.

Kusalo kusalassa upanissayo. Saddhaṃ upanissāya dānaṃ deti, sīlaṃ samādiyatītiādinā supākaṭo. Tathā rāgaṃ upanissāya pāṇaṃ hanati, adinnaṃ ādiyatītiādinā sappāyaṃ utuṃ sappāyaṃ bhojanaṃ upanissāya kāyikaṃ sukhaṃ paccanubhotītiādinā ca akusalo akusalassa, abyākato abyākatassa upanissayopi supākaṭo.

The wholesome is a decisive support for the wholesome. This is very clear in such cases as: relying on faith, one gives a gift or undertakes the precepts. Similarly, that the unwholesome is a decisive support for the unwholesome, and the indeterminate for the indeterminate, is also very clear, as when, relying on lust, one destroys life or takes what is not given; or when, relying on a suitable climate and suitable food, one experiences bodily pleasure.

Kusalo pana akusalassapi balavūpanissayo. Dānaṃ datvā tena dānena attānaṃ ukkaṃseti, paraṃ vambheti. Tathā sīla sampanno hutvā samādhisampanno hutvā paññāsampanno hutvā attānaṃ ukkaṃseti, paraṃ vambheti.

But the wholesome is also a powerful decisive support for the unwholesome. Having given a gift, because of that gift one extols oneself and disparages others. Similarly, having become accomplished in virtuous behavior, accomplished in concentration, or accomplished in wisdom, one extols oneself and disparages others.

Kusalo abyākatassapi balavūpanissayo. Sabbāni catubhūmikakusalakammāni vā kammaparivārāni kusalāni vā kālantare catubhūmikānaṃ vipākābyākatānaṃ balavūpanissayo. Dānapāramiṃ pūrentā pūraṇakāle bahuṃ kāyikadukkhaṃ paccanubhavanti. Tathā sīlapāramiṃ nikkhamapāramiṃ paññāpāramiṃ vīriyapāramiṃ khantipāramiṃ saccapāramiṃ adhiṭṭhānapāramiṃ mettāpāramiṃ upekkhā pāramiṃ. Eseva nayo jhānabhāvanā maggabhāvanāsupi.

The wholesome is also a powerful decisive support for the indeterminate. All wholesome kamma of the four planes, or the wholesome states associated with kamma, are at a later time a powerful decisive support for the indeterminate resultants of the four planes. While fulfilling the perfection of giving, during the time of fulfillment they experience much bodily suffering. The same applies to the perfection of virtuous behavior, the perfection of renunciation, the perfection of wisdom, the perfection of energy, the perfection of patience, the perfection of truthfulness, the perfection of determination, the perfection of loving-kindness, and the perfection of equanimity. This same method applies also to the development of jhāna and the development of the path.

Akusalo kusalassapi balavūpanissayo. Idhekacco pāpaṃ katvā pacchā vippaṭisārī hutvā tassa pāpassa pahānāya dānasīlajhānamaggakusalāni sampādeti. Taṃ pāpaṃ tesaṃ kusalānaṃ balavūpanissayo.

The unwholesome is also a powerful decisive support for the wholesome. Here, a certain person, having done an evil deed, afterwards becomes remorseful and, for the abandoning of that evil deed, accomplishes wholesome states such as giving, virtuous behavior, jhāna, and the path. That evil deed is a powerful decisive support for those wholesome states.

Akusalo abyākatassapi balavūpanissayo. Idha bahū janā duccaritāni katvā catūsu apāyesu patitvā apāyadukkhaṃ paccanubhavanti. Diṭṭhadhammepi keci attano vā parassa vā duccaritakamma paccayā bahuṃ dukkhaṃ paccanubhavanti. Keci duccaritakammena dhanaṃ labhitvā sukhaṃ paccanubhavanti. Bahujjanā rāgamūlakaṃ bahuṃ dukkhaṃ paccanubhavanti, dosamūlakaṃ diṭṭhimūlakaṃ mānamūlakanti.

The unwholesome is also a powerful decisive support for the indeterminate. Here, many people, having committed misconduct, fall into the four planes of misery and experience the suffering of the planes of misery. Even in this very life, some experience much suffering on account of their own or another's misconduct. Some, having obtained wealth through misconduct, experience happiness. Many people experience much suffering rooted in lust, rooted in hatred, rooted in views, and rooted in conceit.

Abyākato kusalassapi balavūpanissayo. Dhana sampattiyā dānaṃ deti, sīlaṃ pūreti, paññaṃ pūreti, bhāvanāsappāyaṃ āvāsaṃ vā leṇaṃ vā guhaṃvā rukkhaṃ vā araññaṃ vā pabbataṃ vā gocaragāmaṃ vā utusappāyaṃ vā āhārasappāyaṃ vā labhitvā taṃ taṃ bhāvanaṃ bhāveti.

The indeterminate is also a powerful decisive support for the wholesome. Through the attainment of wealth one gives gifts, fulfills virtuous behavior, and fulfills wisdom. And having obtained a residence suitable for development—a dwelling, a cave, a grotto, a tree, a forest, a mountain, a resort village for alms, or suitable weather or suitable food—one develops that particular development.

Abyākato akusalassapi balavūpanissayo. Loke cakkhusampadaṃ nissāya bahūni dassanamūlāni akusalāni uppajjanti. Esa nayo sotasampadādīsu[M 0.0481]. Tathā hatthasampadaṃ pādasampadaṃ satthasampadaṃ āvudhasampadantiādināpi vattabbo. Evaṃ upanissayo tividho hoti.

The indeterminate is also a powerful decisive support for the unwholesome. In the world, depending on the perfection of the eye-faculty, many unwholesome states rooted in seeing arise. This method applies to the perfection of the ear-faculty, etc. Similarly, it should be stated regarding the perfection of the hands, the perfection of the feet, the perfection of knives, the perfection of weapons, etc. Thus, the decisive support condition is threefold.

Idha suttantūpanissayopi vattabbo. Kalyāṇamittaṃ upa nissāya pāpamittaṃ upanissāya gāmaṃ upanissāya araññaṃ upanissāyātiādinā bahūsu ṭhānesu āgato. Api ca pañcaniyāmadhammā sattaloka saṅkhāraloka okāsaloka saṅkhātānaṃ tiṇṇaṃ lokānaṃ avicchinnappavattiyā balavapaccayā honti. Ayañca attho niyāmadīpaniyaṃ amhehi vitthārato dīpitoti.

Here, dependence on the discourses should also be adduced. It is found in many places, as in the phrases: 'depending on a good friend,' 'depending on a bad friend,' 'depending on a village,' 'depending on a forest,' etc. Furthermore, the five natural orders are strong conditions for the uninterrupted continuity of the three worlds, namely, the world of beings, the world of formations, and the world of location. And this meaning has been explained by us in detail in the Niyāmadīpanī.

Kenaṭṭhena ārammaṇūpanissayoti. Adhipatibhūtaṃ ārammaṇameva ārammaṇikadhammānaṃ balavanissayaṭṭhena ārammaṇūpa nissayo.

In what sense is it object decisive support? The object itself, being dominant, is a strong support for states related to the object; hence it is object decisive support.

Kenaṭṭhena anantarūpanissayoti. Purimaṃ anantaracittameva pacchimassa anantaracittassa uppajjanatthāya balavanissayaṭṭhena anantarūpanissayo. Mātā viya purimacittaṃ, putto viya pacchimacittaṃ. Yathā mātā attano anantare puttassa uppajjanatthāya balavūpa nissayo hoti, tathā purimacittaṃ pacchimacittassa uppajjanatthāyāti.

In what sense is it contiguity decisive support? The immediately preceding consciousness itself is a powerful support for the arising of the immediately succeeding consciousness; hence it is contiguity decisive support. The preceding consciousness is like a mother, the succeeding consciousness like a child. Just as a mother is a powerful decisive support for the birth of her child immediately after herself, so too is the preceding consciousness for the arising of the succeeding consciousness.

Kenaṭṭhena pakatūpanissayoti. Pakatiyāva loke paṇḍitānaṃ pākaṭo upanissayo pakatūpanissayo.

In what sense is it natural decisive support? A decisive support that is naturally evident to the wise in the world is natural decisive support.

Ettha ca anantarūpanissayānubhāvo anantaracitte eva pharati. Pakatūpanissayānubhāvo pana dūrepi pharatiyeva. Tathā hi imasmiṃ bhave purimesu divasesu vā māsesu vā saṃvaccharesu vā diṭṭhasutaghāyitasāyitaphusitaviññātāni ārammaṇāni pacchātathārūpe paccaye sati vassasatepi manodvāre āpātaṃ āgacchanti. Idaṃ nāma pubbe mayā diṭṭhaṃ sutantiādinā sattā anussaranti. Opapātikasattā pana purimabhavaṃpi anussaranti. Tathā manussesupi appekacce jātissarasaññālābhino. Tathā pubbe anekasatasahassesu diṭṭhādīsu vatthūsu pacchā ekasmiṃ khaṇe ekaṃ vatthuṃ disvā vā sutvā vā tasmiṃ eva khaṇe bahūsupi tesu manoviññāṇasantānaṃ pharamānaṃ pavattatīti. Upanissayapaccaya dīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Here, the influence of the contiguity decisive support condition operates only on the immediately following consciousness. However, the influence of the natural decisive support condition extends even to what is distant. For in this very life, objects seen, heard, smelled, tasted, touched, or cognized on previous days, months, or years may come to the mind-door even a hundred years later when a similar condition is present. Beings recollect, thinking, “This is what I saw before, this is what I heard before,” and so on. Beings of spontaneous rebirth, moreover, recollect their previous existences as well. Likewise, even among humans, a few are able to recall their past births. Furthermore, among many hundreds of thousands of objects previously seen, etc., later—at a single moment—upon seeing or hearing just one object, a stream of mind-consciousness extending to many of those objects may arise at that very moment. The exposition of the decisive support condition is concluded.

10. Purejātapaccayo

10. Pre-nascence Condition

Tividho purejātapaccayo. Vatthupurejātapaccayo ca ārammaṇapurejātapaccayo ca vatthārammaṇapurejātapaccayo ca.

The pre-nascence condition is threefold: base pre-nascence condition, object pre-nascence condition, and base-and-object pre-nascence condition.

Tattha [M 0.0482] vatthupurejāto ca vatthārammaṇapurejāto ca pubbe nissayapaccaye nissayanāmena vuttā eva.

Among these, the base pre-nascence condition and the base-and-object pre-nascence condition have indeed already been stated under the name 'dependence' in the preceding section on the dependence condition.

Ārammaṇapurejāto nāma paccuppannāni aṭṭhārasanipphannarūpāni eva. Tesupi paccuppannāni rūpasaddādīni pañcārammaṇāni pañcannaṃ pañcaviññāṇa vīthicittānaṃ niyamato ārammaṇapurejātapaccayā honti. Yathā hi vīṇāsaddā nāma vīṇātantīsu vīṇādaṇḍakena paharaṇa paccayā eva uppajjanti. Evañca sati te saddā purejātāhi vīṇātanti vīṇādaṇḍakehi vinā uppajjituṃ na sakkonti. Evamevaṃ pañca viññāṇa vīthicittānipi pañcasu vatthudvāresu pañcannaṃ ārammaṇānaṃ āpātāgamana paccayā eva uppajjanti. Āpātāgamanañca tesaṃ dvinnaṃ ṭhitipattakāle eva hoti. Na kevalañca tasmiṃ kāle tāni pañcārammaṇāni tesu pañcavatthūsu eva āpātamāgacchanti. Atha kho bhavaṅgamanodvārepi āpātaṃ āgacchantiyeva. Tasmiṃ āpātagamanattā eva taṃ bhavaṅgampi dvikkhattuṃ calitvā upacchijjati, bhavaṅgupacchede eva tāni vīthicittāni uppajjanti. Evañca sati tāni vīthicittāni purejātehi vatthu dvārārammaṇehi vinā uppajjituṃ na sakkontīti. Tāni pana sabbānipi aṭṭhārasanipphannarūpāni niruddhāni hutvā atītānipi honti, anuppannāni hutvā anāgatānipi honti. Uppannāni hutvā paccuppannānipi honti. Sabbānipi manoviññāṇavīthicittānaṃ ārammaṇāni honti. Tesu paccuppannāni eva tesaṃ ārammaṇapurejātapaccayā honti. Yadā dūre vā paṭicchanne vā ṭhitaṃ taṃ taṃ ārammaṇavatthuṃ manasā eva ārammaṇaṃ karoti, tadā taṃ taṃ vatthu sace tattha tattha vijjamānaṃ hoti, paccuppannaṃ nāma hoti. Purejātapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

The object pre-nascence condition is the eighteen produced material phenomena in their present state. Among them, the five present objects—visible form, sound, etc.—are invariably the object pre-nascence condition for the cognitive processes of the five sense consciousnesses. Just as the sounds of a lute arise conditioned by the striking of the lute strings with the plectrum, and those sounds cannot arise without the pre-arisen lute strings and plectrum, so too the cognitive processes of the five sense consciousnesses arise only conditioned by the impingement of the five objects on their respective bases and doors. Their impingement occurs when they have reached their static phase. Not only do those five objects impinge upon their respective bases at that time, but they also impinge upon the mind-door, which is the life-continuum. Because of this impingement, the life-continuum vibrates twice and is then cut off. It is only when the life-continuum has been cut off that those cognitive processes arise. Thus, without those pre-arisen bases, doors, and objects, those cognitive processes cannot arise. All eighteen produced material phenomena—whether past and ceased, future and unarisen, or present and arisen—can serve as objects for mind-door cognitive processes. Among them, only the present ones act as the object pre-nascence condition. When one mentally takes as an object something distant or concealed, if that object actually exists there, it is called ‘present.’ The exposition of the pre-nascence condition is concluded.

11. Pacchājātapaccayo

11. Post-nascence Condition

Pacchimaṃ pacchimaṃ paccuppannaṃ cittaṃ purejātassa paccuppannassa catu samuṭṭhānikassa rūpakāyassa vuḍḍhiviruḷhiyā pacchājātapaccayo. Yathā taṃ pacchimavassesu anuvassaṃ vassamānāni vassodakāni purimavassesu jātānaṃ rukkhapotakānaṃ vuḍḍhiviruḷhiyā pacchājāta paccayā hontīti.

Each subsequent present consciousness is a post-nascence condition for the growth and development of this previously arisen present material body originating from the four causes. Just as, in the later rainy seasons, the rainwaters falling year after year are post-nascence conditions for the growth and development of the saplings born in the earlier rainy seasons.

Tattha pacchimaṃ pacchimaṃ cittanti paṭhamabhavaṅgato paṭṭhāya yāva cuti cittā sabbaṃ cittaṃ vuccati. Purejātassāti paṭisandhicittena sahuppannaṃ kammajarūpakāyaṃ ādiṃ katvā ajjhattasantānapariyāpanno sabbo catusamuṭṭhānikarūpakāyo vuccati.

Herein, 'each subsequent consciousness' refers to all consciousness from the first life-continuum consciousness up to the death consciousness. By 'previously arisen' is meant the entire material body originating from the four causes included in the individual continuity, beginning with the kamma-born material body that arose together with the rebirth-linking consciousness.

Paṭisandhicittena sahuppannassa kammajarūpakāyassa paṭhama bhavaṅgādīni pannarasabhavaṅgacittāni pacchājātapaccayā honti. Paṭisandhi cittaṃ pana tena kāyena sahuppannattā [M 0.0483] pacchajātaṃ na hoti. Soḷasamabhavaṅgacittañca tassa kāyassa bhijjanakkhette uppannattā paccayo na hoti. Tasmā pannarasabhavaṅgacittānīti vuttaṃ.

For the kamma-born material body that arose together with the rebirth-linking consciousness, the fifteen life-continuum consciousnesses beginning with the first are post-nascence conditions. However, the rebirth-linking consciousness, since it arises together with that body, is not a post-nascence condition. And the sixteenth life-continuum consciousness, since it arises at the moment of dissolution of that body, is not a condition. Therefore it is said, 'fifteen life-continuum consciousnesses'.

Paṭisandhicittassa ṭhitikkhaṇe pana dve rūpakāyā uppajjanti kammajarūpakāyo ca utujarūpakāyoca. Tathā bhaṅgakkhaṇepi. Paṭhamabhavaṅgassa uppādakkhaṇe pana tayo rūpakāyā uppajjanti kammajarūpakāyo ca utujarūpakāyo ca cittajarūpakāyo ca. Yadā bahiddhāhārappharaṇaṃ labhitvā ajjhattāhāro āhārajarūpakāyaṃ janeti, tato paṭṭhāya catusamuṭṭhānikā cattāro rūpakāyā dīpa jālā viya pavattanti. Te uppādakkhaṇaṃ atikkamma yāva ṭhitibhāvena dharanti, tāva pannarasacittāni tesaṃ kāyānaṃ pacchājātapaccayā hontiyeva.

At the moment of presence of the rebirth-linking consciousness, two material bodies arise: the kamma-born material body and the temperature-born material body. Similarly at the moment of dissolution. But at the moment of arising of the first life-continuum consciousness, three material bodies arise: the kamma-born, the temperature-born, and the consciousness-born material body. When, having received the suffusion of external nutriment, internal nutriment generates the nutriment-born material body, from then on the four material bodies originating from the four causes proceed like the flame of a lamp. As long as these, having passed the moment of arising, endure in their state of presence, for so long the fifteen consciousnesses are indeed post-nascence conditions for those bodies.

Vuḍḍhiviruḷhiyāti catusamuṭṭhānikarūpasantatiyā uparūpari vuḍḍhiyā ca viruḷhiyā ca. Tathāhi purimā purimā cattāro rūpakāyā sace pacchājāta paccayaṃ punappunaṃ labhanti, evaṃ sati te nirujjhantāpi pacchārūpasantatiparamparānaṃ vuḍḍhiyā ca viruḷhiyā ca vepullāya ca balava paccayā hutvā nirujjhantīti. Pacchājātapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

By 'growth and development' is meant the successive growth and development of the continuity of material phenomena originating from the four causes. For if the preceding four material bodies repeatedly obtain the post-nascence condition, this being so, though ceasing, they cease having become a strong condition for the growth, development, and abundance of the subsequent series in the continuity of material phenomena. The exposition of the post-nascence condition is concluded.

12. Āsevanapaccayo

12. Repetition Condition

Dvādasa akusalacittāni sattarasa lokiyakusala cittāni āvajjanadvayavajjitāni aṭṭhārasakiriyacittānīti satta cattālīsaṃ lokiyajavanacittāni āsevanapaccayo. Tesu nirantarappavattaṃ javanasantatiṃ patvā purimaṃ purimaṃ javanacittaṃ āsevana paccayo. Catūhi maggacittehi pacchimaṃ pacchimaṃ cittaṃ āsevanapaccayuppannaṃ.

The twelve unwholesome consciousnesses, the seventeen mundane wholesome consciousnesses, and the eighteen functional consciousnesses (excluding the two adverting consciousnesses)—these forty-seven mundane javana consciousnesses—are the repetition condition. Among these, having reached an uninterruptedly proceeding continuity of javanas, each preceding javana consciousness is a repetition condition. In the case of the four path consciousnesses, the immediately subsequent fruit consciousness is conditioned by repetition.

Kenaṭṭhena āsevananti. Uparūparipaguṇabhāvavaḍḍhanatthaṃthāma balavaḍḍhanatthañca parivāsaggāhāpanaṭṭhena.

In what sense is it repetition? In the sense of successively enhancing proficiency, and in the sense of increasing stamina and strength, by causing an imbuing.

Tattha paguṇabhāvoti punappunaṃ sajjhāyitassa pāḷipāṭhassa sukhena pavattanaṃ viya javanaṭṭhānajavanakiccasaṅkhāte ṭhānakiccavisese pacchima pacchimacittassa sukhena pavattanaṃ. Parivāso nāma koseyyavatthaṃ punappunaṃ sugandhena parivāsanaṃ viya cittasantāne punappunnaṃ rajjanadussanādinā vā arajjana adussanādinā vā parivāsanaṃ. Purimasmiṃ javanacitte niruddhepi tassa javanavego na nirujjhati, pacchimaṃ cittasantānaṃ pharamāno pavattatiyeva. Tasmā pacchimaṃ pacchimaṃ javanacittaṃ uppajjamānaṃ tena vegena paggahitaṃ balavataraṃ hutvā uppajjati. Evaṃ [M 0.0484] purimacittaṃ attano parivāsaṃ pacchimacittaṃ gaṇhāpeti. Pacchimañca cittaṃ purimassa cittassa parivāsaṃ gahetvā pavattati. Evaṃ santepi so āsevanavego pakatiyā sattahi cittavārehi parikkhayaṃ gacchati, tato paraṃ tadārammaṇavipāka cittaṃ vā uppajjati, bhavaṅga cittavāro vā pavattati.

Herein, proficiency is the smooth occurrence of each subsequent consciousness in its specific place and function—designated as the place of javana and the function of javana—like the smooth recitation of a Pāli text that has been repeatedly chanted. 'Imbuing' is the repeated imbuing of the mental continuity with passion, aversion, etc., or with non-passion, non-aversion, etc., like the repeated perfuming of a silk cloth with a fragrance. Even when the preceding javana consciousness has ceased, its javana-impetus does not cease but continues, pervading the subsequent mental continuity. Therefore each subsequent javana consciousness, arising, being sustained by that impetus, arises as more powerful. Thus the preceding consciousness causes the subsequent consciousness to take up its imbuing, and the subsequent consciousness proceeds having taken up the imbuing of the preceding consciousness. Even so, that impetus of repetition is naturally exhausted after seven javana moments. After that, either a resultant registration consciousness arises, or a life-continuum process occurs.

Idha suttantāsevanapaccayopi vattabbo. Satipaṭṭhānaṃ bhāveti, sammappadhānaṃ bhāveti, satisambojjhaṅgaṃ bhāveti, dhamma vicayasambojjhaṅgaṃ bhāveti, sammādiṭṭhiṃ bhāveti, sammāsaṅkappaṃ bhāvetītiādinā bahūsu ṭhānesu vutto. Tattha bhāvetīti ekaṃpi divasaṃ bhāveti, sattapi divasāni bhāveti, ekaṃpi māsaṃ bhāveti, sattapi māsāni bhāveti, ekaṃpi saṃvaccharaṃ bhāveti, sattapi saṃvaccharāni bhāvetīti attho.

Here, the repetition condition from the Suttanta should also be mentioned. It is said in many places: 'One develops the foundations of mindfulness, one develops the right strivings, one develops the enlightenment factor of mindfulness, one develops the enlightenment factor of discrimination of states, one develops right view, one develops right intention,' and so on. Therein, 'one develops' means: one develops for a single day, one develops for seven days; one develops for a single month, one develops for seven months; one develops for a single year, one develops for seven years.

Purimapurimesu bhavesu āsevitāni bhāvitāni bahulī katāni kusalāni vā akusalāni vā pacchimapacchimesu bhavesu balavatarānaṃ kusalānaṃ vā akusalānaṃ vā uppattiyā āsevana paccayo.

Wholesome or unwholesome states that were practiced, developed, and cultivated in former existences are a repetition condition for the arising of more powerful wholesome or unwholesome states in later existences.

Kālantare vā bhavantare vā tādisānaṃ kusalākusalānaṃ uppattiyā paccayo upanissayapaccayo nāma. Tesaṃyeva balavataratthāya paccayo āsevanapaccayo nāma.

The condition for the arising of such wholesome and unwholesome states at another time or in another existence is called the decisive support condition. The condition for the strengthening of those very same states is called the repetition condition.

Lokepi mahantesu cittabhāvanākammesu vācā bhāvanākammesu kāyabhāvanākammesu aṅgapaccaṅgabhāvanākammesu kammāyatanasippāyatanavijjāṭhānesu ca āsevanā bhāvanā bahulīkammānaṃ nissandaguṇā nāma sandissantiyeva.

In the world, too, in great undertakings of mental development, verbal development, bodily development, and development of the major and minor limbs, and in the spheres of work, crafts, and knowledge, the resultant qualities of repetition, development, and cultivation are certainly seen.

Sabbesaṃ khaṇikadhammānaṃ majjhe evarūpassa āsevana paccayassa vijjamānattā purisabalapurisathāmānaṃ uparūpari vaḍḍhana vasena cirakālaṃ pavattitāni purisakammāni nipphattiṃ pāpuṇanti, sabbaññubuddhabhāvaṃpi gacchanti. Āsevanapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Because of the existence of such a repetition condition among all momentary phenomena, human deeds, maintained for a long time by way of the successive increase of human strength and human stamina, reach completion, and they even attain the state of an omniscient Buddha. The exposition of the Repetition Condition is finished.

13. Kammapaccayo

13. Kamma Condition

Duvidho kammapaccayo sahajātakammapaccayo nānākkhaṇika kammapaccayo.

The kamma condition is twofold: conascence kamma condition and different-moment kamma condition.

Tattha khaṇattayasamaṅgi bhūtā sabbāpi kusalākusalā byākatacetanā sahajātakamma paccayo. Cetanāsampayuttā sabbepi cittacetasikā dhammāca paṭisandhicittena sahuppannā kammajarūpadhammā ca pavattikāle sabbepi cittajarūpadhammā ca tassa paccayassa paccayuppannā.

Therein, all wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate volitions, endowed with the three moments, are a conascence kamma condition. All consciousness and mental factors associated with that volition, kamma-born material phenomena arising together with the rebirth-linking consciousness, and all consciousness-born material phenomena arising during the course of existence, are the conditionally arisen phenomena of that condition.

Atītā [M 0.0485] kusalākusalacetanā nānākkhaṇikakamma paccayo. Bāttiṃsavidhā lokiyavipāka cittacetasikā dhammā ca sabbe kammajarūpadhammā ca tassa paccayassa paccayuppannā.

Past wholesome and unwholesome volitions are the different-moment kamma condition. The thirty-two kinds of mundane resultant consciousness and mental factors, and all kamma-born material phenomena, are the conditionally arisen phenomena of that condition.

Kenaṭṭhena kammanti. Kiriyāvisesaṭṭhena kammaṃ. Cetanā hi kiriyā viseso hoti sabbakammesu jeṭṭhakattā. Tā hi sabbesu kāyavacīmanokammesu paccupaṭṭhitesu tassa tassa kammassa nipphattatthāya sampayuttadhamme ceteti kappeti saṃvidahati, ekato uṭṭhāpeti, tasmā sabbakammesu jeṭṭhakā hoti. Iti kiriyāvisesaṭṭhena kammaṃ nāma. Karonti etenāti vā kammaṃ. Kiṃ karonti. Kāyikakiriyaṃpi karonti, vācasikakiriyaṃpi karonti, mānasikakiriyaṃpi karonti. Tattha kāyikakiriyā nāma gamanaṭhāna nisajjādayo abhikkamanapaṭikkamanādayo antamaso akkhidalānaṃ ukkhipananikkhipanānipi. Vācasikakiriyā nāma vācāpavattanakiriyā. Mānasikakiriyā nāma sucintita ducintitakiriyā, antamaso pañca viññāṇānaṃ dassanakiccasavanakiccādīnipi. Sabbāpi imā kiriyāyo etāya cetanāya sattā karonti, saṃvidahanti, tasmā sā cetanā kammaṃ nāma.

In what sense is it kamma? It is kamma in the sense of a special action. For volition is a special action, being the chief in all kammas. Indeed, when all bodily, verbal, and mental kammas are at hand, for the accomplishment of each respective kamma, volition intends, arranges, and organizes the associated states, making them arise together; therefore, it is the chief in all kammas. Thus, it is called kamma in the sense of a special action. Or, it is kamma because ‘they act by means of it.’ What do they do? They perform bodily action, verbal action, and mental action. Herein, bodily action is going, standing, sitting, etc.; advancing, retreating, etc.; and even the lifting and lowering of the eyelids. Verbal action is the action of uttering speech. Mental action is the action of thinking well or thinking ill, and even the functions of the five consciousnesses, such as the function of seeing, the function of hearing, etc. Beings perform and organize all these actions by means of this volition; therefore, that volition is called kamma.

Attano paccayuppannena saha jāyatīti sahajātaṃ. Sahajātañca taṃ kammañcāti sahajātakammaṃ. Sahajātakammaṃ hutvā paccayo sahajātakamma paccayo. Sahajātakammabhāvena paccayoti vuttaṃ hoti.

Because it arises together with its own conditionally arisen phenomena, it is called 'conascent.' That which is conascent and is kamma is 'conascence kamma.' Being conascence kamma, it is a condition, hence 'conascence kamma condition.' This means it is a condition by way of being conascence kamma.

Añño kammassa uppattikkhaṇo añño vipākassa uppattikkhaṇoti evaṃ visuṃ visuṃ uppattikkhaṇo etassāti nānākkhaṇikaṃ. Nānākkhaṇikañca taṃ kammañcāti nānākkhaṇikakammaṃ. Nānākkhaṇikakammaṃ hutvā paccayo nānākkhaṇikakammapaccayo. Nānākkhaṇikakammapaccayabhāvena paccayoti vuttaṃ hoti. Ariyamaggasampayuttā cetanā attano niruddhānantare eva ariyaphalavipākaṃ janeti, sāpi nānākkhaṇikā eva hoti.

'The moment of arising of the kamma is one, the moment of arising of the result is another'—since it has such distinct moments of arising, it is called 'different-moment.' That which is different-moment and is kamma is 'different-moment kamma.' Being different-moment kamma, it is a condition, hence 'different-moment kamma condition.' This means it is a condition by way of being a different-moment kamma. The volition associated with the noble path generates the noble fruition immediately after its own cessation; it, too, is of a different moment.

Ettha ca ekā dānakusalacetanā attanā sahajātānaṃ cittacetasikānañca kāyikavācasikakiriyābhūtānaṃ cittajarūpānañca sahajātakammapaccayo. Tāya cetanāya āyatiṃ kālantare uppajjamānassa vipākakkhandhassa ca kammajarūpakkhandhassa ca nānākkhaṇika kammapaccayo. Evaṃ ekā kammapathapattā sucaritaduccaritacetanā dvīsu kālesudvinnaṃ paccayuppannānaṃ dvīhi paccayasattīhi paccayo hotīti.

Herein, a single wholesome volition of giving is a conascence kamma condition for the co-arisen consciousness and mental factors and for the consciousness-born material phenomena that constitute bodily and verbal action. That same volition is a different-moment kamma condition for the resultant aggregates and the aggregate of kamma-born matter that arise at a future time. Thus, a single volition of good or bad conduct that has reached a course of action becomes a condition at two times for two sets of conditionally arisen phenomena with two conditioning potencies.

Ettha ca nānākkhaṇikakammapaccaye kammanti kiriyāviseso. So pana cetanāya niruddhāyapi anirujjhitvā taṃ cittasantānaṃ anugacchatiyeva. Yadā [M 0.0486] vipaccituṃ okāsaṃ labhati, tadā so kiriyā viseso cutinantare eko attabhāvo hutvā vipaccati pātubhavati. Okāsaṃ pana alabhamānā bhavasataṃpi bhavasahassaṃpi bhavasatasahassaṃpi taṃ santānaṃ anugacchatiyeva. Mahaggatakammaṃ pana laddhokāse sati dutiyabhave brahmaloke eko brahmattabhāvo hutvā vipaccati pātubhavati. Suparipakkakammattā pana dutiyabhaveyeva khīyati, tato paraṃ nānugacchatīti. Kammapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Herein, in the case of the different-moment kamma condition, 'kamma' is a special action. Even when the volition has ceased, that special action does not cease but continues to follow that continuity of consciousness. When it finds an opportunity to ripen, that special action, immediately after death, becomes a single individual existence and ripens, that is, it manifests. But not finding an opportunity, it follows that continuity for a hundred existences, a thousand existences, or a hundred thousand existences. When lofty kamma, however, finds an opportunity, it ripens and manifests in the second existence as a single Brahma existence in a Brahma world. But because that kamma is fully mature, it is exhausted in that second existence itself and does not follow further. The exposition of the Kamma Condition is finished.

14. Vipākapaccayo

14. Resultant Condition

Chattiṃsavidhā vipākabhūtā sahajātacittacetasikā dhammā vipāka paccayo. Teyeva aññamaññañca paṭisandhikkhaṇe kammajarūpāni ca pavattikkhaṇe vipāka cittajātāni cittajarūpāni ca vipākapaccayuppannā.

The thirty-six kinds of co-arisen resultant consciousness and mental factors are resultant condition. These same states are mutually a resultant condition for each other; and kamma-born material phenomena at the moment of rebirth-linking, and during the course of existence the consciousness-born material phenomena produced by resultant consciousness, are the conditionally arisen phenomena of resultant condition.

Kenaṭṭhena vipākoti. Vipaccanaṭṭhena vipāko. Vipaccanaṃ nāma mudutaruṇabhāvaṃ atikkamma vipakkabhāvaṃ āpajjanaṃ. Kassa pana dhammassa mudutaruṇabhāvo, kassa vipakkabhāvoti. Nānākkhaṇikakammapaccaya saṅkhātassa atītakammassa mudutaruṇabhāvo, tasseva kammassa vipakkabhāvo.

In what sense is it 'resultant'? It is resultant in the sense of ripening. Ripening means to pass beyond the soft and immature state and to attain the state of maturity. Of which phenomenon is there a soft and immature state, and of which a state of maturity? The soft and immature state belongs to the past kamma designated as 'different-moment kamma condition'; the state of maturity belongs to that same kamma.

Tattha ekassa kammassa catasso avatthāyo honti cetanā vatthā kammāvatthā nimittāvatthā vipākāvatthāti.

Therein, a single kamma has four stages: the stage of volition, the stage of kamma, the stage of the sign, and the stage of the result.

Tattha tāya cetanāya niruddhāyapi tassā kiriyā viseso na nirujjhati, taṃ cittasantānaṃ anugacchatiyeva. Ayaṃ kammāvatthā nāma.

Therein, even when that volition has ceased, its special action does not cease; it continues to follow the continuity of consciousness. This is called the stage of kamma.

Nimittāvatthāti taṃ kammaṃ yadā vipaccituṃ okāsaṃ labhati, tadā maraṇāsannakāle tassa puggalassa tameva kammaṃ vā paccupaṭṭhāti, so puggalo tadā dānaṃ dento viya sīlaṃ rakkhanto viya pāṇaghātaṃ vā karonto viya hoti. Kammanimittaṃ vā paccupaṭṭhāti, dāna vatthuādikaṃ vā satthādikaṃ vā aññaṃ vāpi pubbe tassa kammassa upakaraṇabhūtaṃ ārammaṇaṃ tadā tassa puggalassa hatthagataṃ viya hoti. Gatinimittaṃ vā paccupaṭṭhāti, dibbavimānādikaṃ vā nirayaggijālādikaṃ vā uppajjamānabhave upalabhitabbaṃ vā anubhavitabbaṃ vā ārammaṇaṃ tadā dissamānaṃ hoti. Ayaṃ nimittā vatthā nāma.

The stage of the sign: When that kamma finds an opportunity to ripen, at the time near death either that kamma itself appears to the person, so that the person seems to be giving alms, observing the precepts, or killing a living being. Or a sign of the kamma appears: an object such as an article for offering or a weapon, or any other object that had previously been an instrument for that kamma, seems to have come into the person’s hand. Or a sign of the destination appears: an object to be obtained or experienced in the next existence, such as a celestial mansion or the flames of hell, becomes visible. This is called the stage of the sign.

Vipākāvatthāti sace so puggalo tathā paccupaṭṭhitaṃ taṃ kammaṃ vā kammanimittaṃ vā gatinimittaṃ vā ekaṃ ārammaṇaṃ amuñcamāno marati, tadā taṃ kammaṃ tasmiṃbhave vipaccati, taṃ kammaṃ tasmiṃbhave eko attabhāvo hutvā pātubhavati[M 0.0487]. Tattha purimāsu tīsu avatthāsu taṃ kammaṃ mudutaruṇabhūtaṃ hoti, pacchimaṃ pana vipākāvatthaṃ patvā vipakkabhūtaṃ hoti. Tena vuttaṃ vipaccanaṃ nāma mudutaruṇabhāvaṃ atikkamma vipakkabhāvaṃ āpajjananti. Evaṃ vipakkabhāvaṃ āpanno citta cetasikadhammasamūho vipāko nāma.

The stage of the result: If that person dies without letting go of that one object—the kamma, the kamma-sign, or the destination-sign that has thus appeared—then that kamma ripens in that existence; that kamma manifests as a single individual existence. Therein, in the first three stages that kamma is in a soft and immature state, but having reached the final stage of the result, it becomes mature. Therefore it was said: 'Ripening means to pass beyond the soft and immature state and to attain the state of maturity.' A collection of consciousness and mental factors that has attained this state of maturity is called 'resultant'.

Tattha yathā ambapphalāni nāma yadā vipakkabhāvaṃ āpajjanti, tadā sabbaso siniddharūpāni honti. Evamevaṃ vipākadhammā nāma nirussāhā nibyāpārā hutvā sabbaso santarūpā honti. Tesaṃ santarūpattāyeva bhavaṅgacittānaṃ ārammaṇaṃ avibhūtaṃ hoti, bhavaṅgato vuṭṭhānakāle taṃ ārammaṇaṃ na jānāti. Tathāhi rattiyaṃ niddāyantassa purimabhave maraṇāsannakāle yathāgahitaṃ kammādikaṃ ārammaṇaṃ ārabbha bhavaṅgasotaṃ pavattamānaṃpi tassa bhavaṅgasotassa taṃ ārammaṇaṃ ārabbha idaṃ nāma me purimabhave ārammaṇaṃ diṭṭhanti kassaci jānanavīthicittassa uppattiyā paccayo na hoti. So puggalo niddāyanakālepi uṭṭhānakālepi purimabhavasiddhaṃ taṃ nimittaṃ na jānāti. Evaṃ nirussāhanibyāpārasantarūpa bhāvena upakārakatā vipākapaccayatā nāmāti. Vipāka paccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Therein, just as mango fruits, when they become ripe, become completely smooth in nature, so too resultant phenomena, being without exertion and without activity, are entirely tranquil in nature. Because of their tranquil nature, the object of the life-continuum consciousness is indistinct, and at the time of emerging from the life-continuum, one does not know that object. For example, when a person is sleeping at night, although the stream of life-continuum is proceeding with the kamma, kamma-sign, or destination-sign grasped at the time near death in the previous existence as its object, that stream of life-continuum does not become a condition for the arising of any cognitive-process consciousness that would know, 'This object was seen by me in the previous existence.' That person does not know that sign established in the previous existence either while sleeping or when awake. Thus, being a condition by way of this state of being tranquil, without exertion, and without activity is the nature of the resultant condition. The explanation of Resultant Condition is finished.

15. Āhārapaccayo

15. Nutriment Condition

Duvidho āhārapaccayo rūpāhārapaccayo arūpāhāra paccayo.

The nutriment condition is twofold: material nutriment condition and immaterial nutriment condition.

Tattha rūpāhārapaccayo nāma kabaḷīkārāhārasaṅkhātaṃ oja rūpaṃ vuccati. So ca ajjhattāhāro bahiddhāhāroti duvidho. Kabaḷīkārāhārabhakkhānaṃ sattānaṃ sabbepi catusamuṭṭhānikarūpadhammā tassa duvidhassa rūpāhārassa paccayuppannā.

Herein, material nutriment condition refers to the material form that is nutritive essence, designated as physical nutriment. This is twofold: internal nutriment and external nutriment. For beings who consume physical nutriment, all material phenomena originating from the four sources are conditionally arisen from this twofold material nutriment.

Arūpāhāro pana tividho phassāhāro manosañcetanā hāro viññāṇāhāro ca. Tayopete dhammā sahajātānaṃ nāmarūpadhammānaṃ āhārapaccayo honti. Sahajātā ca nāmarūpa dhammā tesaṃ paccayuppannā.

Immaterial nutriment, however, is threefold: nutriment of contact, nutriment of mental volition, and nutriment of consciousness. These three phenomena are a nutriment condition for co-nascent mentality-materiality. And the co-nascent mentality-materiality are their conditionally arisen phenomena.

Kenaṭṭhena āhāroti. Bhusaṃ haraṇaṭṭhena āhāro. Bhusaṃ haraṇaṭṭhenātica daḷhaṃ pavattāpanaṭṭhena, cirakālaṃ ṭhitiyā vuḍḍhiyā viruḷhiyā vepullāya upatthambhanaṭṭhenāti vuttaṃ hoti. Jananakiccayuttopi āhāro upatthambhanakiccappadhāno hotīti.

In what sense is it 'nutriment'? It is nutriment in the sense of strongly conveying. 'Strongly conveying' means firmly establishing and supporting for a long time for the sake of stability, growth, development, and expansion. Although connected with the function of generating, nutriment is principally concerned with the function of supporting.

Tattha duvidho rūpāhāro ajjhattasantāne catusamuṭṭhānikaṃ rūpakāyaṃ upabrūhayanto bhusaṃ harati, daḷhaṃ pavatteti, ciraṃ addhānaṃ gameti, taṃ taṃ āyukappapariyosānaṃ pāpetīti āhāro.

Herein, the twofold material nutriment, by augmenting the material body originating from the four sources within one's own continuity, strongly supports it, firmly establishes it, makes it endure for a long time, and brings it to the end of its respective life-term; thus it is nutriment.

Phassāhāro [M 0.0488] ārammaṇesu iṭṭhāniṭṭharasaṃ nīharanto sampayuttadhamme bhusaṃ harati. Manosañcetanāhāro kāyavacī manokammesu ussāhaṃ janento sampayuttadhamme bhusaṃ harati. Viññāṇāhāro ārammaṇavijānanaṭṭhena pubbaṅgamakiccaṃ vahanto sampayuttadhamme bhusaṃ harati, daḷhaṃ pavatteti, ciraṃ addhānaṃ gametīti āhāro. Sampayuttadhamme bhusaṃ haranto sahajātarūpadhammepi bhusaṃ haratiyeva.

The nutriment of contact, by extracting the desirable and undesirable flavor from objects, strongly supports the associated states. The nutriment of mental volition, by generating effort in bodily, verbal, and mental actions, strongly supports the associated states. The nutriment of consciousness, by performing the function of a forerunner in the sense of cognizing an object, strongly supports the associated states, firmly establishes them, and makes them endure for a long time; thus it is nutriment. While strongly supporting the associated states, it also strongly supports the co-nascent material phenomena.

Idha suttantanayopi vattabbo. Yathā sakuṇā nāma cakkhūhi disāvidisaṃ vibhāvetvā pattehi rukkhato rukkhaṃ vanato vanaṃ ākāsena pakkhanditvā tuṇḍakehi phalāphalāni tuditvā yāvajīvaṃ attānaṃ yāpenti. Tathā ime sattā chahi viññāṇehi ārammaṇāni vibhāvetvā chahi manosañcetanāhārehi ārammaṇavatthuppaṭilābhatthāya ussukkanaṃ katvā chahi phassāhārehi ārammaṇesu rasaṃ pātubhavantaṃ katvā sukhadukkhaṃ anubhavanti. Viññāṇehi vā ārammaṇāni vibhāvetvā nāmarūpa sampattiṃ sādhenti. Phassehi ārammaṇesu rasaṃ pātubhavantaṃ katvā ārammaṇarasānubhavanaṃ vedanaṃ sampādetvā taṇhāvepullaṃ āpajjanti. Cetanāhi taṇhāmūlakāni nānākammāni pasavetvā bhavato bhavaṃ saṃsaranti. Evaṃ āhāradhammānaṃ mahantaṃ āhārakiccaṃ veditabbanti. Āhārapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Herein, the Suttanta method should also be understood. Just as birds, having discerned the directions with their eyes, fly through the sky with their wings from tree to tree and from forest to forest, and pecking at fruits with their beaks, sustain themselves for as long as they live; so too, these beings, discerning objects with the six consciousnesses, striving by means of the six mental volitions as nutriment for the sake of acquiring object-bases, and making the flavor in objects manifest by means of the six contacts as nutriment, experience pleasure and pain. Or, having discerned objects with the consciousnesses, they accomplish the attainment of mentality-materiality. By making the flavor in objects manifest through contact, they produce feeling—the experiencing of the flavor of objects—and attain an abundance of craving. Through volitions, they generate various kammas rooted in craving and wander from existence to existence. Thus, the great function of the nutriment-states should be understood. The exposition of the Nutriment Condition is concluded.

16. Indriyapaccayo

16. Faculty Condition

Tividho indriyapaccayo sahajātindriyapaccayo pure jātindriyapaccayo rūpajīvitindriyapaccayo.

The faculty condition is threefold: the co-nascence faculty condition, the pre-nascence faculty condition, and the material life faculty condition.

Tattha pannarasindriyadhammā sahajātindriyapaccayo nāma, jīvitindriyaṃ manindriyaṃ sukhindriyaṃ dukkhindriyaṃ somanassindriyaṃ domanassindriyaṃ upekkhindriyaṃ saddhindriyaṃ vīriyindriyaṃ satindriyaṃ samādhindriyaṃ paññindriyaṃ anaññātaññassāmītindriyaṃ aññindriyaṃ aññātāvindriyanti. Tehi sahajātā cittacetasikadhammā ca rūpadhammā ca tassa paccayuppannā.

Herein, fifteen faculty-states constitute the co-nascence faculty condition: the life faculty, mind faculty, pleasure faculty, pain faculty, joy faculty, displeasure faculty, equanimity faculty, faith faculty, energy faculty, mindfulness faculty, concentration faculty, wisdom faculty, the faculty of 'I shall come to know the unknown,' the faculty of final knowledge, and the faculty of one who has final knowledge. The co-nascent consciousness, mental factors, and material phenomena are its conditionally arisen phenomena.

Pañcindriyarūpāni purejātindriyapaccayo, cakkhundriyaṃ sotindriyaṃ ghānindriyaṃ jivhindriyaṃ kāyindriyaṃ. Pañcaviññāṇacitta cetasika dhammā tassa paccayuppannā.

The five material sense faculties are the pre-nascence faculty condition: the eye faculty, ear faculty, nose faculty, tongue faculty, and body faculty. The five sense-consciousnesses and their associated mental factors are its condition-resultants.

Ekaṃ rūpajīvitindriyaṃ rūpajīvitindriyapaccayo. Sabbāni kammaja rūpāni jīvitarūpavajjitāni tassa paccayuppannāni.

The one material life faculty is the material life faculty condition. All kamma-born material phenomena, with the exception of the material life faculty itself, are its condition-resultants.

Kenaṭṭhena indriyanti. Issariyaṭṭhena indriyaṃ. Tattha kattha issariyanti. Attano attano paccayuppannesu dhammesu issariyaṃ. Kasmiṃ kasmiṃ kicce issariyanti[M 0.0489]. Attano attano kicce issariyaṃ. Nāmajīvitaṃ sampayuttadhammānaṃ jīvanakicce issariyaṃ. Jīvanakicceti āyuvaḍḍhanakicce, santatiṭhitiyā cirakālaṭhitikicceti attho. Manindriyaṃ ārammaṇaggahaṇakicce sampayuttadhammānaṃ issariyaṃ. Avaseso indriyaṭṭho pubbe indriyayamakadīpaniyaṃ vuttoyeva.

In what sense is it a 'faculty'? In the sense of sovereignty. Wherein is this sovereignty? It is sovereignty over its own condition-resultant states. In which particular function is this sovereignty? It is sovereignty in its own particular function. The mental life faculty has sovereignty over its associated states in the function of vivifying them. The 'function of vivifying' means the function of extending the life-span, the function of maintaining the continuity, and the function of long-term stability. The mind faculty has sovereignty over its associated states in the function of grasping the object. The meaning of the remaining faculties has already been stated in the earlier Exposition of the Pairs on Faculties.

Ettha vadeyya, dve itthindriyapurisindriyadhammā indriyabhūtā samānāpi kasmā indriyapaccaye visuṃ na gahitāti. Paccayakiccassa abhāvato. Tividhañhi paccayakiccaṃ jananakiccañca upatthambhanakiccañca anupālanakiccañca. Tattha yo paccayo paccayuppannadhammassa uppādāya paccayo hoti, yasmiṃ asati paccayuppanno dhammo na uppajjati, tassa paccayakiccaṃ jananakiccaṃ nāma. Yathā anantarapaccayo. Yo paccayo paccayuppannadhammassa ṭhitiyā ca vuḍḍhiyā ca viruḷhiyā ca paccayo hoti, yasmiṃ asati paccayuppanno dhammo na tiṭṭhati na vaḍḍhati na virūhati, tassa paccayakiccaṃ upatthambhanakiccaṃ nāma. Yathā pacchājātapaccayo. Yo paccayo paccayuppannassa dhammassa pavattiyā paccayo hoti, yena vinā paccayuppanno dhammo cirakālaṃ na pavattati, santati gamanaṃ chijjati, tassa paccaya kiccaṃ anupālanakiccaṃ nāma. Yathā rūpa jīvitindriyapaccayo. Ete pana dve indriyadhammā tesu tīsu paccaya kiccesu ekakiccaṃpi nasādhenti, tasmā ete dve dhammā indriya paccaye visuṃ na gahitāti.

Herein, one might say: 'Although the two states, the female faculty and the male faculty, are faculties, why are they not included separately under the faculty condition?' Because they lack the function of a condition. For the function of a condition is threefold: the function of generating, the function of supporting, and the function of maintaining. Herein, that condition which is a condition for the arising of a condition-resultant state—such that in its absence the condition-resultant state does not arise—its function is called the generating function. For example, the contiguity condition. That condition which is a condition for the stability, growth, and development of a condition-resultant state—such that in its absence the condition-resultant state does not persist, grow, or develop—its function is called the supporting function. For example, the post-nascence condition. That condition which is a condition for the continuance of a condition-resultant state—such that without it the condition-resultant state does not endure for long and its stream is interrupted—its function is called the maintaining function. For example, the material life faculty condition. But these two faculty-states do not accomplish even one of these three functions of a condition. Therefore, these two states are not included separately under the faculty condition.

Etaṃ sante ete dve dhammā indriyātipi na vattabbāti. No na vattabbā. Kasmā. Indriyakiccasabbhāvatoti. Kiṃ pana etesaṃ indriyakiccanti. Liṅganimittakuttaākappesu issaratā indriya kiccaṃ. Tathā hi yassa puggalassa paṭisandhikkhaṇe itthindriyarūpaṃ uppajjati, tassa santāne catūhi kammādīhi paccayehi uppannā pañcakkhandha dhammā itthibhāvāya pariṇamanti, so attabhāvo ekantena itthiliṅga itthinimitta itthikutta itthākappayutto hoti, no aññathā. Na ca itthindriyarūpaṃ te pañcakkhandhadhamme janeti, na ca upatthambhati, nāpi anupāleti, atha kho te dhammā attano attano paccayehi uppajjamānā evañcevañca uppajjantūti āṇaṃ ṭhapentaṃ viya tesu attano anubhāvaṃ pavatteti. Te ca dhammā tatheva uppajjanti, no aññathāti. Ayaṃ itthindriyarūpassa itthiliṅgādīsu issaratā. Esa nayo purisindriyarūpassa purisaliṅgādīsu issaratāyaṃ. Evaṃ ete dve dhammā liṅgādīsu indriyakicca sabbhāvato indriyā nāma hontīti.

This being so, should it be said that these two states are not faculties? It should not be said that they are not. Why? Because of the existence of their faculty-function. But what is their faculty-function? Sovereignty over sex, signs, deportment, and bearing is their faculty-function. For, in the case of a person in whom the material female faculty arises at the moment of rebirth-linking, the states of the five aggregates that arise in their continuity due to the four conditions beginning with kamma mature into the female state. That individual existence becomes entirely endowed with the female sex, female signs, female deportment, and female bearing, and not otherwise. The material female faculty does not generate those states of the five aggregates, nor does it support them, nor does it maintain them. Rather, it exerts its influence on those states, which are arising from their own respective conditions, as if laying down a command: 'Let them arise in just this way!' And those states arise in precisely that way, and not otherwise. This is the sovereignty of the material female faculty over the female sex and so on. The same principle applies to the sovereignty of the material male faculty over the male sex and so on. Thus, because of the existence of their faculty-function in regard to sex and so on, these two states are called faculties.

Hadayavatthurūpaṃ pana dvinnaṃ viññāṇadhātūnaṃ nissayavatthukiccaṃ sādhayamānaṃpi tāsu indriyakiccaṃ na sādheti. Na hi bhāvita cittassa puggalassa hadayarūpe pasannevā [M 0.0490] appasanne vā jātepi manoviññāṇadhātuyo tadanuvattikā hontīti. Indriya paccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

The heart-base materiality, however, although it accomplishes the function of a physical basis for the two consciousness-elements, does not accomplish the function of a faculty in regard to them. For, in the case of a person with a developed mind, even when the heart-base materiality becomes clear or unclear, the mind-consciousness-elements do not follow suit. The exposition of the Faculty Condition is concluded.

17. Jhānapaccayo

17. The Jhāna Condition

Satta jhānaṅgāni jhānapaccayo, vitakko vicāro pīti somanassaṃ domanassaṃ upekkhā ekaggatā. Tehi sahajātā pañcaviññāṇavajjitā cittacetasikadhammā ca rūpadhammā ca tassa paccayuppannā.

The seven jhāna factors are the jhāna condition: initial application, sustained application, rapture, happiness, displeasure, equanimity, and one-pointedness. Conascent with them, the consciousness and mental factors—excluding the five sense-consciousnesses—and material phenomena are phenomena conditioned by it.

Kenaṭṭhena jhānanti. Upanijjhāyanaṭṭhena jhānaṃ. Upanijjhāya naṭṭhenāti ca manasā ārammaṇaṃ upagantvā nijjhāyanaṭṭhena pekkhanaṭṭhena. Yathā hi issāso dūre ṭhatvā khuddake lakkhamaṇḍale saraṃ pavesento hatthehi saraṃ ujukañca niccalañca katvā maṇḍalañca vibhūtaṃ katvā cakkhunā nijjhāyanto paveseti. Evameva imehi aṅgehi cittaṃ ujukañca niccalañca katvā ārammaṇañca vibhūtaṃ katvā nijjhāyanto puggalo upanijjhāyatīti vuccati. Evaṃ upanijjhāyitvā yaṃkiñci kāyakammaṃ vā vacīkammaṃ vā manokammaṃ vā karonto avirajjhamāno karoti.

In what sense is it called jhāna? It is called jhāna in the sense of contemplating closely. Contemplating closely means approaching an object with the mind and examining, observing it. Just as an archer, standing at a distance, makes an arrow straight and steady with his hands in order to shoot it into a small target, and having made the target clear, intently gazes at it with his eye before releasing the arrow—so too, a person makes the mind straight and steady with these factors, clarifies the object, and then contemplates it closely. Thus contemplating closely, whatever bodily action, verbal action, or mental action they perform, they do so without wavering.

Tattha kāyakammaṃ nāma abhikkamappaṭikkamādikaṃ vuccati. Vacīkammaṃ nāma akkharavaṇṇaparipuṇṇaṃ vacībhedakaraṇaṃ vuccati. Manokammaṃ nāma yaṃkiñci manasā ārammaṇavibhāvanaṃ vuccati. Dānakammaṃ vā pāṇātipāta kammaṃ vā anurūpehi jhānaṅgehi vinā dubbalena cittena kātuṃ na sakkā hoti. Esa nayo sesesu kusalākusala kammesūti.

Here, bodily action is referred to as movements such as advancing or retreating. Verbal action is referred to as the articulation of syllables and words complete in letters and sounds. Mental action is referred to as any discernment of an object by the mind. An act of giving or an act of killing cannot be done with a weak mind without suitable jhāna factors. This principle applies to the remaining wholesome and unwholesome actions.

Ayañca attho vitakkādīnaṃ jhānaṅgadhammānaṃ visuṃ visuṃ sabhāva lakkhaṇehi dīpetabbo. Sampayuttadhamme ārammaṇābhiniropana lakkhaṇo vitakko, so cittaṃ ārammaṇe daḷhaṃ niyojeti. Ārammaṇānumajjanalakkhaṇo vicāro, so cittaṃ ārammaṇe daḷhaṃ saṃyojeti. Ārammaṇasampiyāyanalakkhaṇāpīti, sā cittaṃ ārammaṇe parituṭṭhaṃ karoti. Tissopi vedanā ārammaṇarasānu bhavanalakkhaṇā, tāpi cittaṃ ārammaṇe iṭṭhāniṭṭhamajjhattarasānubhavana kiccena daḷhappaṭibaddhaṃ karonti. Samādhānalakkhaṇā ekaggatā, sāpi cittaṃ ārammaṇe niccalaṃ katvā ṭhapetīti. Jhānapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

This meaning should be explained by the individual inherent characteristics of the jhāna-factor states, such as initial application and so on. Initial application has the characteristic of focusing associated states on the object; it firmly fixes the mind on the object. Sustained application has the characteristic of repeatedly examining the object; it firmly connects the mind to the object. Rapture has the characteristic of cherishing the object; it makes the mind fully pleased with the object. Those feelings, too, have the characteristic of experiencing the taste of the object; they firmly bind the mind to the object through their function of experiencing its agreeable, disagreeable, or neutral taste. One-pointedness has the characteristic of unification; it establishes the mind by making it unwavering on the object. The exposition of the Jhāna Condition is concluded.

18. Maggapaccayo

18. The Path Condition

Dvādasa maggaṅgāni maggapaccayo sammādiṭṭhi sammāsaṅkappo sammā vācā sammākammanto sammāājīvo sammāvāyāmo sammāsati sammāsamādhi micchādiṭṭhi [M 0.0491] micchāsaṅkappo micchāvāyāmo micchāsamādhi. Sesā micchāvācā micchākammanto micchāājīvoti tayo dhammā visuṃ cetasikadhammā na honti. Musāvādādivasena pavattānaṃ catunnaṃ akusalakhandhānaṃ nāmaṃ. Tasmā te maggapaccaye visuṃ na gahitāti. Sabbe sahetukā citta cetasikadhammā ca sahetukacittasaha jātā rūpadhammā ca tassa paccayuppannā.

The twelve path factors are the path condition: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration; and wrong view, wrong intention, wrong effort, and wrong concentration. The remaining three—wrong speech, wrong action, and wrong livelihood—are not separate mental factors. They are a name for the four unwholesome aggregates that occur by way of false speech and so on. Therefore, they are not taken separately under the path condition. All rooted consciousness and mental factors, and the material phenomena conascent with rooted consciousness, are phenomena conditioned by it.

Kenaṭṭhena maggoti. Sugatiduggatinibbāna disādesasampā panaṭṭhena maggo. Sammādiṭṭhiādikāni hi aṭṭha sammāmaggaṅgāni sugati disādesañca nibbānadisādesañca sampāpanatthāya saṃvattanti. Cattāri micchāmaggaṅgāni duggatidisādesaṃ sampāpanatthāya saṃvattantīti.

In what sense is it a 'path'? It is a 'path' in the sense of leading to the destinations of happy states, woeful states, and Nibbāna. The eight factors of the right path, beginning with right view, conduce to leading to the destination of happy states and the destination of Nibbāna. The four factors of the wrong path conduce to leading to the destination of woeful states.

Tattha jhānapaccayo ārammaṇe cittaṃ ujuṃ karoti, thiraṃ karoti, appanāpattaṃ karoti. Appanāpattaṃ nāma gambhīre udake pakkhitto macchoviya kasiṇanimittādike nimittārammaṇe anupaviṭṭhaṃ cittaṃ pavuccati. Maggapaccayo vaṭṭapathe cetanākammaṃ vivaṭṭapathe bhāvanākammaṃ ujuṃ karoti, thiraṃ karoti, kammapathapattaṃ karoti, vuddhiṃ viruḷhiṃ vepullaṃ karoti, bhūmantarapattaṃ karoti. Aya metesaṃ dvinnaṃ paccayānaṃ viseso.

Herein, the Jhāna Condition makes the mind straight, steady, and brings it to absorption on the object. A mind that has entered into an object such as a kasiṇa sign, like a fish cast into deep water, is said to have attained absorption. The Path Condition makes the volitional action (kamma) in the round of existence, and the action of development (bhāvanā-kamma) in the path of liberation, straight and steady, and brings it to the path of action. It promotes growth, increase, and abundance; it brings it to another plane. This is the distinction between these two conditions.

Tattha kammapathapattaṃ nāma pāṇātipātādīnaṃ kusalākusala kammānaṃ aṅgapāripūriyā paṭisandhijanane samatthabhāvasaṅkhātaṃ kammagatiṃ pattaṃ cetanākammaṃ. Bhūmantarapattaṃ nāma bhāvanānukkamena kāma bhūmito paṭṭhāya yāva lokuttarabhūmiyā ekasmiṃiriyāpathepi uparūparibhūmiṃ pattaṃ bhāvanākammaṃ. Ayañca attho jhānapaccaye vuttanayena sammādiṭṭhiādikānaṃ maggaṅgadhammānaṃ visuṃ visuṃ sabhāva lakkhaṇehi dīpetabboti. Maggapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

'Attaining the path of action' refers to volitional action concerning wholesome and unwholesome deeds such as killing living beings, which has reached the course of action designated as the capacity to generate rebirth-linking through the complete fulfillment of its factors. 'Reaching another plane' refers to developmental action that, through successive development, starting from the sense-sphere plane up to the supramundane plane, attains successively higher planes even in a single posture. This meaning should be explained by revealing the individual intrinsic characteristics of the path factors, such as right view and so on, in the same way as explained under the Jhāna Condition. The exposition of the Path Condition is concluded.

19. Sampayuttapaccayo

19. Association Condition

Sampayuttapaccayo vippayuttapaccayoti ekaṃ dukkaṃ. Atthi paccayo natthi paccayoti ekaṃ dukkaṃ. Vigatapaccayo avigata paccayoti ekaṃ dukkaṃ. Imāni tīṇi paccayadukkāni visuṃ paccaya visesāni na honti. Pubbe āgatesu paccayesu keci paccayā attano paccayuppannehi sampayuttā hutvā paccayattaṃ gacchanti, keci vippayuttā hutvā, keci vijjamānā hutvā, keci avijjamānā hutvā, keci vigatā hutvā, keci avigatā hutvā paccayattaṃ gacchantīti dassanatthaṃ imāni tīṇi paccayadukkāni vuttāni.

Association condition and dissociation condition, one couplet. Presence condition and absence condition, one couplet. Disappearance condition and non-disappearance condition, one couplet. These three couplets of conditions are not separate, special conditions. These three couplets of conditions are stated in order to show that among the conditions previously mentioned, some become conditions by being associated with their conditionally arisen states, some by being dissociated, some by being present, some by being absent, some by having disappeared, and some by not having disappeared.

Ettha [M 0.0492] ca atthīti kho kaccāna ayameko anto, natthīti kho dutiyo antoti evarūpesu ṭhānesu atthinatthisaddā sassatucchedesupavattanti, tasmā evarūpānaṃ atthānaṃ nivattanatthaṃ puna vigatadukkaṃ vuttaṃ.

'Everything exists,' Kaccāna, this is one extreme; 'everything does not exist,' this is the second extreme. In such instances, the words 'exists' and 'does not exist' are applied to eternalism and annihilationism. Therefore, to avert such meanings, the couplet of disappearance was stated again.

Sabbepi sahajātā cittacetasikā dhammā aññamaññassa paccayā ceva honti paccayuppannā ca.

All co-nascent consciousness and mental factors are mutually conditions and are also mutually conditionally arisen.

Kenaṭṭhena sampayutto. Ekuppādatā ekanirodhatā ekavatthukatā ekārammaṇatāti imehi catūhi sampayogaṅgehi samannāgato hutvā saṃyutto ekībhāvaṃ gatoti sampayutto.

In what sense is it 'associated'? Because it is conjoined, having reached a state of unity by being endowed with these four factors of association—co-arising, co-cessation, a common basis, and a common object—it is called 'associated'.

Tattha ekībhāvaṃ gatoti cakkhuviññāṇaṃ phassādīhi sattahi cetasikehi saha ekībhāvaṃ gataṃ hoti, dassananti ekaṃ vohāraṃ gacchati. Aṭṭha dhammā visuṃ visuṃ vohāraṃ na gacchanti, vinibbhujjitvā viññātuṃ na sakkoti. Esa nayo sesesu sabbacittuppādesūti. Sampayuttapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Therein, 'having reached a state of unity' means that eye-consciousness, together with the seven mental factors beginning with contact, has reached a state of unity; it comes to be designated singularly as 'seeing.' The eight states are not designated separately; it is not possible to discern them by separating them. This is the method in all other occurrences of consciousness. The exposition of the Association Condition is finished.

20. Vippayuttapaccayo

20. Dissociation Condition

Catubbidho vippayuttapaccayo, sahajāto vatthupure jāto vatthārammaṇapurejāto pacchājātoti.

The dissociation condition is fourfold: co-nascent, base-prenascent, base-and-object-prenascent, and post-nascent.

Tattha sahajātavippayutto nāma dvisu sahajātapaccaya paccayuppannesu nāmarūpesu nāmaṃ vā rūpassa rūpaṃ vā nāmassa vippayuttaṃ hutvā paccayo. Tattha nāmanti pavattikāle catukkhandhanāmaṃ, rūpassāti cittajarūpassa, rūpanti paṭisandhikkhaṇe hadayavatthurūpaṃ, nāmassāti paṭisandhicatukkhandhanāmassa. Sesā tayopi vippayuttapaccayā pubbe vibhattā evāti. Vippayuttapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

Therein, co-nascent dissociation is when, of the two—mentality and materiality—that are conditionally arisen by co-nascence condition, either mentality is a condition for materiality by being dissociated from it, or materiality is a condition for mentality by being dissociated from it. Here, 'mentality' refers to the mentality of the four aggregates during the course of life; 'for materiality' refers to mind-generated materiality. 'Materiality' refers to the materiality of the heart-basis at the moment of rebirth-linking; 'for mentality' refers to the mentality of the four aggregates at rebirth-linking. The other three dissociation conditions have already been explained above. The exposition of the Dissociation Condition is finished.

21. Atthipaccayo

21. Presence Condition

Sattavidho atthipaccayo, sahajātatthipaccayo vatthupure jātatthi paccayo ārammaṇapurejātatthipaccayo vatthārammaṇapure jātatthipaccayo pacchājātatthipaccayo rūpāhāratthipaccayo rūpa jīvitindriyatthipaccayoti.

The presence condition is sevenfold: co-nascent presence condition, base-prenascent presence condition, object-prenascent presence condition, base-and-object-prenascent presence condition, post-nascent presence condition, material nutriment presence condition, and material life faculty presence condition.

Tattha sahajātapaccayo eva sahajātatthipaccayo nāma. Esa nayo sesesu chasu. Paccayapaccayuppannavibhāgopi heṭṭhā tattha tattha vuttoyeva.

Therein, the co-nascence condition itself is what is called the co-nascent presence condition. This same method applies to the other six. The analysis of condition and conditionally arisen phenomena has also been stated in the relevant sections above.

Kenaṭṭhena [M 0.0493] atthipaccayo. Sayaṃ khaṇikapaccuppannatā saṅkhātena atthibhāvena paccuppannassa dhammassa paccayo atthipaccayo. Atthipaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

In what sense is it presence condition? It is a presence condition because it is a condition for a conditionally arisen state by means of its own state of presence, which is characterized by its being momentarily present. The exposition of the Presence Condition is finished.

24. Avigatapaccayo

24. Non-disappearance Condition

Sabbo anantarapaccayo natthi paccayo nāma. Tathā vigata paccayo. Avigatapaccayopi atthipaccayena sabbasadiso. Atthītica avigatoti ca atthato ekameva. Tathā natthīti ca vigatotica. Natthivigataavigatapaccayadīpanā niṭṭhitā.

The entire contiguity condition is what is called absence condition. Likewise, it is disappearance condition. Non-disappearance condition, too, is entirely similar to presence condition. 'Presence' and 'non-disappearance' are one in meaning; likewise, 'absence' and 'disappearance.' The exposition of the Absence, Disappearance, and Non-disappearance Conditions is finished.

Paccayasabhāgo

Grouping of Conditions

Paccayasabhāgo vuccate. Pañcadasa sahajātajātikā honti, cattāro mahāsahajātā cattāro majjhimasahajātā satta khuddakasahajātā. Tattha cattāro mahāsahajātā nāma sahajāto sahajātanissayo sahajātatthi sahajāta avigato. Cattāro majjhimasahajātā nāma aññamañño vipāko sampayutto sahajātavippayutto. Satta khuddakasaha jātā nāma hetu sahajātādhipati sahajātakammaṃ saha jātāhāro sahajātindriyaṃ jhānaṃ maggo.

The grouping of conditions will be told. There are fifteen conditions of the co-nascent class: four great co-nascent conditions, four medium co-nascent conditions, and seven minor co-nascent conditions. Therein, the four great co-nascent conditions are: co-nascence, co-nascent support, co-nascent presence, and co-nascent non-disappearance. The four medium co-nascent conditions are: mutuality, result, association, and co-nascent dissociation. The seven minor co-nascent conditions are: root, co-nascent predominance, co-nascent kamma, co-nascent nutriment, co-nascent faculty, jhāna, and path.

Tayo rūpāhārā, rūpāhāro rūpāhāratthi rūpāhārā vigato.

Three are related to material nutriment: material nutriment condition, material nutriment presence condition, and material nutriment non-disappearance condition.

Tīṇi rūpajīvitindriyāni, rūpajīvitindriyaṃ rūpajīvitindriyatthi rūpajīvitindriyāvigataṃ.

Three are related to the material life faculty: material life faculty condition, material life faculty presence condition, and material life faculty non-disappearance condition.

Sattarasa purejātajātikā honti, cha vatthupurejātā cha ārammaṇapurejātā pañca vatthārammaṇapurejātā. Tattha cha vatthupurejātā nāma vatthupurejāto vatthupurejātanissayo vatthupurejātindriyaṃ vatthupurejātavippayuttaṃ vatthupurejātatthi vatthu purejātaavigato. Cha ārammaṇapurejātā nāma ārammaṇa purejāto kiñciārammaṇaṃ koci ārammaṇādhipati koci ārammaṇūpanissaso ārammaṇapurejātatthi [M 0.0494] ārammaṇapurejāta avigato. Kiñci ārammaṇantiādīsu kiñcikocivacanehi paccuppannaṃ nipphannarūpaṃ gayhati. Pañca vatthārammaṇapurejātā nāma vatthārammaṇa purejāto vatthārammaṇapurejātanissayo vatthārammaṇapure jāta vippayutto vatthārammaṇapurejātatthi vatthārammaṇapurejāta avigato.

There are seventeen conditions of the prenascent class: six are base-prenascent, six are object-prenascent, and five are base-and-object-prenascent. Therein, the six base-prenascent conditions are: base-prenascence, base-prenascent support, base-prenascent faculty, base-prenascent dissociation, base-prenascent presence, and base-prenascent non-disappearance. The six object-prenascent conditions are: object-prenascence, object (in part), object-predominance (in part), object-decisive support (in part), object-prenascent presence, and object-prenascent non-disappearance. By the words 'in part' (kiñci/koci) in phrases like 'object (in part),' present, produced materiality is to be understood. The five base-and-object-prenascent conditions are: base-and-object-prenascence, base-and-object-prenascent support, base-and-object-prenascent dissociation, base-and-object-prenascent presence, and base-and-object-prenascent non-disappearance.

Cattāro pacchājātajātikā honti, pacchājāto pacchājātavippayutto pacchājātatthi pacchājātaavigato.

There are four conditions of the postnascent class: postnascence, postnascent dissociation, postnascent presence, and postnascent non-disappearance.

Satta anantarā honti, anantaro samanantaro anantarūpa nissayo āsevanaṃ anantarakammaṃ natthi vigato. Ettha ca anantara kammaṃ nāma ariyamaggacetanā, sā attano anantare ariyaphalaṃ janeti.

There are seven conditions of the contiguity class: contiguity, proximity, contiguity-decisive support, repetition, contiguity-kamma, absence, and disappearance. Herein, contiguity-kamma means the volition of the noble path, which generates the noble fruit immediately after itself.

Pañca visuṃ paccayā honti, avasesaṃ ārammaṇaṃ avaseso ārammaṇādhipati avaseso ārammaṇūpa nissayo sabbo pakatūpanissayo avasesaṃ nānākkhaṇika kammaṃ. Iti vitthārato paṭṭhānapaccayā catupaññāsappabhedā hontīti.

There are five separate conditions: the remainder of object condition, the remainder of object-predominance condition, the remainder of object-decisive support condition, the entire natural decisive support condition, and the remainder of different-moment kamma condition. Thus, in detail, the conditions of the Paṭṭhāna are of fifty-four kinds.

Tattha sabbe sahajātajātikā ca sabbe purejāta jātikā sabbe pacchājātajātikā rūpāhāro rūpajīvitindriyanti ime paccuppannapaccayā nāma. Sabbe anantarajātikā sabbaṃ nānākkhaṇika kammanti ime atītapaccayā nāma. Ārammaṇaṃ pakatūpanissayoti ime tekālikā ca nibbānapaññattīnaṃ vasena kālavimuttā ca honti.

Therein, all conditions of the co-nascent class, all of the prenascent class, all of the postnascent class, material nutriment, and material life faculty—these are called present conditions. All conditions of the contiguity class and all of different-moment kamma—these are called past conditions. Object condition and natural decisive support condition are related to the three times and also, with respect to Nibbāna and concepts, are timeless.

Nibbānañca paññatti cāti ime dve dhammā appaccayā nāma asaṅkhatā nāma. Kasmā. Ajātikattā. Yesañhi jāti nāma atthi, uppādo nāma atthi. Te sappaccayānāma saṅkhatā nāma paṭiccasamuppannā nāma. Ime dve dhammā ajātikattā anuppādattā ajātipaccayattāca appaccayā nāma asaṅkhatā nāmāti.

Nibbāna and concepts—these two phenomena are called not conditionally arisen and unconditioned. Why? Because they are unborn. For those things that have birth, there is arising. They are called conditionally arisen, conditioned, and dependently originated. These two phenomena, because they are unborn, unproduced, and do not have birth as a condition, are called not conditionally arisen and unconditioned.

Sappaccayesu ca dhammesu saṅkhatesu ekopi dhammo nicco dhuvo sassato aviparītadhammo nāma natthi. Atha kho sabbe te khayaṭṭhena aniccā eva honti. Kasmā. Sayañca paccayāyattavutti kattā paccayānañca aniccadhammattā. Nanu nibbānañca paññatti ca paccayā honti. Te ca niccā dhuvāti. Saccaṃ. Kevalena pana nibbānapaccayena vā paññattipaccayena vā uppanno nāma natthi, bahūhi paccayehi eva uppanno, te pana paccayā aniccā eva adhuvāti.

And among conditionally arisen, conditioned phenomena, there is not a single phenomenon that is permanent, stable, eternal, or of an unchanging nature. Rather, they are all impermanent in the sense of passing away. Why? Because their occurrence is dependent on conditions, and because the conditions themselves are of an impermanent nature. But are Nibbāna and concepts not conditions? And are they not permanent and stable? True. But no phenomenon arises from Nibbāna as the sole condition or from a concept as the sole condition; it arises from many conditions, and those other conditions are impermanent and unstable.

Ye [M 0.0495] ca dhammā aniccā honti, te niccakālaṃ satte tividhehi dukkhadaṇḍehi paṭippīḷenti bādhenti, tasmā te dhammā bhayaṭṭhena dukkhā eva honti. Tattha tividhā dukkhadaṇḍā nāma dukkhadukkhatā saṅkhāra dukkhatā vipariṇāmadukkhatā.

And those phenomena that are impermanent constantly oppress and afflict beings with the three rods of suffering. Therefore, those phenomena are suffering in the sense of being fearful. Therein, the three rods of suffering are: the suffering of suffering, the suffering due to formations, and the suffering due to change.

Ye keci aniccā eva honti, ekasmiṃ iriyāpathepi punappunaṃ bhijjanti, te kathaṃ yāvajīvaṃ niccasaññitānaṃ sattapuggalānaṃ attā nāma bhaveyyuṃ, sārā nāma bhaveyyuṃ. Ye ca dukkhā eva honti, te kathaṃ dukkhappaṭikulānaṃ sukhakāmānaṃ sattānaṃ attā nāma bhaveyyuṃ, sārā nāma bhaveyyuṃ. Tasmā te dhammā asārakaṭṭhena anattā eva honti.

Whatever phenomena are impermanent, breaking up again and again even within a single posture, how could they be a self or an essence for beings and persons who perceive them as permanent for a lifetime? And whatever phenomena are suffering, how could they be a self or an essence for beings who are averse to suffering and desire happiness? Therefore, those phenomena are not-self in the sense of being without substance.

Api ca yasmā imāya catuvīsatiyā paccayadesanāya imamatthaṃ dasseti. Sabbepi saṅkhatadhammā nāma paccayāyattavuttikā eva honti, sattānaṃ vasāyattavuttikā na honti. Paccayāyatta vuttikesu ca tesu na ekopi dhammo appakena paccayena uppajjati. Atha kho bahūhi eva paccayehi uppajjatīti, tasmā ayaṃ desanā dhammānaṃ anattalakkhaṇadīpane matthakapattā hotīti.

And furthermore, because this teaching of the twenty-four conditions reveals this meaning: all conditioned phenomena are indeed of a nature that is dependent on conditions, not of a nature dependent on the will of beings. And among those that are of a nature dependent on conditions, not even one phenomenon arises from just a few conditions. Rather, it arises from many conditions. Therefore, this teaching reaches the highest point in illuminating the characteristic of not-self in phenomena.

Paccayasabhāgasaṅgaho niṭṭhito.

The Compendium of Shared Conditions is concluded.

Paccayaghaṭanānayo

The Method of Combining Conditions

Pañcaviññāṇesu paccayaghaṭanānayo

The Method of Combining Conditions in the Five Consciousnesses

Paccayaghaṭanānayo vuccate. Ekekasmiṃ paccayuppanne bahunnaṃ paccayānaṃ samodhānaṃ paccayaghaṭanā nāma. Yena pana dhammā sappaccayā saṅkhatā paṭiccasamuppannāti vuccanti, sabbe te dhammā uppāde ca ṭhitiyañca imehi catuvīsatiyā paccayehi sahitattā sappaccayā nāma, sappaccayattā saṅkhatā nāma, saṅkhatattā paṭiccasamuppannā nāma. Katame pana te dhammāti. Ekavīsasatacittāni ca dvipaññāsacetasikāni ca aṭṭhavīsati rūpāni ca.

The method of combining conditions is now explained. The convergence of many conditions in each single conditionally arisen state is called 'the combining of conditions.' Furthermore, as to those phenomena that are called 'supported by conditions,' 'conditioned,' and 'dependently arisen': all those phenomena, at their arising and during their presence, are called 'supported by conditions' because they are conjoined with these twenty-four conditions; because they are supported by conditions, they are called 'conditioned'; because they are conditioned, they are called 'dependently arisen.' And what are those phenomena? The one hundred and twenty-one consciousnesses, the fifty-two mental factors, and the twenty-eight kinds of material form.

Tattha ekavīsasatacittāni dhātuvasena sattavidhāni bhavanti, cakkhu viññāṇadhātu sotaviññāṇadhātu ghānaviññāṇadhātu jivhā viññāṇadhātu kāyaviññāṇadhātu manodhātu manoviññāṇa dhātūti. Tattha cakkhuviññāṇadvayaṃ cakkhuviññāṇadhātu nāma. Sotaviññāṇadvayaṃ sotaviññāṇadhātu nāma. Ghānaviññāṇadvayaṃ ghānaviññāṇadhātu nāma. Jivhāviññāṇadvayaṃ jivhāviññāṇadhātu nāma. Kāyaviññāṇadvayaṃ kāyaviññāṇadhātu [M 0.0496] nāma. Pañcadvārāvajjana cittañca sampaṭicchanacittadvayañca manodhātu nāma. Sesāni aṭṭhasataṃ cittāni manoviññāṇadhātu nāma.

Among them, the one hundred and twenty-one consciousnesses are sevenfold by way of elements: eye-consciousness element, ear-consciousness element, nose-consciousness element, tongue-consciousness element, body-consciousness element, mind-element, and mind-consciousness element. Herein, the pair of eye-consciousnesses is called the eye-consciousness element. The pair of ear-consciousnesses is called the ear-consciousness element. The pair of nose-consciousnesses is called the nose-consciousness element. The pair of tongue-consciousnesses is called the tongue-consciousness element. The pair of body-consciousnesses is called the body-consciousness element. The five-door-adverting consciousness and the pair of receiving-consciousnesses are called the mind-element. The remaining one hundred and eight consciousnesses are called the mind-consciousness element.

Dvipaññāsacetasikāni ca rāsivasena catubbidhāni bhavanti, satta sabbacittikāni ca cha pakiṇṇakāni ca cuddasa pāpānica pañcavīsati kalyāṇāni ca.

The fifty-two mental factors are fourfold by way of their groups: seven universal mental factors, six particular mental factors, fourteen unwholesome mental factors, and twenty-five beautiful mental factors.

Catuvīsatipaccayesu ca pannarasapaccayā sabbacittuppāda sādhāraṇā honti, ārammaṇañca anantarañca samanantarañca saha jāto ca aññamaññañca nissayo ca upanissayo ca kammañca āhāro ca indriyañca sampayutto ca atthi ca natthi ca vigato ca avigato ca. Na hi kiñci cittaṃ vā cetasikaṃ vā ārammaṇena vinā uppannaṃ nāma atthi. Tathā anantarādīhi ca. Aṭṭhapaccayā kesañci cittuppādānaṃ paccayā sādhāraṇā honti, hetu ca adhipati ca pure jāto ca āsevanañca vipāko ca jhānañca maggo ca vippayutto ca. Tattha hetu sahetukacittuppādānaṃ eva sādhāraṇā, adhipati ca sādhipatijavanānaṃ eva, purejāto ca kesañci cittuppādānaṃ eva, āsevanañca kusalākusalakiriyajavanānaṃ eva, vipāko ca vipākacittuppādānaṃ eva, jhānañca manodhātu manoviññāṇadhātu cittuppādānaṃ eva, maggo ca sahetukacittuppādānaṃ eva, vippayutto ca arūpaloke cittuppādānaṃ natthi, eko pacchājāto rūpadhammānaṃ eva visuṃbhūto hoti.

Among the twenty-four conditions, fifteen are common to all arisings of consciousness: object, contiguity, proximity, conascence, mutuality, support, decisive support, kamma, nutriment, faculty, association, presence, absence, disappearance, and non-disappearance. For there is no consciousness or mental factor whatsoever that arises without an object condition; the same applies to contiguity and the others. Eight conditions are common to some arisings of consciousness: root, predominance, prenascence, repetition, result, jhāna, path, and dissociation. Among these: the root condition is common only to arisings of consciousness accompanied by roots; the predominance condition only to javanas with predominance; the prenascence condition only to some arisings of consciousness; the repetition condition only to wholesome, unwholesome, and functional javanas; the result condition only to arisings of resultant consciousness; the jhāna condition only to arisings of consciousness of the mind-element and the mind-consciousness-element; the path condition only to arisings of consciousness accompanied by roots; the dissociation condition does not occur for arisings of consciousness in the formless realm. The one postnascence condition is distinct, pertaining only to material phenomena.

Tatrāyaṃ dīpanā. Satta sabbacittikāni cetasikāni nāma, phasso vedanā saññā cetanā ekaggatā jīvitaṃ manasikāro. Tattha cittaṃ adhipatijātikañca āhārapaccayo ca indriyapaccayo ca. Phasso āhārapaccayo. Vedanā indriyapaccayo ca jhāna paccayo ca. Cetanā kammapaccayo ca āhārapaccayo ca. Ekaggatā indriyapaccayo ca jhāna paccayo ca maggapaccayo ca. Jīvitaṃ indriyapaccayo. Sesā dve dhammā visesapaccayā na honti.

This is the explanation. The seven universal mental factors are: contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty, and attention. Among these: consciousness can be a predominance condition, and it is a nutriment condition and a faculty condition. Contact is a nutriment condition. Feeling is a faculty condition and a jhāna condition. Volition is a kamma condition and a nutriment condition. One-pointedness is a faculty condition, a jhāna condition, and a path condition. Life faculty is a faculty condition. The remaining two factors, perception and attention, are not specific conditions.

Cakkhuviññāṇe satta sabbacittikāni cetasikāni labbhanti, viññāṇena saddhiṃ aṭṭha nāmadhammā honti. Sabbe te dhammā sattahi paccayehi aññamaññassa paccayā honti catūhi mahāsahajātehi ca vippayuttavajjitehi tīhi majjhimasahajātehi ca. Tesveva aṭṭhasu dhammesu viññāṇaṃ sesānaṃ sattannaṃ dhammānaṃ āhārapaccayena ca indriyapaccayena ca paccayo hoti. Phasso āhārapaccayena, vedanā indriyapaccayamattena, cetanā kammapaccayena ca āhāra paccayena ca, ekaggatā indriyapaccayamattena[M 0.0497], jīvitaṃ sesānaṃ sattannaṃ dhammānaṃ indriyapaccayena paccayo hoti. Cakkhuvatthurūpaṃ pana tesaṃ aṭṭhannaṃ dhammānaṃ chahi vatthupurejātehi tasmiṃ cakkhuvatthumhi āpātamāgatāni paccuppannāni rūpārammaṇāni tesaṃ catūhi ārammaṇa purejātehi, anantaraniruddhaṃ pañcadvārāvajjanacittañca pañcahi anantarehi, pubbe kataṃ kusalakammaṃ vā akusalakammaṃ vā kusalavipākānaṃ vā akusalavipākānaṃ vā tesaṃ nānākkhaṇikakammapaccayena, kamma sahāyabhūtāni purimabhave avijjātaṇhūpādānāni ca imasmiṃ bhave āvāsapuggalautubhojanādayo ca tesaṃ aṭṭhannaṃ dhammānaṃ pakatūpanissayapaccayena paccayā honti. Imasmiṃ citte hetu ca adhipati ca pacchājāto ca āsevanañca jhānañca maggocāti chapaccayā na labbhanti, aṭṭhārasapaccayā labbhanti. Yathā ca imasmiṃ citte, tathā sotaviññāṇādīsupi cha paccayā na labbhanti, aṭṭhārasapaccayā labbhantīti.

In eye-consciousness, the seven universal mental factors are found; together with consciousness, they are eight mental phenomena. All these phenomena are conditions for one another by way of seven conditions: the four great conascent conditions and the three middle conascent conditions, excluding dissociation. Among these same eight phenomena, consciousness is a condition for the other seven phenomena by nutriment condition and faculty condition. Contact is a condition by nutriment condition; feeling, by faculty condition only; volition, by kamma condition and nutriment condition; one-pointedness, by faculty condition only; life faculty is a condition for the other seven phenomena by faculty condition. The eye-base form, however, is a condition for these eight mental phenomena by way of the six base-prenascence conditions. The present visible object that has come into the avenue of the eye-base is a condition for them by way of the four object-prenascence conditions. The immediately ceased five-door-adverting consciousness is a condition by way of the five proximity conditions. Previously performed wholesome or unwholesome kamma is a condition for these wholesome or unwholesome resultants by dissimilar-moment kamma condition. The accessories of kamma in a previous existence—ignorance, craving, and clinging—and in this existence, such things as one's dwelling, person, climate, and food, are conditions for these eight mental phenomena by natural decisive support condition. In this consciousness, six conditions are not found—root, predominance, postnascence, repetition, jhāna, and path—but eighteen conditions are found. Just as in this consciousness, so too in ear-consciousness and the others, six conditions are not found, but eighteen conditions are found.

Pañcaviññāṇesu paccayaghaṭanānayo niṭṭhito.

The Method of Combining Conditions in the Five Consciousnesses is concluded.

Ahetuka cittuppādesu paccayaghaṭanānayo

The Method of Combining Conditions in Rootless Consciousnesses

Cha pakiṇṇakāni cetasikāni nāma, vitakko vicāro adhimokkho vīriyaṃ pīti chando. Tattha vitakko jhānapaccayo ca maggapaccayo ca. Vicāro jhānapaccayo. Vīriyaṃ adhipatijātikañca indriyapaccayo ca maggapaccayo ca. Pīti jhānapaccayo. Chando adhipatijātiko. Adhimokkho pana visesapaccayo na hoti.

The six particular mental factors are these: initial application, sustained application, decision, energy, zest, and will. Of these, initial application is a condition for both jhāna and the path. Sustained application is a jhāna condition. Energy is a condition by way of dominance, a faculty condition, and a path condition. Zest is a jhāna condition. Will is by way of dominance. Decision, however, is not a special condition.

Pañcadvārāvajjanacittañca sampaṭicchanacittadvayañca upekkhāsantīraṇa dvayañcāti pañcasu cittesu dasa cetasikāni labbhanti, satta sabba cittikāni ca pakiṇṇakesu vitakko ca vicāro ca adhimokkho ca. Viññāṇena saddhiṃ paccekaṃ ekādasa nāmadhammā honti. Imesu cittesu jhānakiccaṃ labbhati. Vedanā ca ekaggatā ca vitakko ca vicāro ca jhānapaccayaṃ sādhenti. Pañcadvārāvajjanacittaṃ pana kiriyacittaṃ hoti, vipākapaccayo natthi. Nānākkhaṇikakammañca upanissayaṭṭhāne tiṭṭhati. Vipākapaccayena saddhiṃ cha paccayā na labbhanti. Jhānapaccayena saddhiṃ aṭṭhārasapaccayā labbhanti. Sesesu catūsu vipākacittesu pañca paccayā na labbhanti. Vipākapaccayena ca jhānapaccayena ca saddhiṃ ekūnavīsati paccayā labbhanti.

In the five-door-adverting consciousness, the two receiving consciousnesses, and the two investigating consciousnesses accompanied by equanimity—in these five consciousnesses, ten mental factors are found: the seven universals and, from among the particulars, initial application, sustained application, and decision. Together with consciousness, there are eleven mental phenomena in each. In these consciousnesses, the function of jhāna is obtained. Feeling, one-pointedness, initial application, and sustained application accomplish the jhāna condition. The five-door-adverting consciousness, however, is a functional consciousness; there is no resultant condition. And kamma of a different moment stands in the position of a decisive support. Six conditions, including the resultant condition, are not obtained. Eighteen conditions, including the jhāna condition, are obtained. In the remaining four resultant consciousnesses, five conditions are not obtained. Nineteen conditions, including the resultant condition and the jhāna condition, are obtained.

Somanassasantīraṇe pītiyā saddhiṃ ekādasacetasikā yujjanti, manodvārāvajjanacitte ca vīriyena saddhiṃ ekādasāti viññāṇena saddhiṃ dvādasa [M 0.0498] nāmadhammā honti. Hasituppādacitte pana pītiyā ca vīriyena ca saddhiṃ dvādasa cetasikāni yujjanti. Viññāṇena saddhiṃ terasa nāmadhammā honti. Tattha somanassasantīraṇe jhānaṅgesu pītimattaṃ adhikaṃ hoti, pubbe upekkhāsantīraṇadvaye viya pañcapaccayā na labbhanti. Ekūnavīsatipaccayā labbhanti. Manodvārā vajjanacitte ca vīriyamattaṃ adhikaṃ hoti, tañca indriyakicca jhāna kiccānisādheti. Adhipatikiccañca maggakiccañca na sādheti. Kiriya cittattāvipākapaccayo ca natthi. Pubbe pañcadvārāvajjanacitte viya vipāka paccayena saddhiṃ cha paccayā na labbhanti. Jhānapaccayena saddhiṃ aṭṭharasa paccayā labbhanti. Hasituppādacittepi kiriyacittattā vipākapaccayo natthi, javanacittattā pana āsevanaṃ atthi, vipākapaccayena saddhiṃ pañca paccayā na labbhanti. Āsevanapaccayena saddhiṃ ekūnavīsati paccayā labbhanti.

In the investigating consciousness accompanied by joy, eleven mental factors are associated, including zest; in the mind-door-adverting consciousness, eleven are associated, including energy. Thus, together with consciousness, there are twelve mental phenomena. In the smile-producing consciousness, however, twelve mental factors are associated, including zest and energy. Together with consciousness, there are thirteen mental phenomena. Therein, in the investigating consciousness accompanied by joy, among the jhāna factors, only zest is additional. As in the previous two investigating consciousnesses accompanied by equanimity, five conditions are not obtained. Nineteen conditions are obtained. In the mind-door-adverting consciousness, only energy is additional, and it accomplishes the function of a faculty and the function of jhāna. It does not accomplish the function of dominance or the function of the path. Since it is a functional consciousness, there is no resultant condition. As in the previous five-door-adverting consciousness, six conditions, including the resultant condition, are not obtained. Eighteen conditions, including the jhāna condition, are obtained. In the smile-producing consciousness too, since it is a functional consciousness, there is no resultant condition. However, since it is an impulsion consciousness, the repetition condition is present. Five conditions, including the resultant condition, are not obtained. Nineteen conditions, including the repetition condition, are obtained.

Ahetukacittuppādesu paccayaghaṭanānayo niṭṭhito.

The Method of Combining Conditions in Rootless Consciousnesses is concluded.

Akusalacittuppādesu paccayaghaṭanānayo

The Method of Combining Conditions in Unwholesome Consciousnesses

Dvādasa akusalacittāni, dve mohamūlikāni aṭṭha lobha mūlikāni dve dosamūlikāni. Cuddasa pāpacetasikāni nāma moho ahirikaṃ anottappaṃ uddhaccanti idaṃ mohacatukkaṃ nāma. Lobho diṭṭhi mānoti idaṃ lobhatikkaṃ nāma. Doso issā macchariyaṃ kukkuccanti idaṃ dosacatukkaṃ nāma. Thinaṃ middhaṃ vicikicchāti idaṃ visuṃ tikkaṃ nāma.

There are twelve unwholesome consciousnesses: two rooted in delusion, eight rooted in greed, and two rooted in hatred. The fourteen unwholesome mental factors are: delusion, shamelessness, fearlessness of wrongdoing, and restlessness—this is called the delusion quartet. Greed, wrong view, and conceit—this is called the greed trio. Hatred, envy, avarice, and remorse—this is called the hatred quartet. Sloth, torpor, and doubt—this is called the separate trio.

Tattha lobho doso mohoti tayo mūladhammā hetupaccayā, diṭṭhi maggapaccayo, sesā dasadhammā visesapaccayā na honti.

Therein, the three root states—greed, hatred, and delusion—are the root condition. Wrong view is the path condition. The remaining ten states are not special conditions.

Tattha dve mohamūlikāni nāma vicikicchāsampayuttacittaṃ uddhacca sampayuttacittaṃ. Tattha vicikicchāsampayuttacitte pannarasa cetasikāni uppajjanti satta sabbacittikāni ca vitakko vicāro vīriyanti tīṇi pakiṇṇakāni ca pāpacetasikesu mohacatukkañca vicikicchā cāti, cittena saddhiṃ soḷasa nāmadhammā honti. Imasmiṃ citte hetu paccayopi maggapaccayopi labbhanti. Tattha moho hetupaccayo, vitakko ca vīriyañca maggapaccayo, ekaggatā pana vicikicchāya duṭṭhattā imasmiṃ citte indriyakiccañca maggakiccañca na sādheti, jhānakiccamattaṃ sādheti. Adhipati ca pacchājāto vipāko cāti tayo paccayāna labbhanti, sesā ekavīsatipaccayā labbhanti. Uddhaccasampayuttacittepi vicikicchaṃ pahāya adhimokkhena saddhiṃ pannaraseva cetasikāni, soḷaseva nāmadhammā honti[M 0.0499]. Imasmiṃ citte ekaggatā indriya kiccañca jhānakiccañca maggakiccañca sādheti, tayo paccayā na labbhanti, ekavīsati paccayā labbhanti.

The two consciousnesses rooted in delusion are the consciousness associated with doubt and the consciousness associated with restlessness. In the consciousness associated with doubt, fifteen mental factors arise: the seven universals; three particulars, namely, initial application, sustained application, and energy; the delusion quartet; and doubt. Together with consciousness, there are sixteen mental phenomena. In this consciousness, both the root condition and the path condition are obtained. Here, delusion is the root condition; initial application and energy are the path condition. However, one-pointedness, being corrupted by doubt, does not accomplish the function of a faculty or the function of the path in this consciousness; it accomplishes only the function of jhāna. Three conditions are not obtained: predominance, postnascence, and resultant. The remaining twenty-one conditions are obtained. In the consciousness associated with restlessness, having abandoned doubt and included decision, there are likewise fifteen mental factors, for a total of sixteen mental phenomena. In this consciousness, one-pointedness accomplishes the functions of a faculty, jhāna, and the path. Three conditions are not obtained; twenty-one conditions are obtained.

Aṭṭhasu lobhamūlikacittesu pana satta sabbacittikāni ca cha pakiṇṇakāni ca pāpesu mohacatukkañca lobhatikkañca thinamiddhañcāti dvāvīsati cetasikāni uppajjanti. Tesu lobho ca mohocāti dve mūlāni hetupaccayo. Chando ca cittañca vīriyañcāti tayo adhipatijātikā kadāci adhipatikiccaṃ sādhenti, ārammaṇādhipatipi ettha labbhati. Cetanā kammapaccayo. Tayo āhārā āhārapaccayo. Cittañca vedanā ca ekaggatā ca jīvitañca vīriyañcāti pañca indriyadhammā indriyapaccayo. Vitakko ca vicāro ca pīti ca vedanā ca ekaggatācāti pañca jhānaṅgāni jhānapaccayo. Vitakko ca ekaggatā ca diṭṭhi ca vīriyañcāti cattāri maggaṅgāni maggapaccayo. Pacchājāto ca vipāko cāti dve paccayā na labbhanti. Sesā dvāvīsati paccayā labbhanti.

In the eight consciousnesses rooted in greed, however, twenty-two mental factors arise: the seven universals, the six particulars, the delusion quartet, the greed trio, and sloth and torpor. Among these, the two roots, greed and delusion, are the root condition. The three of the dominance-species—will, consciousness, and energy—sometimes accomplish the function of dominance, and here the object-dominance condition is also obtained. Volition is the kamma condition. The three nutriments are the nutriment condition. The five faculty-states—consciousness, feeling, one-pointedness, life, and energy—are the faculty condition. The five jhāna factors—initial application, sustained application, zest, feeling, and one-pointedness—are the jhāna condition. The four path factors—initial application, one-pointedness, view, and energy—are the path condition. The two conditions, postnascence and result, are not obtained. The remaining twenty-two conditions are obtained.

Dvīsu dosamūlikacittesu pītiñca lobhatikkañca pahāya dosacatukkena saddhiṃ dvāvīsati eva cetasikāni, doso ca moho ca dve mūlāni, tayo adhipati jātikā, tayo āhārā, pañca indriyāni, cattāri jhānaṅgāni, tīṇi maggaṅgāni. Etthāpi dve paccayā na labbhanti, dvāvīsati paccayā labbhanti.

In the two consciousnesses rooted in hatred, having abandoned zest and the greed trio, there are likewise twenty-two mental factors together with the hatred quartet. Hatred and delusion are the two roots; three factors are of the dominance-species; three are nutriments; five are faculties; four are jhāna factors; and three are path factors. Here too, two conditions are not obtained; twenty-two conditions are obtained.

Akusalacittuppādesu paccayaghaṭanānayo niṭṭhito.

The method of combining conditions in unwholesome consciousnesses is concluded.

Cittuppādesu paccayaghaṭanānayo

The Method of Combining Conditions in the Arising of Consciousness

Ekanavuti sobhaṇacittāni nāma, catuvīsati kāma sobhaṇacittāni pannarasa rūpacittāni dvādasa arūpacittāni cattālīsa lokuttaracittāni. Tattha catuvīsati kāmasobhaṇa cittāni nāma aṭṭha kāmakusalacittāni aṭṭha kāmasobhaṇavipāka cittāni aṭṭha kāmasobhaṇakiriyacittāni.

There are ninety-one beautiful consciousnesses, namely: twenty-four beautiful sense-sphere consciousnesses, fifteen fine-material-sphere consciousnesses, twelve immaterial-sphere consciousnesses, and forty supramundane consciousnesses. Among them, the twenty-four beautiful sense-sphere consciousnesses are: eight sense-sphere wholesome consciousnesses, eight sense-sphere beautiful resultant consciousnesses, and eight sense-sphere beautiful functional consciousnesses.

Pañca vīsati kalyāṇacetasikāni nāma, alobho adoso amohoceti tīṇi kalyāṇamūlikāni ca saddhā ca sati ca hirī ca ottappañca tatramajjhattatā ca kāyapassaddhi ca citta passaddhi ca kāyalahutā ca cittalahutā ca kāyamudutā ca citta mudutā ca kāyakammaññatā ca citta kammaññatā ca kāyapāguññatā ca cittapāguññatā ca kāyujukatā ca cittujukatā [M 0.0500] ca sammāvācā sammākammanto sammāājīvoti tisso viratiyo ca karuṇā muditāti dve appamaññāyo ca.

There are twenty-five beautiful mental factors, namely: non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion—these three are the beautiful roots; also, faith, mindfulness, moral shame, moral dread, neutrality of mind, tranquility of the mental body, tranquility of consciousness, lightness of the mental body, lightness of consciousness, malleability of the mental body, malleability of consciousness, wieldiness of the mental body, wieldiness of consciousness, proficiency of the mental body, proficiency of consciousness, rectitude of the mental body, and rectitude of consciousness; also, right speech, right action, and right livelihood—these three are the abstinences; also, compassion and sympathetic joy—these two are the illimitables.

Tattha tīṇi kalyāṇamūlāni hetupaccayo, amoho pana adhipatipaccaye vīmaṃsādhipatināma, indriyapaccaye paññindriyaṃ nāma, maggapaccaye sammādiṭṭhi nāma. Saddhā indriyapaccaye saddhindriyaṃ nāma. Sati indriyapaccaye satindriyaṃ nāma, maggapaccaye sammāsati nāma. Tisso viratiyo maggapaccayo, sesā sattarasa dhammā visesapaccayā na honti.

Herein, the three beautiful roots are the root condition. Non-delusion, in the predominance condition, is called predominance by investigation; in the faculty condition, it is called the wisdom faculty; in the path condition, it is called right view. Faith, in the faculty condition, is called the faith faculty. Mindfulness, in the faculty condition, is called the mindfulness faculty; in the path condition, it is called right mindfulness. The three abstinences are the path condition. The remaining seventeen states are not specific conditions.

Aṭṭhasu kāmakusalacittesu aṭṭhatiṃsa cetasikāni saṅgayhanti, satta sabbacittikāni cha pakiṇṇakāni pañcavīsati kalyāṇāni. Tesu ca pīti catūsu somanassikesu eva, amoho catūsu ñāṇasampayuttesu eva, tisso viratiyo sikkhāpadasīlapūraṇa kāle eva, dve appamaññāyo sattesu kāruññamodanākāresu evāti. Imesupi aṭṭhasu cittesu dve vā tīṇi vā kalyāṇamūlāni hetupaccayo, chando ca cittañca vīriyañca vīmaṃsācāti catūsu adhipatijātikesu ekamekova kadāci adhipatipaccayo. Cetanā kammapaccayo. Tayo āhārā āhārapaccayo. Cittañca vedanā ca ekaggatā ca jīvitañca saddhā ca sati ca vīriyañca paññācāti aṭṭha indriyāni indriyapaccayo. Vitakko ca vicāro ca pīti ca vedanā ca ekaggatācāti pañcajhānaṅgāni jhānapaccayo. Paññā ca vitakko ca tisso viratiyo ca sati ca vīriyañca ekaggatācāti aṭṭha maggaṅgāni maggapaccayo. Imesupi aṭṭhasu cittesu pacchājāto ca vipākocāti dve paccayā na labbhanti, sesā dvāvīsatipaccayā labbhanti.

In the eight sense-sphere wholesome consciousnesses, thirty-eight mental factors are comprised: seven universals, six particulars, and twenty-five beautiful factors. Among them, zest is only in the four consciousnesses accompanied by gladness; non-delusion only in the four associated with knowledge; the three abstinences only at the time of fulfilling the precepts of the training; and the two illimitables only in relation to beings, in the modes of compassion and rejoicing. Even in these eight consciousnesses, two or three beautiful roots are the root condition. Among the four that can be dominant—will, consciousness, energy, and investigation—only one at a time is occasionally the predominance condition. Volition is the kamma condition. The three nutriments are the nutriment condition. The eight faculties—consciousness, feeling, one-pointedness, life, faith, mindfulness, energy, and wisdom—are the faculty condition. The five jhāna factors—initial application, sustained application, zest, feeling, and one-pointedness—are the jhāna condition. The eight path factors—wisdom, initial application, the three abstinences, mindfulness, energy, and one-pointedness—are the path condition. In these eight consciousnesses, two conditions—postnascence and result—are not obtained. The remaining twenty-two conditions are obtained.

Aṭṭhasu kāmasobhaṇakiriyacittesu tisso viratiyo na labbhanti, kusalesu viya dve paccayā na labbhanti, dvāvīsati paccayā labbhanti.

In the eight sense-sphere beautiful functional consciousnesses, the three abstinences are not obtained; as in the wholesome states, two conditions are not obtained, while twenty-two conditions are obtained.

Aṭṭhasu kāmasobhaṇavipākesu tisso viratiyo ca dve appamaññāyo ca na labbhanti. Adhipatipaccayo ca pacchājāto ca āsevanañcāti tayo paccayā na labbhanti, ekavīsati paccayā labbhanti.

In the eight sense-sphere beautiful resultants, the three abstinences and the two illimitables are not obtained. Three conditions—predominance, postnascence, and repetition—are not obtained, while twenty-one conditions are obtained.

Upari rūpārūpalokuttaracittesupi dvāvīsatipaccayato atirekaṃ natthi. Tasmā catūsu ñāṇasampayuttakāmakusala cittesu viya imesu paccayaghaṭanā veditabbā.

In the higher consciousnesses of the fine-material sphere, immaterial sphere, and supramundane sphere, there is nothing in excess of the twenty-two conditions. Therefore, the combination of conditions in these should be understood as it is in the four sense-sphere wholesome consciousnesses associated with knowledge.

Evaṃ sante kasmā tāni cittāni kāmacittato mahanta tarāni ca paṇītatarāni ca hontīti. Āsevanamahantattā. Tāni hi cittāni bhāvanākammavisesehi [M 0.0501] siddhāni honti, tasmā tesu āsevanapaccayo mahanto hoti. Āsevana mahantattā ca indriyapaccayopi jhānapaccayopi maggapaccayopi aññepi vā tesaṃ paccayā mahantā honti. Paccayānaṃ uparūpari mahantattā tāni cittāni uparūpari ca kāmacittato mahantatarāni ca paṇītatarāni ca hontīti.

If that is so, why are those consciousnesses greater and more sublime than sense-sphere consciousnesses? Because of the greatness of repetition. For those consciousnesses are accomplished through the special kamma of development; therefore, in them the repetition condition is great. And due to the greatness of repetition, the faculty condition, the jhāna condition, the path condition, and their other conditions also become great. Because the conditions become successively greater, those consciousnesses become successively greater and more sublime than sense-sphere consciousnesses.

Cittuppādesu paccayaghaṭanānayo niṭṭhito.

The Method of Combining Conditions in the Arising of Consciousness is concluded.

Rūpakalāpesu paccayaghaṭanānayo

The Method of Combining Conditions in Material Groups

Rūpakalāpesu paccayaghaṭanānayo vuccate. Aṭṭhavīsati rūpāni nāma, cattāri mahābhūtāni pathavī āpo tejo vāyo. Pañca pasādarūpāni cakkhu sotaṃ ghānaṃ jivhā kāyo. Pañca gocararūpāni rūpaṃ saddo gandho raso phoṭṭhabbaṃ. Tattha phoṭṭhabbaṃ tividhaṃ pathavīphoṭṭhabbaṃ tejophoṭṭhabbaṃ vāyophoṭṭhabbaṃ. Dve bhāvarūpāni itthibhāvarūpaṃ pumbhāvarūpaṃ. Ekaṃ jīvitarūpaṃ, ekaṃ hadayarūpaṃ. Ekaṃ āhārarūpaṃ. Ekaṃ ākāsadhāturūpaṃ. Dve viññattirūpāni kāyaviññattirūpaṃ vacīviññatti rūpaṃ. Tīṇi vikārarūpāni lahutā mudutā kammaññatā. Cattāri lakkhaṇarūpāni upacayo santati jaratā aniccatā.

The method of combining conditions in material groups is now explained. There are twenty-eight kinds of material phenomena: the four great elements—earth, water, fire, and air; the five sensitive materialities—eye, ear, nose, tongue, and body; the five sense objects—visible form, sound, odor, taste, and the tangible. Herein, the tangible is threefold, consisting of the earth element, the fire element, and the air element. There are two sex materialities—femininity and masculinity; one life faculty; one heart-base; one nutriment; one space element; two intimations—bodily intimation and verbal intimation; three alterations of matter—lightness, softness, and wieldiness; and four characteristics of matter—production, continuity, aging, and impermanence.

Tattha cha rūpadhammā rūpadhammānaṃ paccayā honti cattāri mahābhūtāni ca jīvitarūpañca āhārarūpañca. Tattha cattāri mahā bhūtāni aññamaññassa pañcahi paccayehi paccayā honti sahajātena ca aññamaññena ca nissayena ca atthiyā ca avigatenaca. Sahajātānaṃ upādārūpānaṃ aññamaññavajjitehi catūhi paccayehi paccayā honti. Jīvitarūpaṃ sahajātānaṃ kammajarūpānaṃ indriyapaccayena paccayo. Āhārarūpaṃ sahajātānañca asahajātānañca sabbesaṃ ajjhattarūpadhammānaṃ āhārapaccayena paccayo.

Among these, six material phenomena are conditions for other material phenomena: the four great elements, the life faculty, and nutriment. Here, the four great elements are conditions for one another by five conditions: conascence, mutuality, support, presence, and non-disappearance. They are conditions for the co-nascent derived material phenomena by four conditions, excluding the mutuality condition. The life faculty is a condition by way of the faculty condition for co-nascent kamma-born material phenomena. Nutriment is a condition by way of the nutriment condition for all internal material phenomena, both co-nascent and non-co-nascent.

Tattheva terasa rūpadhammā nāmadhammānaṃ visesapaccayā honti, pañcapasādarūpāni ca satta gocararūpāni ca hadayavatthurūpañca. Tattha pañca pasādarūpāni pañcannaṃ viññāṇadhātūnaṃ mātaro viya puttakānaṃ vatthupurejātena ca vatthupurejātindriyena ca vatthu purejātavippayuttena ca paccayā honti. Satta gocararūpāni pañcannaṃ viññāṇadhātūnaṃ tissannaṃ manodhātūnañca pitaro viya puttakānaṃ ārammaṇapurejātena paccayā honti. Hadayavatthurūpaṃ dvinnaṃ manodhātu manoviññāṇadhātūnaṃ rukkho viya rukkhadevatānaṃ yathārahaṃ [M 0.0502] paṭisandhikkhaṇe sahajātanissayena pavattikāle vatthupurejātena ca vatthupurejātavippayuttena ca paccayo hoti.

Therein, thirteen material phenomena are specific conditions for mental phenomena: the five sensitive materialities, the seven sense objects, and the heart-base materiality. Herein, the five sensitive materialities are conditions for the five consciousness-elements—like mothers for their children—by way of base-prenascence, base-prenascence-faculty, and base-prenascence-dissociation conditions. The seven sense objects are conditions for the five consciousness-elements and the three mind-elements—like fathers for their children—by way of the object-prenascence condition. The heart-base materiality is a condition for the mind-element and the mind-consciousness-element—like a tree for its tree-deity—as appropriate: at the moment of rebirth-linking by way of the conascence-support condition, and during the course of life by way of the base-prenascence and base-prenascence-dissociation conditions.

Tevīsati rūpakalāpā. Tattha ekāya rajjuyā bandhitānaṃ kesānaṃ kesakalāpo viya tiṇānaṃ tiṇakalāpo viya ekena jātirūpena bandhitānaṃ rūpadhammānaṃ kalāpo paripiṇḍirūpa kalāpo nāma.

There are twenty-three material groups. Herein, just as a bundle of hair is a group of hairs bound by a single string, or a bundle of grass is a group of grass blades bound by a single string, so a group of material phenomena bound together by a single birth-materiality is called a compact material group.

Tattha cattāri mahābhūtāni vaṇṇo gandho raso ojāti ime aṭṭha dhammā eko sabbamūlakalāpo nāma, sabbamūlaṭṭhakanti ca vuccati.

Therein, the four great elements, color, odor, taste, and nutritive essence—these eight phenomena are called one fundamental group, and are also called the fundamental octad.

Nava kammajarūpakalāpā, – jīvitanavakaṃ vatthudasakaṃ kāya dasakaṃ itthibhāvadasakaṃ pumbhāvadasakaṃ cakkhudasakaṃ sotadasakaṃ ghānadasakaṃ jivhādasakaṃ. Tattha sabbamūlaṭṭhakamevajīvitarūpena saha jīvitanavakaṃ nāma. Etadeva kammajakalāpesu mūlanavakaṃ hoti. Mūlanavakameva yathākkamaṃ hadayavatthurūpādīhi aṭṭharūpehi saha vatthudasakādīni aṭṭhadasakāni bhavati. Tattha jīvitanavakañca kāyadasakañca bhāvadasakānicāti cattāro kalāpā sakala kāye pavattanti. Tattha jīvitanavakanti pācakaggi ca kāyaggi ca vuccati. Pācakaggi nāma pācakatejo koṭṭhāso, so āmāsaye pavattitvā asitapītakhāyitasāyitāni paripāceti. Kāyaggi nāma sakalakāyabyāpako usmātejo koṭṭhāso, so sakala kāye pavattitvā pittasemhalohitāni apūtīni vippasannāni karoti. Tesaṃ dvinnaṃ visamavuttiyā sati sattā bahvābādhā honti, samavuttiyā sati appābādhā. Tadubhayaṃ jīvitanavakaṃ sattānaṃ āyuṃ sampādeti. Vaṇṇaṃ sampādeti. Kāyadasakaṃ sakalakāye sukhasamphassa dukkhasamphassāni sampādeti. Bhāva dasakāni itthīnaṃ sabbe itthākāre sampādeti. Purisānaṃ sabbe purisākāre sampādehi. Sesāni vatthudasakādīni pañcadasakāni padesadasakāni nāma. Tattha vatthudasakaṃ hadayakosabbhantare pavattitvā sattānaṃ nānāpakārāni sucintitaducintitāni sampādeti. Cakkhudasakādīni cattāri dasakāni cakkhuguḷa kaṇṇabila nāsabila jivhātalesu pavattitvā dassana savana ghāyana sāyanāni sampādeti.

Nine kamma-born material groups: the life-nonad, the basis-decad, the body-decad, the femininity-decad, the masculinity-decad, the eye-decad, the ear-decad, the nose-decad, and the tongue-decad. Among these, the life-nonad is the fundamental octad together with the life-faculty materiality. This itself is the basic nonad among the kamma-born groups. The basic nonad, in due order, becomes the eight decads—such as the basis-decad—by combining with the eight material phenomena beginning with the heart-base. Among these, four groups—the life-nonad, the body-decad, and the two sex-decads—occur throughout the entire body. Herein, the life-nonad is called the digestive fire and the bodily fire. The digestive fire is the portion of the heat element that functions in the stomach, digesting what is eaten, drunk, chewed, and tasted. The bodily fire is the portion of the heat element that pervades the entire body, keeping the bile, phlegm, and blood unputrefied and clear. When these two function unevenly, beings are very sickly; when they function evenly, they are not very sickly. Both these aspects of the life-nonad sustain the lifespan of beings and their complexion. The body-decad produces pleasant and painful tangible sensations throughout the body. The sex-decads produce all the feminine characteristics in women and all the masculine characteristics in men. The remaining five decads, the basis-decad and so on, are called localized decads. Among these, the basis-decad functions inside the heart cavity and produces for beings various kinds of well-considered and ill-considered thoughts. The four decads—the eye-decad, ear-decad, nose-decad, and tongue-decad—function in the eyeball, ear-hole, nostril, and on the tongue's surface, producing seeing, hearing, smelling, and tasting.

Aṭṭha cittajarūpakalāpā, sabbamūlaṭṭhakaṃ saddanavakaṃ kāya viññattinavakaṃ saddavacīviññattidasakanti cattāro mūlakalāpā ca teyeva lahutā mudutā kammaññatāsaṅkhātehi tīhi vikāra rūpehi saha cattāro savikārakalāpā ca.

Eight mind-born material groups: the four basic groups are the fundamental octad, the sound-nonad, the bodily-intimation-nonad, and the sound-and-speech-intimation-decad; and these same four together with the three material alterations—lightness, softness, and wieldiness—constitute the four groups with alterations.

Tattha [M 0.0503] sarīradhātūnaṃ visamappavattikāle gilānassa mūla kalāpāni eva pavattanti. Tadā hi tassa sarīrarūpāni garūni vā thaddhāni vā akammaññāni vā honti, yathāruci iriyapathaṃpi pavattetuṃ aṅga paccaṅgānipi cāletuṃ vacanaṃpi kathetuṃ dukkho hoti. Sarīradhātūnaṃ samappavattikāle pana agilānassa garuthaddhādīnaṃ sarīradosānaṃ abhāvato savikārā pavattanti. Tesu ca cittaṅgavasena kāyaṅga calane dve kāyaviññattikalāpā pavattanti. Cittavaseneva mukhato vacanasaddappavattikāle dve vacīviññattikalāpā, cittavaseneva akkharavaṇṇarahitānaṃ hasanarodanādīnaṃ avacanasaddānaṃ mukhato pavattikāle dve saddakalāpā, sesakālesu dve ādi kalāpā pavattanti.

Herein, when the bodily elements are functioning unevenly, only the basic groups occur in a sick person. For then their bodily phenomena become heavy, stiff, or unwieldy, and it is difficult for them to adopt any posture they wish, to move their major and minor limbs, or even to speak. But when the bodily elements are functioning evenly in a healthy person, due to the absence of bodily defects such as heaviness and stiffness, the groups with alterations occur. Among these, when the body moves by the power of the mind, the two bodily intimation groups occur. When speech sounds are produced from the mouth by the power of the mind, the two speech intimation groups occur. When non-verbal sounds such as laughter and crying, which are devoid of letters and phonemes, are produced from the mouth by the power of the mind, the two sound groups occur. At other times, the two initial groups occur.

Cattāro utujarūpakalāpā, sabbamūlaṭṭhakaṃ saddanavakanti dve mūlakalāpā ca dve savikārakalāpā ca. Tattha ayaṃ kāyo yāvajīvaṃ iriyāpathasotaṃ anugacchanto yāpeti, tasmā iriyāpathanānattaṃ paṭicca imasmiṃ kāye khaṇe khaṇe dhātūnaṃ samappavattivisamappavattiyo paññāyanti. Tathā utunānattaṃ paṭicca āhāranānattaṃ paṭicca vātātapasapphassanānattaṃ paṭicca kāyaṅga parihāranānattaṃ paṭicca attūpakkamaparūpakkamanānattaṃ paṭicca. Tattha visamappavattikāle dve mūlakalāpā eva pavattanti, samappavattikāle dve savikārā. Tesu ca dve saddakalāpā cittajasaddato paramparasaddesu ca aññesu loke nānappakārasaddesu ca pavattanti.

Four temperature-born material groups: the two basic groups are the fundamental octad and the sound-nonad; and there are also these two with alterations. Herein, this body is maintained for as long as life lasts by following the stream of postures. Therefore, conditioned by the variety of postures, the even and uneven functioning of the elements in this body is discerned from moment to moment. Likewise, it is conditioned by the variety of temperature, food, contact with wind and sun, care of the bodily limbs, and by one's own and others' undertakings. Herein, at a time of uneven functioning, only the two basic groups occur; at a time of even functioning, the two with alterations occur. Among these, the two sound-groups occur as sounds derived from mind-born sound, as successive sounds, and as other various kinds of sounds in the world.

Dve āhārajarūpakalāpā, – sabbamūlaṭṭhakaṃ savikāranti. Ime dve kalāpā sappāyena vā asappāyena vā āhārena jātānaṃ samarūpavisamarūpānaṃ vasena veditabbā.

Two nutriment-born material groups: the fundamental octad and the fundamental octad with alterations. These two groups should be understood by way of their even or uneven nature, which arises from suitable or unsuitable nutriment, respectively.

Ākāsadhātu ca lakkhaṇarūpānicāti pañcarūpāni kalāpa muttāni honti. Tesu ākāsadhātu kalāpānaṃ antarā pariccheda mattattā kalāpamuttā hoti. Lakkhaṇarūpāni saṅkhatabhūtānaṃ rūpa kalāpānaṃ saṅkhatabhāvajānanatthāya lakkhaṇamattattā kalāpa muttāni.

The space element and the characteristic phenomena are five material phenomena that are free from groups. Among these, the space element is free from groups because it is merely the delimitation between the groups. The characteristic phenomena are free from groups because they are mere characteristics for the purpose of making known the conditioned state of the conditioned material groups.

Ime tevīsati kalāpā ajjhattasantāne labbhanti. Bahiddhā santāne pana dve utujamūlakalāpā eva labbhanti. Tattha dve rūpa santānāni ajjhattasantānañca bahiddhāsantānañca. Tattha ajjhattasantānaṃ nāma sattasantānaṃ vuccati. Bahiddhāsantānaṃ nāma pathavīpabbatanadīsamudda rukkhatiṇādīni vuccati. Tattha ajjhattasantāne aṭṭhavīsati rūpāni tevīsati rūpakalāpāni labbhanti.

These twenty-three groups are found in the internal continuity. In the external continuity, however, only the two basic temperature-born groups are found. Herein, there are two material continuities: the internal continuity and the external continuity. Among them, the internal continuity is called the continuity of a being. The external continuity refers to the earth, mountains, rivers, oceans, trees, grass, and so on. In the internal continuity, twenty-eight material phenomena and twenty-three material groups are found.

Tattha [M 0.0504] paṭisandhināmadhammā paṭisandhikkhaṇe kammajarūpakalāpānaṃ chadhā paccayā honti catūhi mahāsahajātehi ca vipākena ca vippayuttena ca. Hadayavatthurūpassa pana aññamaññena saha sattadhā paccayā honti. Tesveva nāmadhammesu hetudhammā hetubhāvena, cetanā kammabhāvena, āhāradhammā āhārabhāvena, indriya dhammā indriyabhāvena, jhānadhammā jhāna bhāvena, maggadhammā magga bhāvenāti yathārahaṃ chadhā paccayā honti. Atītāni pana kusalākusalakammāni ekadhāva paccayā honti kammapaccayena, paṭhamabhavaṅgādikā pacchājātā pavattanāmadhammā purejātānaṃ kammajarūpa kalāpānaṃ ekadhā paccayā honti pacchājātena. Ettha ca pacchājāta vacanena cattāro pacchājātajātikā paccayā gahitā honti. Atītāni ca kammāni ekadhāva paccayā honti. Evaṃ nāmadhammā kammajarūpakalāpānaṃ yathārahaṃ cuddasahi paccayehi paccayā honti. Idha dasapaccayā na labbhanti ārammaṇañca adhipati ca anantarañca samanantarañca upanissayo ca purejāto ca āsevanañca sampayutto ca natthi ca vigato ca.

Herein, at the moment of rebirth-linking, the mental phenomena of rebirth-linking are conditions in six ways for the kamma-born material groups: by the four great conascent conditions, by resultant condition, and by dissociation condition. For the heart-base material phenomenon, however, they are conditions in seven ways, with the addition of mutuality condition. Among those same mental phenomena, the root-phenomena are conditions by way of root condition, volition by way of kamma condition, nutriment-phenomena by way of nutriment condition, faculty-phenomena by way of faculty condition, jhāna-phenomena by way of jhāna condition, and path-phenomena by way of path condition—thus, as appropriate, they are conditions in six ways. Past wholesome and unwholesome kammas are conditions in one way: by kamma condition. The subsequently arisen mental phenomena of the course of life, beginning with the first life-continuum, are conditions in one way for the pre-arisen kamma-born material groups: by postnascence condition. Here, by the term 'postnascence,' the four conditions of the postnascence group are comprised. And past kammas are conditions in one way. Thus, mental phenomena are conditions for the kamma-born material groups in fourteen ways, as appropriate. Here, ten conditions are not obtained: object, predominance, proximity, contiguity, decisive support, prenascence, repetition, association, absence, and disappearance.

Pavattikāle rūpajanakā nāmadhammā attanā sahajātānaṃ cittajarūpakalāpānaṃ pañcadhā paccayā honti catūhi mahāsaha jātehi ca vippayuttena ca. Tesveva nāmadhammesu hetudhammā hetubhāvena, adhipatidhammā adhipatibhāvena, cetanā kammabhāvena, vipākadhammā vipākabhāvena, āhāradhammā āhāra bhāvena, indriyadhammā indriyabhāvena, jhānadhammā jhānabhāvena, maggadhammā maggabhāvenāti yathārahaṃ aṭṭhadhā paccayā honti. Pacchājātā sabbe nāmadhammā purejātānaṃ cittajarūpakalāpānaṃ ekadhā paccayā honti pacchājātena. Evaṃ nāmadhammā cittajarūpa kalāpānaṃ yathārahaṃ cuddasahi paccayehi paccayā honti. Idhapi dasa paccayā na labbhanti ārammaṇañca anantarañca samanantarañca aññamaññañca upanissayo ca purejāto ca āsevanañca sampayutto ca natthi ca vigato ca.

During the course of life, the mental phenomena that generate matter are conditions in five ways for the mind-born material groups that are conascent with them: by the four great conascent conditions and by dissociation condition. Among those same mental phenomena, the root-phenomena are conditions by way of root condition, predominance-phenomena by way of predominance condition, volition by way of kamma condition, resultant-phenomena by way of resultant condition, nutriment-phenomena by way of nutriment condition, faculty-phenomena by way of faculty condition, jhāna-phenomena by way of jhāna condition, and path-phenomena by way of path condition—thus, as appropriate, they are conditions in eight ways. All subsequently arisen mental phenomena are conditions in one way for the pre-arisen mind-born material groups: by postnascence condition. Thus, mental phenomena are conditions for the mind-born material groups in fourteen ways, as appropriate. Here too, ten conditions are not obtained: object, proximity, contiguity, mutuality, decisive support, prenascence, repetition, association, absence, and disappearance.

Paṭisandhicittassa ṭhitikālato paṭṭhāya pavattikāle sabbepi nāmadhammā sabbesaṃ utujarūpakalāpānañca āhārajarūpa kalāpānañca ekadhā paccayā honti pacchājātavasena. Etthapi pacchājātavacanena cattāro pacchājātajātikā gahitā honti, sesā vīsati paccayā na labbhanti.

Beginning from the static phase of the rebirth-linking consciousness, and throughout the course of life, all mental phenomena are a condition in one way for all temperature-born and nutriment-born material groups: by way of postnascence. Here also, by the term 'postnascence,' the four conditions of the postnascence group are comprised. The remaining twenty conditions are not obtained.

Sabbesu pana tevīsatiyā rūpakalāpesucattāromahā bhūtā aññamaññassa pañcadhā paccayā honti catūhi mahāsaha jātehi ca aññamaññena ca, sahajātānaṃ upādārūpānaṃ catudhā paccayā honti catūhi mahāsahajātehi[M 0.0505]. Āhārarūpaṃ sahajātānañca asahajātānañca sabbesaṃ ajjhattarūpakalāpānaṃ āhāra paccayena paccayo hoti. Navasu kammajarūpakalāpesu jīvitarūpaṃ sahajātānameva rūpānaṃ indriyapaccayena paccayo hoti. Evaṃ ajjhattarūpadhammā ajjhattarūpadhammānaṃ sattadhā paccayā honti. Bahiddhā rūpadhammā pana bahiddhābhūtānaṃ dvinnaṃ utujarūpakalāpānaṃ pañcadhā paccayā hontīti.

In all twenty-three material groups, the four great elements are a condition for one another in five ways: by means of the four great conascent conditions and by mutuality. For the conascent derived material phenomena, they are a condition in four ways by means of the four great conascent conditions. The nutritive essence is a condition for all conascent and non-conascent internal material groups by means of nutriment condition. In the nine kamma-born material groups, the life faculty is a condition, by means of faculty condition, only for conascent material phenomena. Thus, internal material phenomena are a condition for internal material phenomena in seven ways. External material phenomena, however, are a condition in five ways for the two external temperature-born material groups.

Rūpakalāpesu paccayaghaṭanānayo niṭṭhito.

The method of combining conditions in the material groups is concluded.

Ettha ca paṭṭhānasaddassa attho vattabbo. Padhānaṃ ṭhānanti paṭṭhānaṃ. Tattha padhānanti pamukhaṃ, ṭhānanti paccayo, pamukhapaccayo mukhyapaccayo ekantapaccayoti vuttaṃ hoti. So ca ekanta paccayo ekantapaccayuppannaṃ paṭicca vattabbo.

Here the meaning of the word 'paṭṭhāna' should be stated. 'Paṭṭhāna' means a principal condition. Therein, 'padhāna' means principal, and 'ṭhāna' means condition. The principal condition is said to be the chief condition, the decisive condition. And that decisive condition, in relation to a decisively conditioned phenomenon, is to be called 'paṭṭhāna'.

Duvidhañhi paccayuppannaṃ mukhyapaccayuppannaṃ nissandapaccayuppannanti. Tattha mukhyapaccayuppannaṃ nāma mūlapaccayuppannaṃ, nissandapaccayuppannaṃ nāma parampara paccayuppannaṃ. Tattha mūlapaccayuppannameva ekantapaccayuppannaṃ nāma. Tañhi attano paccaye sati ekantena uppajjatiyeva, no nuppajjati. Paramparapaccayuppannaṃ pana anekantapaccayuppannaṃnāma, tañhi tasmiṃ paccaye satipi uppajjati vā, na vā uppajjati. Tattha ekantapaccayuppannaṃ paṭicca so paccayo ekantapaccayo nāma. So eva imasmiṃ mahāpakaraṇe vutto. Tato eva ayaṃ catuvīsatipaccayagaṇo ca imaṃ mahā pakaraṇañca paṭṭhānanti vuccati.

For a conditioned phenomenon is of two kinds: directly conditioned and consequentially conditioned. Herein, the directly conditioned is the root-conditioned, while the consequentially conditioned is the sequentially conditioned. Among these, only the root-conditioned is called the invariably conditioned. For when its condition is present, it invariably arises; it does not fail to arise. The sequentially conditioned, however, is called the variably conditioned, for even when its condition is present, it may or may not arise. Herein, the condition for an invariably conditioned phenomenon is called an invariable condition. This very condition is discussed in this great treatise. For this reason, both the group of twenty-four conditions and this great treatise are called the Paṭṭhāna.

Tattha ekassa purisassa dhanadhaññatthāya lobho uppajjati. So lobhavasena uṭṭhāya araññaṃ gantvā ekasmiṃ padese khettāni karoti, vatthūni karoti, uyyānāni karoti. Tesu sampajjamānesu so puriso bahūni dhanadhaññāni labhitvā attanā ca paribhuñjati, puttadāre ca poseti, puññāni ca karoti, puññaphalāni ca āyatiṃ paccanubhavissati. Tattha lobhasaha jātāni nāmarūpāni mukhyapaccayuppannāni nāma. Tato paraṃ yāva āyatiṃ bhavesu puññaphalāni paccanubhoti, tāva uppannāni paramparaphalāni tassa lobhassa nissandapaccayuppannāni nāma. Tesu dvīsu paccayuppannesu mukhyapaccayuppannameva paṭṭhāne vuttaṃ. Nissandapaccayuppannaṃ pana suttantanayena kathetabbaṃ. Tattha suttantanayo nāma imasmiṃ sati idaṃ hoti, imassa uppādā idaṃ uppajjatīti evarūpo paccayanayo. Api ca lobho doso mohoti tayo dhammā sakalassa sattalokassapi saṅkhāralokassapi okāsa lokassapi vipattiyā mūlaṭṭhena hetū nāma. Alobho adoso amohoti [M 0.0506] tayo dhammā sampattiyā mūlaṭṭhena hetūnāmāti kathetabbaṃ. Esa nayo sabbesu paṭṭhānapaccayesu yathārahaṃ veditabbo. Evañca sati loke sabbā lokappavattiyo imesaṃ catuvīsatiyā paccayānaṃ mukhya paccayuppannena saddhiṃ nissandapaccayuppannā eva hontīti veditabboti.

Here, greed for wealth and grain arises in a person. Driven by greed, he goes to the forest and establishes fields, estates, and gardens in a certain place. As these prosper, he acquires much wealth and grain, enjoys it himself, supports his wife and children, performs meritorious deeds, and will experience the fruits of merit in the future. Herein, the mentality-materiality conascent with greed are called the directly conditioned phenomena. Thereafter, for as long as he experiences the fruits of merit in future existences, the sequential results that arise are called the consequentially conditioned phenomena of that greed. Among these two kinds of conditioned phenomena, only the directly conditioned is discussed in the Paṭṭhāna. The consequentially conditioned, however, should be explained according to the Suttanta method. Herein, the Suttanta method is this causal principle: 'When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises.' Moreover, greed, hatred, and delusion are called causes in the sense of being the root of misfortune for the entire world of beings, the world of formations, and the world of space. Non-greed, non-hatred, and non-delusion are called causes in the sense of being the root of success. This principle should be understood appropriately for all the conditions of the Paṭṭhāna. Thus it should be understood that all worldly processes are the directly conditioned and consequentially conditioned phenomena of these twenty-four conditions.

Ettāvatā paccayānaṃ atthadīpanā paccayānaṃ sabhāgasaṅgaho paccayānaṃ ghaṭanānayoti tīhi kaṇḍehi paricchinnā paṭṭhānuddesa dīpanī niṭṭhitā hoti.

Thus, the Paṭṭhānuddesa Dīpanī, defined by three sections—the explanation of the meaning of the conditions, the grouping of conditions by similarity, and the method of combining conditions—is concluded.
