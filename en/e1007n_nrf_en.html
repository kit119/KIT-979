Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Sūrassatīnīti

Paṭhamo bhāgo

First Part

Paṇāmagāthā

Verses of Salutation

1.

1.

Mukhamhā [M 1.0001] bhagavantassa,

Sugandhakamalā subhā;

Sañjātaṃ uttamaṃ vāṇiṃ,

Vandāmi vittamānasā.

From the mouth of the Blessed One has arisen the supreme speech, fragrant and beautiful like a lotus; with a gladdened mind, I offer veneration.

2.

2.

Samajjhiṭṭho racissāmi,

Dakkhena rājamantinā;

Nāmena santena,

Nītiṃ lokahitāvahaṃ;

Sālaṅkāraṃ sopadesaṃ,

Nāmenāhaṃ svarassatiṃ.

Having been well-requested by the skillful royal minister, Santa by name, I shall compose a Nīti that brings welfare to the world, adorned and instructive, which I name Svarassatī.

3.

3.

Pekkhantvimaṃ nītiṃ santā,

Mañjūsarukkhasannibhaṃ;

Nānopadesasaṃpuṇṇaṃ,

Sadatthakusalā sadā.

May the good, who are always skillful in true welfare, behold this Nīti, which is like a Mañjūsaka tree, filled with various instructions.

Sūrassatīnīti

Kataññutā [M 1.0003] ca saccañca,

Lokasārā hi te duve;

Lokāpi tehi tiṭṭhanti,

Raṭṭhaṃ akaṃsu issaraṃ.

Gratitude and truthfulness—these two are indeed the world's essence; by them the worlds also stand, and they make a country supreme.

2.

Kākāca [M 1.0008] dujjanā loke,

Malībhūtāva sabbadā;

Iṭṭhaṃ guṇaṃ nāsayanti,

Te ve lokassa verino.

The wicked in the world are like crows, always defiled; they destroy desirable qualities, truly they are enemies of the world.

Migānaṃ [M 1.0010] siṅgālo anto,

Pakkhīnaṃ pana vāyaso.

Among beasts, the jackal is the lowest; and among birds, the crow.

Akāraṇaverī [M 1.0012] honti,

Macchānaṃ dhīvarā yathā;

Guṇīnaṃ sajjanānañca,

Dujjanā niccaverino.

The wicked are hostile without cause, like fishermen to fish; they are the constant enemies of the virtuous and the good.

3.

3.

Siriṃ [M 1.0014] bhonto suposetha,

Siri mūlā hi sampadā;

Siriya idha jotanti,

Sirī sā sabbasiddhikā.

Sirs, you should cherish fortune, for fortune is indeed the root of prosperity; by fortune one shines in this world, for that fortune is the accomplisher of all success.

4.

4.

Musā [M 1.0020] tamokarā loke,

Saccaṃ mīditikārakaṃ;

Musātamena dukkhanti,

Saccābhāya sukhantive.

Falsehood creates darkness in the world; truth brings about delight. Through the darkness of falsehood they suffer; through the light of truth they find happiness.

Suvijānaṃ [M 1.0023] siṅgālānaṃ,

Sakuṇānañca vassitaṃ;

Manussavassitaṃ rāja,

Dubbijānataraṃ tato.

The cry of jackals is easy to understand, and so is the call of birds; but the speech of humans, O king, is much harder to understand than that.

5.

5.

Kappaggisadisā [M 1.0025] issā,

Jhāpeti sabbasampadaṃ;

Muditā kappameghova,

Ropeti sabbasampadaṃ.

Envy, like the fire at the aeon's end, burns up all prosperity; sympathetic joy, like a cloud at the aeon's beginning, cultivates all prosperity.

6.

6.

Yathā [M 1.0030] asanthirā thambhā,

Thusarāsimhi ussitā;

Tatheva kapicittānaṃ,

Kammantā cañcalaṅgatā.

Just as pillars erected on a heap of husks are unsteady, so too are the actions of those with fickle minds unstable.

Haliddirāgaṃ [M 1.0034] kapicittaṃ;

Purisaṃ rāgavirāginaṃ,

Edisaṃ tāta māsevi;

Nimmanussampi ce siyā.

The fickle mind is like turmeric dye; a person now passionate, now dispassionate. Dear, do not associate with such a one, even if he were a non-human.

Pupphacumbi [M 1.0035] cittapattī,

Sakavaṇṇena majjito;

Vikkamī pupphato pupphaṃ,

Nassāgāraṃ na saṅgamo.

The butterfly, a kisser of flowers, resplendent in its own color, flits from flower to flower; for it there is no home, no association.

Samaggāca parakkamā;

Mā hotha pupphacumbīva,

Hotha vo pacikā yathā.

Though ants are of poor color, they are united and persevering; do not be like flower-kissers, but be indeed like ants.

Svānā aññoññamoditā;

Disvāna chaṭṭitaṃ bhattaṃ,

Sīghaverī vihiṃsare.

Those dogs, delighting and playing with each other, upon seeing discarded food, attack one another as swift enemies.

Tathekepi [M 1.0041] janā dāni,

Samaggā ññoññamoditā;

Dhanahetu vihiṃsanti,

Dhīratthu sīghaverikā.

Just so, some people now, though united and delighting in each other, do injury for the sake of wealth—shame on such swift enmity!

Vasante [M 1.0045] hemante gimhe;

Neva tālā visesino;

Thiracittā janā santā;

Sukhadukkhesu niccalā.

In spring, winter, and summer, palmyra trees show no change; likewise, good people with steadfast minds are unwavering amidst pleasure and pain.

10.

Yathā [M 1.0050] pavaṭṭamānamhi,

Suṭṭhu tiṭṭhati geṇṭhuke;

Appavaṭṭe bhūmyaṃ seti,

Tatheva geṇṭhuko jano.

Just as a ball of grass stands up well where water flows, but lies on the ground where there is no flow, so too is a person of that kind.

Agghāpetuṃ [M 1.0055] nasakkonti,

Kālañhi kālikā janā;

Vajirādiñca sakkonti,

Tena kālo anagghiko.

Temporal people cannot appraise time; but they can appraise diamonds and other things, therefore time is invaluable.

12. [Ka]

12. [Ka]

Asano [M 1.0059] hi dīghaddhāno,

Sārasāro sugandhiko;

Niggandho tveva nissāro,

Dīghaddhānopi simbalī.

The Asana tree, though long-lived, is full of essence and fragrant; but the silk-cotton tree, though also long-lived, is scentless and void of essence.

[Kha]

[Kha]

Tatheveke [M 1.0060] janā loke,

Dīghaddhānā susārakā;

Nissārā keci pheggūva,

Dīghaddhānāpi goyathā.

Just so in the world, some people are long-lived and have substance; others are without substance, like pith, though long-lived, like cattle.

Upakāro [M 1.0064] cāpakāro,

Yasmiṃ gacchati naṭṭhataṃ;

Pāsāṇahadayassassa,

Jīvatītyā bhidhāmudhā.

For whom helpfulness and harmfulness come to naught, and who is stone-hearted, to call him 'living' is a vain designation.

Pasādo nipphalo yassa,

Kopocāpi niratthako;

Na taṃ saṅgantu miccheyya,

Thīpumāva napuṃsakaṃ.

He whose favor is fruitless, and whose anger is also pointless; one should not wish to associate with him, as a woman would not with a eunuch.

14.

14.

Upacāro [M 1.0069] hi kātabbo,

Na yāva sohadaṃ bhave;

Upacāro sumittamhi,

Māyā ca hoti koṭilaṃ.

Courtesy should indeed be shown, but not so far as to become intimacy. To show mere courtesy to a good friend is deceit and crookedness.

15.

15.

Kamena [M 1.0075] aggato ucchu,

Raso sādutaro yathā;

Tatheva sumitto loke,

Dummitto pana nediso.

Just as the juice of the sugarcane is progressively sweeter from the top, so too is a good friend in the world; but a bad friend is not so.

16.

16.

Sokārāti [M 1.0080] parittāṇaṃ,

Vissāsapītibhājanaṃ;

Ratanābhiratanaṃ icche,

Sumittaṃ akkharattayaṃ.

A protection from sorrow and enemies, a vessel of trust and joy; one should desire a jewel beyond jewels: a good friend, that three-syllabled word.

17.

17.

Dampatīnaṃ [M 1.0085] sumittānaṃ,

Mukhaṃ aññoññadappaṇaṃ,

Sukhe sukhaṃ dukkhe dukkhaṃ,

Paṭicchāyeva dappaṇe.

For spouses who are good friends, the face is a mirror to the other; in happiness there is happiness, in suffering there is suffering, like a reflection in a mirror.

Kathaṃ [M 1.0088] nu tāsaṃ hadayaṃ,

Sukharāvata itthiyo;

Yāsāmike dukkhitamhi,

Sukhamicchanti attano.

How indeed are the hearts of those women, those women attached to pleasure, who desire their own happiness while their husband is suffering?

18.

18.

Nivātañca [M 1.0090] pure katvā,

Mānaṃ katvāna pacchato;

Sakatthaṃ dhāraye dhīro,

Atthabhañjo hi muḷhatā.

Having first shown humility, and having put pride behind, the wise one should maintain his own welfare; for folly is the destroyer of welfare.

19.

19.

Purecāraṃ [M 1.0094] satiṃ katvā,

Saddhaṃ kareyya pacchato;

Turaṃ na saddahe dhīro,

Sīghasaddho hi mandako.

Having first made mindfulness one's guide, one should place faith thereafter; the wise one should not believe hastily, for one who is quick to believe is a fool.

20.

Vane 0.0099 bahūni kaṭṭhāni;

Dullabhaṃ rattacandanaṃ;

Tathā janā bahū loke;

Pumā jañño sudullabho.

In the forest are many kinds of wood, but red sandalwood is rare; just so, people are many in the world, but a noble person is very rare.

21.

Tiṇakaṭṭhapalāsehi[M 1.0103];

Sukkhehi dayhate vanaṃ;

Etādīhi asārehi;

Loko janehi dayhate.

By dry grass, wood, and leaves the forest is burned; by such worthless people as these the world is burned.

22.

Antovasse [M 1.0107] timāsamhi,

Puññakammena moditā;

Sukhaṃ vasiṃsu porāṇā,

Buddhasāsanamāmakā.

During the three months of the rains retreat, the ancients dwelled in happiness, delighting in meritorious deeds, devoted to the Buddha's Dispensation.

23.

Migamadena [M 1.0111] ekena,

Taṃ vanaṃ surabhigandhikaṃ;

Tathā janena taṃ raṭṭhaṃ,

Guṇinā hi sirīmatā.

By a single musk deer, that forest is made fragrant; just so, by one virtuous and glorious person, that country is made prosperous.

Sarīraṃ [M 1.0115] khaṇaviddhaṃsī,

Kappantaṭṭhāyino guṇā.

The body is destroyed in a moment; virtues endure to the end of the aeon.

24.

24.

Kharānaṃ [M 1.0116] sīhabyagghānaṃ,

Saṅgamo no hisabbadā;

Tatheva byagghacittānaṃ,

Sajātikā khayonatā.

Fierce lions and tigers do not always associate; just so, for those with tiger-like minds, association with their own kind leads to destruction.

25.

25.

Saka [M 1.0121] sādhupi no sādhū,

Yo ceñña duṭṭhakārako;

Bahūnaṃ sādhū pāyena,

Sa ve sādhūti vuccate.

One's own good is not good if it causes harm to another; that which is good for the many, for the most part, that indeed is called good.

26.

26.

Bahūdake [M 1.0126] samuddepi,

Jalaṃ nattheva pātave;

Khuddake khatakūpamhi,

Sāduṃ atthi bahuṃ dakaṃ.

Even in the ocean with its great waters, there is no water to drink; but in a small, dug well, there is much sweet water.

27.

Mā [M 1.0130] sīghaṃ vivareyyātha,

Nindituñca pasaṃsituṃ;

Mukhañhi vo kathādvāraṃ,

Nirundheyyātha sabbadā.

Do not be quick to open your mouth to blame or to praise; for your mouth is the door of speech, which you should always restrain.

28.

28.

Mā [M 1.0134] sīghaṃ vivareyyātha,

Cakkhuṃ vo dassituṃ piyaṃ;

Saṇikañhi piyalābhaṃ,

Dhanalābhaṃ turaṃ kare.

Do not be quick to open your eye to show what is dear; for the gain of affection should be slow, while the gain of wealth should be swift.

29.

Anārambho [M 1.0138] hi kammānaṃ,

Paṭhamaṃ buddhilakkhaṇaṃ;

Niṭṭhaṅgataṃ āraddhassa,

Dutiyaṃ buddhilakkhaṇaṃ.

Indeed, the non-commencement of deeds is the first mark of wisdom; bringing to completion what has been commenced is the second mark of wisdom.

Asamekkhitakammantaṃ[M 1.0140],

Turitābhinipātinaṃ;

Sāni kammāni tappenti,

Uṇhaṃ vajjhohaṭaṃ mukhe.

For one who acts without reflection, who rushes hastily into things, his own actions torment him, like a hot iron ball placed in the mouth.

30.

Aphalāni [M 1.0143] durantāni,

Janatā ninditāni ca;

Asakyāni ca kammāni,

Nārabhetha vicakkhaṇo.

The discerning one should not undertake deeds that are fruitless, that have a bad end, that are blamed by the people, and that are impossible to achieve.

31.

31.

Ativirodhabhītānaṃ[M 1.0147],

Saṅkitānaṃ pade pade;

Parappavādatāsānaṃ,

Dūrato yanti sampadā.

From those who fear excessive opposition, who are suspicious at every step, who are terrified by the talk of others—from them, prosperity goes far away.

Saddamattaṃ [M 1.0150] na bhetabbaṃ,

Loko saddassa gocaro;

Yo ca saddaparittāso,

Vane bhantamigo hi so.

Mere sound should not be feared; the world is the domain of sound. Whoever is terrified by sound is indeed like a deer wandering in the forest.

32.

Dvinnaṃ [M 1.0151] taṇḍulathūsānaṃ,

Viseso suṭṭhu khāyati;

Randhitopi siniddho no,

Thuso virasaphāruso;

Taṇḍulaṃ siniddhaṃ rasaṃ,

Evaṃ lokepi ñāyate.

The difference between rice grain and husk is clearly perceived. Even when cooked, the husk is not smooth; it is tasteless and rough. The rice grain is smooth and flavorful. Thus it is known in the world.

33.

Eraṇḍaṃ [M 1.0155] nissitā valli,

Ruhate kiṃ yathābalaṃ;

Mahāsālaṃ sunissāya,

Ruhate brahataṃ gatā.

A vine that relies on a castor-oil plant grows only according to its own strength; but having relied well on a great Sāla tree, it grows to greatness.

34.

Mettā [M 1.0160] hi sīmasambhedā,

Pakkhapāta vighātikā;

Pakkhapātena dukkhanti,

Nippakkho vasate sukhaṃ.

Loving-kindness indeed breaks down boundaries and destroys partiality. Through partiality one suffers; the impartial one dwells in happiness.

35.

Yathāṭhāne niyujjare;

No hi cūḷāmaṇi pāde,

Pādukā ca siropari.

People, wisdom, and ornaments should be employed in their proper place; for a crest-jewel does not belong on the foot, nor a sandal upon the head.

Ukkuṭṭhe [M 1.0167] sūra micchanti;

Mantīsu akutūhalaṃ;

Viyañca annapānamhi;

Atthe jāte ca paṇḍitaṃ.

In battle, a hero is desired; in council, one who is unagitated; at the time of food and drink, a dear one; and when a need arises, a wise person.

Kālo coro bhayānako;

Kāyagehaṃ bahuchiddaṃ,

Kālacorassa coritaṃ.

Heedlessness is indeed darkness in the world; time is a fearful thief. The body-house has many holes, stolen by the thief of time.

Cañcalo [M 1.0173] kāladāso hi,

Dhītimā kālaissaro;

Kālissaro raṭṭhissaraṃ,

Ativattati sabbaso.

The fickle one is indeed a slave of time, the steadfast one is lord of time; the lord of time entirely surpasses the lord of the realm.

Tesu kālavilopāva,

Bhayānakā tikakkhaḷā.

Some plunder wealth, others are heedless of time. For them, the plunderings of time are fearful and very harsh.

Rañño hitaṃ janehi ve;

Desso attahitaṃ dassī,

The country's welfare is to be done by the king; and the king's welfare is done by the people indeed. Detestable is one who sees only his own welfare—how censurable indeed is such a doer!

Attadatthaṃ [M 1.0189] paratthena;

Bahunāpi na hāpaye.

One should not neglect one's own welfare for the sake of another's, however great.

To whom a favor is given, he should give a favor in return; then one should recognize the favor done, for the grateful are rare in this world.

Kaṭṭhaṃ niplavitaṃ seyyo,

Indeed, some people here have spoken truly: 'Better is a floating log than a certain kind of man.'

Mahaggho lokamānito.

A craftsman with his own skill may make a piece of wood valuable; just so, a person may make himself valuable and honored by the world.

The Second Part

1.

1.

Dhanassa [M 2.0001] dubbidhaṃ kiccaṃ,

Pāpeti uṇṇataṃ dhaniṃ;

Adhaniṃ oṇataṃ loke,

Sañcine tena taṃ dhanaṃ.

Wealth has a twofold function: it brings the wealthy to a high state and the poor to a low state in the world. Therefore, that wealth should be accumulated.

2.

2.

Vaṭṭate [M 2.0006] satataṃ sīghaṃ,

Kālacakkaṃ avāritaṃ;

Tena ghaṭī dinaṃ māso,

Vasso bhavatya cīrato.

The wheel of time turns constantly, swiftly, and unobstructed. By it, the hour, day, month, and year quickly come to be.

3.

3.

Sattunā [M 2.0011] na hi sandheyya,

Ekadā so bhayaṃ karo;

Sutattamapi pānīyaṃ,

Samayate nu pāvakaṃ.

One should not make an alliance with an enemy, for at some time he will cause fear. Does even well-heated water quench a fire?

4.

4.

Vijahaṃ [M 2.0016] pakatiṃ yo hi,

Vikatiṃ puna gacchati;

Sabhāvena ākārena,

Vippallāsaṃ sa gacchati;

Saṃsumāra gatā godhā,

Yathā thī pumavesikā.

Indeed, whoever, abandoning their natural state, goes again to an altered state, that one, by their inherent nature and outward appearance, goes to perversion; like a monitor lizard that has become a crocodile, or a woman in the guise of a man.

5.

5.

Pakkhaṃ [M 2.0020] laddhāna uḍḍenti,

Upacikā hi vammikā;

Nikkhantā maraṇaṃ yanti,

Uppatā nippataṃ gatā.

Indeed, termites from an anthill, having gained wings, fly up. Having gone forth, they go to their death; having flown up, they fall down.

6.

6.

Sabbaṃpiyassa [M 2.0023] dajjeyya,

Nissesaṃ piyamānasaṃ;

Saddhācittaṃ tu no viññū,

Saddhāyiko pakkhalito.

One should give everything, without remainder, to one who is dear to one's heart. But the wise do not approve a mind of mere faith; one who relies on faith alone is unsteady.

7.

7.

Sakkoti [M 2.0027] laṅghituṃ byāmaṃ,

Mahussāhena yo hi so;

Tadaḍḍhaṃ anussāhena,

Nossāho tesu thomito;

Mahussāho dukkho loke,

Anussāho sadā sukho.

Indeed, he who has great energy can leap a fathom; with no energy, half of that. Among them, the one with no energy is praised. Great exertion is suffering in the world; non-exertion is always happiness.

8.

8.

Dukkhamaṃ [M 2.0032] akkhamanto yo,

Piṭṭhikārīca dukkaraṃ,

Kadā labheyya so loke,

Sukhamaṃ sukaraṃ mudhā;

Paccakkhañhi so kareyya,

Dukkhamañcāpi dukkaraṃ.

He who, not enduring suffering, turns his back on what is difficult, when in this world will he obtain effortless and easy happiness? Indeed, he would make suffering and difficulty manifest for himself.

9.

9.

Anaggho [M 2.0036] manusso loke,

Tosanāposanādinā;

Tena so mahagghaṃ kammaṃ,

Kare lokahitāyutaṃ.

A human being is invaluable in the world, through pleasing, nurturing, and so on. Therefore, such a one should perform greatly valuable deeds connected with the welfare of the world.

10.

10.

Vātena [M 2.0041] nappabhijjanti,

Ninnā veḷū kasā naḷā;

Yathāvātaṃ nagacchanti,

Tathā care jane kadā.

Bamboos, reeds, and grasses in the lowlands are not broken by the wind; they do not go against the wind. Thus should one ever conduct oneself among people.

11.

11.

Purato [M 2.0044] ca pacchato ce,

Nissayo natthi passato;

Adhikaṃ vīriyaṃ hoti,

Attanātho tadā bhave.

For one who sees no support before or behind, greater effort arises; then one becomes one's own refuge.

12.

12.

Khaṇaṃ [M 2.0048] ākhubilaṃ sīho,

Pāsāṇasakalākulaṃ;

Pappoti nakhabhaṅgaṃvā,

Phalaṃvā mūsiko bhave.

A lion digging at a rat-hole full of rock fragments may get a broken claw, or the mouse may find success.

13.

13.

Maggamuḷhā [M 2.0052] janā andhā,

Amaggā maggasaññino;

Tatheva duppaññā muḷhā,

Tathatthaṃ nāva bujjhare.

Blind people are deluded about the path, perceiving a path where there is no path. Just so, the unwise and deluded do not awaken to the true meaning.

14.

14.

Piyarūpaṃ [M 2.0056] vīrarūpaṃ,

Duvidhā rūpasampadā,

Nāriṃ icche piyarūpiṃ,

Purisaṃ vīrarūpakaṃ.

Lovely form and heroic form: these are the two kinds of excellence of form. One should desire a woman of lovely form, and a man of heroic form.

15.

15.

Pajjalanti [M 2.0060] hi khajjotā,

Pakkhacālanakammunā;

Kusitā supitā nete,

Tathā janāpi kammikā.

Indeed, fireflies glow by the action of moving their wings; they are not lazy or asleep. Just so are people who are industrious.

16.

Lions with lofty minds do not eat leftovers. They do not bow down to another. One desiring growth should conduct oneself thus.

Whom do diseases not torment? What foolish-minded person do faults of policy not approach? Whom does fortune not honor, and whom does death not slay? Whom do sensual pleasures and the feminine state not oppress?
