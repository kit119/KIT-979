1. Nissayavaggo

1. The Chapter on Support

1. Kimatthiyasuttaṃ

1. What is the Purpose?

1. a. ni. 10.1 Evaṃ [V 3.0279][M 3.0515] me sutaṃ – ekaṃ [T 5.0335] samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Atha [P 5.0311] kho āyasmā ānando yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – “kimatthiyāni, bhante, kusalāni sīlāni kimānisaṃsānī”ti? “Avippaṭisāratthāni kho, ānanda, kusalāni sīlāni avippaṭisārānisaṃsānī”ti.

1. Thus have I heard. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in Sāvatthī, in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park. Then the Venerable Ānanda approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. Sitting there, the Venerable Ānanda said to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, what is the purpose of wholesome virtues? What is their benefit?” “Ānanda, wholesome virtues have non-remorse as their purpose and non-remorse as their benefit.”

“Avippaṭisāro [V 3.0280] pana, bhante, kimatthiyo kimānisaṃso”? “Avippaṭisāro kho, ānanda, pāmojjattho pāmojjānisaṃso”.

“But, venerable sir, what is the purpose of non-remorse? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, non-remorse has gladness as its purpose and gladness as its benefit.”

“Pāmojjaṃ pana, bhante, kimatthiyaṃ kimānisaṃsaṃ”? “Pāmojjaṃ kho, ānanda, pītatthaṃ pītānisaṃsaṃ”.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of gladness? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, gladness has rapture as its purpose and rapture as its benefit.”

“Pīti pana, bhante, kimatthiyā kimānisaṃsā”? “Pīti kho, ānanda, passaddhatthā passaddhānisaṃsā”.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of rapture? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, rapture has tranquillity as its purpose and tranquillity as its benefit.”

“Passaddhi pana, bhante, kimatthiyā kimānisaṃsā”? “Passaddhi kho, ānanda, sukhatthā sukhānisaṃsā”.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of tranquillity? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, tranquillity has happiness as its purpose and happiness as its benefit.”

“Sukhaṃ pana, bhante, kimatthiyaṃ kimānisaṃsaṃ”? “Sukhaṃ kho, ānanda, samādhatthaṃ samādhānisaṃsaṃ”.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of happiness? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, happiness has concentration as its purpose and concentration as its benefit.”

“Samādhi [M 3.0516] pana, bhante, kimatthiyo kimānisaṃso”? “Samādhi kho, ānanda, yathābhūtañāṇadassanattho yathābhūtañāṇadassanānisaṃso”[T 5.0336].

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of concentration? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, concentration has the knowledge and vision of things as they really are as its purpose and the knowledge and vision of things as they really are as its benefit.”

“Yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ pana, bhante, kimatthiyaṃ kimānisaṃsaṃ”? “Yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ kho, ānanda, nibbidatthaṃ nibbidānisaṃsaṃ”.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of the knowledge and vision of things as they really are? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, the knowledge and vision of things as they really are has disenchantment as its purpose and disenchantment as its benefit.”

“Nibbidā, pana, bhante, kimatthiyā kimānisaṃsā”? “Nibbidā kho, ānanda, virāgatthā virāgānisaṃsā [P 5.0312]”.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of disenchantment? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, disenchantment has dispassion as its purpose and dispassion as its benefit.”

“Virāgo pana, bhante, kimatthiyo kimānisaṃso”? “Virāgo kho, ānanda, vimuttiñāṇadassanattho vimuttiñāṇadassanānisaṃso.

“And, venerable sir, what is the purpose of dispassion? What is its benefit?” “Ānanda, dispassion has the knowledge and vision of liberation as its purpose and the knowledge and vision of liberation as its benefit.”

“Iti kho, ānanda, kusalāni sīlāni avippaṭisāratthāni avippaṭisārānisaṃsāni, avippaṭisāro [V 3.0281] pāmojjattho pāmojjānisaṃso, pāmojjaṃ pītatthaṃ pītānisaṃsaṃ, pīti passaddhatthā passaddhānisaṃsā, passaddhi sukhatthā sukhānisaṃsā, sukhaṃ samādhatthaṃ samādhānisaṃsaṃ, samādhi yathābhūtañāṇadassanattho yathābhūtañāṇadassanānisaṃso, yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ nibbidatthaṃ nibbidānisaṃsaṃ, nibbidā virāgatthā virāgānisaṃsā, virāgo vimuttiñāṇadassanattho vimuttiñāṇadassanānisaṃso. Iti kho, ānanda, kusalāni sīlāni anupubbena aggāya parentī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

“Thus, Ānanda, wholesome virtues have non-remorse as their purpose and non-remorse as their benefit. Non-remorse has gladness as its purpose and gladness as its benefit. Gladness has rapture as its purpose and rapture as its benefit. Rapture has tranquillity as its purpose and tranquillity as its benefit. Tranquillity has happiness as its purpose and happiness as its benefit. Happiness has concentration as its purpose and concentration as its benefit. Concentration has the knowledge and vision of things as they really are as its purpose and the knowledge and vision of things as they really are as its benefit. The knowledge and vision of things as they really are has disenchantment as its purpose and disenchantment as its benefit. Disenchantment has dispassion as its purpose and dispassion as its benefit. Dispassion has the knowledge and vision of liberation as its purpose and the knowledge and vision of liberation as its benefit. Thus, Ānanda, wholesome virtues lead sequentially to the supreme goal.” The First.

2. Cetanākaraṇīyasuttaṃ

2. The Sutta on Intention

2. a. ni. 10.2 “Sīlavato, bhikkhave, sīlasampannassa na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘avippaṭisāro me uppajjatū’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ sīlavato sīlasampannassa avippaṭisāro uppajjati.

2. “For one who is virtuous, monks, for one accomplished in virtue, one need not make an act of will: ‘May non-remorse arise in me.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that for one who is virtuous, for one accomplished in virtue, non-remorse arises.

“Avippaṭisārissa, bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘pāmojjaṃ me uppajjatū’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ avippaṭisārissa pāmojjaṃ uppajjati.

“For one who has non-remorse, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May gladness arise in me.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that for one who has non-remorse, gladness arises.

“Pamuditassa, bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘pīti me uppajjatū’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ pamuditassa pīti uppajjati.

“For one who is glad, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May rapture arise in me.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that for one who is glad, rapture arises.

“Pītimanassa, bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘kāyo me [T 5.0337] passambhatū’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ pītimanassa kāyo passambhati.

“For one with a rapturous mind, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May my body be tranquil.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that for one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil.

“Passaddhakāyassa[M 3.0517], bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘sukhaṃ vediyāmī’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati.

“For one with a tranquil body, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May I experience happiness.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that one with a tranquil body experiences happiness.

“Sukhino, bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘cittaṃ me samādhiyatū’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati.

“For one who is happy, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May my mind become concentrated.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that the mind of one who is happy becomes concentrated.

“Samāhitassa[V 3.0282], bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘yathābhūtaṃ jānāmi passāmī’ti. Dhammatā esā[P 5.0313], bhikkhave, yaṃ samāhito yathābhūtaṃ jānāti passati.

“For one who is concentrated, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May I know and see things as they truly are.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that one who is concentrated knows and sees things as they truly are.

“Yathābhūtaṃ, bhikkhave, jānato passato na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘nibbindāmī’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ yathābhūtaṃ jānaṃ passaṃ nibbindati.

“For one who knows and sees things as they truly are, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May I become disenchanted.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that one who knows and sees things as they truly are becomes disenchanted.

“Nibbinnassa, bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘virajjāmī’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ nibbinno virajjati.

“For one who is disenchanted, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May I become dispassionate.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that one who is disenchanted becomes dispassionate.

“Virattassa, bhikkhave, na cetanāya karaṇīyaṃ – ‘vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ sacchikaromī’ti. Dhammatā esā, bhikkhave, yaṃ viratto vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ sacchikaroti.

“For one who is dispassionate, monks, one need not make an act of will: ‘May I realize the knowledge and vision of liberation.’ It is the nature of things, monks, that one who is dispassionate realizes the knowledge and vision of liberation.

“Iti kho, bhikkhave, virāgo vimuttiñāṇadassanattho vimuttiñāṇadassanānisaṃso, nibbidā virāgatthā virāgānisaṃsā, yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ nibbidatthaṃ nibbidānisaṃsaṃ, samādhi yathābhūtañāṇadassanattho yathābhūtañāṇadassanānisaṃso, sukhaṃ samādhatthaṃ samādhānisaṃsaṃ, passaddhi sukhatthā sukhānisaṃsā, pīti passaddhatthā passaddhānisaṃsā, pāmojjaṃ pītatthaṃ pītānisaṃsaṃ, avippaṭisāro pāmojjattho pāmojjānisaṃso, kusalāni sīlāni avippaṭisāratthāni avippaṭisārānisaṃsāni. Iti kho, bhikkhave, dhammā dhamme [T 5.0338] abhisandenti, dhammā dhamme paripūrenti apārā pāraṃ gamanāyā”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

Thus, monks, dispassion has the knowledge and vision of liberation as its purpose and benefit. Disenchantment has dispassion as its purpose and benefit. The knowledge and vision of things as they truly are has disenchantment as its purpose and benefit. Concentration has the knowledge and vision of things as they truly are as its purpose and benefit. Happiness has concentration as its purpose and benefit. Tranquility has happiness as its purpose and benefit. Rapture has tranquility as its purpose and benefit. Joy has rapture as its purpose and benefit. Non-remorse has joy as its purpose and benefit. Wholesome virtues have non-remorse as their purpose and benefit. Thus, monks, states lead on to states, states bring states to fulfillment, for going from the near shore to the far shore.

3. Paṭhamaupanisāsuttaṃ

3. The First Discourse on Supporting Conditions

3. a. ni. 5.24; 10.3 “Dussīlassa, bhikkhave, sīlavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti avippaṭisāro. Avippaṭisāre asati avippaṭisāravipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ. Pāmojje asati pāmojjavipannassa hatūpanisā hoti pīti. Pītiyā asati pītivipannassa hatūpanisā hoti passaddhi. Passaddhiyā asati [M 3.0518] passaddhivipannassa hatūpanisaṃ [P 5.0314] hoti sukhaṃ. Sukhe asati sukhavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti sammāsamādhi. Sammāsamādhimhi asati sammāsamādhivipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ. Yathābhūtañāṇadassane asati yathābhūtañāṇadassanavipannassa hatūpanisā [V 3.0283] hoti nibbidā. Nibbidāya asati nibbidāvipannassa hatūpaniso hoti virāgo. Virāge asati virāgavipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

3. Monks, for one who is immoral, whose virtue is corrupted, non-remorse has its supporting condition destroyed. When non-remorse is absent, for one whose non-remorse is corrupted, joy has its supporting condition destroyed. When joy is absent, for one whose joy is corrupted, rapture has its supporting condition destroyed. When rapture is absent, for one whose rapture is corrupted, tranquility has its supporting condition destroyed. When tranquility is absent, for one whose tranquility is corrupted, happiness has its supporting condition destroyed. When happiness is absent, for one whose happiness is corrupted, right concentration has its supporting condition destroyed. When right concentration is absent, for one whose right concentration is corrupted, the knowledge and vision of things as they are has its supporting condition destroyed. When the knowledge and vision of things as they are is absent, for one whose knowledge and vision of things as they are is corrupted, disenchantment has its supporting condition destroyed. When disenchantment is absent, for one whose disenchantment is corrupted, dispassion has its supporting condition destroyed. When dispassion is absent, for one whose dispassion is corrupted, the knowledge and vision of liberation has its supporting condition destroyed.

“Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, rukkho sākhāpalāsavipanno. Tassa papaṭikāpi na pāripūriṃ gacchati, tacopi… pheggupi… sāropi na pāripūriṃ gacchati. Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, dussīlassa sīlavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre asati avippaṭisāravipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ…pe… vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Just as, monks, a tree deficient in branches and leaves does not reach fullness in its outer bark, nor its inner bark, nor its sapwood, nor its heartwood; even so, monks, for one who is immoral, whose virtue is corrupted, non-remorse has its supporting condition destroyed. When non-remorse is absent, for one whose non-remorse is corrupted, joy has its supporting condition destroyed… and so on up to… the knowledge and vision of liberation has its supporting condition destroyed.

“Sīlavato, bhikkhave, sīlasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre sati avippaṭisārasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ, pāmojje sati pāmojjasampannassa upanisasampannā hoti pīti, pītiyā sati pītisampannassa upanisasampannā hoti passaddhi, passaddhiyā sati passaddhisampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti sukhaṃ, sukhe sati sukhasampannassa upanisasampanno [T 5.0339] hoti sammāsamādhi, sammāsamādhimhi sati sammāsamādhisampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ, yathābhūtañāṇadassane sati yathābhūtañāṇadassanasampannassa upanisasampannā hoti nibbidā, nibbidāya sati nibbidāsampannassa upanisasampanno hoti virāgo, virāge sati virāgasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Monks, for one who is virtuous, who is accomplished in virtue, non-remorse has a firm supporting condition. When non-remorse is present, for one accomplished in non-remorse, joy has a firm supporting condition. When joy is present, for one accomplished in joy, rapture has a firm supporting condition. When rapture is present, for one accomplished in rapture, tranquility has a firm supporting condition. When tranquility is present, for one accomplished in tranquility, happiness has a firm supporting condition. When happiness is present, for one accomplished in happiness, right concentration has a firm supporting condition. When right concentration is present, for one accomplished in right concentration, the knowledge and vision of things as they are has a firm supporting condition. When the knowledge and vision of things as they are is present, for one accomplished in the knowledge and vision of things as they are, disenchantment has a firm supporting condition. When disenchantment is present, for one accomplished in disenchantment, dispassion has a firm supporting condition. When dispassion is present, for one accomplished in dispassion, the knowledge and vision of liberation has a firm supporting condition.

“Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, rukkho sākhāpalāsasampanno. Tassa papaṭikāpi pāripūriṃ gacchati, tacopi… pheggupi… sāropi pāripūriṃ gacchati. Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, sīlavato sīlasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre sati avippaṭisārasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti…pe… vimuttiñāṇadassana”nti. Tatiyaṃ.

Just as, monks, a tree endowed with branches and leaves reaches fullness in its outer bark, its inner bark, its sapwood, and its heartwood; even so, monks, for one who is virtuous, who is accomplished in virtue, non-remorse has a firm supporting condition… and so on up to… the knowledge and vision of liberation has a firm supporting condition.

4. Dutiyaupanisāsuttaṃ

4. The Second Discourse on Supporting Conditions

4. Tatra [M 3.0519][P 5.0315] kho āyasmā sāriputto bhikkhū āmantesi – “āvuso bhikkhave”ti bhikkhavoti (sī. syā. pī.) evaṃ sabbattha a. ni. 10.4. “Āvuso”ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato sāriputtassa paccassosuṃ. Āyasmā sāriputto etadavoca –

4. There, the Venerable Sāriputta addressed the monks, saying, “Friends, monks.” “Friend,” those monks replied to the Venerable Sāriputta. The Venerable Sāriputta said this:

“Dussīlassa[V 3.0284], āvuso, sīlavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre asati avippaṭisāravipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ, pāmojje asati pāmojjavipannassa hatūpanisā hoti pīti, pītiyā asati pītivipannassa hatūpanisā hoti passaddhi, passaddhiyā asati passaddhivipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti sukhaṃ, sukhe asati sukhavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti sammāsamādhi, sammāsamādhimhi asati sammāsamādhivipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ, yathābhūtañāṇadassane asati yathābhūtañāṇadassanavipannassa hatūpanisā hoti nibbidā[T 5.0340], nibbidāya asati nibbidāvipannassa hatūpaniso hoti virāgo, virāge asati virāgavipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Friends, for an immoral person, one deficient in virtue, non-remorse has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no non-remorse, for one deficient in non-remorse, joy has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no joy, for one deficient in joy, rapture has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no rapture, for one deficient in rapture, tranquility has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no tranquility, for one deficient in tranquility, happiness has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no happiness, for one deficient in happiness, right concentration has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no right concentration, for one deficient in right concentration, the knowledge and vision of things as they really are has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no knowledge and vision of things as they really are, for one deficient in the knowledge and vision of things as they really are, disenchantment has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no disenchantment, for one deficient in disenchantment, dispassion has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no dispassion, for one deficient in dispassion, the knowledge and vision of liberation has its supporting condition destroyed.

“Seyyathāpi, āvuso, rukkho sākhāpalāsavipanno. Tassa papaṭikāpi na pāripūriṃ gacchati, tacopi… pheggupi… sāropi na pāripūriṃ gacchati. Evamevaṃ kho, āvuso, dussīlassa sīlavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre asati avippaṭisāravipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ…pe… vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Friends, it is like a tree deficient in branches and leaves. Its outer bark does not reach fulfillment, nor its inner bark, nor its sapwood, nor its heartwood. So too, friends, for an immoral person, one deficient in virtue, non-remorse has its supporting condition destroyed. When there is no non-remorse, for one deficient in non-remorse, joy has its supporting condition destroyed… and so on… the knowledge and vision of liberation has its supporting condition destroyed.

“Sīlavato, āvuso, sīlasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre sati avippaṭisārasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ, pāmojje sati pāmojjasampannassa upanisasampannā hoti pīti, pītiyā sati pītisampannassa upanisasampannā hoti passaddhi, passaddhiyā sati passaddhisampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti sukhaṃ, sukhe sati sukhasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti sammāsamādhi, sammāsamādhimhi sati sammāsamādhisampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ, yathābhūtañāṇadassane sati yathābhūtañāṇadassanasampannassa upanisasampannā hoti nibbidā, nibbidāya sati nibbidāsampannassa [M 3.0520][P 5.0316] upanisasampanno hoti virāgo, virāge sati virāgasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Friends, for a virtuous person, one accomplished in virtue, non-remorse has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is non-remorse, for one accomplished in non-remorse, joy has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is joy, for one accomplished in joy, rapture has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is rapture, for one accomplished in rapture, tranquility has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is tranquility, for one accomplished in tranquility, happiness has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is happiness, for one accomplished in happiness, right concentration has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is right concentration, for one accomplished in right concentration, the knowledge and vision of things as they really are has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is knowledge and vision of things as they really are, for one accomplished in the knowledge and vision of things as they really are, disenchantment has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is disenchantment, for one accomplished in disenchantment, dispassion has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is dispassion, for one accomplished in dispassion, the knowledge and vision of liberation has a sufficient proximate cause.

“Seyyathāpi, āvuso, rukkho sākhāpalāsasampanno. Tassa papaṭikāpi pāripūriṃ gacchati, tacopi… pheggupi… sāropi pāripūriṃ gacchati. Evamevaṃ kho, āvuso, sīlavato sīlasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre [T 5.0341] sati avippaṭisārasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ…pe… vimuttiñāṇadassana”nti. Catutthaṃ.

Friends, it is like a tree complete with branches and leaves. Its outer bark reaches fulfillment, as does its inner bark, its sapwood, and its heartwood. So too, friends, for a virtuous person, one accomplished in virtue, non-remorse has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is non-remorse, for one accomplished in non-remorse, joy has a sufficient proximate cause… and so on… the knowledge and vision of liberation has a sufficient proximate cause. The Fourth.

5. Tatiyaupanisāsuttaṃ

5. The Third Discourse on Proximate Causes

5. a. ni. 10.5 Tatra [V 3.0285] kho āyasmā ānando bhikkhū āmantesi…pe… “dussīlassa, āvuso, sīlavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre asati avippaṭisāravipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ, pāmojje asati pāmojjavipannassa hatūpanisā hoti pīti, pītiyā asati pītivipannassa hatūpanisā hoti passaddhi, passaddhiyā asati passaddhivipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti sukhaṃ, sukhe asati sukhavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti sammāsamādhi, sammāsamādhimhi asati sammāsamādhivipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ, yathābhūtañāṇadassane asati yathābhūtañāṇadassanavipannassa hatūpanisā hoti nibbidā, nibbidāya asati nibbidāvipannassa hatūpaniso hoti virāgo, virāge asati virāgavipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

5. There, the Venerable Ānanda addressed the monks… "Friends, for an immoral person, one deficient in virtue, non-remorse lacks a proximate cause. When non-remorse is absent, for one deficient in non-remorse, joy lacks a proximate cause. When joy is absent, for one deficient in joy, rapture lacks a proximate cause. When rapture is absent, for one deficient in rapture, tranquility lacks a proximate cause. When tranquility is absent, for one deficient in tranquility, happiness lacks a proximate cause. When happiness is absent, for one deficient in happiness, right concentration lacks a proximate cause. When right concentration is absent, for one deficient in right concentration, the knowledge and vision of things as they really are lacks a proximate cause. When the knowledge and vision of things as they really are is absent, for one deficient in the knowledge and vision of things as they really are, disenchantment lacks a proximate cause. When disenchantment is absent, for one deficient in disenchantment, dispassion lacks a proximate cause. When dispassion is absent, for one deficient in dispassion, the knowledge and vision of liberation lacks a proximate cause."

“Seyyathāpi, āvuso, rukkho sākhāpalāsavipanno. Tassa papaṭikāpi na pāripūriṃ gacchati, tacopi… pheggupi… sāropi na pāripūriṃ gacchati. Evamevaṃ kho, āvuso, dussīlassa sīlavipannassa hatūpaniso hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre asati avippaṭisāravipannassa hatūpanisaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ…pe… vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Friends, it is like a tree deficient in branches and leaves. Its outer bark does not reach fulfillment, nor does its inner bark, its sapwood, or its heartwood. So too, friends, for an immoral person, one deficient in virtue, non-remorse lacks a proximate cause. When non-remorse is absent, for one deficient in non-remorse, joy lacks a proximate cause… and so on… the knowledge and vision of liberation lacks a proximate cause.

“Sīlavato, āvuso, sīlasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre sati avippaṭisārasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ, pāmojje sati pāmojjasampannassa [P 5.0317] upanisasampannā hoti pīti, pītiyā sati pītisampannassa upanisasampannā [T 5.0342] hoti passaddhi, passaddhiyā sati passaddhisampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti sukhaṃ, sukhe sati sukhasampannassa [M 3.0521] upanisasampanno hoti sammāsamādhi, sammāsamādhimhi sati sammāsamādhisampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti yathābhūtañāṇadassanaṃ, yathābhūtañāṇadassane sati yathābhūtañāṇadassanasampannassa upanisasampannā hoti nibbidā, nibbidāya sati nibbidāsampannassa upanisasampanno hoti virāgo, virāge sati virāgasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti vimuttiñāṇadassanaṃ.

Friends, for a virtuous person, one accomplished in virtue, non-remorse has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is non-remorse, for one accomplished in non-remorse, joy has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is joy, for one accomplished in joy, rapture has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is rapture, for one accomplished in rapture, tranquility has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is tranquility, for one accomplished in tranquility, happiness has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is happiness, for one accomplished in happiness, right concentration has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is right concentration, for one accomplished in right concentration, the knowledge and vision of things as they really are has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is knowledge and vision of things as they really are, for one accomplished in the knowledge and vision of things as they really are, disenchantment has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is disenchantment, for one accomplished in disenchantment, dispassion has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is dispassion, for one accomplished in dispassion, the knowledge and vision of liberation has a sufficient proximate cause.

“Seyyathāpi, āvuso, rukkho sākhāpalāsasampanno. Tassa papaṭikāpi pāripūriṃ gacchati[V 3.0286], tacopi… pheggupi… sāropi pāripūriṃ gacchati. Evamevaṃ kho, āvuso, sīlavato sīlasampannassa upanisasampanno hoti avippaṭisāro, avippaṭisāre sati avippaṭisārasampannassa upanisasampannaṃ hoti pāmojjaṃ…pe… vimuttiñāṇadassana”nti. Pañcamaṃ.

Friends, it is like a tree complete with branches and leaves. Its outer bark reaches fulfillment, as does its inner bark, its sapwood, and its heartwood. So too, friends, for a virtuous person, one accomplished in virtue, non-remorse has a sufficient proximate cause. When there is non-remorse, for one accomplished in non-remorse, joy has a sufficient proximate cause… and so on… the knowledge and vision of liberation has a sufficient proximate cause. The Fifth.

6. Byasanasuttaṃ

6. The Discourse on Ruin

6. “Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu akkosako paribhāsako ariyūpavādo sabrahmacārīnaṃ, ṭhānametaṃ avakāso yaṃ so ekādasannaṃ byasanānaṃ aññataraṃ byasanaṃ nigaccheyya.

6. Monks, whatever monk is an insulter and reviler of his fellow practitioners of the holy life, and a denigrator of the noble ones, it is possible, it is the case, that he will come to one or another of eleven kinds of ruin.

Katamesaṃ ekādasannaṃ? Anadhigataṃ nādhigacchati, adhigatā parihāyati, saddhammassa na vodāyanti, saddhammesu vā adhimāniko hoti, anabhirato vā brahmacariyaṃ carati, aññataraṃ vā saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ [P 5.0318] āpattiṃ āpajjati, sikkhaṃ vā paccakkhāya hīnāyāvattati, gāḷhaṃ vā rogātaṅkaṃ phusati, ummādaṃ vā pāpuṇāti cittakkhepaṃ vā, sammūḷho kālaṃ karoti, kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjati – yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu akkosako paribhāsako ariyūpavādo sabrahmacārīnaṃ, ṭhānametaṃ avakāso yaṃ so imesaṃ ekādasannaṃ byasanānaṃ aññataraṃ byasanaṃ nigaccheyya. ( ) etthantare pāṭho sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu na dissati

What eleven? He does not attain the unattained; he falls away from what has been attained; the good Dhamma does not become pure for him; or he becomes conceited regarding the good Dhamma; or he practices the holy life without delight; or he commits some defiled offense; or, having rejected the training, he reverts to the lower life; or he is afflicted by a severe illness; or he attains madness or mental distraction; or he dies bewildered; or, with the breakup of the body, after death, he is reborn in a state of deprivation, an unhappy destination, a place of downfall, in hell. Monks, whatever monk is an insulter and reviler of his fellow practitioners of the holy life, and a denigrator of the noble ones, it is possible, it is the case, that he will come to one or another of these eleven kinds of ruin.

“Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu akkosako paribhāsako ariyūpavādo sabrahmacārīnaṃ, aṭṭhānametaṃ anavakāso yaṃ so ekādasannaṃ byasanānaṃ aññataraṃ byasanaṃ na nigaccheyya.

Monks, whatever monk is a reviler and a reproacher of his fellow practitioners, and an accuser of the noble ones, it is impossible, it is not a possibility, that he would not come to one or another of the eleven kinds of ruin.

Katamesaṃ [M 3.0522] ekādasannaṃ? Anadhigataṃ nādhigacchati, adhigatā parihāyati, saddhammassa na vodāyanti, saddhammesu vā adhimāniko hoti, anabhirato vā brahmacariyaṃ carati, aññataraṃ vā saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ āpattiṃ āpajjati, sikkhaṃ vā paccakkhāya hīnāyāvattati, gāḷhaṃ vā rogātaṅkaṃ phusati, ummādaṃ vā pāpuṇāti cittakkhepaṃ vā, sammūḷho kālaṃ karoti, kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ [T 5.0343] nirayaṃ upapajjati – yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu akkosako paribhāsako ariyūpavādo sabrahmacārīnaṃ, aṭṭhānametaṃ anavakāso yaṃ so imesaṃ ekādasannaṃ byasanānaṃ aññataraṃ byasanaṃ na nigaccheyyā”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

Of which eleven? He does not attain the unattained; he falls away from the attained; the good Dhamma does not become pure for him; or he becomes conceited regarding the good Dhamma; or he practices the holy life without delight; or he commits some defiled offense; or, having rejected the training, he reverts to the low life; or he is afflicted by a severe disease; or he reaches madness or distraction of mind; or he dies bewildered; or, with the breaking up of the body, after death, he is reborn in a state of loss, an unhappy destination, a place of downfall, in hell. Monks, whatever monk is a reviler and a reproacher of his fellow practitioners, and an accuser of the noble ones, it is impossible, it is not a possibility, that he would not come to one or another of these eleven kinds of ruin. Sixth.

“Siyā, ānanda, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa, na āpasmiṃ āposaññī assa, na tejasmiṃ tejosaññī assa, na vāyasmiṃ vāyosaññī assa, na ākāsānañcāyatane ākāsānañcāyatanasaññī assa, na viññāṇañcāyatane viññāṇañcāyatanasaññī assa, na ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññī [T 5.0344] assa, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññī assa, na idhaloke idhalokasaññī assa[M 3.0523], na paraloke paralokasaññī [P 5.0319] assa, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti.

“There could be, Ānanda, for a monk such an attainment of concentration that he would not be percipient of earth in the earth element, nor percipient of water in the water element, nor percipient of fire in the fire element, nor percipient of air in the air element, nor percipient of the base of the infinity of space in the base of the infinity of space, nor percipient of the base of the infinity of consciousness in the base of the infinity of consciousness, nor percipient of the base of nothingness in the base of nothingness, nor percipient of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, nor percipient of this world in this world, nor percipient of the other world in the other world; and in regard to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and pondered over by the mind, even in that he would not be percipient; and yet he would be percipient.”

“Yathā kathaṃ pana, bhante, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa, na āpasmiṃ āposaññī assa, na tejasmiṃ tejosaññī assa, na vāyasmiṃ vāyosaññī assa, na ākāsānañcāyatane ākāsānañcāyatanasaññī assa, na viññāṇañcāyatane viññāṇañcāyatanasaññī assa, na ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññī [V 3.0288] assa, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññī assa, na idhaloke idhalokasaññī assa, na paraloke paralokasaññī assa, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tatrāpi na saññī assa, saññī ca pana assāti.

“But how, venerable sir, could there be for a monk such an attainment of concentration that he would not be percipient of earth in the earth element, nor percipient of water in the water element, nor percipient of fire in the fire element, nor percipient of air in the air element, nor percipient of the base of the infinity of space in the base of the infinity of space, nor percipient of the base of the infinity of consciousness in the base of the infinity of consciousness, nor percipient of the base of nothingness in the base of nothingness, nor percipient of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, nor percipient of this world in this world, nor percipient of the other world in the other world; and in regard to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and pondered over by the mind, even in that he would not be percipient; and yet he would be percipient?”

“Idhānanda, bhikkhu evaṃsaññī hoti – ‘etaṃ santaṃ etaṃ paṇītaṃ, yadidaṃ sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbāna’nti. Evaṃ kho, ānanda, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa, na āpasmiṃ āposaññī assa, na tejasmiṃ tejosaññī assa, na vāyasmiṃ vāyosaññī assa, na ākāsānañcāyatane ākāsānañcāyatanasaññī assa, na viññāṇañcāyatane viññāṇañcāyatanasaññī [T 5.0345] assa, na ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññī assa, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññī assa, na idhaloke idhalokasaññī assa, na paraloke paralokasaññī assa, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tatrāpi na saññī assa, saññī ca pana assā”ti.

“Siyā [M 3.0524] nu kho, āvuso sāriputta, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa…pe… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tatrāpi na saññī assa, saññī pana assāti. “Siyā, āvuso ānanda, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa…pe… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tatrāpi na saññī assa, saññī ca pana assā”ti.

Acchariyaṃ, āvuso, abbhutaṃ, āvuso, yatra hi nāma satthu ceva sāvakassa ca atthena attho byañjanena byañjanaṃ saṃsandissati samessati na viggayhissati, yadidaṃ aggapadasmi”nti! Sattamaṃ.

It is wonderful, friend, it is astonishing, how the meaning and the phrasing of both the Teacher and the disciple coincide and agree without conflict, that is, in regard to the supreme state.” Seventh.

8. Manasikārasuttaṃ

8. The Discourse on Attention

8. Atha kho āyasmā ānando yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho āyasmā ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca –

8. Then the Venerable Ānanda approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, the Venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One:

“Siyā [M 3.0525] nu kho, bhante, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā na cakkhuṃ manasi kareyya, na rūpaṃ manasi kareyya, na sotaṃ manasi kareyya, na saddaṃ manasi kareyya, na [T 5.0347] ghānaṃ manasi kareyya, na gandhaṃ manasi kareyya, na jivhaṃ manasi kareyya, na rasaṃ manasi kareyya, na kāyaṃ manasi kareyya, na phoṭṭhabbaṃ manasi kareyya, na pathaviṃ manasi kareyya, na āpaṃ manasi kareyya, na tejaṃ manasi kareyya, na vāyaṃ manasi kareyya, na ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ manasi [V 3.0290] kareyya, na viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na idhalokaṃ manasi kareyya, na paralokaṃ manasi kareyya, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi na manasi kareyya; manasi ca pana kareyyā”ti?

“Venerable sir, could there be for a bhikkhu such an attainment of concentration that he would not attend to the eye, would not attend to visible forms; would not attend to the ear, would not attend to sounds; would not attend to the nose, would not attend to smells; would not attend to the tongue, would not attend to tastes; would not attend to the body, would not attend to tangible objects; would not attend to the earth element, would not attend to the water element, would not attend to the fire element, would not attend to the air element; would not attend to the base of the infinity of space; would not attend to the base of the infinity of consciousness; would not attend to the base of nothingness; would not attend to the base of neither perception nor non-perception; would not attend to this world, would not attend to the next world; and in regard to whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and pondered over by the mind, he would not attend to that either—and yet he would still attend?”

“Siyā, ānanda, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā na cakkhuṃ manasi kareyya, na rūpaṃ manasi kareyya, na sotaṃ manasi kareyya, na saddaṃ manasi kareyya, na ghānaṃ manasi kareyya, na gandhaṃ manasi kareyya, na jivhaṃ manasi kareyya, na rasaṃ manasi kareyya, na kāyaṃ manasi kareyya, na phoṭṭhabbaṃ manasi kareyya, na pathaviṃ manasi kareyya, na āpaṃ manasi kareyya, na tejaṃ manasi [P 5.0322] kareyya, na vāyaṃ manasi kareyya, na ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ manasi kareyya, na idhalokaṃ manasi kareyya, na paralokaṃ manasi kareyya, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi na manasi kareyya; manasi ca pana kareyyā”ti.

“It is possible, Ānanda, for a bhikkhu to have such an attainment of concentration that he would not attend to the eye, would not attend to visible forms; would not attend to the ear, would not attend to sounds; would not attend to the nose, would not attend to smells; would not attend to the tongue, would not attend to tastes; would not attend to the body, would not attend to tangible objects; would not attend to the earth element, would not attend to the water element, would not attend to the fire element, would not attend to the air element; would not attend to the base of the infinity of space; would not attend to the base of the infinity of consciousness; would not attend to the base of nothingness; would not attend to the base of neither perception nor non-perception; would not attend to this world, would not attend to the next world; and in regard to whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and pondered over by the mind, he would not attend to that either—and yet he would still attend.”

“Yathā kathaṃ pana, bhante, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā na cakkhuṃ manasi kareyya, na rūpaṃ manasi kareyya… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ [T 5.0348] anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi na manasi kareyya; manasi ca pana kareyyā”ti?

“But in what way, venerable sir, could there be for a bhikkhu such an attainment of concentration that he would not attend to the eye, would not attend to visible forms… and in regard to whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and pondered over by the mind, he would not attend to that either—and yet he would still attend?”

“Idhānanda, bhikkhu evaṃ manasi karoti – ‘etaṃ santaṃ etaṃ paṇītaṃ, yadidaṃ sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbāna’nti. Evaṃ kho, ānanda, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā na cakkhuṃ manasi kareyya, na rūpaṃ manasi kareyya…pe… yampidaṃ [M 3.0526] diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi na manasi kareyya; manasi ca pana kareyyā”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

Here, Ānanda, a bhikkhu attends in this way: ‘This is peaceful, this is sublime, namely, the stilling of all formations, the relinquishment of all substrates of existence, the destruction of craving, dispassion, cessation, Nibbāna.’ It is in this way, Ānanda, that there can be for a bhikkhu such an attainment of concentration that he would not attend to the eye, would not attend to visible forms… and in regard to whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and explored by the mind, he would not attend to that either—and yet he would still attend. The eighth.

9. Saddhasuttaṃ

9. The Discourse to Saddha

9. Ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā nātike viharati giñjakāvasathe[P 5.0323]. Atha kho āyasmā saddho yena [V 3.0291] bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho āyasmantaṃ saddhaṃ bhagavā etadavoca –

9. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Nātika in the brick hall. Then the Venerable Saddha approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. To the Venerable Saddha, as he was sitting to one side, the Blessed One said this:

“Ājānīyajhāyitaṃ kho, saddha, jhāya; mā khaḷuṅkajhāyitaṃ ājānīyajjhāyitaṃ kho saddha jhāyatha, mā khaḷuṅkajjhāyitaṃ (sī. pī.). Kathañca, khaḷuṅkajhāyitaṃ hoti? Assakhaḷuṅko hi[T 5.0349], saddha, doṇiyā baddho bandho (syā. ka.) ‘yavasaṃ yavasa’nti jhāyati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Na hi, saddha, assakhaḷuṅkassa doṇiyā baddhassa evaṃ hoti – ‘kiṃ nu kho maṃ ajja assadammasārathi kāraṇaṃ kāressati, kimassāhaṃ kammassāhaṃ (ka.) paṭikaromī’ti. So doṇiyā baddho ‘yavasaṃ yavasa’nti jhāyati. Evamevaṃ kho, saddha, idhekacco purisakhaḷuṅko araññagatopi rukkhamūlagatopi suññāgāragatopi kāmarāgapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati kāmarāgaparetena uppannassa ca kāmarāgassa nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. So kāmarāgaṃyeva antaraṃ katvā jhāyati pajjhāyati nijjhāyati avajjhāyati, byāpādapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati… thinamiddhapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati… uddhaccakukkuccapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati… vicikicchāpariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati vicikicchāparetena, uppannāya ca vicikicchāya nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. So vicikicchaṃyeva antaraṃ katvā jhāyati pajjhāyati nijjhāyati [P 5.0324] avajjhāyati. So pathavimpi nissāya jhāyati, āpampi nissāya jhāyati, tejampi nissāya jhāyati, vāyampi nissāya jhāyati, ākāsānañcāyatanampi nissāya jhāyati, viññāṇañcāyatanampi nissāya jhāyati, ākiñcaññāyatanampi nissāya jhāyati, nevasaññānāsaññāyatanampi nissāya jhāyati, idhalokampi nissāya jhāyati, paralokampi nissāya jhāyati, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi nissāya jhāyati. Evaṃ kho, saddha, purisakhaḷuṅkajhāyitaṃ hoti.

“Saddha, one should meditate like a thoroughbred, not like an inferior horse. And how, Saddha, is the meditation of an inferior horse? An inferior horse, Saddha, tied to the feeding trough, meditates: ‘Fodder, fodder!’ For what reason? Because, Saddha, it does not occur to the inferior horse tied to the feeding trough: ‘What task will the horse trainer have me do today? How shall I repay his service?’ So, tied to the trough, it meditates: ‘Fodder, fodder!’ In the same way, Saddha, here a certain inferior man, having gone to the forest, or to the root of a tree, or to a secluded dwelling, abides with a mind assailed and overcome by sensual desire, and he does not understand as it really is the escape from arisen sensual desire. He, having made sensual desire his inner focus, meditates, contemplates, reflects upon, and contemplates basely. He abides with a mind assailed by ill will… with a mind assailed by sloth and torpor… with a mind assailed by restlessness and remorse… with a mind assailed and overcome by doubt, and he does not understand as it really is the escape from arisen doubt. He, having made doubt his inner focus, meditates, contemplates, reflects upon, and contemplates basely. He meditates relying on earth, he meditates relying on water, he meditates relying on fire, he meditates relying on air, he meditates relying on the base of the infinity of space, he meditates relying on the base of the infinity of consciousness, he meditates relying on the base of nothingness, he meditates relying on the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, he meditates relying on this world, he meditates relying on the other world, and he meditates relying on whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and repeatedly traversed by the mind. This, Saddha, is how the meditation of an inferior man occurs.

“Kathañca[M 3.0527], saddha[T 5.0350], ājānīyajhāyitaṃ hoti? Bhadro hi, saddha, assājānīyo doṇiyā baddho na ‘yavasaṃ yavasa’nti jhāyati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Bhadrassa hi, saddha, assājānīyassa doṇiyā baddhassa evaṃ hoti – ‘kiṃ nu kho maṃ ajja assadammasārathi kāraṇaṃ kāressati, kimassāhaṃ paṭikaromī’ti. So doṇiyā baddho na ‘yavasaṃ yavasa’nti jhāyati. Bhadro hi, saddha, assājānīyo yathā iṇaṃ yathā bandhaṃ yathā jāniṃ yathā kaliṃ evaṃ patodassa ajjhoharaṇaṃ samanupassati. Evamevaṃ kho, saddha, bhadro purisājānīyo araññagatopi rukkhamūlagatopi suññāgāragatopi na kāmarāgapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati na kāmarāgaparetena, uppannassa ca kāmarāgassa [V 3.0292] nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti, na byāpādapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati… na thinamiddhapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati… na uddhaccakukkuccapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati… na vicikicchāpariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati na vicikicchāparetena, uppannāya ca vicikicchāya nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. So neva pathaviṃ nissāya jhāyati, na āpaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na tejaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na vāyaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na idhalokaṃ [P 5.0325] nissāya jhāyati, na paralokaṃ nissāya jhāyati, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi nissāya na jhāyati; jhāyati ca pana. Evaṃ [T 5.0351] jhāyiñca pana, saddha, bhadraṃ purisājānīyaṃ saindā devā sabrahmakā sapajāpatikā ārakāva namassanti –

“And how, Saddha, is the meditation of a thoroughbred? A fine thoroughbred horse, Saddha, tied to the feeding trough, does not meditate: ‘Fodder, fodder!’ For what reason? Because, Saddha, it occurs to the fine thoroughbred horse tied to the feeding trough: ‘What task will the horse trainer have me do today? How shall I repay his service?’ So, tied to the trough, it does not meditate: ‘Fodder, fodder!’ Indeed, Saddha, a fine thoroughbred horse regards the application of the goad as being like a debt, like a bond, like a loss, like a demerit. In the same way, Saddha, a fine thoroughbred man, having gone to the forest, or to the root of a tree, or to a secluded dwelling, does not abide with a mind assailed or overcome by sensual desire, and he understands as it really is the escape from arisen sensual desire. He does not abide with a mind assailed by ill will… not with a mind assailed by sloth and torpor… not with a mind assailed by restlessness and remorse… not with a mind assailed or overcome by doubt, and he understands as it really is the escape from arisen doubt. He does not meditate relying on earth, nor relying on water, nor relying on fire, nor relying on air, nor relying on the base of the infinity of space, nor relying on the base of the infinity of consciousness, nor relying on the base of nothingness, nor relying on the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, nor relying on this world, nor relying on the other world, nor does he meditate relying on whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and repeatedly traversed by the mind; and yet, he does meditate. And, Saddha, to such a fine thoroughbred man who meditates in this way, the devas with Inda, with Brahmā, and with Pajāpati pay homage from afar, saying:

“Namo te purisājañña, namo te purisuttama;

Yassa te nābhijānāma, yampi nissāya jhāyasī”ti.

‘Homage to you, O thoroughbred of men! Homage to you, O supreme among men! We do not know that, relying upon which you meditate.’”

Evaṃ vutte āyasmā saddho bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – “kathaṃ jhāyī pana, bhante, bhadro purisājānīyo purisājānīyo jhāyati, so (sī. syā. pī.), purisājānīyo, so (ka.) neva pathaviṃ nissāya jhāyati, na āpaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na tejaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na vāyaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ nissāya jhāyati, na idhalokaṃ nissāya jhāyati[M 3.0528], na paralokaṃ nissāya jhāyati, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi nissāya na jhāyati; jhāyati ca pana? Kathaṃ jhāyiñca pana, bhante, bhadraṃ purisājānīyaṃ saindā devā sabrahmakā sapajāpatikā ārakāva namassanti –

When this was said, the Venerable Saddha said to the Blessed One: “But how, venerable sir, does a fine thoroughbred man meditate without relying on earth, nor relying on water, nor relying on fire, nor relying on air, nor relying on the base of the infinity of space, nor relying on the base of the infinity of consciousness, nor relying on the base of nothingness, nor relying on the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, nor relying on this world, nor relying on the other world, nor relying on whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and repeatedly traversed by the mind—and yet, he does meditate? And how is it, venerable sir, that the devas with Inda, with Brahmā, and with Pajāpati pay homage from afar to such a fine thoroughbred man who meditates, saying:

“Namo te purisājañña, namo te purisuttama;

Yassa te nābhijānāma, yampi nissāya jhāyasī”ti.

‘Homage to you, O thoroughbred of men! Homage to you, O supreme among men! We do not know that on which you rely to meditate.’”

“Idha, saddha, bhadrassa purisājānīyassa pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññā vibhūtā hoti, āpasmiṃ āposaññā [V 3.0293] vibhūtā hoti, tejasmiṃ tejosaññā vibhūtā hoti, vāyasmiṃ vāyosaññā vibhūtā hoti, ākāsānañcāyatane ākāsānañcāyatanasaññā [T 5.0352] vibhūtā hoti, viññāṇañcāyatane viññāṇañcāyatanasaññā vibhūtā [P 5.0326] hoti, ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññā vibhūtā hoti, nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññā vibhūtā hoti, idhaloke idhalokasaññā vibhūtā hoti, paraloke paralokasaññā vibhūtā hoti, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tatrāpi saññā vibhūtā hoti. Evaṃ jhāyī kho, saddha, bhadro purisājānīyo neva pathaviṃ nissāya jhāyati…pe… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā, tampi nissāya na jhāyati; jhāyati ca pana. Evaṃ jhāyiñca pana, saddha, bhadraṃ purisājānīyaṃ saindā devā sabrahmakā sapajāpatikā ārakāva namassanti –

“Here, Saddha, for the fine thoroughbred man, the perception of earth is manifest in the earth element; the perception of water is manifest in the water element; the perception of fire is manifest in the fire element; the perception of air is manifest in the air element; the perception of the base of infinite space is manifest in the base of infinite space; the perception of the base of infinite consciousness is manifest in the base of infinite consciousness; the perception of the base of nothingness is manifest in the base of nothingness; the perception of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception is manifest in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; the perception of this world is manifest in this world; the perception of the other world is manifest in the other world; and in whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, or explored by the mind, perception is manifest even there. Meditating thus, Saddha, the fine thoroughbred man does not meditate relying on earth… nor does he meditate relying on whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, or explored by the mind; and yet he does meditate. And to such a fine thoroughbred man, Saddha, who meditates thus, the devas with Inda, Brahmā, and Pajāpati pay homage from afar:

“Namo te purisājañña, namo te purisuttama;

Yassa te nābhijānāma, yampi nissāya jhāyasī”ti. navamaṃ;

‘Homage to you, O thoroughbred of men! Homage to you, O supreme man! We do not know that on which you rely to meditate.’ The ninth sutta.

10. Moranivāpasuttaṃ

10. 10. The Discourse at the Peacock Feeding Ground

10. Ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati moranivāpe paribbājakārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – “bhikkhavo”ti. “Bhadante”ti [T 5.0353] te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

10. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling in Rājagaha, at the Peacock Feeding Ground, in the wanderers’ park. There the Blessed One addressed the monks: “Monks.” “Venerable sir,” those monks replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Tīhi, bhikkhave, dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ. Katamehi tīhi? Asekhena sīlakkhandhena, asekhena samādhikkhandhena[M 3.0529], asekhena paññākkhandhena – imehi, kho, bhikkhave, tīhi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ.

“Monks, a monk endowed with three qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans. With which three? With the aggregate of virtue of one beyond training, with the aggregate of concentration of one beyond training, and with the aggregate of wisdom of one beyond training. Monks, a monk endowed with these three qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans.

“Aparehipi, bhikkhave, tīhi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ. Katamehi tīhi[V 3.0294]? Iddhipāṭihāriyena, ādesanāpāṭihāriyena, anusāsanīpāṭihāriyena – imehi kho, bhikkhave, tīhi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho [P 5.0327] hoti, accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ.

“Furthermore, monks, a monk endowed with three other qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans. With which three? With the miracle of psychic power, the miracle of mind-reading, and the miracle of instruction. Monks, a monk endowed with these three qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans.

“Aparehipi, bhikkhave, tīhi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ. Katamehi tīhi? Sammādiṭṭhiyā, sammāñāṇena, sammāvimuttiyā – imehi kho, bhikkhave, tīhi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ.

“Furthermore, monks, a monk endowed with three other qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans. With which three? With right view, right knowledge, and right liberation. Monks, a monk endowed with these three qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans.

“Dvīhi[T 5.0354], bhikkhave, dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ. Katamehi dvīhi? Vijjāya, caraṇena – imehi kho, bhikkhave, dvīhi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu accantaniṭṭho hoti accantayogakkhemī accantabrahmacārī accantapariyosāno seṭṭho devamanussānaṃ. Brahmunā pesā, bhikkhave, sanaṅkumārena gāthā bhāsitā –

“Monks, a monk endowed with two qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans. With which two? With knowledge and conduct. Monks, a monk endowed with these two qualities is one who has reached the ultimate conclusion, attained ultimate security from the yokes, lives the ultimate holy life, has reached the ultimate end, and is the foremost among devas and humans. Monks, this verse was also spoken by the Brahmā Sanaṅkumāra:

“Khattiyo seṭṭho janetasmiṃ, ye gottapaṭisārino;

Vijjācaraṇasampanno, so seṭṭho devamānuse”ti dī. ni. 1.277; saṃ. ni. 1.182; 2.245.

“The Khattiya is foremost among the populace who are mindful of lineage; but one endowed with knowledge and conduct is foremost among devas and humans.”

“Sā kho panesā, bhikkhave, sanaṅkumārena gāthā bhāsitā subhāsitā, no dubbhāsitā; atthasaṃhitā[P 5.0328], no anatthasaṃhitā; anumatā mayā. Ahampi, bhikkhave, evaṃ vadāmi –

“Indeed, monks, this verse spoken by the Brahmā Sanaṅkumāra is well-spoken, not ill-spoken; it is connected with benefit, not with non-benefit; it is approved by me. I too, monks, say this:

“Khattiyo [M 3.0530] seṭṭho janetasmiṃ, ye gottapaṭisārino;

Vijjācaraṇasampanno, so seṭṭho devamānuse”ti. dasamaṃ;

“The Khattiya is foremost among the populace who are mindful of lineage; but one endowed with knowledge and conduct is foremost among devas and humans.” The tenth sutta.

11. Ekaṃ [T 5.0355][V 3.0296] samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – “niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī”ti. Assosi kho mahānāmo sakko – “sambahulā kira bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – ‘niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī’”ti.

11. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling among the Sakyans at Kapilavatthu in the Banyan Park. At that time, many bhikkhus were making robes for the Blessed One, thinking: “When his robes are finished, after the passing of three months, the Blessed One will set out on a journey.” Mahānāma the Sakyan heard: “It is said that many bhikkhus are making robes for the Blessed One, thinking: ‘When his robes are finished, after the passing of three months, the Blessed One will set out on a journey.’”

Atha kho mahānāmo sakko yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho mahānāmo sakko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – “sutaṃ metaṃ, bhante – ‘sambahulā kira bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī’ti. Tesaṃ no, bhante, nānāvihārehi viharataṃ kenassa kena (syā. kaṃ.) vihārena vihātabba”nti?

Then Mahānāma the Sakyan went to the Blessed One. Having gone to him, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Mahānāma the Sakyan said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, this has been heard by me: ‘It is said that many bhikkhus are making robes for the Blessed One, thinking: “When his robes are finished, after the passing of three months, the Blessed One will set out on a journey.”’ For us, venerable sir, who dwell with various dwellings, with which dwelling should we dwell?”

“Sādhu [P 5.0329] sādhu, mahānāma! Etaṃ kho, mahānāma, tumhākaṃ patirūpaṃ kulaputtānaṃ, yaṃ tumhe tathāgataṃ upasaṅkamitvā puccheyyātha – ‘tesaṃ no, bhante, nānāvihārehi viharataṃ kenassa vihārena vihātabba’”nti? Saddho kho, mahānāma, ārādhako hoti, no assaddho; āraddhavīriyo ārādhako hoti, no kusīto; upaṭṭhitassati ārādhako hoti[M 3.0531], no muṭṭhassati; samāhito ārādhako hoti, no asamāhito; paññavā ārādhako hoti, no duppañño. Imesu kho tvaṃ, mahānāma, pañcasu dhammesu patiṭṭhāya cha dhamme uttari uttariṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) bhāveyyāsi. a. ni. 6.10 “Idha tvaṃ, mahānāma, tathāgataṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘itipi so bhagavā [T 5.0356] arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako tathāgataṃ anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ [V 3.0297] samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti tathāgataṃ ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Ayaṃ vuccati, mahānāma, ariyasāvako visamagatāya pajāya samappatto viharati, sabyāpajjāya pajāya abyāpajjo viharati, dhammasotasamāpanno buddhānussatiṃ bhāveti.

“Good, good, Mahānāma! It is fitting for you, Mahānāma, a clansman, that you should approach the Tathāgata and ask: ‘For us, venerable sir, who dwell with various dwellings, with which dwelling should we dwell?’ A faithful one, Mahānāma, succeeds, not a faithless one; an energetic one succeeds, not a lazy one; one with established mindfulness succeeds, not one with forgetful mindfulness; a concentrated one succeeds, not an unconcentrated one; a wise one succeeds, not an unwise one. Established in these five qualities, Mahānāma, you should further develop six qualities. Here, Mahānāma, you should recollect the Tathāgata thus: ‘That Blessed One is an Arahant, a Perfectly Enlightened One, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, a Sugata, a knower of the world, an unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed, a teacher of gods and humans, a Buddha, a Blessed One.’ At whatever time, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects the Tathāgata, at that time his mind is not assailed by lust, not assailed by hatred, not assailed by delusion. At that time, his mind becomes upright, with the Tathāgata as its object. With an upright mind, Mahānāma, a noble disciple gains knowledge of the meaning, gains knowledge of the Dhamma, gains joy connected with the Dhamma. For one who is joyful, rapture arises. For one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil. One with a tranquil body experiences happiness. The mind of a happy one becomes concentrated. This, Mahānāma, is called a noble disciple who dwells evenly among an uneven populace, who dwells without affliction among an afflicted populace, who has entered the stream of the Dhamma and develops the recollection of the Buddha.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, mahānāma, dhammaṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opaneyyiko opanayiko (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) paccattaṃ veditabbo viññūhī’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako dhammaṃ anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ [P 5.0330] hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti dhammaṃ ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Ayaṃ [T 5.0357] vuccati, mahānāma, ariyasāvako visamagatāya pajāya samappatto viharati, sabyāpajjāya pajāya abyāpajjo viharati, dhammasotasamāpanno dhammānussatiṃ bhāveti.

With an upright mind, Mahānāma, a noble disciple gains knowledge of the meaning, gains knowledge of the Dhamma, gains joy connected with the Dhamma. For one who is joyful, rapture arises. For one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil. One with a tranquil body experiences happiness. The mind of a happy one becomes concentrated. This, Mahānāma, is called a noble disciple who dwells evenly among an uneven populace, who dwells without affliction among an afflicted populace, who has entered the stream of the Dhamma and develops the recollection of the Dhamma.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, mahānāma, saṅghaṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ujuppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ñāyappaṭipanno bhagavato [M 3.0532] sāvakasaṅgho, sāmīcippaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, yadidaṃ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā, esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassā’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako saṅghaṃ anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti saṅghaṃ ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. At that time, his mind becomes upright, with the Sangha as its object. With an upright mind, Mahānāma, a noble disciple gains knowledge of the meaning, gains knowledge of the Dhamma, gains joy connected with the Dhamma. For one who is joyful, rapture arises. For one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil. One with a tranquil body experiences happiness. The mind of a happy one becomes concentrated. This, Mahānāma, is called a noble disciple who dwells evenly among an uneven populace, who dwells without affliction among an afflicted populace, who has entered the stream of the Dhamma and develops the recollection of the Sangha.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, mahānāma, attano sīlāni anussareyyāsi akhaṇḍāni acchiddāni asabalāni akammāsāni bhujissāni viññuppasatthāni aparāmaṭṭhāni samādhisaṃvattanikāni. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako sīlaṃ anussarati, nevassa [T 5.0358] tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ [P 5.0331] cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti sīlaṃ ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Ayaṃ vuccati, mahānāma, ariyasāvako visamagatāya pajāya samappatto viharati, sabyāpajjāya pajāya abyāpajjo viharati, dhammasotasamāpanno sīlānussatiṃ bhāveti.

“Furthermore, Mahānāma, you should recollect your own virtues as unbroken, unimpaired, unspotted, unblemished, liberating, praised by the wise, not clung to, and conducive to concentration. When, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects virtue, at that time his mind is not assailed by lust, not assailed by hatred, not assailed by delusion; at that time, his mind is quite upright, with virtue as its object. With an upright mind, Mahānāma, the noble disciple gains a sense of the meaning, gains a sense of the Dhamma, gains joy connected with the Dhamma. When he is joyful, rapture arises; for one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil; with a tranquil body, he feels happiness; for one who is happy, the mind becomes concentrated. “Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, mahānāma, attano cāgaṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘lābhā vata me, suladdhaṃ vata me, yohaṃ maccheramalapariyuṭṭhitāya pajāya vigatamalamaccherena cetasā agāraṃ ajjhāvasāmi muttacāgo payatapāṇi vossaggarato yācayogo dānasaṃvibhāgarato’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma[M 3.0533], samaye ariyasāvako cāgaṃ anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti cāgaṃ ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Ayaṃ vuccati, mahānāma, ariyasāvako visamagatāya pajāya samappatto viharati, sabyāpajjāya pajāya abyāpajjo viharati, dhammasotasamāpanno cāgānussatiṃ [T 5.0359] bhāveti.

With an upright mind, Mahānāma, the noble disciple gains a sense of the meaning, gains a sense of the Dhamma, gains joy connected with the Dhamma. This, Mahānāma, is called a noble disciple who dwells evenly among an uneven populace, free from affliction among an afflicted populace, who has entered the stream of the Dhamma and develops the recollection of generosity.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, mahānāma, devatā anussareyyāsi – ‘santi devā cātumahārājikā, santi devā tāvatiṃsā, santi devā yāmā, santi devā tusitā, santi devā nimmānaratino, santi devā paranimmitavasavattino, santi devā brahmakāyikā, santi devā tatuttari. Yathārūpāya paññāya samannāgatā tā devatā ito cutā tatthūpapannā, mayhampi tathārūpā paññā saṃvijjatī’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako attano ca tāsañca devatānaṃ saddhañca sīlañca sutañca cāgañca paññañca anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti devatā ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Ayaṃ [T 5.0360] vuccati, mahānāma, ariyasāvako visamagatāya pajāya samappatto [M 3.0534] viharati, sabyāpajjāya pajāya abyāpajjo viharati, dhammasotasamāpanno devatānussatiṃ bhāvetī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

“Furthermore, Mahānāma, you should recollect the devas: ‘There are the devas of the Four Great Kings, the devas of the Thirty-Three, the devas of Yāma, the devas of Tusita, the devas who delight in their own creations, the devas who control the creations of others, the devas of Brahmā’s retinue, and the devas who are still higher. 12. Ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Tena kho pana samayena mahānāmo sakko gilānā vuṭṭhito hoti aciravuṭṭhito gelaññā. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – “niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī”ti.

Now at that time, Mahānāma the Sakyan had risen from an illness, not long after having recovered from his sickness. And at that time, many monks were doing robe-work for the Blessed One, thinking, ‘When his robe is finished, the Blessed One will set out on a journey after three months have passed.’”

Assosi kho mahānāmo sakko – “sambahulā kira bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – ‘niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī’”ti. “Sādhu sādhu, mahānāma! Etaṃ kho, mahānāma, tumhākaṃ patirūpaṃ kulaputtānaṃ yaṃ tumhe tathāgataṃ upasaṅkamitvā puccheyyātha – ‘tesaṃ no, bhante, nānāvihārehi viharataṃ kenassa vihārena vihātabba’nti? Imesu kho tvaṃ, mahānāma, pañcasu dhammesu patiṭṭhāya cha dhamme uttari bhāveyyāsi.

“Excellent, excellent, Mahānāma! It is indeed fitting for you, sons of good families, that you should approach the Tathāgata and ask: ‘Venerable sir, for us who dwell in various ways, by which way of dwelling should we dwell?’ A person with faith, Mahānāma, is one who accomplishes, not one without faith; a person with aroused energy is one who accomplishes, not a lazy one; a person with established mindfulness is one who accomplishes, not one with lapsed mindfulness; a person with a concentrated mind is one who accomplishes, not one with an unconcentrated mind; a wise person is one who accomplishes, not an unwise one. Being established in these five qualities, Mahānāma, you should further develop six qualities.”

a. ni. 6.9 “Idha tvaṃ, mahānāma, tathāgataṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘itipi so bhagavā…pe… satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako tathāgataṃ anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti tathāgataṃ [M 3.0535] ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Imaṃ kho tvaṃ, mahānāma, buddhānussatiṃ gacchantopi bhāveyyāsi, ṭhitopi bhāveyyāsi, nisinnopi bhāveyyāsi, sayānopi bhāveyyāsi, kammantaṃ adhiṭṭhahantopi bhāveyyāsi, puttasambādhasayanaṃ ajjhāvasantopi bhāveyyāsi.

“Here, Mahānāma, you should recollect the Tathāgata thus: ‘Indeed, the Blessed One is… the Teacher of gods and humans, the Buddha, the Blessed One.’ When, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects the Tathāgata, at that time their mind is not overcome by lust, not overcome by hatred, and not overcome by delusion. At that time, with the Tathāgata as its object, their mind becomes upright. With an upright mind, Mahānāma, a noble disciple gains inspiration in the meaning, gains inspiration in the Dhamma, and gains the joy connected with the Dhamma. For one who is joyful, rapture arises. For one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil. With a tranquil body, one experiences happiness. For one who is happy, the mind becomes concentrated. This recollection of the Buddha, Mahānāma, you should develop while walking, while standing, while sitting, while lying down, while undertaking work, and while dwelling in a home crowded with family.”

“Puna [P 5.0334] caparaṃ tvaṃ, mahānāma, dhammaṃ anussareyyāsi…pe… saṅghaṃ anussareyyāsi…pe… attano sīlāni anussareyyāsi…pe… attano cāgaṃ anussareyyāsi…pe… devatā anussareyyāsi – ‘santi devā cātumahārājikā…pe… santi devā tatuttari. Yathārūpāya saddhāya samannāgatā tā devatā ito cutā tatthūpapannā, mayhampi tathārūpā saddhā saṃvijjati. Yathārūpena sīlena… sutena… cāgena… paññāya samannāgatā tā devatā ito cutā tatthūpapannā[T 5.0362], mayhampi tathārūpā paññā saṃvijjatī’ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako attano ca tāsañca devatānaṃ saddhañca sīlañca sutañca [V 3.0301] cāgañca paññañca anussarati, nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti devatā ārabbha. Ujugatacitto kho pana, mahānāma, ariyasāvako labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati. Imaṃ kho tvaṃ, mahānāma, devatānussatiṃ gacchantopi bhāveyyāsi, ṭhitopi bhāveyyāsi, nisinnopi bhāveyyāsi, sayānopi bhāveyyāsi, kammantaṃ adhiṭṭhahantopi bhāveyyāsi, puttasambādhasayanaṃ ajjhāvasantopi bhāveyyāsī”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

“Furthermore, Mahānāma, you should recollect the Dhamma… you should recollect the Saṅgha… you should recollect your own virtue… you should recollect your own generosity… you should recollect the deities thus: ‘There are the devas of the Four Great Kings… there are devas beyond them. Endowed with such faith, those devas passed away from here and were reborn there; such faith also exists in me. Endowed with such virtue… learning… generosity… wisdom, those devas passed away from here and were reborn there; such wisdom also exists in me.’ When, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects their own and those deities’ faith, virtue, learning, generosity, and wisdom, at that time their mind is not overcome by lust, not overcome by hatred, and not overcome by delusion. At that time, with the deities as its object, their mind becomes upright. With an upright mind, Mahānāma, a noble disciple gains inspiration in the meaning, gains inspiration in the Dhamma, and gains the joy connected with the Dhamma. For one who is joyful, rapture arises. For one with a rapturous mind, the body becomes tranquil. With a tranquil body, one experiences happiness. For one who is happy, the mind becomes concentrated. This recollection of the deities, Mahānāma, you should develop while walking, while standing, while sitting, while lying down, while undertaking work, and while dwelling in a home crowded with family.” Second.

3. Nandiyasuttaṃ

3. The Nandiya Sutta

13. Ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sakkesu viharati kapilavatthusmiṃ nigrodhārāme. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ vassāvāsaṃ upagantukāmo hoti ahosi (ka.).

13. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling among the Sakyans at Kapilavatthu in the Banyan Grove Monastery. At that time, the Blessed One was intending to enter the rains residence at Sāvatthī.

Assosi [M 3.0536] kho nandiyo sakko – “bhagavā kira sāvatthiyaṃ vassāvāsaṃ upagantukāmo”ti. Atha kho nandiyassa sakkassa [P 5.0335] etadahosi – “yaṃnūnāhampi sāvatthiyaṃ vassāvāsaṃ upagaccheyyaṃ. Tattha kammantañceva adhiṭṭhahissāmi, bhagavantañca lacchāmi kālena kālaṃ dassanāyā”ti.

Nandiya the Sakyan heard: “The Blessed One, it is said, intends to enter the rains residence at Sāvatthī.” Then this thought occurred to Nandiya the Sakyan: “It would be good if I too were to enter the rains residence at Sāvatthī. There I shall both manage my work and get to see the Blessed One from time to time.”

Atha kho bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ vassāvāsaṃ upagacchi upagañchi (sī. pī.). Nandiyopi kho sakko sāvatthiyaṃ vassāvāsaṃ upagacchi. Tattha kammantañceva adhiṭṭhāsi [T 5.0363] adhiṭṭhāya (syā.), adhiṭṭhāti (ka.), bhagavantañca labhi lacchati (syā. ka.) kālena kālaṃ dassanāya. Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – “niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī”ti.

Then the Blessed One entered the rains residence at Sāvatthī. Nandiya the Sakyan also entered the rains residence at Sāvatthī. There he both managed his work and was able to see the Blessed One from time to time. At that time, many bhikkhus were making robes for the Blessed One, thinking: “When the Blessed One’s robe-work is finished, after the passing of three months, he will set out on his travels.”

Assosi kho nandiyo sakko – “sambahulā kira bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – ‘niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī’”ti. Atha kho nandiyo sakko yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho nandiyo sakko bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – “sutaṃ metaṃ, bhante [V 3.0302] – ‘sambahulā kira bhikkhū bhagavato cīvarakammaṃ karonti – niṭṭhitacīvaro bhagavā temāsaccayena cārikaṃ pakkamissatī’ti. Tesaṃ no, bhante, nānāvihārehi viharataṃ kenassa vihārena vihātabba”nti?

Nandiya the Sakyan heard: “Many bhikkhus, it is said, are making robes for the Blessed One, thinking, ‘When the Blessed One’s robe-work is finished, after the passing of three months, he will set out on his travels.’” Then Nandiya the Sakyan approached the Blessed One, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Nandiya the Sakyan said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, I have heard this: ‘Many bhikkhus are making robes for the Blessed One, thinking, “When the Blessed One’s robe-work is finished, after the passing of three months, he will set out on his travels.”’ For us, venerable sir, who dwell in various ways, by which way of dwelling should we dwell?”

“Sādhu sādhu, nandiya! Etaṃ kho, nandiya, tumhākaṃ patirūpaṃ kulaputtānaṃ, yaṃ tumhe tathāgataṃ upasaṅkamitvā puccheyyātha – ‘tesaṃ no, bhante, nānāvihārehi viharataṃ kenassa vihārena vihātabba’nti? Saddho kho, nandiya, ārādhako hoti, no assaddho; sīlavā ārādhako hoti, no dussīlo; āraddhavīriyo ārādhako hoti, no kusīto; upaṭṭhitassati ārādhako hoti, no muṭṭhassati; samāhito ārādhako hoti, no asamāhito; paññavā ārādhako hoti, no duppañño. Imesu kho te, nandiya, chasu dhammesu patiṭṭhāya pañcasu dhammesu ajjhattaṃ sati upaṭṭhāpetabbā.

“Good, good, Nandiya! It is indeed fitting for you, Nandiya, as sons of good family, to approach the Tathāgata and ask: ‘Venerable sir, for us who dwell in various ways, by which dwelling should we dwell?’ Nandiya, one who has faith is successful, not one who is faithless; one who is virtuous is successful, not one who is immoral; one who has aroused energy is successful, not one who is lazy; one who has established mindfulness is successful, not one who is of lapsed mindfulness; one who is concentrated is successful, not one who is unconcentrated; one who is wise is successful, not one who is unwise. Having established yourself in these six qualities, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally in five things.”

“Idha tvaṃ, nandiya, tathāgataṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘itipi [P 5.0336] so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū [T 5.0364] anuttaro purisadammasārathi[M 3.0537], satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā’ti. Iti kho te, nandiya, tathāgataṃ ārabbha ajjhattaṃ sati upaṭṭhāpetabbā.

“Here, Nandiya, you should recollect the Tathāgata thus: ‘The Blessed One is an Arahant, a perfectly self-enlightened one, endowed with knowledge and conduct, a well-gone one, a knower of the world, an unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed, a teacher of gods and humans, an enlightened one, a blessed one.’ Thus, Nandiya, you should establish mindfulness internally with the Tathāgata as the object.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, nandiya, dhammaṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘svākkhāto bhagavatā dhammo sandiṭṭhiko akāliko ehipassiko opaneyyiko paccattaṃ veditabbo viññūhī’ti. Iti kho te, nandiya, dhammaṃ ārabbha ajjhattaṃ sati upaṭṭhāpetabbā.

“Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect the Dhamma: ‘The Dhamma is well-proclaimed by the Blessed One, directly visible, immediate, inviting one to come and see, leading onward, to be personally understood by the wise.’ Thus, Nandiya, mindfulness is to be established internally, with the Dhamma as its object.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, nandiya, kalyāṇamitte anussareyyāsi – ‘lābhā vata me, suladdhaṃ vata me, yassa me kalyāṇamittā anukampakā atthakāmā ovādakā anusāsakā’ti. Iti kho te, nandiya, kalyāṇamitte ārabbha ajjhattaṃ sati upaṭṭhāpetabbā.

“Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect your good friends: ‘It is truly a gain for me, it is truly well-gained by me, that I have good friends who are compassionate, who desire my welfare, who give admonition, and who give instruction.’ Thus, Nandiya, mindfulness is to be established internally, with good friends as its object.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, nandiya, attano cāgaṃ anussareyyāsi – ‘lābhā vata me, suladdhaṃ vata me, yohaṃ maccheramalapariyuṭṭhitāya pajāya vigatamalamaccherena cetasā agāraṃ ajjhāvasāmi muttacāgo [V 3.0303] payatapāṇi vossaggarato yācayogo dānasaṃvibhāgarato’ti. Iti kho te, nandiya, cāgaṃ ārabbha ajjhattaṃ sati upaṭṭhāpetabbā.

“Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect your own generosity: ‘It is truly a gain for me, it is truly well-gained by me, that I, among a populace beset by the stain of stinginess, dwell at home with a mind free from the stain of stinginess, freely generous, open-handed, delighting in relinquishment, fit to be asked, delighting in giving and sharing.’ Thus, Nandiya, mindfulness is to be established internally, with generosity as its object.”

“Puna caparaṃ tvaṃ, nandiya, devatā anussareyyāsi – ‘yā devatā atikkammeva kabaḷīkārāhārabhakkhānaṃ kabaḷiṃkārabhakkhānaṃ (sī.), kabaḷīkārabhakkhānaṃ (syā. kaṃ. pī.) devatānaṃ sahabyataṃ aññataraṃ manomayaṃ kāyaṃ upapannā, tā karaṇīyaṃ attano na samanupassanti katassa vā paticayaṃ. Seyyathāpi, nandiya, bhikkhu asamayavimutto karaṇīyaṃ attano na samanupassati katassa vā paticayaṃ; evamevaṃ kho, nandiya, yā tā devatā [T 5.0365] atikkammeva kabaḷīkārāhārabhakkhānaṃ devatānaṃ sahabyataṃ aññataraṃ manomayaṃ kāyaṃ upapannā, tā karaṇīyaṃ [P 5.0337] attano na samanupassanti katassa vā paticayaṃ. Iti kho te, nandiya, devatā ārabbha ajjhattaṃ sati upaṭṭhāpetabbā.

“Furthermore, Nandiya, you should recollect the deities: ‘There are deities who, having transcended the company of deities who consume physical food, have been reborn in a certain mind-made body. They do not perceive any duty for themselves, or any accumulation from what has been done. Just as, Nandiya, a timelessly liberated monk does not perceive any duty for himself, or any accumulation from what has been done; even so, Nandiya, those deities who, having transcended the company of deities who consume physical food, have been reborn in a certain mind-made body, do not perceive any duty for themselves, or any accumulation from what has been done.’ Thus, Nandiya, mindfulness is to be established internally, with the deities as its object.”

“Imehi kho, nandiya, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato ariyasāvako pajahateva pāpake akusale dhamme, na upādiyati. Seyyathāpi, nandiya, kumbho nikkujjo nikujjo (ka.) vamateva udakaṃ, no vantaṃ paccāvamati paccāmasati (syā.); seyyathāpi vā pana, nandiya, sukkhe tiṇadāye aggi mutto ḍahaññeva gacchati, no daḍḍhaṃ paccudāvattati[M 3.0538]; evamevaṃ kho, nandiya, imehi ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato ariyasāvako pajahateva pāpake akusale dhamme, na upādiyatī”ti. Tatiyaṃ.

“Nandiya, a noble disciple who is endowed with these eleven qualities abandons evil, unwholesome states and does not cling to them. Just as, Nandiya, an overturned pot vomits out water and does not take back what has been vomited; or just as a fire released in a dry grass-wood goes along burning and does not return to what has been burnt; even so, Nandiya, a noble disciple who is endowed with these eleven qualities abandons evil, unwholesome states and does not cling to them.” The third.

4. Subhūtisuttaṃ

4. The Discourse to Subhūti

14. Atha kho āyasmā subhūti saddhena bhikkhunā saddhiṃ yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinnaṃ kho āyasmantaṃ subhūtiṃ bhagavā etadavoca – “ko nāmāyaṃ ko nāmo ayaṃ (sī. ka.), ko nāma ayaṃ (syā. kaṃ.), subhūti, bhikkhū”ti? “Saddho nāmāyaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu, sudattassa saddhassa (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) upāsakassa putto, saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito”ti.

14. Then the Venerable Subhūti, together with the bhikkhu Saddha, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. To the Venerable Subhūti, who was sitting to one side, the Blessed One said this: 'What is the name of this bhikkhu, Subhūti?' 'Venerable sir, this bhikkhu is named Saddha, the son of the lay follower Sudatta. Out of faith, he has gone forth from the household life into homelessness.'

“Kacci panāyaṃ, subhūti, saddho bhikkhu sudattassa upāsakassa putto saddhā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajito sandissati saddhāpadānesū”ti? “Etassa, bhagavā, kālo; etassa, sugata, kālo, yaṃ bhagavā saddhassa saddhāpadānāni bhāseyya. Idānāhaṃ jānissāmi yadi vā ayaṃ bhikkhu sandissati saddhāpadānesu yadi vā no”ti.

“But, Subhūti, is this bhikkhu Saddha, the son of the lay follower Sudatta, who has gone forth from the household life into homelessness out of faith, seen among the exemplars of faith?” “This is the time, Blessed One; this is the time, Sugata, for the Blessed One to speak on the exemplars of faith of Saddha. Now I shall know whether this bhikkhu is seen among the exemplars of faith or not.”

“Tena [V 3.0304] hi, subhūti, suṇāhi, sādhukaṃ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī”ti. “Evaṃ, bhante”ti kho āyasmā subhūti bhagavato paccassosi[T 5.0366]. Bhagavā etadavoca –

“Then, Subhūti, listen and pay close attention; I will speak.” “Yes, venerable sir,” the Venerable Subhūti replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Idha[P 5.0338], subhūti, bhikkhu sīlavā hoti, pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto viharati ācāragocarasampanno aṇumattesu vajjesu bhayadassāvī, samādāya sikkhati sikkhāpadesu. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu sīlavā hoti…pe… samādāya sikkhati sikkhāpadesu, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

“Here, Subhūti, a bhikkhu is virtuous; he dwells restrained by the Pātimokkha, perfect in conduct and resort, seeing danger in the slightest faults, undertaking and training in the rules of training. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, is virtuous... undertaking and training in the rules of training, this too, Subhūti, is an exemplar of one with faith.”

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu bahussuto hoti sutadharo sutasannicayo; ye te dhammā ādikalyāṇā majjhekalyāṇā pariyosānakalyāṇā sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ abhivadanti, tathārūpāssa dhammā bahussutā honti dhātā vacasā paricitā manasānupekkhitā diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu bahussuto [M 3.0539] hoti…pe… diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu is learned, remembers what he has heard, and has accumulated what he has heard. Those teachings that are good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with the right meaning and phrasing, which proclaim the holy life that is utterly complete and pure—such teachings have been much heard by him, are retained, are familiar in speech, are examined by the mind, and are well penetrated by wisdom. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, is learned... and well penetrated by wisdom, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu kalyāṇamitto hoti kalyāṇasahāyo kalyāṇasampavaṅko. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu kalyāṇamitto hoti kalyāṇasahāyo kalyāṇasampavaṅko, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu has good friends, good companions, and inclines towards good friends. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, has good friends, good companions, and inclines towards good friends, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu suvaco hoti sovacassakaraṇehi dhammehi samannāgato khamo padakkhiṇaggāhī anusāsaniṃ. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu suvaco hoti sovacassakaraṇehi dhammehi samannāgato khamo padakkhiṇaggāhī anusāsaniṃ, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu is easy to admonish, possessing qualities that make him so; he is patient and accepts instruction respectfully. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, is easy to admonish... and accepts instruction respectfully, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu yāni tāni sabrahmacārīnaṃ uccāvacāni kiṃkaraṇīyāni tatra dakkho hoti analaso tatrupāyāya vīmaṃsāya samannāgato [T 5.0367] alaṃ kātuṃ alaṃ saṃvidhātuṃ. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu yāni tāni sabrahmacārīnaṃ uccāvacāni kiṃkaraṇīyāni tatra dakkho hoti analaso tatrupāyāya vīmaṃsāya samannāgato alaṃ kātuṃ alaṃ saṃvidhātuṃ, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu is skilled and tireless in the various duties for his companions in the holy life, endowed with inquiry into the means for accomplishing them, able to carry them out and to organize them. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, is skilled... and able to carry them out and to organize them, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna [V 3.0305][P 5.0339] caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu dhammakāmo hoti piyasamudāhāro abhidhamme abhivinaye uḷārapāmojjo. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu dhammakāmo hoti piyasamudāhāro abhidhamme abhivinaye uḷārapāmojjo, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu is a lover of the Dhamma, pleasant in conversation, and has great joy in the higher Dhamma and the higher Vinaya. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, is a lover of the Dhamma... and has great joy in the higher Dhamma and the higher Vinaya, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu āraddhavīriyo viharati akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upasampadāya, thāmavā daḷhaparakkamo anikkhittadhuro kusalesu dhammesu. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu āraddhavīriyo viharati akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upasampadāya thāmavā daḷhaparakkamo anikkhittadhuro kusalesu dhammesu, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu dwells with energy aroused for the abandoning of unwholesome states and for the attainment of wholesome states; he is steadfast, firm in his exertion, not laying down the burden in regard to wholesome states. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, dwells with energy aroused... not laying down the burden in regard to wholesome states, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu catunnaṃ jhānānaṃ ābhicetasikānaṃ diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārānaṃ nikāmalābhī hoti akicchalābhī akasiralābhī. Yampi[M 3.0540], subhūti, bhikkhu catunnaṃ jhānānaṃ ābhicetasikānaṃ diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārānaṃ nikāmalābhī hoti akicchalābhī akasiralābhī, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu obtains at will, without difficulty, and without trouble, the four jhānas that belong to the higher mind, pleasant abidings in this very life. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, obtains at will... the four jhānas... pleasant abidings in this very life, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo tissopi jātiyo catassopi jātiyo pañcapi jātiyo dasapi jātiyo vīsampi jātiyo tiṃsampi jātiyo cattārīsampi jātiyo paññāsampi jātiyo jātisatampi jātisahassampi jātisatasahassampi anekepi saṃvaṭṭakappe anekepi vivaṭṭakappe anekepi [T 5.0368] saṃvaṭṭavivaṭṭakappe – ‘amutrāsiṃ evaṃnāmo evaṃgotto evaṃvaṇṇo evamāhāro evaṃsukhadukkhappaṭisaṃvedī evamāyupariyanto, so tato cuto amutra udapādiṃ; tatrāpāsiṃ evaṃnāmo evaṃgotto evaṃvaṇṇo evamāhāro evaṃsukhadukkhappaṭisaṃvedī evamāyupariyanto, so tato cuto idhūpapanno’ti. Iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu [P 5.0340] anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ, ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo…pe… iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati. Idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu recollects his manifold past existences, that is to say: one birth, two births, three births, four births, five births, ten births, twenty births, thirty births, forty births, fifty births, a hundred births, a thousand births, a hundred thousand births, many aeons of cosmic contraction, many aeons of cosmic expansion, many aeons of cosmic contraction and expansion: ‘There I was with such a name, such a clan, such an appearance, such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my lifespan. Passing away from there, I was reborn elsewhere. There too I was with such a name, such a clan, such an appearance, such was my food, such my experience of pleasure and pain, such my lifespan. Passing away from there, I have been reborn here.’ Thus he recollects his manifold past existences with their aspects and details. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, recollects his manifold past existences... with their aspects and details, this too, Subhūti, is a mark of faith for a faithful one.

“Puna caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati [V 3.0306] cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe, sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāti – ‘ime vata bhonto sattā kāyaduccaritena samannāgatā vacīduccaritena samannāgatā manoduccaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ upavādakā micchādiṭṭhikā micchādiṭṭhikammasamādānā, te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapannā. Ime vā pana bhonto sattā kāyasucaritena samannāgatā vacīsucaritena samannāgatā manosucaritena samannāgatā ariyānaṃ anupavādakā sammādiṭṭhikā sammādiṭṭhikammasamādānā, te kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapannā’ti. Iti dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passati cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe, sugate duggate yathākammūpage [T 5.0369] satte pajānāti. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena…pe… yathākammūpage satte pajānāti, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hoti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses the human, a bhikkhu sees beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations. He understands beings as they fare according to their kamma: ‘Indeed, these honorable beings were endowed with misconduct of body, misconduct of speech, and misconduct of mind, were revilers of the noble ones, held wrong view, and undertook actions based on wrong view. At the breaking up of the body, after death, they have been reborn in a state of deprivation, a bad destination, a place of ruin, in hell. Or, indeed, these honorable beings were endowed with good conduct of body, good conduct of speech, and good conduct of mind, were not revilers of the noble ones, held right view, and undertook actions based on right view. At the breaking up of the body, after death, they have been reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world.’ Thus, with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses the human, he sees beings passing away and being reborn—inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations. He understands beings as they fare according to their kamma. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, with the divine eye, which is purified… and so on… understands beings as they fare according to their kamma—this too, Subhūti, is a testament of the bhikkhu Saddha.

“Puna [M 3.0541] caparaṃ, subhūti, bhikkhu āsavānaṃ khayā anāsavaṃ cetovimuttiṃ paññāvimuttiṃ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati. Yampi, subhūti, bhikkhu āsavānaṃ khayā…pe… sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati, idampi, subhūti, saddhassa saddhāpadānaṃ hotī”ti.

Furthermore, Subhūti, a bhikkhu, through the destruction of the taints, has realized for himself with direct knowledge, in this very life, the taintless liberation of mind, liberation by wisdom, and having attained it, he dwells therein. That a bhikkhu, Subhūti, through the destruction of the taints… and so on… having realized it, having attained it, dwells therein—this too, Subhūti, is a testament of the bhikkhu Saddha.

Evaṃ vutte āyasmā subhūti bhagavantaṃ etadavoca – “yānimāni, bhante, bhagavatā saddhassa saddhāpadānāni bhāsitāni, saṃvijjanti tāni imassa bhikkhuno, ayañca bhikkhu etesu sandissati.

When this was said, the Venerable Subhūti said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable Sir, these testaments of the bhikkhu Saddha that have been spoken by the Blessed One are present in this bhikkhu, and this bhikkhu is seen in them.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu sīlavā hoti, pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto viharati ācāragocarasampanno aṇumattesu vajjesu bhayadassāvī, samādāya sikkhati [P 5.0341] sikkhāpadesu.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu is virtuous; he dwells restrained by the Pātimokkha restraint, is endowed with proper conduct and resort, and sees danger in the slightest faults. Having undertaken the training rules, he trains in them.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu bahussuto hoti sutadharo sutasannicayo; ye te dhammā ādikalyāṇā majjhekalyāṇā pariyosānakalyāṇā sātthaṃ sabyañjanaṃ kevalaparipuṇṇaṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ abhivadanti, tathārūpāssa dhammā bahussutā honti dhātā vacasā paricitā manasānupekkhitā diṭṭhiyā suppaṭividdhā.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu is very learned, he retains what he has learned, and has accumulated what he has learned. Those teachings that are good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, with right meaning and phrasing, which proclaim the entirely complete and purified holy life—such teachings have been much learned by him, retained, recited, mentally contemplated, and well penetrated by view.

“Ayaṃ[V 3.0307], bhante, bhikkhu kalyāṇamitto hoti kalyāṇasahāyo kalyāṇasampavaṅko.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu has good friends, good companions, and is inclined towards good friends.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu suvaco hoti…pe… anusāsaniṃ.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu is easy to admonish… and respectfully accepts instruction.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu yāni tāni [T 5.0370] sabrahmacārīnaṃ uccāvacāni kiṃkaraṇīyāni tattha dakkho hoti analaso tatrupāyāya vīmaṃsāya samannāgato alaṃ kātuṃ alaṃ saṃvidhātuṃ.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu is skilled and not lazy in the various duties for his companions in the holy life. He is endowed with the investigation into the means for them, is able to perform them, and is able to arrange them.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu dhammakāmo hoti piyasamudāhāro abhidhamme abhivinaye uḷārapāmojjo.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu is a lover of the Dhamma, has pleasant speech, and has great joy in the higher Dhamma and the higher Vinaya.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu āraddhavīriyo viharati…pe… thāmavā daḷhaparakkamo anikkhittadhuro kusalesu dhammesu.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu dwells with aroused energy… and so on… he is strong, of firm exertion, and has not laid down the burden in regard to wholesome states.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu catunnaṃ jhānānaṃ ābhicetasikānaṃ diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārānaṃ nikāmalābhī hoti akicchalābhī akasiralābhī.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu is an obtainer at will, an obtainer without difficulty, an obtainer without trouble of the four jhānas which pertain to the higher mind and are pleasant abidings in the present life.

“Ayaṃ[M 3.0542], bhante, bhikkhu anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati, seyyathidaṃ – ekampi jātiṃ dvepi jātiyo…pe… iti sākāraṃ sauddesaṃ anekavihitaṃ pubbenivāsaṃ anussarati.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu recollects his manifold past existences, that is to say: one birth, two births… and so on… thus he recollects his manifold past existences with their aspects and details.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena…pe… yathākammūpage satte pajānāti.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu, with the divine eye, which is purified and surpasses the human… and so on… understands beings as they fare according to their kamma.

“Ayaṃ, bhante, bhikkhu āsavānaṃ khayā…pe… sacchikatvā upasampajja viharati. Yānimāni, bhante, bhagavatā saddhassa saddhāpadānāni bhāsitāni, saṃvijjanti tāni imassa bhikkhuno, ayañca bhikkhu etesu sandissatī”ti.

Venerable Sir, this bhikkhu, through the destruction of the taints… and so on… having realized it, having attained it, dwells therein. Venerable Sir, these testaments of the bhikkhu Saddha that have been spoken by the Blessed One are present in this bhikkhu, and this bhikkhu is seen in them.”

“Sādhu sādhu, subhūti! Tena hi tvaṃ, subhūti, iminā ca saddhena bhikkhunā saddhiṃ vihareyyāsi. Yadā ca tvaṃ, subhūti, ākaṅkheyyāsi tathāgataṃ dassanāya, iminā saddhena bhikkhunā saddhiṃ upasaṅkameyyāsi tathāgataṃ dassanāyā”ti. Catutthaṃ.

“Good, good, Subhūti! Therefore, Subhūti, you should dwell together with this bhikkhu Saddha. And whenever you, Subhūti, should wish to see the Tathāgata, you should approach the Tathāgata to see him together with this bhikkhu Saddha.” The Fourth Sutta is finished.

5. Mettāsuttaṃ

5. The Discourse on Loving-Kindness

15. paṭi. ma. 2.22; mi. pa. 4.4.6 “Mettāya[V 3.0308][P 5.0342], bhikkhave, cetovimuttiyā āsevitāya bhāvitāya bahulīkatāya yānīkatāya vatthukatāya anuṭṭhitāya paricitāya susamāraddhāya ekādasānisaṃsā pāṭikaṅkhā.

15. “Bhikkhus, eleven benefits are to be expected from the liberation of mind through loving-kindness that has been cultivated, developed, made much of, made a vehicle, made a basis, established, familiarized, and well undertaken.

Katame ekādasa? Sukhaṃ supati, sukhaṃ paṭibujjhati[T 5.0371], na pāpakaṃ supinaṃ passati, manussānaṃ piyo hoti, amanussānaṃ piyo hoti, devatā rakkhanti, nāssa aggi vā visaṃ vā satthaṃ vā kamati, tuvaṭaṃ cittaṃ samādhiyati, mukhavaṇṇo vippasīdati, asammūḷho kālaṃ karoti, uttari appaṭivijjhanto brahmalokūpago hoti. Mettāya, bhikkhave, cetovimuttiyā āsevitāya bhāvitāya bahulīkatāya yānīkatāya vatthukatāya anuṭṭhitāya paricitāya susamāraddhāya ime ekādasānisaṃsā pāṭikaṅkhā”ti. Pañcamaṃ.

“What are the eleven? One sleeps happily, wakes happily, sees no evil dream, is dear to humans, is dear to non-humans, deities protect one, fire, poison, or a weapon does not harm one, the mind is quickly concentrated, the facial complexion is serene, one dies unconfused, and if not penetrating higher, one is reborn in the Brahma world. Bhikkhus, these are the eleven benefits to be expected from the liberation of mind through loving-kindness that has been cultivated, developed, made much of, made a vehicle, made a basis, established, familiarized, and well undertaken.” The Fifth Sutta is finished.

6. Aṭṭhakanāgarasuttaṃ

6. The Discourse at Aṭṭhakanāgara

16. Ekaṃ samayaṃ āyasmā ānando vesāliyaṃ viharati beluvagāmake veḷuvagāmake (syā. kaṃ. ka.). Tena kho pana samayena dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputtaṃ anuppatto hoti kenacideva karaṇīyena.

16. At one time the Venerable Ānanda was dwelling at Veḷuvagāmaka, in Vesālī. Now at that time the householder Dasama from Aṭṭhakanāgara had arrived at Pāṭaliputta on some business.

Atha [M 3.0543] kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro yena kukkuṭārāmo yena aññataro bhikkhu tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā taṃ bhikkhuṃ etadavoca – “kahaṃ nu kho, bhante, āyasmā ānando etarahi viharati? Dassanakāmā hi mayaṃ, bhante, āyasmantaṃ ānanda”nti. “Eso, gahapati, āyasmā ānando vesāliyaṃ viharati beluvagāmake”ti.

Then the householder Dasama from Aṭṭhakanāgara approached a certain bhikkhu at the Kukkuṭārāma. Having approached, he said this to that bhikkhu: "Venerable sir, where is the Venerable Ānanda dwelling now? For we are desirous of seeing the Venerable Ānanda." "Householder, the Venerable Ānanda is dwelling at Vesālī in Veḷuvagāmaka."

Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro pāṭaliputte taṃ karaṇīyaṃ tīretvā yena vesālī beluvagāmako yenāyasmā ānando tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdi. Ekamantaṃ nisinno kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro āyasmantaṃ [P 5.0343] ānandaṃ etadavoca – “atthi nu kho, bhante ānanda, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo sammadakkhāto, yattha bhikkhuno [T 5.0372] appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā vā āsavā parikkhayaṃ [V 3.0309] gacchanti, ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī”ti? “Atthi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo sammadakkhāto, yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā vā āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī”ti.

Then the householder Dasama from Aṭṭhakanāgara, having concluded that business at Pāṭaliputta, went to Vesālī, to Veḷuvagāmaka, and approached the Venerable Ānanda. Having approached, he paid homage to the Venerable Ānanda and sat down at a suitable place. Seated at a suitable place, the householder Dasama from Aṭṭhakanāgara said this to the Venerable Ānanda: "Venerable Ānanda, is there one Dhamma well declared by that Blessed One, who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby for a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unliberated mind is liberated, his undestroyed taints go to destruction, or he attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage?" "There is, householder, one Dhamma well declared by that Blessed One, who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby for a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unliberated mind is liberated, his undestroyed taints go to destruction, or he attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage."

“Katamo pana, bhante ānanda, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo sammadakkhāto, yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā vā āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī”ti? “Idha, gahapati, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘idampi kho paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ, tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti; no ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena [T 5.0373][M 3.0544] ekadhammo sammadakkhāto, yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ vimuccati, aparikkhīṇā vā āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti.

“But what, Venerable Ānanda, is that one Dhamma well declared by that Blessed One, who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby for a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unliberated mind is liberated, his undestroyed taints go to destruction, or he attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage?” “Here, householder, a bhikkhu, secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by initial thought and sustained thought, with rapture and happiness born of seclusion. He reflects thus: ‘This first jhāna is also conditioned and volitionally produced.’ He knows: ‘Whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent and subject to cessation.’ Established therein, he attains the destruction of the taints. If he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then through that very passion for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, with the destruction of the five lower fetters, he is one of spontaneous rebirth, attaining final Nibbāna there, not liable to return from that world. This, householder, is that one Dhamma well declared by that Blessed One, who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby for a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unliberated mind is liberated, his undestroyed taints go to destruction, or he attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage.”

“Puna [P 5.0344] caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ…pe… tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ…pe… catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘idampi kho catutthaṃ jhānaṃ abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci [V 3.0310] abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti; no ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena ekadhammo sammadakkhāto, yattha bhikkhuno appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato avimuttaṃ vā cittaṃ vimuccati aparikkhīṇā vā āsavā parikkhayaṃ gacchanti, ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti.

“Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu, with the stilling of initial and sustained thought, enters and dwells in the second jhāna, which has internal confidence and unification of mind, is without initial and sustained thought, and is born of concentration, with rapture and happiness… the third jhāna… the fourth jhāna. He reflects thus: ‘This fourth jhāna too is conditioned and volitionally produced.’ He knows: ‘Whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’ Established therein, he attains the destruction of the taints. If he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then through that very passion for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, with the destruction of the five lower fetters, he is one of spontaneous rebirth, attaining final Nibbāna there, not liable to return from that world. This too, householder, is that one Dhamma well declared by that Blessed One, who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby for a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unliberated mind is liberated, his undestroyed taints go to destruction, or he attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage.”

“Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati tathā dutiyaṃ tathā tatiyaṃ tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho [T 5.0374] tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjena pharitvā viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho mettā cetovimutti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti; no ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā…pe. [P 5.0345] … ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti.

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness, likewise the second, likewise the third, and likewise the fourth. Thus above, below, and across, everywhere and for all, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness—vast, exalted, immeasurable, without enmity, without ill will. He reflects thus: ‘This liberation of mind through loving-kindness is also conditioned and volitionally produced.’ He understands: ‘Whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’ Established therein, he attains the destruction of the taints. If he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then through that very passion for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, with the utter destruction of the five lower fetters, he is spontaneously reborn and attains final Nibbāna there, not being of a nature to return from that world. This too, householder, is that one Dhamma well-declared by that Blessed One who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby one attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage.

“Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu karuṇāsahagatena cetasā…pe… muditāsahagatena cetasā…pe… upekkhāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati [M 3.0545] tathā dutiyaṃ tathā tatiyaṃ tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ upekkhāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjena pharitvā viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho upekkhācetovimutti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti [T 5.0375] pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti; no ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho[V 3.0311], gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā…pe… ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti.

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu dwells pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by compassion… with a mind accompanied by appreciative joy… with a mind accompanied by equanimity, likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth. Thus above, below, and across, everywhere and for all, he dwells pervading the entire world with a mind accompanied by equanimity—vast, exalted, immeasurable, without enmity, without ill will. He reflects thus: ‘This liberation of mind through equanimity is also conditioned and volitionally produced.’ He understands: ‘Whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’ Established therein, he attains the destruction of the taints; if he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then through that very passion for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, with the utter destruction of the five lower fetters, he becomes one who is spontaneously reborn and attains final Nibbāna there, not being of a nature to return from that world. This too, householder, is that one Dhamma well-declared by that Blessed One who knows and sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, whereby one attains the unattained, supreme security from bondage.

“Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā ‘ananto ākāso’ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho ākāsānañcāyatanasamāpatti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti; no ce āsavānaṃ [P 5.0346] khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā…pe… ananuppattaṃ [T 5.0376] vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇāti.

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu, by completely transcending perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, and by not attending to perceptions of diversity, aware that ‘space is infinite,’ attains and dwells in the base of infinite space. He reflects thus: ‘This attainment of the base of infinite space is also conditioned and volitionally produced.’ He understands: ‘Whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’ Established therein, he attains the destruction of the taints. If he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then through that very passion for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, with the utter destruction of the five lower fetters, he becomes one who is spontaneously reborn, there to attain final Nibbāna, not being of a nature to return from that world. This too, householder, is that one Dhamma well-declared by that Blessed One—who knows, who sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One—whereby a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unreleased mind becomes released, his undestroyed taints come to destruction, and he attains the unattained supreme security from bondage.

“Puna caparaṃ, gahapati, bhikkhu sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘anantaṃ viññāṇa’nti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati…pe… sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘natthi kiñcī’ti ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. So iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ayampi kho ākiñcaññāyatanasamāpatti abhisaṅkhatā abhisañcetayitā’. ‘Yaṃ kho pana kiñci abhisaṅkhataṃ abhisañcetayitaṃ tadaniccaṃ nirodhadhamma’nti pajānāti. So tattha ṭhito āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti; no ce āsavānaṃ khayaṃ pāpuṇāti, teneva dhammarāgena tāya dhammanandiyā pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko hoti tattha parinibbāyī anāvattidhammo tasmā lokā. Ayampi [M 3.0546] kho, gahapati, tena bhagavatā jānatā…pe… ananuppattaṃ vā anuttaraṃ yogakkhemaṃ anupāpuṇātī”ti.

Furthermore, householder, a bhikkhu, by completely transcending the base of infinite space, aware that ‘consciousness is infinite,’ attains and dwells in the base of infinite consciousness… By completely transcending the base of infinite consciousness, aware that ‘there is nothing,’ he attains and dwells in the base of nothingness. He reflects thus: ‘This attainment of the base of nothingness is also conditioned and volitionally produced.’ He understands: ‘Whatever is conditioned and volitionally produced is impermanent, subject to cessation.’ Established therein, he attains the destruction of the taints; if he does not attain the destruction of the taints, then through that very passion for the Dhamma, that delight in the Dhamma, by the utter destruction of the five lower fetters, he is spontaneously reborn, there he attains final Nibbāna, and is not of a nature to return from that world. This too, householder, is that one Dhamma well-declared by that Blessed One—who knows, who sees, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One—whereby a bhikkhu dwelling heedful, ardent, and resolute, his unreleased mind becomes released, his undestroyed taints come to destruction, and he attains the unattained supreme security from bondage.

Evaṃ vutte dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ etadavoca – “seyyathāpi, bhante ānanda, puriso ekaṃ nidhimukhaṃ gavesanto sakideva sabbatthapi evameva dissati ekādasa nidhimukhāni adhigaccheyya; evamevaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, ekaṃ amatadvāraṃ gavesanto sakideva ekādasa amatadvārāni ekādasannaṃ amatadvārānaṃ (sabbattha) ma. ni. 2.21 passitabbaṃ alatthaṃ sevanāya savanāya (syā.) sī. pī. majjhimapaṇṇāsakadutiyasuttepi, bhāvanāya (ma. ni. 2.21). Seyyathāpi, bhante, purisassa agāraṃ ekādasa dvāraṃ. So [P 5.0347] tasmiṃ [V 3.0312] agāre āditte [T 5.0377] ekamekenapi dvārena sakkuṇeyya attānaṃ sotthiṃ kātuṃ; evamevaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhante, imesaṃ ekādasannaṃ amatadvārānaṃ ekamekenapi amatadvārena sakkuṇissāmi attānaṃ sotthiṃ kātuṃ. Ime hi nāma, bhante, aññatitthiyā ācariyassa ācariyadhanaṃ pariyesissanti. Kiṃ kimaṅgaṃ (ma. ni. 2.21) panāhaṃ āyasmato ānandassa pūjaṃ na karissāmī”ti!

When this was said, the householder Dasama of Aṭṭhaka said this to the Venerable Ānanda: “Venerable Ānanda, it is as if a man, while seeking one pot of treasure, were to find eleven pots of treasure at once. So too, venerable sir, while seeking one door to the Deathless, I have at once found eleven doors to the Deathless to resort to. It is as if, venerable sir, a man’s house had eleven doors, and when that house caught fire he could make himself safe by any one of those doors. So too, venerable sir, I can make myself safe by any one of these eleven doors to the Deathless. Venerable sir, the adherents of other sects will indeed seek a teacher’s fee for their teacher. Why, then, should I not make an offering to the Venerable Ānanda?”

Atha kho dasamo gahapati aṭṭhakanāgaro vesālikañca pāṭaliputtakañca bhikkhusaṅghaṃ sannipātāpetvā paṇītena khādanīyena bhojanīyena sahatthā santappesi sampavāresi. Ekamekañca bhikkhuṃ paccekaṃ dussayugena acchādesi, āyasmantañca ānandaṃ ticīvarena. Āyasmato ānandassa pañcasataṃ vihāraṃ kārāpesīti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

Then the householder Dasama of Aṭṭhaka had the Saṅgha of bhikkhus from Vesālī and Pāṭaliputta assembled and, with his own hands, served and satisfied them with excellent hard and soft food. He presented each bhikkhu individually with a pair of cloths, and the Venerable Ānanda with a set of three robes. And he had a monastery worth five hundred built for the Venerable Ānanda. The Sixth.

7. Gopālasuttaṃ

7. The Discourse on the Cowherd

17. “Ekādasahi, bhikkhave, aṅgehi samannāgato gopālako abhabbo gogaṇaṃ pariharituṃ phātiṃ kātuṃ phātikattuṃ (sī.), phātikātuṃ (syā. pī.). Katamehi ekādasahi? Idha, bhikkhave, gopālako na rūpaññū hoti, na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti, na āsāṭikaṃ hāretā sāṭetā (sī. syā. pī.) hoti, na vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti, na dhūmaṃ kattā hoti, na titthaṃ jānāti, na pītaṃ jānāti, na vīthiṃ jānāti, na gocarakusalo hoti, anavasesadohī ca hoti, ye te usabhā gopitaro gopariṇāyakā te na atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti[M 3.0547]. Imehi kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi aṅgehi samannāgato gopālako abhabbo gogaṇaṃ pariharituṃ phātiṃ kātuṃ.

17. “Bhikkhus, a cowherd endowed with eleven factors is incapable of managing a herd of cattle and causing it to prosper. With what eleven? Here, bhikkhus, a cowherd does not know the forms of cattle, is not skilled in their marks, does not remove gadflies’ eggs, does not cover their wounds, does not make smoke, does not know the watering place, does not know what has been drunk, does not know the path, is not skilled in the grazing grounds, milks without leaving a remainder, and does not venerate with special veneration those bulls that are the fathers and leaders of the herd. Bhikkhus, a cowherd endowed with these eleven factors is incapable of managing a herd of cattle and causing it to prosper.”

“Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu abhabbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjituṃ. Katamehi ekādasahi? Idha[P 5.0348], bhikkhave, bhikkhu na rūpaññū hoti, na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti, na āsāṭikaṃ [T 5.0378] hāretā hoti, na vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti, na dhūmaṃ kattā hoti, na titthaṃ jānāti, na pītaṃ jānāti, na vīthiṃ jānāti, na gocarakusalo hoti, anavasesadohī ca hoti, ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā te na atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti.

“So too, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu endowed with eleven qualities is incapable of achieving growth, flourishing, and expansion in this Dhamma and Discipline. With what eleven? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know material form, is not skilled in marks, does not remove what is like gadflies’ eggs, does not cover what is like a wound, does not make what is like smoke, does not know what is like a watering place, does not know what is like having drunk, does not know what is like a path, is not skilled in what is like a grazing ground, is one who milks without remainder, and does not venerate with special veneration those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers of the Saṅgha, and leaders of the Saṅgha.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na rūpaññū hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yaṃ kiñci rūpaṃ [V 3.0313] ( ) (sabbaṃ rūpaṃ) ma. ni. 1.347 ( ) katthaci dissati ‘cattāri mahābhūtāni, catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpa’nti yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na rūpaññū hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not a knower of material form? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know as it truly is any kind of material form as: ‘The four great elements and the material form derived from the four great elements.’ In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not a knower of material form.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ‘kammalakkhaṇo bālo, kammalakkhaṇo paṇḍito’ti yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na lakkhaṇakusalo hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not skilled in marks? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know as it truly is: ‘A fool is marked by his kamma, a wise person is marked by his kamma.’ In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not skilled in marks.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu uppannaṃ kāmavitakkaṃ adhivāseti nappajahati na vinodeti na byantīkaroti na anabhāvaṃ gameti, uppannaṃ byāpādavitakkaṃ… uppannaṃ vihiṃsāvitakkaṃ… uppannuppanne pāpake akusale dhamme adhivāseti nappajahati na vinodeti na byantīkaroti na anabhāvaṃ gameti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not one who removes what is like gadflies’ eggs? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu tolerates an arisen thought of sensual desire; he does not abandon it, does not dispel it, does not bring it to an end, and does not cause it to be unarisen. He tolerates an arisen thought of ill will… an arisen thought of cruelty… He tolerates arisen evil, unwholesome states; he does not abandon them, does not dispel them, does not bring them to an end, and does not cause them to be unarisen. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not one who removes what is like gadflies’ eggs.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā nimittaggāhī hoti anubyañjanaggāhī; yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ cakkhundriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā [T 5.0379] dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya na paṭipajjati; na rakkhati cakkhundriyaṃ, cakkhundriye saṃvaraṃ nāpajjati. Sotena saddaṃ sutvā… ghānena gandhaṃ [P 5.0349] ghāyitvā… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā… manasā [M 3.0548] dhammaṃ viññāya nimittaggāhī hoti anubyañjanaggāhī; yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ manindriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya na paṭipajjati; na rakkhati manindriyaṃ, manindriye saṃvaraṃ nāpajjati. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not one who covers what is like a wound? Here, bhikkhus, having seen a form with the eye, a bhikkhu grasps its signs and features. Because of which—his dwelling with the eye faculty unrestrained—evil, unwholesome states of covetousness and displeasure might assail him, he does not practice for its restraint; he does not guard the eye faculty, he does not attain restraint in the eye faculty. Having heard a sound with the ear… Having smelled an odor with the nose… Having tasted a flavor with the tongue… Having touched a tangible with the body… Having cognized a mental object with the mind, he grasps its signs and features. Because of which—his dwelling with the mind faculty unrestrained—evil, unwholesome states of covetousness and displeasure might assail him, he does not practice for its restraint; he does not guard the mind faculty, he does not attain restraint in the mind faculty. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not one who covers what is like a wound.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na dhūmaṃ kattā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na ma. ni. 1.346-347 pana ayaṃ nakāro dhammantipadassa anantaraṃ dissati yathāsutaṃ yathāpariyattaṃ dhammaṃ vitthārena paresaṃ desetā hoti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na dhūmaṃ kattā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not one who makes what is like smoke? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not teach the Dhamma to others in detail as he has heard it and as he has learned it. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not one who makes what is like smoke.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na titthaṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū [V 3.0314] bahussutā āgatāgamā dhammadharā vinayadharā mātikādharā, te kālena kālaṃ upasaṅkamitvā na paripucchati na paripañhati – ‘idaṃ, bhante, kathaṃ, imassa ko attho’ti? Tassa te āyasmanto avivaṭañceva na vivaranti, anuttānīkatañca na uttānīkaronti, anekavihitesu ca kaṅkhāṭhāniyesu dhammesu kaṅkhaṃ na paṭivinodenti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na titthaṃ jānāti.

“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu not know the ford? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not from time to time approach those bhikkhus who are very learned, who have memorized the teachings, bearers of the Dhamma, bearers of the Vinaya, and bearers of the mātikās, to question and query them: ‘Venerable sirs, how is this? What is its meaning?’ Those venerable ones do not reveal to him what has not been revealed, do not make plain what has not been made plain, and do not dispel his perplexity about many perplexing points in the Dhamma. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know the ford.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na pītaṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye desiyamāne na labhati atthavedaṃ, na labhati dhammavedaṃ[T 5.0380], na labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na pītaṃ jānāti.

“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu not know what has been drunk? Here, bhikkhus, when the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata is being taught, a bhikkhu does not gain knowledge of the meaning, does not gain knowledge of the Dhamma, and does not gain gladness connected with the Dhamma. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know what has been drunk.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na vīthiṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na vīthiṃ jānāti.

“And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu not know the road? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know the Noble Eightfold Path as it truly is. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not know the road.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na gocarakusalo hoti? Idha[P 5.0350], bhikkhave, bhikkhu cattāro satipaṭṭhāne yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu na gocarakusalo hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu not skilled in the pastures? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not understand the four establishments of mindfulness as they really are. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is not skilled in the pastures.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anavasesadohī hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhuṃ saddhā gahapatikā abhihaṭṭhuṃ pavārenti cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānapaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi. Tatra bhikkhu mattaṃ na jānāti paṭiggahaṇāya. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anavasesadohī hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who milks dry? Here, bhikkhus, faithful householders invite a bhikkhu with offerings of robes, almsfood, lodging, and medicinal requisites for the sick. There the bhikkhu does not know the proper measure for acceptance. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is one who milks dry.”

“Kathañca[M 3.0549], bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, te na atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, tesu na mettaṃ kāyakammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca, na mettaṃ vacīkammaṃ… na mettaṃ manokammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, na te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who does not honor with excessive honor those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers and leaders of the Saṅgha? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not maintain loving bodily action toward those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers and leaders of the Saṅgha, both openly and in private; he does not maintain loving verbal action… he does not maintain loving mental action toward them, both openly and in private. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is one who does not honor with excessive honor those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers and leaders of the Saṅgha.”

“Imehi [V 3.0315] kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu [T 5.0381] abhabbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjituṃ.

“Bhikkhus, a bhikkhu endowed with these eleven qualities is incapable of attaining growth, development, and abundance in this Dhamma and Discipline.”

“Ekādasahi, bhikkhave, aṅgehi samannāgato gopālako bhabbo gogaṇaṃ pariharituṃ phātiṃ kātuṃ. Katamehi ekādasahi? Idha, bhikkhave, gopālako rūpaññū hoti, lakkhaṇakusalo hoti, āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti, vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti, dhūmaṃ kattā hoti, titthaṃ jānāti, pītaṃ jānāti, vīthiṃ jānāti, gocarakusalo hoti, sāvasesadohī ca hoti, ye te usabhā gopitaro gopariṇāyakā te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti – imehi kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi aṅgehi samannāgato gopālako bhabbo gogaṇaṃ pariharituṃ phātiṃ kātuṃ.

“Bhikkhus, a cowherd endowed with eleven factors is capable of maintaining and increasing a herd of cattle. What eleven? Here, bhikkhus, a cowherd knows forms, is skilled in characteristics, removes gadfly eggs, covers wounds, makes smoke, knows the ford, knows what has been drunk, knows the path, is skilled in the pasture, milks leaving a remainder, and honors with excessive honor those bulls that are the fathers and leaders of the herd. Bhikkhus, a cowherd endowed with these eleven factors is capable of maintaining and increasing a herd of cattle.”

“Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu bhabbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjituṃ. Katamehi ekādasahi? Idha[P 5.0351], bhikkhave, bhikkhu rūpaññū hoti, lakkhaṇakusalo hoti, āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti, vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti, dhūmaṃ kattā hoti, titthaṃ jānāti, pītaṃ jānāti, vīthiṃ jānāti, gocarakusalo hoti, sāvasesadohī ca hoti, ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti.

“Even so, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu endowed with these eleven qualities is capable of attaining growth, development, and abundance in this Dhamma and Discipline. What eleven? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu knows forms, is skilled in characteristics, removes gadfly eggs, covers wounds, makes smoke, knows the ford, knows what has been drunk, knows the path, is skilled in the pasture, milks leaving a remainder, and honors with excessive honor those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers and leaders of the Saṅgha.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu rūpaññū hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yaṃ kiñci rūpaṃ ‘cattāri mahābhūtāni, catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpa’nti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu rūpaññū hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who knows forms? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands as it actually is any form whatsoever: ‘The four great elements and form derived from the four great elements.’ In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is one who knows forms.”

“Kathañca[M 3.0550], bhikkhave, bhikkhu lakkhaṇakusalo hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ‘kammalakkhaṇo bālo, kammalakkhaṇo paṇḍito’ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu lakkhaṇakusalo [T 5.0382] hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu skilled in characteristics? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu understands as it actually is: ‘A fool is characterized by his action; a wise person is characterized by his action.’ In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is skilled in characteristics.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu uppannaṃ kāmavitakkaṃ nādhivāseti pajahati vinodeti byantīkaroti anabhāvaṃ gameti, uppannaṃ byāpādavitakkaṃ… uppannaṃ vihiṃsāvitakkaṃ… uppannuppanne pāpake akusale dhamme nādhivāseti pajahati [V 3.0316] vinodeti byantīkaroti anabhāvaṃ gameti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu āsāṭikaṃ hāretā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who removes gadfly eggs? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu does not tolerate an arisen thought of sensuality; he abandons it, dispels it, terminates it, and obliterates it. He does not tolerate an arisen thought of ill will… an arisen thought of harming… He does not tolerate any evil, unwholesome states that have arisen; he abandons them, dispels them, terminates them, and obliterates them. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is one who removes gadfly eggs.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī; yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ cakkhundriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya paṭipajjati; rakkhati cakkhundriyaṃ, cakkhundriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Sotena saddaṃ sutvā… ghānena gandhaṃ ghāyitvā… jivhāya rasaṃ sāyitvā… kāyena phoṭṭhabbaṃ phusitvā… manasā dhammaṃ viññāya na nimittaggāhī hoti nānubyañjanaggāhī; yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ manindriyaṃ asaṃvutaṃ viharantaṃ abhijjhādomanassā pāpakā akusalā dhammā anvāssaveyyuṃ, tassa saṃvarāya [P 5.0352] paṭipajjati; rakkhati manindriyaṃ, manindriye saṃvaraṃ āpajjati. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vaṇaṃ paṭicchādetā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who covers a wound? Here, bhikkhus, having seen a form with the eye, a bhikkhu is not one who grasps at signs and features. Because of which—were he to dwell with the eye faculty unrestrained—evil, unwholesome states of covetousness and displeasure would assail him, for the sake of its restraint, he practices; he guards the eye faculty, he undertakes the restraint of the eye faculty. Having heard a sound with the ear… having smelled an odor with the nose… having tasted a flavor with the tongue… having felt a tangible object with the body… having cognized a mental object with the mind, he is not one who grasps at signs and features. Because of which—were he to dwell with the mind faculty unrestrained—evil, unwholesome states of covetousness and displeasure would assail him, for the sake of its restraint, he practices; he guards the mind faculty, he undertakes the restraint of the mind faculty. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is one who covers a wound.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhūmaṃ kattā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu yathāsutaṃ yathāpariyattaṃ dhammaṃ vitthārena paresaṃ desetā hoti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu dhūmaṃ kattā hoti.

“And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu a maker of smoke? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu teaches the Dhamma to others in detail, just as he has heard it and just as he has learned it. In this way, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is a maker of smoke.”

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu titthaṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū bahussutā āgatāgamā dhammadharā vinayadharā mātikādharā, te [T 5.0383] kālena kālaṃ upasaṅkamitvā paripucchati paripañhati – ‘idaṃ, bhante, kathaṃ, imassa ko attho’ti? Tassa te āyasmanto avivaṭañceva vivaranti, anuttānīkatañca uttānīkaronti, anekavihitesu ca kaṅkhāṭhāniyesu dhammesu kaṅkhaṃ paṭivinodenti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu titthaṃ jānāti.

And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu know the ford? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu from time to time approaches those bhikkhus who are of great learning, who have memorized the transmissions, who are bearers of the Dhamma, bearers of the Vinaya, and bearers of the matrices. He questions and inquires of them: ‘Venerable sirs, how is this? What is the meaning of this?’ Then those venerable ones reveal to him what is unrevealed, make plain what is not plain, and dispel his doubt about various matters that are grounds for doubt. It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu knows the ford.

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu pītaṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu tathāgatappavedite dhammavinaye desiyamāne labhati atthavedaṃ, labhati dhammavedaṃ, labhati dhammūpasaṃhitaṃ pāmojjaṃ. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu pītaṃ jānāti.

And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu know what has been drunk? Here, bhikkhus, when the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata is being taught, a bhikkhu obtains knowledge of the meaning, obtains knowledge of the Dhamma, and obtains the gladness connected with the Dhamma. It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu knows what has been drunk.

“Kathañca[V 3.0317][M 3.0551], bhikkhave, bhikkhu vīthiṃ jānāti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vīthiṃ jānāti.

And how, bhikkhus, does a bhikkhu know the path? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu distinctly knows the Noble Eightfold Path as it really is. It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu knows the path.

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu gocarakusalo hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu cattāro satipaṭṭhāne yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu gocarakusalo hoti.

And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu skilled in the pasture? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu distinctly knows the four foundations of mindfulness as they really are. It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu is skilled in the pasture.

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sāvasesadohī hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhuṃ saddhā gahapatikā abhihaṭṭhuṃ pavārenti cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānapaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi. Tatra bhikkhu mattaṃ jānāti paṭiggahaṇāya. Evaṃ [P 5.0353] kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu sāvasesadohī hoti.

And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who milks leaving a remainder? Here, bhikkhus, faithful householders invite a bhikkhu with offerings of robes, almsfood, lodging, and medicinal requisites for the sick. In that matter, the bhikkhu knows the proper measure for acceptance. It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu is one who milks leaving a remainder.

“Kathañca, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, tesu mettaṃ kāyakammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca, mettaṃ vacīkammaṃ… mettaṃ manokammaṃ paccupaṭṭhāpeti āvi ceva raho ca. Evaṃ kho[T 5.0384], bhikkhave, bhikkhu ye te bhikkhū therā rattaññū cirapabbajitā saṅghapitaro saṅghapariṇāyakā, te atirekapūjāya pūjetā hoti.

And how, bhikkhus, is a bhikkhu one who honors with surpassing honor those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers of the Saṅgha, and leaders of the Saṅgha? Here, bhikkhus, towards those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers of the Saṅgha, and leaders of the Saṅgha, he establishes loving bodily action, both in public and in private; he establishes loving verbal action... he establishes loving mental action, both in public and in private. It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu is one who honors with surpassing honor those bhikkhus who are elders, of long standing, long gone forth, fathers of the Saṅgha, and leaders of the Saṅgha.

“Imehi kho, bhikkhave, ekādasahi dhammehi samannāgato bhikkhu bhabbo imasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjitu”nti. Sattamaṃ.

Bhikkhus, a bhikkhu who is endowed with these eleven qualities is capable of attaining growth, development, and abundance in this Dhamma and Discipline." Seventh.

8. Paṭhamasamādhisuttaṃ

8. The First Discourse on Concentration

18. a. ni. 10.6 Atha kho sambahulā bhikkhū yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu. Ekamantaṃ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṃ etadavocuṃ –

18. Then a number of bhikkhus went to the Blessed One, paid homage to him, sat down to one side, and said to him:

“Siyā nu kho, bhante, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa, na āpasmiṃ āposaññī assa, na tejasmiṃ tejosaññī assa, na vāyasmiṃ vāyosaññī assa, na ākāsānañcāyatane [M 3.0552] ākāsānañcāyatanasaññī assa, na viññāṇañcāyatane [V 3.0318] viññāṇañcāyatanasaññī assa, na ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññī assa, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññī assa, na idhaloke idhalokasaññī assa, na paraloke paralokasaññī assa, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti?

“Venerable sir, could a bhikkhu obtain such an attainment of concentration that he would not be percipient of earth in the earth element, nor of water in the water element, nor of fire in the fire element, nor of air in the air element; nor of the base of the infinity of space in the base of the infinity of space, nor of the base of the infinity of consciousness in the base of the infinity of consciousness, nor of the base of nothingness in the base of nothingness, nor of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; nor of this world in this world, nor of the next world in the next world; and that he would not be percipient even of what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and mentally traversed; and yet he would be percipient?”

“Siyā, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa…pe. [P 5.0354]… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti.

“It could be, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu might obtain such an attainment of concentration that he would not be percipient of earth in the earth element … and that he would not be percipient even of what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and mentally traversed; and yet he would be percipient.”

“Yathā kathaṃ pana, bhante, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho [T 5.0385] yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa…pe… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti?

“But how, venerable sir, could a bhikkhu obtain such an attainment of concentration that he would not be percipient of earth in the earth element … and that he would not be percipient even of what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and mentally traversed; and yet he would be percipient?”

“Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu evaṃsaññī hoti – ‘etaṃ santaṃ etaṃ paṇītaṃ, yadidaṃ sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbāna’nti. Evaṃ kho, bhikkhave, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa, na āpasmiṃ āposaññī assa, na tejasmiṃ tejosaññī assa, na vāyasmiṃ vāyosaññī assa, na ākāsānañcāyatane ākāsānañcāyatanasaññī assa, na viññāṇañcāyatane viññāṇañcāyatanasaññī assa, na ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññī assa, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññī assa, na idhaloke idhalokasaññī assa, na paraloke paralokasaññī assa, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

“Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu is percipient thus: ‘This is peaceful, this is sublime, that is, the stilling of all formations, the relinquishing of all substrates of existence, the destruction of craving, dispassion, cessation, Nibbāna.’ It is in this way, bhikkhus, that a bhikkhu might obtain such an attainment of concentration that he would not be percipient of earth in the earth element, nor of water in the water element, nor of fire in the fire element, nor of air in the air element; nor of the base of the infinity of space in the base of the infinity of space, nor of the base of the infinity of consciousness in the base of the infinity of consciousness, nor of the base of nothingness in the base of nothingness, nor of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception in the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; nor of this world in this world, nor of the next world in the next world; and that he would not be percipient even of what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and mentally traversed; and yet he would be percipient.” Eighth.

9. Dutiyasamādhisuttaṃ

9. The Second Discourse on Concentration

19. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – “bhikkhavo”ti. “Bhadante”ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

19. There, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus.” “Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Siyā [M 3.0553][V 3.0319] nu kho bhikkhave, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī [P 5.0355] assa, na āpasmiṃ āposaññī assa…pe… [T 5.0386] na ākiñcaññāyatane ākiñcaññāyatanasaññī assa, na nevasaññānāsaññāyatane nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaññī assa, na idhaloke idhalokasaññī assa, na paraloke paralokasaññī assa, yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti? “Bhagavaṃmūlakā no, bhante, dhammā bhagavaṃnettikā bhagavaṃpaṭisaraṇā. Sādhu vata, bhante, bhagavantaṃyeva paṭibhātu etassa bhāsitassa attho. Bhagavato sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī”ti.

“Bhikkhus, could there be for a bhikkhu such an attainment of concentration whereby he would not be percipient of earth in regard to earth, nor percipient of water in regard to water... and so on... nor percipient of the base of nothingness in regard to the base of nothingness, nor percipient of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception in regard to the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception; nor percipient of this world in regard to this world, nor percipient of the next world in regard to the next world; and even in regard to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and repeatedly considered by the mind, he would not be percipient; and yet he would still be percipient?” “Venerable sir, for us the teachings have the Blessed One as their root, the Blessed One as their guide, and the Blessed One as their refuge. It would be good, venerable sir, if the meaning of this statement would become clear to the Blessed One himself. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will bear it in mind.”

“Tena hi, bhikkhave, suṇātha, sādhukaṃ manasi karotha; bhāsissāmī”ti. “Evaṃ, bhante”ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

“In that case, bhikkhus, listen and attend carefully; I will speak.” “Yes, venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Siyā, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa…pe… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti.

“It is possible, bhikkhus, for a bhikkhu to have such an attainment of concentration whereby he would not be percipient of earth in regard to earth... and so on... and even in regard to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and repeatedly considered by the mind, he would not be percipient; and yet he would still be percipient.”

“Yathā kathaṃ pana, bhante, siyā bhikkhuno tathārūpo samādhipaṭilābho yathā neva pathaviyaṃ pathavisaññī assa…pe… yampidaṃ diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā tatrāpi na saññī assa; saññī ca pana assā”ti?

“But how, venerable sir, could there be for a bhikkhu such an attainment of concentration whereby he would not be percipient of earth in regard to earth... and so on... and even in regard to what is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought after, and repeatedly considered by the mind, he would not be percipient; and yet he would still be percipient?”

“Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu evaṃsaññī hoti – ‘etaṃ santaṃ etaṃ paṇītaṃ, yadidaṃ sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbāna’nti[T 5.0387].
