Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa.

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Vessantarāgīti

The Recitation of Vessantara

[Ka]

[Ka]

Bāttiṃsalakkhaṇūpeta[M 0.0001],

Dātabbaṃ dadataṃ vara;

Vessantaratta māpādi,

Sañcaraṃ bhavasāgare.

Endowed with the thirty-two marks, the best of givers of what ought to be given, he attained the state of Vessantara while wandering in the ocean of existence.

[Kha]

[Kha]

Dvandaṃdvandaṃ [M 0.0002] sañcarantī,

Janānande yasodhīre;

Maddittaṃ samupāgañchi,

Tvaṃpitena bhavaṇṇave.

Wandering in pairs, O famed and steadfast one, delighting the people; you came to be subdued by your father in the ocean of becoming.

[Ga]

[Ga]

Tumhaṃdāni pavakkhāmi,

Upattiṃ nandiniṃ tahiṃ;

Dajjahvovo mamokāsaṃ,

Kavayo pyābhinandatha.

Now I will declare to you the delightful origin of this; grant me leave, and may the poets also rejoice.

1.

1.

Ekanavutehito kappe,

Vipassīnāma nāyako;

Vineyye [M 0.0003] tārayaṃkhemaṃ,

Loke uppajjicakkhumā.

Ninety-one aeons ago, the leader named Vipassī, leading to safety those who were to be trained, arose in the world as the one with the Eye.

2.

2.

Khemārāmamhi sambuddho,

Migadāye manorame;

Nissāya bandhumatiṃso,

Vihāsi nagaraṃtadā.

The Fully Enlightened One, residing in the delightful Migadāya at Khemārāma, then dwelt near the city of Bandhumatī.

3.

3.

Rajjaṃdhammena kāresi,

Nagaretamhi bandhumā;

Rājātassa duvedhītā,

Āsuṃ sabbaṅgasobhaṇā.

In that city, King Bandhumā ruled the kingdom righteously; that king had two daughters who were beautiful in all their limbs.

4.

4.

Ekohi [M 0.0004] ekadārājā,

Bandhumassa supesayi;

Sahassagghanikaṃ soṇṇa-

Mālañcā naggha candanaṃ.

Once, a certain king sent to Bandhumā a golden garland worth a thousand, and priceless sandalwood.

5.

5.

Adāsi bandhumārājā,

Pitājeṭṭhāya candanaṃ;

Soṇṇamālaṃ kaniṭṭhāya,

Tahiṃ viyāya dhītuyā.

King Bandhumā, the father, gave the sandalwood to the elder daughter and the golden garland to the younger daughter there.

6.

6.

Ubhotāpīti [M 0.0005] cintesuṃ,

Natthatthovata tehino;

Bhagyavantassa pūjema,

Saṃpasādena cetasā.

Both of them thought thus: 'Indeed, these are of no use to us. Let us honor the Blessed One with a confident heart.'

7.

7.

Tatosācandanaṃ cuṇṇaṃ-

Kārāpetvāna jeṭṭhakā;

Gantvāna vihāraṃ soṇṇa,

Vaṇṇaṃpūjesi nāyakaṃ.

Then the elder sister, having had the sandalwood ground to powder, went to the monastery and honored the golden-hued Leader.

8.

8.

Vikiritvā tu sāgandha,

Kuṭiyaṃ sesacandanaṃ;

Evañhi patthanaṃ kāsi,

Buddhamātā bhavāmahaṃ.

And having scattered the remaining sandalwood in the fragrant chamber, she thus made an aspiration: 'May I become the mother of a Buddha.'

9.

9.

Kāretvā [M 0.0006] soṇṇamālaṃtu,

Uracchada pasādanaṃ;

Pūjesi hemavaṇṇaṃsā,

Bhagavantaṃ kaniṭṭhakā.

And having had a golden garland made, an ornament for the breast, the younger sister honored the golden-hued Blessed One.

10.

10.

Evampi patthanaṃkāsi,

Idaṃmama pasādanaṃ;

Sarīre niccalā ṭhātu,

Carantiyā bhavābhavaṃ.

She also made this aspiration: 'May this adornment of mine remain immovably on my body as I wander from one state of existence to another.'

11.

11.

Ubhopi [M 0.0007] rājakaññātā,

Ṭhatvāna yāvatā yukaṃ;

Upapajjiṃsu sovaggaṃ,

Cutāsukha pamodanaṃ.

Both those royal maidens, having lived for the duration of their lifespan, passed away and were reborn in heaven, rejoicing in happiness.

12.

12.

Manussadeva lokesu,

Saṃsariṃsu sukhaṃduve;

Ekanavuti kappāni,

Muccitvā pāyadukkhato.

For ninety-one aeons, the two wandered happily in the worlds of humans and gods, freed from the suffering of the lower realms.

13.

13.

Kassapassa kāletāsu,

Kaniṭṭhā kikirājino;

Dhītā uracchadānāma,

Hutvāna parinibbutā.

In the time of Kassapa, the younger of them, the daughter of King Kiki, named Uracchadā, attained final Nibbāna.

14.

14.

Jeṭṭhakā [M 0.0008] pana dhītāsi,

Kikissa rājino tahiṃ;

Dhammānāma damitā sā,

Komāra brahmacārinī.

But the elder daughter of King Kiki there was named Dhammā; she was self-controlled, a maiden observing the holy life.

15.

15.

Tatoca vitvāna sā deva,

Manussesu punappunaṃ;

Saṃsarantī mahesīsi,

Pūrindadassa ekadā.

And having passed away from there, she, wandering again and again among gods and humans, was once the chief queen of Pūriṇḍada.

16.

16.

Rattacandana [M 0.0009] lepena,

Limpita dehinī hisā;

Katapuñña visesena,

Nāmena phussatīhvitā.

Her body was indeed anointed with a paste of red sandalwood; due to the excellence of her accumulated merit, she was called by the name Phussatī.

17.

17.

Devindo aññadādisvā,

Parikkhīṇāyukaṃ viyaṃ;

Deviṃ taṃ parivārena,

Mahatā nesinandanaṃ.

Then the lord of gods, seeing that goddess whose lifespan was, as it were, exhausted, led her with a great retinue to the Nandana grove.

18.

18.

Sirīsayana piṭṭhamhi,

Taṃsayāviya māghavā;

Ṭhito [M 0.0010] sayanapassamhi,

Phussatiṃ etadabravī.

As she lay upon the splendid couch, Māghavā, standing by the side of the couch, spoke this to Phussatī.

19.

19.

Varetedasadassāmi,

Phussatevaravaṇṇike;

Tvaṃñhiicchasiyeladdhuṃ,

Labhāpessāmitevare.

I shall give you ten boons, O Phussatī of excellent complexion; whatever you wish to obtain, I shall cause you to obtain those boons.

20.

20.

Sutvātaṃ phussatīdevī,

Lomahaṃ sanarūpinī;

Khīṇāyutamajānantī,

Attano etadabravī.

Hearing that, the goddess Phussatī, whose form was thrilling, not knowing that her own lifespan was exhausted, spoke this.

21.

21.

Pāpaṃnu [M 0.0011] tvayi deviṃnda,

Kataṃme appiyāthate;

Rammāme cavanaṃ icchaṃ,

Maññebhaṇaṃti īdisaṃ.

'O lord of gods, what evil have I done to you, that I am displeasing to you? I delight in this life, yet you desire my passing away—why, I wonder, do you say such a thing?'

22.

22.

Natebhadde kataṃpāpaṃ,

Nacame appiyātuvaṃ;

Puññaṃtute parikkhīṇaṃ,

Tasmā te vaṃvadāmahaṃ.

'No evil have you done, dear lady, nor are you displeasing to me; but your merit is exhausted, therefore I speak thus to you.'

23.

23.

Santike [M 0.0012] maraṇaṃ tuyhaṃ,

Vinābhāvo bhavissati;

Paṭiggaṇhāhi me ete,

Varedasa pavacchato.

'Death is near for you, separation will come to be; accept these ten boons from me as I bestow them.'

24.

24.

Sakkassavacanaṃ sutvā,

Ñātvāmaraṇa mattano;

Varesā laddhumicchantī,

Devindameta dabravī.

Having heard Sakka's words and knowing her own death, she, desiring to obtain the boons, spoke this to the lord of the gods.

25.

25.

Ito cutāsahassakkhi,

Sīvirājanivesane;

Mahesī phussatīnāma,

Nīlanettā yathāmigī.

'Having passed away from here, O thousand-eyed one, in the palace of King Sivi may I become the chief queen named Phussatī, with eyes blue like those of a doe.'

26.

26.

Assaṃ [M 0.0013] nīlabhamukāhaṃ,

Labheyyaṃ puttamuttamaṃ;

Dhārenti yācamegabbhaṃ,

Majjhimaṅgaṃ anunnataṃ.

'May I have blue-black eyebrows, and may I obtain a most excellent son; and while I am carrying him in the womb, may my belly be of moderate size and not protruding.'

27.

27.

Palitāna virūhantu,

Alambāca payoro;

Kāyerajo nalimpetha,

Vajjhañcāpi pamocaye.

'May grey hairs not grow upon me, and may my breasts not be pendulous; may dust not adhere to my body, and may I be able to free one condemned to death.'

28.

28.

Yeyācitā [M 0.0014] varābhadde,

Tayādasa mayātava;

Dinnāte tuṭṭhacittena,

Sabbelacchasi mānuse.

'Whatever ten boons you have asked, O gracious lady, they are given to you by me with a joyful mind; you shall obtain them all in the human world.'

29.

29.

Tatocavitvāna sādeva-

Lokā nibbatti icchiyaṃ;

Mahesiyā paṭisandhi-

Vasena maddarājino.

Then, having passed away from that deva-world, she was reborn as she had wished, taking conception in the chief queen of the King of Madda.

30.

30.

Dasamā [M 0.0015] saccayenesā,

Dhītaraṃ maddarājinī;

Heṭṭhāsusetacchattassa,

Vijāyi seṭṭharūpiniṃ.

At the end of ten months, the queen of Madda, beneath a very white parasol, gave birth to a daughter supreme in beauty.

31.

31.

Rattacandana lepena,

Esālimpi tadehinī;

Tenassā brāhmaṇānāmaṃ,

Akaṃsu phussatīiti.

With an ointment of red sandalwood her body was anointed; therefore the brahmins gave her the name Phussatī.

32.

32.

Saññākāra doḷāruḷa-

Maṅgalādīni kātuna;

Mahatā parihārena,

Posiṃsu khattiyāniyo.

Having performed the naming ceremony, the cradle ceremony, and other such auspicious rites, the royal ladies nurtured her with great care.

33.

33.

Madhūrakhīra [M 0.0016] sampannā,

Siniddhapīṇa yobbanā;

Dhātiyo khattiyānītaṃ,

Khīraṃpāyiṃsu yāpanaṃ.

Nurses, endowed with sweet milk, sleek, plump, and youthful, gave the khattiya maiden milk for her sustenance.

34.

34.

Saṅkamantīva vuddhāsā,

Aṅkāaṅkaṃ karākaraṃ;

Rājakaññāca tosiṃsu,

Naccagītehi nāṭakī.

As she grew, passing from lap to lap and from hand to hand, dancers and actresses delighted the royal maiden with dances and songs.

35.

35.

Devaccharāvarūpena,

Subhāsoḷasa vassikā;

Harantī [M 0.0017] nettarasaṃsā,

Passantānaṃsukhedhitā.

With the excellent form of a celestial nymph, beautiful and sixteen years of age, she captivated the eyes and increased the happiness of all who saw her.

36.

36.

Siviraṭṭhe tadārajjaṃ,

Sivirājā jetuttare;

Kāresisiñcayonāma,

Puttotassa mahāyaso.

At that time, in Jetuttara in the Sivi kingdom, King Sivi caused his son of great fame, named Siñcaya, to rule.

37.

37.

Vayappatto bhirūposi,

Devaputtova siñcayo;

Aroha pariṇāhena,

Nānāsibbesu kovido.

Having come of age, Siñcaya was exceedingly handsome, like a son of a deva, endowed with height and girth, and skilled in various arts.

38.

38.

Siñcayassa [M 0.0018] niyyādesi,

Rajjaṃputtassa attano;

Jīvanto passituṃrāja-

Siriṃicchaṃ mahārahaṃ.

He entrusted the kingdom to his own son, Siñcaya, desiring while still living to see his great and worthy royal splendor.

39.

39.

Ānetvā maddarājassa,

Dhītaraṃ phussatiṃtahiṃ;

Soḷasitthi sahassānaṃ,

Mahesiṃkāsi jeṭṭhikaṃ.

Having brought there Phussatī, the daughter of the King of Madda, he made her the chief queen among sixteen thousand women.

40.

40.

Phussatīsiñcayoññoñña- [M 0.0019]

Mukhaṃdvepiyacakkhunā;

Passamānāvaacchiṃsu,

Aññamaññapiyaṃvadā.

Phussatī and Siñcaya, the two of them, dwelt together, looking upon each other's faces with loving eyes and speaking kindly to one another.

41.

41.

Athacinti yadevindo,

Dinnāphussatiyāmayā;

Varādasa tesuputta-

Varonahisamijjhati.

Then the lord of the gods thought: 'Of the ten boons given by me to Phussatī, the excellent boon concerning a son is indeed not fulfilled.'

42.

42.

Samijjhā pesāmietaṃti,

Ajeyyebhidasālaye;

Upadhāritthatissāya,

Puttabhāvārahaṃmaruṃ.

'I shall cause this to be fulfilled.' Thus he thought, and in the unconquerable abode of the gods, he considered a deva who was worthy of becoming her son.

43.

43.

Cavitvāhuuddhaṃgāmī[M 0.0020],

Bodhidevotatotahiṃ;

Brahmaṃgantvāsahassakkhī,

Tassataṃetadabravī.

Sahassakkha, having approached the Bodhi-deva who was about to pass away from there and go upward, spoke this to him:

44.

44.

Ito bho sugatiṃgaccha,

Anukampāhimarisa;

Siñcayaṃdeviyaṃtassa,

Sandhiṃ gaṇhajutindhara.

'Go hence, good sir, to a happy destiny. Out of compassion, O noble friend, take conception in the queen of Siñcaya, O radiant one.'

45.

45.

Aññepi [M 0.0021] saṭṭhisahassa-

Deve cavana dhammine;

Upasaṅkamma soyāci,

Gacchahvo sugatiṃiti.

He also approached sixty thousand other gods who were due to pass away, and entreated them, saying: 'Go forth to a happy destiny!'

46.

46.

Bodhisattoca aññeca,

Sādhūti sampaṭicchisuṃ;

Tatocutā catedevā,

Āguṃ mānusakaṃimaṃ.

The Bodhisatta and the others accepted, saying, 'It is good!'; then those devas, having passed away from there, came to this human world.

47.

47.

Phussatyaṃhi [M 0.0022] mahāsatto,

Nibbatti paṭisandhiyā;

Kulesvaññeca maccānaṃ,

Nibbattiṃsu visuṃvisuṃ.

The great being was reborn through conception in Phussatī, and the others were reborn separately in other families of mortals.

48.

48.

Kucchiṅgate mahāsatte,

Devī do haḷinīahu;

Dātukāmā mahādānaṃ,

Visajjiya buhuṃdhanaṃ.

When the great being had entered her womb, the queen experienced a pregnancy craving: desiring to give great gifts, she distributed much wealth.

49.

49.

Rājamandiradvāramhi,

Vemajjhenagarassaca;

Catudvāresuchaddāna-

Sālāyo deyyapūritā.

At the gate of the royal palace, in the middle of the city, and at the four city gates, six alms-halls were established, filled with things to be given.

50.

50.

Kārāpetvāna [M 0.0023] chassata-

Sahassāni dinedine;

Visajjitvāna sabbesaṃ,

Icchantī āsidātave.

Causing six hundred thousand to be spent each day and given away to all, she was ever desirous of giving.

51.

51.

Utvātaṃ kāraṇaṃrājā,

Pakkosāviya brāhmaṇe;

Nimitta pāṭhakepucchi,

Kiṃ bhavissatītiñātave.

The king, having heard of this matter, summoned the brahmins who were interpreters of omens and asked them, wishing to know what would come to pass.

52.

52.

Jānāma no mahārāja,

Gatokucchimhi deviyā;

Hehitya [M 0.0024] bhiratodāne,

Evaṃvadiṃsu brāhmaṇā.

'We know, O great king, that an embryo has entered the queen's womb. He will be one who delights in giving.' Thus spoke the brahmins.

53.

53.

Brāhmaṇānaṃ vacosutvā,

Narindo tuṭṭhamānaso;

Dānaṃadāsi chaddāna-

Sālāyo suṭṭhumāviya.

Hearing the words of the brahmins, the king, with a joyful mind, gave alms and had six alms-halls very well established.

54.

54.

Paṭisandhiggahaṇamhā,

Bodhisattassa viññuno;

Paṭṭhāyaṇṇava vegova,

Lābhoḷāro anappako.

From the moment of the wise Bodhisatta's conception, great and abundant gain arose, starting like the surge of the ocean.

55.

55.

Paṇṇākāraṃ [M 0.0025] pahiṇiṃsu,

Rājino jambumaṇḍale;

Bodhisattā nubhāvena,

Nānāraṭṭhindakhattiyā.

By the power of the Bodhisatta, kings and khattiyas from various lands sent gifts to the king in the Jambu realm.

56.

56.

Parihāraṃ pavacchesi,

Mahesiṃ paṭisandhiniṃ;

Rājāsukhaṃ vasāpesi,

Kāresiicchiticchitaṃ.

He proclaimed protection for the pregnant chief queen; the king caused her to dwell in happiness and had her every wish fulfilled.

57.

57.

Buddhaṅkurānubhāvena,

Janāsuṃ modamānasā;

Kālevassatimeghopi,

Subhikkhosamayoahu.

By the power of the Buddha-sprout, the people were joyful-minded; the clouds rained in due season, and it was a time of abundance.

58.

58.

Saddhiṃputtena [M 0.0026] juṇhāya,

Kiḷantiyāvamātuyā;

Mahantīratisabbesaṃ,

Janānaṃvipulātahiṃ.

As the mother played with her son in the moonlight, there arose great and abundant delight for all the people.

59.

59.

Gabbhaṃhi dhārentīdevī,

Dasamāsamhipūrite;

Nagaraṃdaṭṭhu kāmāsi,

Tadārocesirājino.

Indeed, the queen carrying the womb, when the tenth month was complete, desired to see the city and then informed the king.

60.

60.

Sādhubhaddeti [M 0.0027] vatvāna,

Puraṃdeva puraṃviya;

Rājā alaṅkarāpesi,

Deviyā pitivaḍḍhanaṃ.

Saying, 'Good, excellent!' the king had the city decorated like a city of the gods, to increase the queen's delight.

61.

61.

Āropiya tatodeviṃ,

Vicittaṃ lakkhikaṃrathaṃ;

Padakkhiṇaṃ kārāpesi,

Nagaraṃ sabbalaṅkataṃ.

Then, having placed the queen upon a variegated and auspicious chariot, he had a circumambulation of the fully decorated city made.

62.

62.

Vessānaṃ [M 0.0028] vīthiyāmajjhe,

Ṭhānāṭhānaṃ pathāpathaṃ;

Caliṃsu kammajāvātā,

Carantiyāva deviyā.

In the middle of the merchants' street, as the queen was proceeding, the winds born of kamma stirred, at an inopportune place and on an unsuitable path.

63.

63.

Utvāmaccāpavattiṃtaṃ,

Raññorociṃsusīghaso;

Sutigehaṃkārāpesi,

Taṃvīthiyaṃvasiñcayo.

The ministers, having heard of that event, quickly informed the king; Siñcaya had a birthing-house made on that very street.

64.

64.

Tatthasāvasamānāva,

Vijāyiakutobhayā;

Nimmalaṃsusuddhaṃputtaṃ,

Suvaṇṇakkhandhasannibhaṃ.

There, while she was dwelling, she gave birth, fearless from any quarter, to a spotless, very pure son, resembling a mass of gold.

65.

65.

Akkhīniummilitvāna[M 0.0029],

Nikkhantomātu kucchito;

Pasannappiyākārena,

Passittamātuyāmukhaṃ.

Having opened his eyes upon emerging from his mother's womb, with a serene and pleasing appearance, he looked upon his mother's face.

66.

66.

Amma dānaṃ dadissāmi,

Atthinu kiñci tedhanaṃ;

Hattaṃso pasāretvāna,

Itibravitahiṃ muhuṃ.

'Mother, I shall give alms. Is there any wealth of yours?' Having stretched out his hand, he spoke thus there repeatedly.

67.

67.

Sutvāmodiya [M 0.0030] taṃmātā,

Dehidānaṃ yathicchitaṃ;

Tātāti tassapādāsi,

Sahassatthavikaṃtahiṃ.

Hearing this, his mother rejoiced, saying, 'Give alms as you wish, dear son!' and there she gave him a purse containing a thousand.

68.

68.

Mahosadha vessantara-

Mahāsiddhattha jātisu;

Kathesi mātarābodhi-

Sasatto jātakkhaṇetisu.

In the Mahosadha, Vessantara, and Mahāsiddhattha births, the Bodhisatta spoke to his mother at the very moment of being born.

69.

69.

Acchera mabbhutaṃdisvā,

Janāmodiṃ sutāvade;

Apphoṭento hasantāca,

Jayanādaṃ panādayuṃ.

Seeing this wonder and marvel, the people rejoiced at that time; clapping and laughing, they raised a cry of victory.

70.

70.

Vessantaroti [M 0.0031] hvāyiṃsu,

Nāmaggahaṇa maṅgalaṃ;

Saṅkhatā brāhmaṇātassa,

Jātattā vessavīthiyaṃ.

At the auspicious name-giving ceremony, the brahmins, having deliberated, called him Vessantara, because he was born in the merchants' street.

71.

71.

Vijāta divasetassa,

Ekāākāsacārinī;

Hatthinīhi sabbasetaṃ,

Ekaṃ kuñjaraposakaṃ.

On the day of his birth, a certain sky-faring female elephant indeed brought a completely white elephant calf.

72.

72.

Bodhisattā nubhāvena,

Ānetvā maṅgalasammataṃ;

Thapetvāna pakkāmi,

Hatthisālāya sādhukaṃ.

By the power of the Bodhisatta, having brought the one considered auspicious, she placed it securely in the elephant stable and then departed.

73.

73.

Uppannattāmahāsattaṃ[M 0.0032],

Tassakatvānapaccayaṃ;

Akaṃsunāgarānāmaṃ,

Samaggāpaccayoiti.

Because the great being had been born, making him the reason, they, united, gave the elephant the name 'Paccaya', meaning 'Support'.

74.

74.

Taddinevavijāyiṃsu,

Amaccānaṃkumārakā;

Gehesusaṭhisahassā,

Mahāsattasajātakā.

On that very day, sixty thousand sons of ministers were born in their houses, born at the same time as the Great Being.

75.

75.

Atidīghādayodose[M 0.0033],

Vivajjetvānasiñcayo;

Kumāraṃcatussaṭhikā,

Upaṭṭhāpesidhātiyo.

King Siñcaya, having avoided those with faults such as being too tall, appointed sixty-four nurses to attend to the prince.

76.

76.

Alambatthaniyocetā,

Bhirūpāyobbaneṭhitā;

Āsuṃmadhurathaññāyo,

Saṃsuddhāseṭṭhakoliyo.

They were very beautiful and established in youth, possessed of sweet breast-milk, and of the purest, most excellent Koliyan lineage.

77.

77.

Sususaṭṭhisahassānaṃ[M 0.0034],

Ekekādhātiyotathā;

Buddhaṅkurasajātānaṃ,

Upaṭṭhāpesisuṃndarā.

Thus, for each of the sixty thousand born together with the Buddha-sprout, a beautiful nurse was appointed to attend them.

Samāgantā ujjhāyiṃsu,

Titthe amittatāpanaṃ.

Having thus consulted, they gathered at the river while fetching water and grumbled against Amittatāpanā at the ford.

484.

Amittānunatemātā[M 0.0202],

Amittonunatepitā;

Yesaṃjiṇṇassapādaṃsu,

Evaṃ dahariyaṃ satiṃ.

“Surely your mother is no friend, surely your father is no friend, who gave you, being so young, to an old man.”

485.

485.

Ahitaṃvatateññātī,

Mantayiṃsu rahogatā;

Yetaṃjiṇṇassa pādaṃsu,

Evaṃ dahariyaṃsatiṃ.

“Indeed, your relatives plotted what is unbeneficial for you, conferring in secret, they who gave you, being so young, to an old man.”

486.

486.

Amanāpavāsaṃvasati[M 0.0203],

Jiṇṇenapatināsaha;

Yātvaṃvasasijiṇṇassa,

Matantejīvitāvaraṃ.

“One lives an unpleasant life with an aged husband. For you, who live with an old man, death is better than life.”

487.

487.

Nahinunatevindiṃsu,

Mātāpitācasobhaṇe;

Tuyhatthāyajūcakamhā,

Patiṃaññaṃsuyobbanaṃ.

“Surely your mother and father, O beautiful one, did not find for you another very youthful husband instead of Jūjaka.”

488.

488.

Duyiṭṭhaṃtenavamiyā,

Akataṃaggihutakaṃ;

Yetaṃjiṇṇassapādaṃsu,

Evaṃdahariyaṃsatiṃ.

“Wrong sacrifices have they performed, the fire offering they have not made, they who gave you, being so young, to an old man.”

489.

489.

Samaṇe [M 0.0204] brāhmaṇe suddhe,

Akkosi brāhmacārine;

Yenatenadaharītvaṃ,

Maññejiṇṇenasaṅgamī.

“One must have reviled pure ascetics and brahmins, celibate ones; for that reason, I think, you, though young, have come together with an old man.”

490.

490.

Nadukkhaṃahinādaṭṭhaṃ,

Nadukkhaṃsattiyāhataṃ;

Tañcadukkhañcatibbañca,

Yaṃpassejiṇṇakaṃpatiṃ.

“Not painful is the bite of a snake, not painful is being struck by a spear; but that pain is truly intense, when one sees an aged husband.”

491.

491.

Natthikhiṭṭānatthirati[M 0.0205],

Jiṇṇenapatināsaha;

Natthiallāpasallāpo,

Jagghituṃvinasobhati.

“There is no sport, there is no delight with an aged husband. There is no pleasant conversation; even to laugh is not seemly.”

492.

492.

Yadādaharodaharī,

Mantayiṃsurahogatā;

Sabbasokāvinassanti,

Yekecihadayassitā.

“When a young man and a young woman confer together in a secret place, all sorrows vanish, whatever are based in the heart.”

493.

493.

Daharītvaṃrūpātī,

Purisātvamabhipatthitā;

Gaccha ñātikule accha,

Kiṃ jiṇṇo ramayissati.

“You are young and beautiful, desired by men. Go, stay in your relatives’ home. What delight can an old man provide?”

494.

494.

Rodamānāvaāgañchi[M 0.0206],

Sāgharaṃparibhāsitā;

Brāhmaṇīhi hirāyantī,

Ghaṭamādāyasīghaso.

Reviled by the brahmin women, she, ashamed and weeping, quickly took her water pot and came home.

495.

495.

Rodantiṃ āgataṃjāyaṃ,

Disvākiṃ bhotirodiya;

Āgaccha sītipucchittha,

Paccuggacchaṃ vajūcako.

Seeing his wife returning weeping, Jūjaka went out to meet her and asked, “Why do you weep, lady, as you return?”

496.

496.

Natebrāhmaṇa [M 0.0207] gacchāmi,

Nadiṃ udakahāriyā;

Thiyomaṃparibhāsiṃsu,

Tayājiṇṇenabrāhmaṇa.

“I will not go to the river to fetch water for you, brahmin. The women reviled me because of you, an old brahmin.”

497.

497.

Brāhmaṇyābhāsamānāya,

Kathaṃsutvānabrāhmaṇo;

Tosentobhariyaṃruṇṇaṃ,

Jiṇṇosoetadabravī.

Having heard the words of the speaking brahmin woman, that old brahmin, consoling his weeping wife, said this:

498.

498.

Māmetvaṃakarākammaṃ,

Mameudakamāhari;

Ahaṃudakamāhissaṃ,

Mābhotikupitāahu.

“Do not do work for me, do not fetch water for me. I will fetch the water. Do not be angry, lady.”

499.

499.

Nāhaṃtamhikulejātā[M 0.0208],

Yaṃtvaṃudakamāhari;

Evaṃbrāhmaṇajānāhi,

Natevacchāmihaṃghare.

“I was not born in a family where you would fetch water. Know this, brahmin: I will not live in your house.”

500.

500.

Sacemedāsaṃdāsiṃ vā,

Nānayissasibrāhmaṇa;

Evaṃbrāhmaṇajānāhi,

Natevacchāmasantike.

“If you do not bring me a male or female slave, O brahmin, know this, O brahmin: I will not dwell near you.”

501.

501.

Natthime [M 0.0209] sibbaṭṭhānaṃvā,

Dhanadhaññañca brahmaṇi;

Kutotaṃ dāsaṃdāsiṃ vā,

Ānayissāmi bhotiyā,

Ahaṃbhotiṃ upaṭṭhissaṃ,

Mābhotiku pitāahu.

“I have no craft or position, nor wealth and grain, O brahmin woman. From where could I bring a male or female slave for you, lady? I will attend to you myself, lady. Do not be angry, lady.”

502.

502.

Ehite aha makkhissaṃ,

Yathāmevacanaṃsutaṃ;

Esavessantaro rājā,

Vaṅkevasati pabbate.

“Come, I will tell you, according to the word that I have heard: King Vessantara dwells on Mount Vaṅka.”

503.

503.

Taṃtvaṃgantvāna yācassu,

Dāsaṃdāsiñca brahmaṇa;

Sotedassati yācito,

Dāsaṃdāsiñca khatthiyo.

Go there and beg, O brahmin, for a male and female slave. He, the khattiya, when asked, will give you a male and female slave.

504.

504.

Jiṇṇohasmi [M 0.0210] dubbalo,

Dīghocaddhāsu duggamo;

Kathaṃgantuṃ sakkhissami,

Upaṭhissāmi taṃ ahaṃ.

I am old and weak, and the path is long and very difficult to traverse. How shall I be able to go? I will attend upon you.

505.

505.

Yathāagantvā saṅgāmaṃ,

Ayuddhova parājito,

Evameva tuvaṃbrahme,

Agantvāva parājito.

Just as one, without going to battle, is defeated without fighting, just so, O brahmin, you are defeated without even going.

506.

506.

Saceme [M 0.0211] dāsaṃdāsiṃ vā,

Nānayissasi brahmaṇa;

Evaṃ brahmaṇa jānāhi,

Natevacchā mahaṃghare;

Amanāpaṃ tekarissāmi,

Taṃtedukkhaṃ bhavissati.

If you do not bring me a male or female slave, O brahmin, know this, O brahmin: I will not stay in your house. I will make it unpleasant for you, and that will be sorrow for you.

507.

507.

Nakkhatte utupupphesu,

Yadāmaṃdakkhasi laṅkataṃ;

Aññehi saddhiṃ ramamānaṃ,

Tantedukkhaṃ bhavissati.

When you see me adorned at festivals, in seasons, and with flowers, enjoying myself with others, that will be sorrow for you.

508.

508.

Adassanena [M 0.0212] mayhaṃte,

Jiṇṇassa paridevato;

Bhiyyovaṅkā palitāca,

Bahūhessanti brahmaṇa.

Through my absence from you, as you lament, old man, you will become even more bent and have more gray hairs, O brahmin.

509.

509.

Tato so brahmaṇo bhīto,

Brahmaṇiyāva sānugo;

Aṭṭito kāmarāgena,

Brahmaṇiṃ etadabravī.

Then that brahmin, terrified, devoted to his wife, and afflicted by sensual lust, spoke thus to the brahmin woman:

510.

510.

Pātheyyaṃmekarohitvaṃ[M 0.0213],

Saṃkulyāsakalānica;

Madhupiṇḍikācasukatāyo,

Satthu bhattañcabrahmaṇi.

Prepare provisions for me, O brahmin woman: all kinds of rice cakes, well-made honey balls, and food for the journey.

511.

511.

Ānayissaṃ methunake,

Ubhodāsakumārake;

Tetaṃparicarissanti,

Rattiṃdivamatanditā.

I will bring back a pair, both a young male and female slave. They will attend to you night and day, unwearied.

512.

512.

Sākhippaṃpaṭiyādesi[M 0.0214],

Pātheyyaṃmittatāpanā;

Paṭiyāditanti ācikkhi,

Brahmaṇassaalakkhino.

She quickly prepared the provisions, she who torments friends. She told the unlucky brahmin, 'It is ready.'

513.

513.

Gehedubbalaṭhānaṃso,

Thiraṃkatvānabrahmaṇo;

Bhariyāyagopanatthaṃ,

Guttadvārañcasaṅkhari.

Having made firm the weak places in the house, the brahmin secured the doors for the purpose of guarding his wife.

514.

514.

Dārūniāharitvāna,

Ghaṭepūresivārinā;

Jāyāyasukhavāsatthaṃ,

Anikkhantuṃcagehato.

Having brought wood and filled the pots with water for his wife's comfortable dwelling, so that she would not go out from the house,

515.

515.

Paccakkheyeva [M 0.0215] jāyāya,

Isivesaṃsamādiya;

Passitvābhariyaṃevaṃ,

Ovadesisajūcako.

In the very presence of his wife, having taken up the ascetic's garb, that Jūcaka, looking upon his wife, advised her thus:

516.

516.

Itopaṭhāyatvaṃbhadde,

Vikālemāssunikkhami;

Mamaāgamanāyāva,

Vasesiappamattakā.

From now on, dear one, do not go out at the wrong time. Until my return, you should dwell heedfully.

517.

Idaṃvatvānalaggitvā[M 0.0216],

Sopātheyyapasibbahaṃ;

Upāhanañcaāruyha,

Gaṇhīkattaradaṇḍakaṃ.

Having said this, he slung his provision bag over his shoulder, put on his sandals, and grasped his walking stick.

518.

518.

Tatosoruṇṇamukhena,

Bhariyaṃkatvāpadakkhiṇaṃ;

Socamāno vapakkāmi,

Dāsake pariyesituṃ.

Then, with a tearful face, having respectfully circumambulated his wife, grieving, he departed to seek for servants.
