Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Mahāpaṇāmapāṭha

The Great Homage

(Buddhavandanā)

Veneration to the Buddha

1.

1.

So[M 0.0041], ko; Ne, saṃ;

He, who? Not, indeed.

2.

2.

Satthā, nesaṃ; Khemaṃ, dātā;

The Teacher for them; the Giver of security.

3.

3.

Heḷāta-kkāro so; Jantūnaṃ, no mā nu;

He is no agent of contempt for beings; homage to him.

4.

4.

So lokandhaṃ, hantvā nakko;

Sattambojaṃ, bodhetā nu.

Having destroyed the world's darkness, he is no monster; he is indeed the awakener of the lotus of beings.

5.

5.

Jinasūriyo, tibhavanabhe;

Muhatimiraṃ, abhitapaye.

The Conqueror-Sun in the sky of the three worlds burns away the darkness of delusion.

6.

6.

Jeno jinamūle, disvā janakāyaṃ;

Saṃsāranimuggaṃ, sabbaññuta micchaṃ.

The Conqueror, seeing at the foot of the Jina tree the host of people submerged in saṃsāra, desiring omniscience,

7.

7.

Hitvā karupetaṃ, mokkhaṃ paṇidhānaṃ;

Vattesi dayāṇā, yo taṃ muni vande.

He who, endowed with compassion, having abandoned the aspiration for his own liberation, set his course with compassion and knowledge; that sage I venerate.

8.

8.

Sutāyubhariyā me, dhanaṅgamapi cajjaṃ.

Cinaṃ budhinidāne, agā muniyaṃ metaṃ.

“My son, life, and wife, my wealth and limbs too are to be given up. For the cause of enlightenment I accumulated this; I went towards this sagehood.”

9.

9.

So sasajātiya dehaṃ, cajjiya dānavarañca;

Nāgusabho varasīlaṃ, pūrayi samparicāgo.

He, a noble bull-elephant and a complete sacrificer, having given up his own body and the best of gifts, fulfilled supreme virtue.

10.

10.

Nekkhammaggaṃ rājā hutvā, senabbidvā paññāseṭṭhaṃ;

Vedehindo vīrukkaṃsaṃ, khantīvādī khantīseṭṭhaṃ.

As a king, he fulfilled supreme renunciation; supreme in wisdom, he routed the army. As the king of Videha, he was eminent in energy; as the Preacher of Patience, he was supreme in forbearance.

11.

11.

So sutasomo tathagaṃ, temiya dhiṭṭhānavaraṃ;

Ekabhugo mettavaraṃ, lomahasopekkhataraṃ.

As Sutasoma, he fulfilled supreme truthfulness; as Temiya, supreme determination; as Ekarāja, supreme loving-kindness; and as Lomahaṃsa, he was preeminent in equanimity.

12.

12.

Pāramī [M 0.0042] tidukkarāva, pūriyāna seṭṭhabodhi;

Pāpuṇittha yo ananta-dhammasāradaṃ namāmi.

I bow to him who, having fulfilled the exceedingly difficult perfections, attained supreme enlightenment and endless mastery of the Dhamma.

13.

13.

Pahāya cakkavattikaṃ, karopanīta maddiyaṃ;

Bhusidhare sasenakaṃ, ajeni pāpimaṃ name.

Having abandoned universal kingship, he conquered the Evil One with his army on the mountain; to him I bow.

14.

14.

Athuttaraṃ jayākaraṃ, bahūpakārataṃ jino;

Paṭicca nimmisakkhibhi, udikkhate namāmi taṃ.

I bow to the unsurpassed, victory-making, greatly beneficial Conqueror, who gazes upon dependent origination with unblinking eyes.

15.

15.

Caṅkame maruvimati yo, chindayaṃ kami rataniye;

Iddhipāṭihiraṇakaro, cakkhumaṃ tamabhipaṇame.

I pay homage to that maker of miracles, the Seeing One, who on the walkway cut off doubt and desire for the delightful.

16.

16.

Atha ratanaghare buddho, chadidhiti janayaṃ saṅkhyaṃ;

Sīmīsā tamabhidhammaṃ yo, suranaramahitaṃ vande.

Then in the Jeweled House, the Buddha, generating six-colored rays, contemplated the Abhidhamma; I venerate him, who is honored by gods and humans.

17.

17.

Nigrodhantika nubbhavī sukhaṃ, dhammaṃ yo vicinaṃ nadhīvaro;

Kicchāladdha manaññabodhiyaṃ, vande taṃ sugataṃ nigrocaraṃ.

Near the Banyan tree, the great sage experienced bliss while investigating the Dhamma. I venerate that Sugata, who frequents the Banyan, whose unique enlightenment was won with difficulty.

18.

18.

Sabbhogehi tha mucalindena,

Nāgindena rahasi pāvutto;

Sokhyaṃ yo nubhavi vimuttiṃtaṃ,

Vande mārāji atulappañño.

I venerate that conqueror of Māra, of incomparable wisdom, who, sheltered in solitude by all the coils of Mucalinda, the Nāga king, experienced the bliss of liberation.

19.

19.

Kāruṇiko rājāyatane yo,

Bhoja muḷāraṃ bhojjarasekaṃ;

Vāsa makā nantagguṇadhāro,

Ekagato vandāmi mahesī.

I venerate that solitary Great Seer, the compassionate one, bearer of endless qualities, who dwelt at the Rājāyatana tree and partook of the sublime food.

20.

20.

Keyyaṃ keyyaṃ abhijayaketuṃ,

Jeyyaṃ jeyyaṃ varajayapānaṃ;

Peyyaṃ peyyaṃ suvici name taṃ,

Neyyaṃ neyyaṃ samatamupekkhiṃ.

I bow to him: the banner of super-victory for what is to be known; the excellent drink of victory over what is to be conquered; the one of excellent investigation concerning what is to be drunk; the one who has attained equanimity concerning what is to be led to.

21.

21.

Satta ca sattāhe vijitāvī,

Khepiya nigrodhaṃ puna gantvā;

Dhammasududdasyaṃ pati jāto,

Dhammakathā pposukka vitakko.

Having spent seven weeks as a conqueror, he went again to the Banyan tree. A thought of disinclination to teach the Dhamma arose, that the Dhamma he had realized was very difficult to see.

22.

22.

Marugaṇehi [M 0.0043] brahmunā samaṃ,

Ratanadāmameruṇā dadā;

Samabhiyācito paṭissavaṃ,

Dasabalo namāmi desituṃ.

Requested by the host of gods along with Brahmā, the Ten-Powered One, like Meru with a jeweled garland, gave his consent to teach; to him I bow.

23.

23.

Gantvā isippātakkānanañca,

Satthā migaddāyaṃ desayittha;

Saccappakāraṃ yo dhammacakka-

Suttaṃ name chabbīdhaṃsu tāva.

Having gone to the forest of Isipatana, the Teacher taught in the Deer Park. I bow to him who taught the Dhammacakka Sutta, which explains the nature of the truths.

24.

24.

Rañño magadhassa katappaṭiñño,

Gantvānatha rājagahaṃ vineti;

Satte malarogayute gadaggaṃ,

Pāyetuna taṃ paṇame bhisakkaṃ.

Having made a promise to the king of Magadha, he then went to Rājagaha to train beings afflicted with the disease of defilements. To make them drink the supreme medicine, I bow to that great physician.

25.

25.

Sakyādhivāse kapile saññatiṃ,

Mānaddhajaṃ bhindiya middhivātā;

Dhammāmataṃ pāyi pituppabhuti,

Vandāmi sakyinda manomadassiṃ.

In the Sakyan dwelling at Kapila, he tamed his kinsmen, breaking their banner of pride with the wind of his psychic power. He had his father and others drink the deathless nectar of the Dhamma. I venerate the Lord of the Sakyans, who is delightful to the mind.

26.

26.

Anāthapiṇḍoti vhayena nanta-

Ddhanaṃ cajitvā sukate vihāre;

Vihāsi bhiyyo janatā hitatthaṃ,

Sudhammabheriṃ vadayaṃ bhivande.

He dwelt in the well-made monastery built by Anāthapiṇḍika, who had given up endless wealth, and for the great welfare of the people, sounded the drum of the good Dhamma; to him I bow.

27.

27.

Upetapuññaṃ varalakkhaṇokaṃ-

Ṇokaṃ visuddhaṃ jalitappadīmaṃ;

Dīpaṃ pajānaṃ anighaṃ navajjaṃ-

Navajjavācaṃ sugataṃ namāmi.

Endowed with merit, the abode of excellent characteristics, pure, like a lit lamp, an island for beings, untroubled, and blameless—I bow to the Sugata of blameless speech.

28.

28.

Munibhānu dhammapabhāhi jana-

Mbuja muddhatandhatamo sutapo;

Karuṇāruṇo samabodhayi yo,

Bhavisītalattapano paṇame.

The sun of sages, of excellent practice, who with the light of the Dhamma awakened the lotus of the people from the thick darkness; the dawn of compassion, the cooler of the heat of existence—to him I bow.

29.

29.

Indu viyambaramajjhatale yo,

Rājati tāragaṇena munindo;

Ariyagaṇapparito tibhave taṃ,

Sādara muggatasobha mavandi.

Like the moon in the middle of the sky, the Lord of Sages shines with his host of stars. Surrounded by the noble assembly in the three worlds, I respectfully venerate that risen splendor.

30.

30.

Mūlāmūlā [M 0.0044] tiṃsatipāramiyo,

Sākhāsākhā majjhimajjhānakāyā;

Pattāpattā buddhacakkhuni yassa,

Pupphāpupphā kāruṇā maggakhandho.

Of which the roots and rootlets are the thirty perfections; the branches and twigs, the collection of meditative absorptions; the leaves and leaflets, the eyes of a Buddha; the flowers and blossoms, compassion and the path factors.

31.

31.

Sārāsārā yamakā pheggubhiññā,

Sabbālambaṅkurakaṃ sīlavaṇṭaṃ;

Sesādhammā madhupakkapphalāni,

Raṃsīchallī sutacā lakkhaṇāni.

Its heartwood is the twin miracles, its sapwood the higher knowledges; its stalk is virtue, its sprout all supports. The remaining dhammas are its fruits, ripe with honey; its skin is the rays, its bark the teachings and the characteristics.

32.

32.

Chāyupagā suṃ puthunaradevā,

Mārajidīpaṅkara mahijātaṃ;

Patthatanantañca kusalabījaṃ,

Ekaruhaṃ pādapamuni vande.

I venerate the Sage-Tree, grown from a single, endless seed of merit sown before Dīpaṅkara, the Conqueror of Māra. Born on this earth, many humans and gods have gone to its shade for refuge.

33.

33.

Māratitthiyaripuṃ tamañca yo,

Dhaṃsayaṃ nagaradhamma māpayaṃ;

Sīlanīvaraṇa dvārakoṭṭhakaṃ,

Esikāparamasaddha muttamaṃ.

Destroying the enemy—Māra, heretics, and darkness—he built the city of Dhamma, with virtue as its rampart and gate-tower, and supreme faith as its main pillar.

34.

34.

Dvārapālasuyataṃ satiṭālaṃ,

Ñāṇacaccara sighāṭapadiddhiṃ;

Dhammamaggakuṭisāla sahittaṃ,

Dhammasenapatisārijabhūtaṃ.

Having mindfulness as its well-restrained gatekeeper and watchtower; wisdom as its square, and the bases of psychic power as its crossroads; furnished with the huts and halls of the Dhamma path, with Sāriputta as its General of the Dhamma.

35.

35.

Dutiyasutaparohitaṃ mahā-

Dhutaguṇadharamaggha dassikaṃ;

Vinayacinaka dhammarakkhakaṃ,

Tibhavapatika dhammarājiyaṃ.

Its chaplain is the second disciple; its path-shower, the great holder of the ascetic virtues; its collector of the Vinaya; its guardian of the Dhamma. Such is the Dhamma-kingdom of the Lord of the Three Worlds.

36.

36.

Sutaṃ sutaṃ sabbadhi sajjanaṃjanaṃ,

Mitaṃ mitaṃ nantaguṇālayaṃ layaṃ;

Hutaṃ hutaṃ jantunamānamaṃ namaṃ,

Bhave bhave tebhavamaṅgalaṃ galaṃ.

Heard everywhere, delighting the virtuous; though measured, he is an immeasurable abode of endless virtues, free from attachment. Honored by offerings, he humbles the pride of beings who bow to him; in every existence, may the blessings of the three worlds flow.

37.

37.

Lokābhirāmaṃ ramaṇeyyaveduraṃ,

Lokantaguṃ antubhayānupāgataṃ;

Lokaddhajaṃ mānadhajappahārakaṃ,

Lokaggaruṃ aggaru paccaye name.

The delight of the world, a gem of loveliness; gone to the world's end, having passed beyond all fear. The banner of the world, who strikes down the banner of pride; I bow to the world's most honored one, the supreme support.

38.

38.

Makuṭaggaphalaṃ [M 0.0045] nayanañcanikaṃ,

Savanabbhusanaṃ vadanabbhusanaṃ;

Galamaṇḍanamiddhi manaññurasaṃ,

Kiriyaññudaraṃ dvikhaṇaññukaraṃ.

The fruit of the crest-jewel, the collyrium for the eyes, the ornament for the ears and the ornament for the mouth; the adornment for the neck, psychic power, a pleasing taste, the core of functional knowledge, the maker of knowledge in two moments.

39.

39.

Kaṭivibhūsana majjhimaññaṇakaṃ,

Dayavilepana dhāraṇamaṇḍanaṃ;

Paṭakhilaññunivattha manuttaraṃ,

Munimaruttama meka mahaṃ name.

His waist-adornment is the middle-way knowledge, his unguent is compassion, his ornament is retention of the Dhamma; he is clad in the unsurpassed robe of knowledge that removes barrenness. To that one, the supreme sage, I bow.

40.

40.

Sativara sucakaṃ ibbhaggapaññaṃ,

Suvīriyavasuvāhaṃ pītiselaṃ;

Gahapatisamathaggaṃ thīpasaddhiṃ,

Asaṇiyasunupekkhaṃ sattabhogaṃ.

The noble indicator of mindfulness, with the foremost wisdom of a master, the bearer of the wealth of great energy, a mountain of joy; foremost in serenity, with steadfast tranquility, unshakable equanimity, enjoying the sevenfold wealth.

41.

41.

Catudisikiddhippada masahāyaṃ,

Suparivutaṃ khattiyavirajānaṃ;

Suciparisaṃ devanara manāpaṃ,

Dasabalacakkādhipati bhivande.

Unmatched, possessing the four bases of psychic power, well-surrounded by passionless nobles; having a pure assembly, beloved by gods and humans, I worship the sovereign Lord of the Ten Powers.

42.

42.

Samādhibbahiddhaṃ saticchaṭṭhaselaṃ,

Virappañcamā jīvamajjhaṃ sukamma-

Ttatiyaṃ suvācādutiyaṃ bhiseṭṭhaṃ,

Samātakkapubbañca sattappavāraṃ.

Concentration is the fortress, mindfulness its sixth rocky battlement, energy the fifth, livelihood the center, right action the third, right speech the second and supreme, and right thought the first—the sevenfold excellence.

43.

43.

Vividhañāṇapabhutibbasākaraṃ,

Bahumahiddhika marūna māsayaṃ;

Dhuṃvapabhājalitarāmaṇeyyakaṃ,

Sugatanerunagarāja māname.

An ocean of various knowledges and powers, an abode for the fearless and those of great psychic power; delightful, blazing with a constant light, I honor the Sugata, the kingly Mount Meru.

44.

44.

Sīlajjalaṃ ratanadhamma mākaraṃ,

Bhiññāviciṃ satikulaṭṭhi gambhiraṃ;

Vitthiṇṇañāṇa mariyodajāsayaṃ,

Aṭṭhambīdhacchariyakaṃ asandanaṃ.

A mine of the jewel of the Dhamma, its water is virtue; its waves are the higher knowledges, its shore mindfulness, its depth profundity; its expanse vast knowledge, its water the noble ones—an eightfold wonder, an unmoving ocean.

45.

45.

Bahunajjamosaraṇa mekarasaṃ,

Durupaggamaṃ acitasambharinaṃ;

Puthupotapuññavatameva name,

Dvīpaduttamaṇṇava nadindavaraṃ.

The refuge for many rivers, of a single taste; difficult to approach, holding immeasurable treasures; a broad ship for the meritorious. I bow to the supreme among bipeds, the excellent ocean, the lord of rivers.

46.

46.

Abhiniharadhanuṃ [M 0.0046] guṇapāramiṃ,

Naḷamatimanabājarahammukhā;

Dhitisamitasarena sudhārayaṃ,

Malama gagaṇa mekapavedanā.

Drawing the bow of resolve, with the perfection of virtue as the diamond-tipped arrow of wisdom from a mind free of pride; with the shaft of firmness, holding it steady, he penetrates with a single shot the defilements in the sky.

47.

47.

Yo pāvedhesiṃ aggamaggañca tūṇiṃ,

Dhammālaṅkārabbamma mekabbalaṃ taṃ;

Nettiṃsānantañāṇa gīrellimantaṃ,

Sabbaññussāsaṃ iddhidaṇḍaṃ namāmi.

He who revealed the supreme path, his quiver the Dhamma's adornments, his single strength that of Brahma; his sword infinite knowledge, his song the Netti, his breath omniscience, his staff psychic power—to him I bow.

48.

48.

Varatapavuṭṭhi subījakasaddhā,

Matiyuganaṅgala hīrutapīsā;

Susamatharajju satidvijatuttaṃ,

Vīriyadurāvaha saccanidānaṃ.

The rain is noble austerity, the good seed is faith; wisdom is the plow and yoke, conscience and fear of wrongdoing the plow-shaft; serenity is the rope, mindfulness the goad; energy the strong draft animals, truth the foundation.

49.

49.

Vatisaṃvaraṃ soratamocanañcaṃ,

Saraṇā nivattabbahanatthaṃ yassa;

Amatapphalaṃ nekarasehupetaṃ,

Sugataṃ mahākassaka mābhivande.

His field is the restraint of the precepts, its clearing is gentleness, his refuge is for the sake of not turning back; its fruit is the deathless, endowed with many tastes. I venerate the Sugata, the great farmer.

50.

50.

Devaggo tidasapure varāsane yo,

Devānaṃ janikapabhutinaṃ bhidhammaṃ;

Bālakkova laḷayamācale timāsaṃ,

Desesyā paravisayaṃ name ajeyyo.

The chief of gods, who on the excellent throne in the city of the Thirty-three, taught the Abhidhamma to the gods and their progenitors; for three months, on the unmoving rock, as if at play, he taught in a realm beyond others. I bow to the Unconquered One.

51.

51.

Mahāyaso vividhasubhappakāsakaṃ,

Kurūsu yo amitaguṇo tamonudo;

Parappavādahari subhānuyoginaṃ,

Namāmi taṃ kathayi satippaṭhānakaṃ.

Of great fame, revealer of manifold goodness, he of immeasurable virtue, the dispeller of darkness in the Kuru country; a lion against the views of others, devoted to the good—I bow to him who taught the Foundations of Mindfulness.

52.

52.

Samathakapaliko sasnehasammāsati,

Paramamatiginī savaṭṭiparakkamo;

Sakalajutikaro sudhammapadīpako,

Ima mupajalito jinena namāmahaṃ.

The bowl of serenity, with the oil of right mindfulness, the wick of steadfast effort, the flame of supreme knowledge; the maker of all light, the lamp of the good Dhamma, lit by the Conqueror—to him I bow.

53.

53.

Vigatagatamalaṃ malagatavigataṃ,

Mahita hitamanaṃ manahitamahitaṃ;

Vibhavabhavakaraṃ karabhavavibhavaṃ,

Sujana janaguṇaṃ guṇajanasujanaṃ.

He from whom defilement has gone, who has gone beyond the state of defilement; honored, with a mind for the welfare of others, whose mind is for the welfare of the honored; the maker of non-becoming and becoming, from whom becoming and non-becoming are made; the virtue of good people, the good person for the virtuous.

54.

54.

Sīlaggadaṇḍavicitaṃ [M 0.0047] susamādhipattaṃ,

Sobhāsamujjala manantaga ñāṇasīkhaṃ;

Saddhammaseṭṭharatanañca tilokaketuṃ,

Vandāmi locanabhiseka susobhayuttaṃ.

Its excellent staff variegated with morality, its banner-cloth perfect concentration; radiant with splendor, its peak infinite knowledge; the supreme jewel of the good Dhamma, the banner of the three worlds—I venerate him, endowed with the beautiful anointment of vision.

55.

55.

Vinayanaya manayavinaya manamitaṃ,

Vijayajaya majayavijaya matulitaṃ;

Vibhajabhaja mabhajavibhaja mananakaṃ,

Visamasama masamavisama mabhiname.

The way of discipline, taming the undisciplined, immeasurable; victory and triumph, conquering the unconquered, incomparable; analyzing for those who partake and those who do not, he is blameless; harmonizing the unequal and the equal, the unequalled and the dissimilar—to him I bow.

56.

56.

Paramarama maramaparama matiguṇaṃ,

Pagahagati magatipagata mamamakaṃ;

Pacayacaya macayapapaya manaṇakaṃ,

Pakatakata makatapakata macalakaṃ.

Supreme in delight, deathless, possessing supreme qualities of wisdom; whose progress is firm, who has gone beyond wrong paths, who is free from my-making; who is beyond accumulation and decline, unindebted; who has done what is natural and not what is artificial, the immovable one.

57.

57.

Ujuka mayanamagge mokkhadesaṃ niyāsi,

Vararathakujarena cammacakkena satthā;

Hiritapadukapālambena dhammassutena,

Satinivarayutena ppāṭihiraddhajena.

The Teacher leads to the land of liberation by the straight path with a noble chariot as mighty as an elephant, which has wheels of leather, conscience as its foot-board and support, the Dhamma as its pole, is yoked with the restraint of mindfulness, and has miracles as its banner.

58.

58.

Avihananakkhinā suyama nemupakkharā,

Udariyamabbhinā paripuraṅgasaccinā;

Kusalavibhūsinā nimadakupparena yo,

Akharanatesinā gupitasilanandanā.

He who has unwavering eyes, is well-tamed like the rim and fittings of a chariot; whose belly is not distended, whose limbs are full and true; who is adorned with skillfulness, free from arrogance and anger; who seeks the imperishable, delighting in guarded virtue.

59.

59.

Anusanughātinā matipurejavena kāla-

Ññutamatisārike na ca visāradatthidaṇḍā;

Satituda dhītirasmi manadammasindhavena,

Vinayagaṇe namāmi ta matulyasatthavāhaṃ.

With wisdom that precedes his swiftness, striking in accord with the teaching; with essential wisdom in knowing the right time, and not wielding a harsh rod; with mindfulness as his goad and patience as his reins for the thoroughbred of the mind to be tamed—I bow to him, that peerless caravan leader, amidst the Vinaya assembly.

60.

60.

Yamakkaggijālaṃ paravisaya maccherasahitaṃ,

Dudiṭṭhandhubbāhaṃ yugagahaṇatitthīna makarī;

Bahūnaṃ majjhe yo ratanakamane pāṭihariyaṃ,

Jayakketussāpi ta mabhinami kaṇṭambasamipe.

He who, near the Kaṇḍamba tree, in the domain of others filled with stinginess, performed the miracle of the twin flames, uprooting the blindness of wrong views for the sectarians who grasp at dualities; who, in the midst of many, displayed this wonder on the jeweled walkway and raised the banner of victory—to him I bow.

61.

61.

Nakhajutirajaṃ cakkaṅgopeta pādavarambujaṃ,

Subhasirimato raṃsijālaṅgulidasasaṃsubhiṃ;

Pavarasirasā devādevā sadā na pilandhayuṃ,

Ta mativa manorammaṃ tittīkarā nami yassa ke.

His noble lotus-feet are endowed with the mark of the wheel and have ridges of splendor on the nails; the ten toes of that auspicious and glorious one shine with a web of rays. The gods of gods always adorn their noble heads with them. I bow to him, who is exceedingly delightful to the mind, to whom even partridges pay homage.

62.

62.

Buddhopyeko [M 0.0048] nidhanaguṇino vaṇṇaye yāvajīvaṃ,

Kāmaṃ aññaṃ kathamabhaṇa māsuṃ khiyethā yukappo;

Na tvevā yaṃ khaya mupavajje yassa vaṇṇo ananto,

Taṃ sabbaññuṃ sakalarirī nekanāthaṃ namāmi.

Even one Buddha, were he to praise the treasure of virtues of that virtuous one for his entire life, could not come to an end. How much less could others? An eon of life would be exhausted long before the praise was complete. For the praise of him whose glory is endless never comes to an end. I bow to that All-Knowing One, the protector of many, the refuge for all.

63.

63.

Pādidīpādaṃ dvinayanadijaṃ dhammakāyaṃ dhisoṇḍaṃ,

Bhāṇīsoṇḍaggaṃ saraṇasirasiṃ maggāvālaṃ subhaṅgaṃ;

Sīlālaṅkāraṃ vimalibhavu taṃ sattatiṭṭhidhidabbaṃ,

Nāthebhindaggaṃ phalakariṇukaṃ mokkhabhojaṃ namāmi.

I bow to him, the foremost lord, the king of elephants, who partakes of the food of liberation: the Dhamma-body whose feet are lamps, whose two eyes are birds, whose wisdom is his trunk, and his speech the tip of his trunk; whose head is the refuges and whose tail is the path, auspicious in all his limbs; who is adorned with virtue, of pure existence, the substance of the thirty-seven requisites of enlightenment, the young elephant that has attained the fruit.

64.

64.

Malāloḷullolaṃ atibhayajanaṃ duggasaṃsārasinduṃ,

Phiyabbhāniggāho sivataṭamukho nāvi kajjeṭṭhanātho;

Padappārakkāmaṃ bahujanagaṇaṃ ekamaggattaramhi,

Samāropetvā mattari ta matulasādarañcā bhivande.

Having made the multitude of people who desire the further shore embark upon the ship of the single path, the chief captain, the Lord, ferries them across the impassable ocean of saṃsāra—which is stirred up with the filth and great waves of defilements and generates great fear—in the ship whose prow is the shore of peace. To that peerless one I pay respectful homage.

65.

65.

Byāmaṃsugghanadhāra makkuḷusatabbhāṇujjalantattanaṃ,

Ukkaṃsajjuti ketumālavicitaṃ saddhammajotindharaṃ;

Bundinniggatapajjalanta didhitiṃ ajjatthanā yāva ca,

Vandetaṃ muni sakyapuṅgava mahaṃ puṇṇinduvattampi ca.

I worship that sage, the bull of the Sakyans, whose face is like the full moon; who bears a dense, fathom-wide halo, whose body shines with the light of a hundred suns, who is adorned with the crest-garland of supreme radiance, who bears the light of the True Dhamma, and whose splendor shines forth from its source, from today until the end of time.

66.

66.

Sattamaṃtamaṃ vināsakaṃsakaṃ dadaṃ vineyyakānameva,

Bhāvanaṃvanaṃ dhulīkaraṃ karaṃ tidukkaraṃ pajābhibhuñja;

Gāravaṃravaṃ manoharaṃharaṃ narānarānayaṃ namāmi,

Sādaraṃdaraṃ vinodakaṃdakaṃ pavassakaṃ pajānamiva.

I respectfully bow to the guide of men and devas, who destroys the darkness of beings, giving his own to those to be trained; who, with his hand, reduces the forest of craving to dust through meditation, having experienced the three hardships for the sake of beings; whose delightful voice, worthy of respect, takes away sorrow, dispelling fear and raining down the water of the deathless for beings.

67.

67.

Buddho nigrodhabimbo mudukaracaraṇo brahmaghoseṇijaṅgho,

Kosacchādaṅgajāto punarapi sugato suppatiṭṭhitapādo;

Mudodātuṇṇalomo athamapi sugato brahmujuggattabhāvo,

Nīlakkhī dīghapaṇhī sukhumamalachavī thomyarassaggasaggī.

The Buddha has the proportions of a banyan tree, soft hands and feet, a voice like a Brahma, and shins like an antelope. The Sugata's private parts are concealed in a sheath, and his feet are well-placed. The Sugata has a soft, white ūrṇā hair, and a body as straight as a Brahma's. He has deep blue eyes, long heels, fine and spotless skin, and is the supreme taster of tastes.

68.

68.

Cattālīsaggadanto samakalapanajo antaraṃsappapīṇo,

Cakkenaṅkītapādo aviraḷadasano mārajussaṅkhapādo;

Tiṭṭhantonomento bhayakaramudunā jaṇṇukānā masanto,

Vaṭṭakkhandho jino gotaruṇapakhumako sīhapubbaḍḍhukāyo.

The Victor has forty teeth, all of them even, and the space between his shoulders is filled out. His feet are marked with wheels, and his teeth are without gaps. Standing upright, without bending, he can touch his knees with his soft hands. He has rounded shoulders, eyelashes like a young heifer, and a torso like a lion's.

69.

69.

Sattappīṇo ca dīghaṅgulaṃ matha sugato lomakūpekalomo,

Sampannodātadāṭho kanakasamataco nīlamuddhaggalomo;

Sambuddho thūlajivho atha sīhahanuko jālikappādahattho,

Nātho uṇhīsasīso iti guṇasahitaṃ taṃ mahesiṃ namāmi.

I bow to that Great Sage endowed with these qualities: he has the seven convex surfaces, long fingers, and one hair to every pore; he is the Fortunate One with perfectly white teeth, skin the color of gold, and dark body-hairs that curl upwards; the Fully Awakened One with a broad tongue, a jaw like a lion's, and hands and feet like a net; the Protector with a cranial protuberance like a royal turban.

70.

70.

Vaṭṭacitānupubbakasubhaṅgulī [M 0.0049] ruhiramaṭṭhatuṅganakhako,

Nigguḷagopphako samapado sīhosabhibha haṃsasannibhakamo;

Dakkhiṇatāvatakkami samantamaṇḍala nigaṇṭhi jāṇusubhako,

Byañjanapuṇṇaposatanu nābhigambhīra achiddadakkhiṇavaṭo.

He has beautiful fingers that are rounded and tapering, and nails that are coppery, smooth, and high. His ankles are hidden, and his feet are even. His gait is like that of a lion, a bull, and a swan. His steps turn to the right, and his knees are well-rounded, knotless, and beautiful. His body is complete with all the marks, and his navel is deep, without hollows, and spirals to the right.

71.

71.

Dviradakarappakāsurubhujo suvibbhajanupubbamaṭṭhaanunā-

Nunaalinānupubbarucira ttilādirahitabbisuddhatanuko;

Dasasatakoṭi hatthibaladhāraṇo kanakatuṅganāsikasubho,

Suruhiradantamaṃsatha sucisiniddhadasano tha lokasaraṇo.

His beautiful arms are like an elephant's trunk; his limbs are well-proportioned, rounded, neither too long nor too short, and lovely. His body is pure, free from moles and other blemishes. He possesses the strength of ten billion elephants, and has a beautiful, high nose like gold. The gums of the world's refuge are very red, and his teeth are pure and smooth.

72.

72.

Suddhapasādindri ca vaṭṭataradāṭho ruhiroṭṭha ca suranaranātho,

Āyatasobhabbadano tha muni gambhīrujukāyatasuruciralekho;

Byāmapabhāmaṇḍalabundi supuraggaṇṇi ca āyatavisaṭasubhakkhī,

Pañcapasādakkhi ca kuñcikasubhaggapakhumo mudutanuruṇajivho.

The Lord of gods and men has faculties that are pure and clear, very rounded canine teeth, and red lips. The sage has a long and beautiful face, and the lines on his palms are deep, straight, long, and lovely. His body is surrounded by a fathom-wide halo, his cheeks are full, and his eyes are long, wide, and beautiful. His eyes have five kinds of clarity, his eyelashes are curved with beautiful tips, and his tongue is soft, thin, and red.

73.

73.

Sommasiniddha tyujjalakomala varuṇavimalatanu ca amitaguṇo,

Komala dakkhiṇāvaṭa añjanabhidasarisanilaka mudutanuruho;

Dakkhiṇavaṭṭakomala saṇusamasunila alulita siraruhi jino,

Sobhaṇasaṇṭhāno tha siniddhasiraruhi ca supacitasatakusala jo.

His body is gentle, smooth, radiant, soft, excellent, and immaculate, and he is endowed with immeasurable virtues. His soft body-hair curls to the right and is fine and dark blue like crushed collyrium. The Victor’s head-hair is soft, curls to the right, is fine, even, very dark blue, and untangled. He is of beautiful proportions, with smooth hair on his head, born of a hundred well-accumulated skillful deeds.

74.

74.

Nigguḷonigganticchattassarisaatisubhagasira cāyatāruci kaṇṇako,

Sosaṇṭhānassaṇhāhārānukamapahutabhamutha suāyatabbhamuko ca so;

Suggandhaggatto muddhicātha vadani ca puthulakanalāṭa āyatasobhaṇo,

Assāsappassāsātissaṇu dharamasamasama nami ketumālavicittakaṃ.

His head is exceedingly beautiful, like a parasol, without lumps or knots, and his ears are long and lovely. His eyebrows are well-formed, fine, orderly, and abundant, and they are beautifully long. His body has a supreme fragrance, and his head and face, with a broad forehead, are long and beautiful. His in-breaths and out-breaths are exceedingly subtle, and his lips are perfectly even. I bow to him who is adorned as with a crest-garland.

75.

75.

Buddhuppādo kimaṅgaṃ atidulabhataraṃ ghosamattampi loke,

Tasmā nānappakāraṃ saparahitasukhaṃ viddhasū patthayantā;

Yātiṭṭhatthāva haṃ taṃ suranarasaraṇaṃ antarāyappahānaṃ,

Puññakkhettekabhūtaṃ sugatamavirataṃ sādhu vandantu santo.

The arising of a Buddha is exceedingly rare in the world; how much more so even the mere sound of his name! Therefore, let the virtuous who desire various kinds of welfare and happiness for themselves and others pay homage well and unceasingly to the Sugata—the refuge for gods and humans, the dispeller of obstacles, the unique field of merit.

76.

76.

Khettavaraṅgatatthutipure javapaṇama tejasā idha bhave,

Rogabhayādyupaddavahato anunasukha bhogapuññamatiko;

Devamanussabhogapavaraṃ paratthanubhāvañca antimabhave,

Aññataro tibodhipavare bhavissati yathāsayaṃ katanato.

By the power of this swift homage and praise offered to the one who has gone to the excellent field, one will, in this very life, have all dangers such as disease and fear destroyed and possess undiminished happiness, wealth, and merit. Enjoying the excellent states of gods and humans and the power to help others, in the final existence one will become excellent in one of the three kinds of enlightenment, according to one's aspiration and deeds.

77.

77.

Puññenānena [M 0.0050] sohaṃ nipuṇajavamati pemavāco sakhīlo,

Saddho kalyāṇamittotisaraṇagamano sīlavā cāgayogo;

Hirottappī sudakkho avitasucarito dhitimā saccabhāṇī,

Bāhussacci vibhāgi saparahitakaro vaggurāvo bhirupo.

By this merit may I be one with subtle and swift wisdom, of loving speech, friendly, faithful, having good friends, one who has gone for refuge to the Three Jewels, virtuous, devoted to generosity; conscientious and scrupulous, very skillful, of well-guarded good conduct, resolute, truthful in speech; learned, discerning, working for the welfare of myself and others, with a pleasant voice, and handsome.

78.

78.

Dīghajjīvi nirogo sucikulapassuto dhammaratto viratto,

Niccāpalyo kataññu atimudujumano sādhubhāvādiviññū;

Dhammājīvo bhaveyyaṃ bahukusalarato appakodho aluddho,

Evañcaññaṃ kareyyaṃ paṇidhi carimake mokkhanibbānabhāgī.

May I be long-lived, healthy, born into a pure family, delighting in the Dhamma, and detached; constantly steadfast, grateful, with a very gentle and upright mind, discerning of what is good. May I live righteously, delighting in much that is skillful, with little anger and no greed. And thus may I make this aspiration, to be a partaker of liberation, Nibbāna, in my final existence.

79.

Cajaṃ heyyā diyā deyyaṃ, akarittha yathā tathā.

(1) Though the great commentary by Buddhaghosa was made concise, he did it accordingly: abandoning what should be rejected, and including what should be given.

80.

(2) Mahāpaṇāmaporāṇaṃ, kiñci eva punappunaṃ;

Kāmokkamaṃ dudhārañca, cajaṃ deyyā diyaññattha.

(2) From the great ancient homage, whatever was repetitive, or dealt with transcending desire and other difficult matters, I have omitted, giving what should be given and not taking from elsewhere.

81.

81.

(3) Sutajjaya anubhava-ṭṭhapanatthena lañchinā;

Sutena garunānena, katoyaṃ paṇāmo navo.

(3) This new homage has been made by the venerable Sutajjaya, marked with the seal of establishing the experience of his learning for the benefit of others.

82.

82.

(4) Ekakkharāya gāthāyo, yāva chabbisatakkharā;

Jātiya pajjasattatyā, saṅkhato caturādhikā.

(4) There are verses from one syllable up to twenty-six syllables. With the seventy verses on the birth story, the count is four more than that.

(5) Aṭṭhādhikā sahevuyyo- janādīhi midaṃ nataṃ;

Yathāvuttatthakā kāmā, ye niccaṃ dhārayantu te.

(5) With the exhortations and so on, this homage has eight more verses. May those who desire the meaning as stated herein always bear it in mind.

Rājātirājātimanoharo yo,

Devātidevātiguṇoghadhārī;

Brahmātibrahmātibhavantagū taṃ,

Saṅghātisaṅghātivirāva vande.

I bow to him who is more delightful than a king of kings, who bears a flood of virtues greater than a god of gods, who has gone to the end of existence, surpassing even a great Brahmā, the supreme one of the Saṅgha whose fame resounds.

85.

85.

Anaṅganaṅgaṃ naradevadevaṃ,

Aniñjaniñjaṃ bhayatāṇatāṇaṃ;

Anaṇḍanaṇḍañca anāthanāthaṃ,

Khayantayantaṃ paṇamāmi māmi.

I bow down to him whose limbs are without blemish, the god of gods and men; whose own state is imperturbable, the shelter and protection from fear; the mighty one without defect, the protector of the unprotected; who is proceeding to the end of all destruction.

86.

86.

Tambasiniddhatuṅganakhako nuṃvaṭṭasucituraṅguli ca muniso,

Sīhusabhobhahaṃsasamago nigūḷasama gopphakāyatamukho;

Komaladakkhāṇāvaṭatanuruho sucimalujjalābhasariro,

Pañcapasādayuttanayano sugandhamukhatuṅganāsikayuto.

That sage has high, smooth, copper-colored nails and rounded, well-adorned fingers. His gait is like that of a lion, a bull, and a swan; his ankles are concealed and even, and his face is long. His body-hair is soft and curls to the right, and his body is pure, stainless, radiant, and gleaming. His eyes are endowed with the five kinds of clarity, and he has a fragrant mouth and a high nose.

87.

87.

Koṭisahassanāgabaliko [M 0.0051] surattamadharo suvaṭṭadasano,

Āyatasaṇulomabhamūko muduttanukaratta jivhasahito;

Chattasamānasobhaṇasiro sukesavara ketumālavicito,

Iccanubyañjanebhi sahitaṃ munindapavaraṃ namāmi sirasā.

I bow my head to the excellent lord of sages, who is endowed with all the minor characteristics: he possesses the strength of a thousand koṭi of elephants, has very red lips and well-rounded teeth, long and fine-haired eyebrows, and a soft, slender, red tongue. His head is beautiful like a parasol, adorned with a crest-garland of excellent, beautiful hair.

88.

88.

Sakalamalehi so muni sudūratāya ca malāriniṃ hatatayā,

Tibhavarathe samānitamanārakāni ca namālayo naravaro;

Malakaraṇe rahāraha manantañeyya mabhijānanā muni tathā,

Caraṇayuto tivijji ca suvācatā sugamanā janesu sugato.

That sage is far removed from all stains, for he has destroyed the enemy which is defilement. He is the best of men, an abode of homage, honored in the three existences, and without attachment. He is a sage worthy of secret places, having no stain-making deeds, and he knows the endless knowable. Endowed with conduct and the three true knowledges, he is called the Sugata because of his good speech and his good going among people.

89.

89.

Lokavidū so nitalokatayatā sākalato asamanaradamā so,

Sārathi jino anusāsanakaro satthavaho dupathataraṇasattā;

Bujjhati sāmaṃ catusaccamakhilaṃ bodhayi jantugaṇamiti ca buddho,

Bhākaraābhāphuṭapaṅkajasamo maggiyañāṇaphuvikasito ca.

He is the Knower of the Worlds, having penetrated the world in its entirety; the unsurpassed tamer of persons, the Victorious Charioteer, the instructor, the caravan leader helping beings cross the difficult path. He awakened by himself to the Four Noble Truths and awakens the hosts of beings—thus he is the Buddha, like a lotus blossomed by the sun, fully opened with knowledge of the path.

90.

90.

Bhaggakileso so bhagavā tibhava vamita gamana sujana bhajanato,

So bhaji saddhamme pavibhatta sarasa chabhagayuta garukaraṇiyato;

Sattanikāye kenapitulyaguṇamapamita sirighaṃnajutisusubhaṃ,

Devanarānaṃ ekapatiṭṭha mavitathutiyasa masaki mabhaṃname.

He is the Blessed One with defilements destroyed, who has rejected journeying in the three realms and is honored by good people. He partook of the good Dhamma, which is well-expounded and full of essence; he is endowed with the six fortunes and is worthy of reverence. In the host of beings, his virtues are unequalled by anyone; he is of immeasurable glory, dense radiance, and exceedingly beautiful. He is the sole refuge for gods and humans, whose fame is based on truthful speech. I bow to him, the unequalled.

91.

91.

Buddhuppādo kimmaṅgaṃ bho atidulabhataramidha bhave sughosa mapāparo,

Tasmā patthentā sabbaññuṃ vividhahitasukha manadhikaṃ namantu ca sādhavo;

Puññenānenete diṭṭhe bhayaaghaṃpīḷanaṭṭha virahitā parattha cubho subhe,

Bhutvānante ve hessante avikalasukhasirimatikā anuttara bhāgino.

The arising of a Buddha—what a blessing, friends! It is most difficult to obtain in this existence, a proclamation without parallel. Therefore, let the good people, aspiring for the Omniscient One who brings manifold welfare and unsurpassed happiness, pay homage to him. By this merit, may they, in this very life, be free from fear, evil, affliction, and misfortune, and in the next world attain both kinds of good. Having enjoyed boundless bliss, they will become possessors of unimpaired happiness and glory, partakers of the supreme.

92.

92.

So cakkopetapādo mudubhujacaraṇo suppatiṭṭhitapādo,

Eṇījaṅgho ca buddho kanakanibhataco āyatapaṇhi nātho;

Kosonaddhaṅgajāto atisudhumachavī jālikappānaheṭṭhā,

Ussaṅkhapādayutto abhinilanayano āyataṅguliyogo.

He has feet marked with wheels, soft hands and feet, and well-planted feet. The Buddha has calves like an antelope, skin the color of gold, and long heels; he is the Protector. His male organ is enclosed in a sheath, his skin is extremely fine, and his hands and feet are webbed. He has prominent ankles, deep blue eyes, and long fingers.

Lomakūpekekalomo samataladasano añjanuddhaggalomo;

Brahmaddehujjugatto aviraḷamukhato sattakaṅgussado so,

Nigrodhappāribimbo migapatihanuko sīhapubbaḍḍhakāyo.

Standing, without bending, he can touch his knees with both hands. He has one hair per pore, evenly spaced teeth, and body-hairs that grow upwards. His body is as straight as Brahma's, he has the seven prominences, and his teeth are without gaps. His proportions are like a banyan tree's, his jaws are like a lion's, and his torso is like a lion's.

94.

94.

Puṇṇattālīsadanto supahutarasano sobhaṇodātadāṭho,

Saṇhodātuṇṇalomo samavaṭalagalo antaraṃsapiṇo so;

Brahmagghoso munindo punapi gupakhumo uṇhisasamphullasīso,

Bāttiṃsaṅgopasobhaṃ mudurasahaṇī lokajeṭṭhaṃ name taṃ.

He has forty teeth, a large tongue, and very white canine teeth. The tuft of hair between his eyebrows is white and soft; his neck is evenly rounded, and his shoulders are full. The Lord of Sages has a voice like Brahmā's, eyelashes like a cow's, and a cranial protuberance on his head. I bow to him, the eldest in the world, adorned with the thirty-two marks, gentle and endowed with sublime taste.

Tigumbacetiya thomanā

Praise of the Tigumba Cetiya

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

1.

1.

Yo [M 0.0118] dīpaṅkaramūlamhi, padaṃ hattagataṃ caji;

Sammāsambodhi mākaṅkhaṃ, vande tassa siroruhaṃ.

He who, at the feet of Dīpaṅkara, renounced the state that was within his grasp, aspiring to perfect self-enlightenment—I venerate the hair of his head.

(Pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse).

2.

2.

Pūretvā bodhisambhāra-mahesaṃ yo anuttaraṃ;

Alattha buddhattaṃ tassa, kesadhātuvaraṃ name.

He who fulfilled the great, unsurpassed requisites for enlightenment and attained Buddhahood—to his excellent hair relic I pay homage.

(Pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse).

3.

3.

Laddhā buddhattaṃ chaṭhāna-matikkamma pavedayi;

Vimuttiṃ rājāyatane, tassa kesavaraṃ name.

Having attained Buddhahood and passed beyond six places, he experienced liberation at the Rājāyatana tree. To his excellent hair I pay homage.

(Rakāra bhakāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'r' and 'bh').

4.

4.

Tattha sakkadattiyampi, nāgalataṃ harītakaṃ;

Anotattodakaṃ bhuñji, vande tassa siroruhaṃ.

There he used the toothbrush-stick of nāgalatā and the myrobalan fruit given by Sakka, and partook of water from Lake Anotatta. I venerate the hair of his head.

(Rakāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'r').

5.

5.

Tadā [M 0.0119] muggaselapattaṃ, mahārājūhi dinnakaṃ;

Tathāgato paṭiggaṇhi, vandāmi tassa sīsajaṃ.

Then the Tathāgata accepted the stone bowl given by the Great Kings. I venerate that which grew on his head.

(Rakāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'r').

6.

6.

Tadā dvinnaṃ dvebhātika-janehi madhupiṇḍikaṃ;

Paribhuñjesi manthampi, tassa sīsasiriṃ name.

Then he partook of the honey-cake and barley-gruel from the two brothers. I bow to the glory of his head.

(Takāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 't').

7.

Yo tapussabhallikānaṃ, tadā dvisaraṇaṃ adā;

Lokamhi sabbapaṭhamaṃ, tassa sīrivahaṃ name.

He who, first of all in the world, gave the two-fold refuge to Tapussa and Bhallika—I bow to him, the bearer of glory.

(Rakāra nakāra vipulāpathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'r' and 'n').

8.

Tadā [M 0.0120] tatthupaṭṭhakānaṃ, tesaṃ kese adā aṭha;

Lokahita mapekkhanto, nātho yo tassa te name.

Then to those attendants he gave eight hairs. I pay homage to him, the Protector, who had regard for the welfare of the world.

(Rakāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'r').

9.

Tepi taṃ āhāritvāna, pokkharabbatiyaṃ karuṃ;

Sajīvakesa cetiyaṃ, nametaṃ sabbapubbakaṃ.

And they, having taken them, built in the city of Pokkharavatī the Cetiya of the Living Hairs. I bow to it, the very first of all.

(Makāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'm').

10.

Uposathuposathamhi, muñcantaṃ nīlarasmiyo;

Bhagavāva lokaatthaṃ, karontaṃ taṃ sadā name.

On every Uposatha day it releases blue rays, acting for the welfare of the world just like the Blessed One. I always pay homage to it.

(Rakāravipulā pathyāvattagāthā).

(Pathyāvatta verse, Vipulā type with 'r').

11.

Surely he dwells here, and then again elsewhere, great in bliss and virtue, unconquered like the city of the Thirty-three. Praised by the age, the crosser-over indeed spoke what is not vain or fruitless. I bow to the peerless, deathless boundary-maker.

The Lord of Victors, he of diverse knowledge, dwells on Mount Susumāra in the Bhesakaḷā forest, in the abode of bliss. Here, in this stainless state, with upright body, he abandons the remainder of division and torment. To him, the subtle one, I bow from every side.

Having left the great ones, he was attended by an elephant in the Pārileyyaka forest; here in the tenth year, abandoning the fools, he dwelt in bodily and mental happiness, and was then in bliss. I bow to him who is yoked to endurance, from whom Māra has departed.

He who is complete, dwelt during the thirteenth [rains] upon Mount Cāliya, in blooming splendor, desired by other immeasurable ones; and during the fourteenth in the constructed dwelling in Sāvatthī. I bow to the fearless sage who has severed desire, known to his kinsmen at the gates.

The Able One subdued the proud in Kapila, thus in the fifth [year]; the skillful one dwelt here, performing the twin miracle, making it suitable, while others became prosperous. The yakkha was tamed in the city of Dabbika, and also in the sixteenth [year] in Vaṅka. I bow to the enlightener of sage-like wisdom, who has ripened, having been touched by the foremost.

During the seventeenth year, frequenting the royal palace, he acted for the beings on the bank, abandoning the trembling abode. Standing firm by a tree, he who delighted in the unmoving gave this during the eighteenth [year]. Endowed with virtue, I bow here to the noble one, enduring bodily fatigue.

In the delightful city made by another, and also in the twentieth year, I bow to this one in the charming dwelling, who has conquered, abandoning pride, with the root of defilements destroyed.

In the Pubbārāma, through the laywoman's offering, he entered the excellent rains-residence. With a mind utterly pure and stainless, with surpassing dignity, I bow to him.

Having reflected on this, he dwelt there happily for some days.
