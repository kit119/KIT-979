Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Worthy One, the Perfectly Enlightened One.

Khuddakanikāye

The Khuddaka Nikāya

Dhammapadapāḷi

The Dhammapada

1. Yamakavaggo

1. The Twin Verses

1.

1.

Manopubbaṅgamā [M 0.0013][V 0.0013][P 0.0001][T 0.0015] dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;

Manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā;

Tato naṃ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṃva vahato padaṃ.

Mind precedes all mental states. Mind is their chief; they are mind-wrought. If with a corrupt mind one speaks or acts, suffering follows him like the wheel that follows the foot of the ox.

2.

2.

Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;

Manasā ce pasannena, bhāsati vā karoti vā;

Tato naṃ sukhamanveti, chāyāva anapāyinī anupāyinī (ka.).

Mind precedes all mental states. Mind is their chief; they are mind-wrought. If with a pure mind one speaks or acts, happiness follows him like a shadow that never departs.

3.

3.

Akkocchi [V 0.0014] maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini ajinī (?) maṃ ahāsi me;

Ye ca taṃ upanayhanti, veraṃ tesaṃ na sammati.

“He abused me, he struck me, he overcame me, he robbed me”—in those who harbor such thoughts, hatred is not appeased.

4.

4.

Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini maṃ ahāsi me;

Ye ca taṃ nupanayhanti, veraṃ tesūpasammati.

“He abused me, he struck me, he overcame me, he robbed me”—in those who do not harbor such thoughts, hatred is appeased.

5.

5.

Na [M 0.0014][P 0.0002] hi verena verāni, sammantīdha kudācanaṃ;

Averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano.

Hatreds never cease by hatred in this world; by non-hatred alone do they cease. This is an eternal law.

6.

6.

Pare [T 0.0016] ca na vijānanti, mayamettha yamāmase;

Ye ca tattha vijānanti, tato sammanti medhagā.

Others do not realize that we must all come to an end here; but those who do realize this have their quarrels calmed.

7.

7.

Subhānupassiṃ viharantaṃ, indriyesu asaṃvutaṃ;

Bhojanamhi cāmattaññuṃ, kusītaṃ hīnavīriyaṃ;

Taṃ ve pasahati māro, vāto rukkhaṃva dubbalaṃ.

One who dwells contemplating the attractive, unrestrained in the senses, immoderate in eating, lazy, and lacking energy—Māra will certainly overpower him, as the wind does a weak tree.

8.

8.

Asubhānupassiṃ viharantaṃ, indriyesu susaṃvutaṃ;

Bhojanamhi ca mattaññuṃ, saddhaṃ āraddhavīriyaṃ;

Taṃ ve nappasahati māro, vāto selaṃva pabbataṃ.

One who dwells contemplating the unattractive, well-restrained in the senses, moderate in eating, faithful, and with aroused energy—Māra will certainly not overpower him, as the wind does not a mountain of rock.

9.

9.

Anikkasāvo kāsāvaṃ, yo vatthaṃ paridahissati;

Apeto damasaccena, na so kāsāvamarahati.

Whoever, while still defiled, without self-restraint and truthfulness, should wear the yellow robe, is unworthy of it.

10.

10.

Yo ca vantakasāvassa, sīlesu susamāhito;

Upeto damasaccena, sa ve kāsāvamarahati.

But one who has discarded defilements, is well-established in virtue, and is endowed with self-restraint and truth, is indeed worthy of the yellow robe.

11.

11.

Asāre sāramatino, sāre cāsāradassino;

Te sāraṃ nādhigacchanti, micchāsaṅkappagocarā.

Those who mistake the unessential for the essential and the essential for the unessential, dwelling in wrong thoughts, never arrive at the essential.

12.

12.

Sārañca [V 0.0015] sārato ñatvā, asārañca asārato;

Te sāraṃ adhigacchanti, sammāsaṅkappagocarā.

Those who know the essential as essential and the unessential as unessential, dwelling in right thought, do attain the essential.

13.

13.

Yathā agāraṃ ducchannaṃ, vuṭṭhī samativijjhati;

Evaṃ abhāvitaṃ cittaṃ, rāgo samativijjhati.

Just as rain penetrates a poorly roofed house, so does lust penetrate an undeveloped mind.

14.

14.

Yathā [M 0.0015][P 0.0003] agāraṃ suchannaṃ, vuṭṭhī na samativijjhati;

Evaṃ subhāvitaṃ cittaṃ, rāgo na samativijjhati.

Just as rain does not penetrate a well-roofed house, so lust does not penetrate a well-developed mind.

15.

15.

Idha [T 0.0017] socati pecca socati, pāpakārī ubhayattha socati;

So socati so vihaññati, disvā kammakiliṭṭhamattano.

Here he sorrows, hereafter he sorrows; the evildoer sorrows in both states. He sorrows and is tormented, seeing his own defiled actions.

16.

16.

Idha modati pecca modati, katapuñño ubhayattha modati;

So modati so pamodati, disvā kammavisuddhimattano.

Here he rejoices, hereafter he rejoices; the doer of merit rejoices in both states. He rejoices and is greatly delighted, seeing the purity of his own actions.

17.

17.

Idha tappati pecca tappati, pāpakārī pāpakāri (?) ubhayattha tappati;

“Pāpaṃ me kata”nti tappati, bhiyyo bhīyo (sī.) tappati duggatiṃ gato.

Here he is tormented, hereafter he is tormented; the evildoer is tormented in both states. He is tormented, thinking, “I have done evil.” He is tormented even more when gone to a state of woe.

18.

18.

Idha nandati pecca nandati, katapuñño ubhayattha nandati;

“Puññaṃ me kata”nti nandati, bhiyyo nandati suggatiṃ gato.

Here he rejoices, hereafter he rejoices; the doer of merit rejoices in both states. He rejoices, thinking, “I have done merit,” and rejoices even more when gone to a good destination.

19.

19.

Bahumpi ce saṃhita sahitaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) bhāsamāno, na takkaro hoti naro pamatto;

Gopova [M 0.0016] gāvo gaṇayaṃ paresaṃ, na bhāgavā sāmaññassa hoti.

Though he recites many sacred texts, if he is heedless and does not act accordingly, he is like a cowherd who counts the cows of others: he has no share in the fruits of the holy life.

20.

20.

Appampi ce saṃhita bhāsamāno, dhammassa hoti hotī (sī. pī.) anudhammacārī;

Rāgañca dosañca pahāya mohaṃ, sammappajāno suvimuttacitto;

Anupādiyāno idha vā huraṃ vā, sa bhāgavā sāmaññassa hoti.

Though he recites but little of the sacred texts, if he lives in accordance with the Dhamma, having abandoned lust, hatred, and delusion, possessing right knowledge and a well-liberated mind, clinging to nothing in this world or the next—he is a partaker of the fruits of the holy life.

Yamakavaggo paṭhamo niṭṭhito.

The First Chapter, the Twin Verses, is concluded.

2. Appamādavaggo

2. The Chapter on Heedfulness

21.

21.

Appamādo [V 0.0016][P 0.0004][T 0.0018] amatapadaṃ amataṃ padaṃ (ka.), pamādo maccuno padaṃ;

Appamattā na mīyanti, ye pamattā yathā matā.

Heedfulness is the path to the Deathless; heedlessness is the path to death. The heedful do not die; the heedless are as if already dead.

22.

22.

Evaṃ etaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) visesato ñatvā, appamādamhi paṇḍitā;

Appamāde pamodanti, ariyānaṃ gocare ratā.

Having understood this distinction, the wise rejoice in heedfulness, delighting in the domain of the Noble Ones.

23.

23.

Te jhāyino sātatikā, niccaṃ daḷhaparakkamā;

Phusanti dhīrā nibbānaṃ, yogakkhemaṃ anuttaraṃ.

Those wise ones, meditative and steadfast, constantly persevering, attain Nibbāna, the unsurpassed security from bondage.

24.

24.

Uṭṭhānavato satīmato satimato (sī. syā. ka.), sucikammassa nisammakārino;

Saññatassa dhammajīvino, appamattassa apamattassa (?) yasobhivaḍḍhati.

For one who is energetic, mindful, pure in deed, considerate in action, self-restrained, righteous in living, and heedful—fame ever increases.

25.

25.

Uṭṭhānenappamādena[M 0.0017], saṃyamena damena ca;

Dīpaṃ kayirātha medhāvī, yaṃ ogho nābhikīrati.

By exertion, by heedfulness, by restraint and self-control, the wise one should make for himself an island which no flood can overwhelm.

26.

26.

Pamādamanuyuñjanti, bālā dummedhino janā;

Appamādañca medhāvī, dhanaṃ seṭṭhaṃva rakkhati.

Fools, dull-witted people, indulge in heedlessness; but the wise one guards heedfulness as a supreme treasure.

27.

27.

Mā pamādamanuyuñjetha, mā kāmaratisanthavaṃ sandhavaṃ (ka);

Appamatto hi jhāyanto, pappoti vipulaṃ sukhaṃ.

Do not indulge in heedlessness, nor in intimacy with sensual pleasures. For the heedful one, meditating, attains abundant happiness.

28.

28.

Pamādaṃ appamādena, yadā nudati paṇḍito;

Paññāpāsādamāruyha, asoko sokiniṃ pajaṃ;

Pabbataṭṭhova bhūmaṭṭhe bhummaṭṭhe (sī. syā.), dhīro bāle avekkhati.

When the wise one dispels heedlessness with heedfulness, having ascended the palace of wisdom, sorrowless, he surveys the sorrowing people. As one standing on a mountain peak beholds those on the ground, so the steadfast one beholds the fools.

29.

29.

Appamatto [V 0.0017][P 0.0005] pamattesu, suttesu bahujāgaro;

Abalassaṃva [T 0.0019] sīghasso, hitvā yāti sumedhaso.

Heedful among the heedless, wide-awake among the sleeping, the wise one advances like a swift horse, leaving a weak one behind.

30.

30.

Appamādena maghavā, devānaṃ seṭṭhataṃ gato;

Appamādaṃ pasaṃsanti, pamādo garahito sadā.

By heedfulness, Maghavā attained supremacy among the gods. Heedfulness is praised; heedlessness is always censured.

31.

31.

Appamādarato bhikkhu, pamāde bhayadassi vā;

Saṃyojanaṃ aṇuṃ thūlaṃ, ḍahaṃ aggīva gacchati.

A bhikkhu who delights in heedfulness and sees danger in heedlessness advances like a fire, burning all fetters, both subtle and gross.

32.

32.

Appamādarato bhikkhu, pamāde bhayadassi vā;

Abhabbo parihānāya, nibbānasseva santike.

A bhikkhu who delights in heedfulness and sees danger in heedlessness is incapable of falling away; he is in the very presence of Nibbāna.

Appamādavaggo dutiyo niṭṭhito.

The Second Chapter on Heedfulness is concluded.

3. Cittavaggo

3. The Chapter on the Mind

33.

33.

Phandanaṃ [M 0.0018] capalaṃ cittaṃ, dūrakkhaṃ durakkhaṃ (sabbattha) dunnivārayaṃ;

Ujuṃ karoti medhāvī, usukārova tejanaṃ.

The mind is trembling and fickle, difficult to guard and difficult to restrain. The wise one straightens it, as a fletcher straightens an arrow.

34.

34.

Vārijova thale khitto, okamokataubbhato;

Pariphandatidaṃ cittaṃ, māradheyyaṃ pahātave.

Like a fish taken from its watery home and thrown on land, this mind flutters to abandon the realm of Māra.

35.

35.

Dunniggahassa lahuno, yatthakāmanipātino;

Cittassa damatho sādhu, cittaṃ dantaṃ sukhāvahaṃ.

The taming of the mind is good, for the mind is difficult to restrain, swift, and alights wherever it desires. A tamed mind brings happiness.

36.

36.

Sududdasaṃ [V 0.0018][P 0.0006] sunipuṇaṃ, yatthakāmanipātinaṃ;

Cittaṃ rakkhetha medhāvī, cittaṃ guttaṃ sukhāvahaṃ.

The wise one should guard the mind, which is very difficult to perceive, extremely subtle, and alights wherever it desires. A guarded mind brings happiness.

37.

37.

Dūraṅgamaṃ ekacaraṃ ekacāraṃ (ka.), asarīraṃ guhāsayaṃ;

Ye [T 0.0020] cittaṃ saṃyamessanti, mokkhanti mārabandhanā.

Wandering far, solitary, bodiless, and dwelling in a cave is the mind. Those who restrain it will be freed from the bonds of Māra.

38.

38.

Anavaṭṭhitacittassa, saddhammaṃ avijānato;

Pariplavapasādassa, paññā na paripūrati.

For one of unsteady mind, who does not know the true Dhamma and whose faith is wavering, wisdom does not reach fulfillment.

39.

39.

Anavassutacittassa, ananvāhatacetaso;

Puññapāpapahīnassa, natthi jāgarato bhayaṃ.

For one whose mind is not sodden with lust, whose heart is unassailed by hatred, and who has gone beyond both merit and demerit, there is no fear for such a vigilant one.

40.

40.

Kumbhūpamaṃ kāyamimaṃ viditvā, nagarūpamaṃ cittamidaṃ ṭhapetvā;

Yodhetha māraṃ paññāvudhena, jitañca rakkhe anivesano siyā.

Knowing this body to be like a clay pot, and establishing this mind like a fortified city, one should fight Māra with the weapon of wisdom. One should guard what has been won and remain without attachment.

41.

41.

Aciraṃ [M 0.0019] vatayaṃ kāyo, pathaviṃ adhisessati;

Chuddho apetaviññāṇo, niratthaṃva kaliṅgaraṃ.

Before long, alas, this body will lie upon the earth, cast aside and devoid of consciousness, like a useless log.

42.

42.

Diso disaṃ yaṃ taṃ kayirā, verī vā pana verinaṃ;

Micchāpaṇihitaṃ cittaṃ, pāpiyo pāpiyaṃ (?) naṃ tato kare.

Whatever an enemy may do to an enemy, or a foe to a foe, a wrongly directed mind can do one far greater harm.

43.

43.

Na taṃ mātā pitā kayirā, aññe vāpi ca ñātakā;

Sammāpaṇihitaṃ cittaṃ, seyyaso naṃ tato kare.

Neither mother, nor father, nor any other relative can do one as much good as a rightly directed mind.

Cittavaggo tatiyo niṭṭhito.

The Third Chapter on the Mind is concluded.

4. Pupphavaggo

4. The Chapter on Flowers

44.

44.

Ko [V 0.0019][P 0.0007][T 0.0021] imaṃ komaṃ (ka.) pathaviṃ vicessati vijessati (sī. syā. pī.), yamalokañca imaṃ sadevakaṃ;

Ko dhammapadaṃ sudesitaṃ, kusalo pupphamiva pacessati pupphamivappacessati (ka.).

Who will conquer this earth, and the realm of Yama with the world of the gods? Who will select the well-taught verses of Dhamma, as a skilled florist picks a flower?

45.

45.

Sekho pathaviṃ vicessati, yamalokañca imaṃ sadevakaṃ;

Sekho dhammapadaṃ sudesitaṃ, kusalo pupphamiva pacessati.

A trainee will investigate this earth, and this realm of Yama together with the world of the gods. A trainee will discern the well-taught collection of Dhamma, as a skilled florist selects a flower.

46.

46.

Pheṇūpamaṃ [M 0.0020] kāyamimaṃ viditvā, marīcidhammaṃ abhisambudhāno;

Chetvāna mārassa papupphakāni sapupphakāni (ṭīkā), adassanaṃ maccurājassa gacche.

Knowing this body to be like foam, and fully realizing its mirage-like nature, one should cut off the flower-tipped arrows of Māra and go beyond the sight of the King of Death.

47.

47.

Pupphāni heva pacinantaṃ, byāsattamanasaṃ byāsattamānasaṃ (ka.) naraṃ;

Suttaṃ gāmaṃ mahoghova, maccu ādāya gacchati.

Just as a great flood sweeps away a sleeping village, death carries off the person whose mind is deeply attached to sensual pleasures, even while they are still gathering flowers.

48.

48.

Pupphāni heva pacinantaṃ, byāsattamanasaṃ naraṃ;

Atittaññeva kāmesu, antako kurute vasaṃ.

Death brings under his control the person whose mind is deeply attached to sensual pleasures, who is still gathering flowers, and who remains unsatiated in those desires.

49.

49.

Yathāpi bhamaro pupphaṃ, vaṇṇagandhamaheṭhayaṃ vaṇṇagandhamapoṭhayaṃ (ka.);

Paleti rasamādāya, evaṃ gāme munī care.

Just as a bee takes the nectar and flies away without harming the flower, its color, or its fragrance, so should a sage wander for alms in a village.

50.

50.

Na paresaṃ vilomāni, na paresaṃ katākataṃ;

Attanova avekkheyya, katāni akatāni ca.

One should not look at the faults of others, nor at what they have done or left undone. One should only consider what oneself has done and left undone.

51.

51.

Yathāpi [P 0.0008][T 0.0022] ruciraṃ pupphaṃ, vaṇṇavantaṃ agandhakaṃ;

Evaṃ subhāsitā vācā, aphalā hoti akubbato.

Just as a lovely flower, colorful but scentless, even so is the well-spoken word fruitless for one who does not act upon it.

52.

52.

Yathāpi [V 0.0020] ruciraṃ pupphaṃ, vaṇṇavantaṃ sugandhakaṃ sagandhakaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.);

Evaṃ subhāsitā vācā, saphalā hoti kubbato sakubbato (sī. pī.), pakubbato (sī. aṭṭha.), sukubbato (syā. kaṃ.).

Just as a lovely flower, colorful and fragrant, even so is the well-spoken word fruitful for one who acts upon it.

53.

53.

Yathāpi [M 0.0021] puppharāsimhā, kayirā mālāguṇe bahū;

Evaṃ jātena maccena, kattabbaṃ kusalaṃ bahuṃ.

As from a heap of flowers many garlands can be made, so by one born a mortal much good should be done.

54.

54.

Na pupphagandho paṭivātameti, na candanaṃ tagaramallikā tagaramallikā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.);

Satañca gandho paṭivātameti, sabbā disā sappuriso pavāyati.

The scent of flowers does not travel against the wind, nor that of sandalwood, tagara, or jasmine. But the fragrance of the virtuous travels against the wind; the good person pervades all directions.

55.

55.

Candanaṃ tagaraṃ vāpi, uppalaṃ atha vassikī;

Etesaṃ gandhajātānaṃ, sīlagandho anuttaro.

Sandalwood, tagara, blue lotus, or jasmine—among these kinds of fragrance, the fragrance of virtue is unsurpassed.

56.

56.

Appamatto ayaṃ gandho, yvāyaṃ tagaracandanaṃ yāyaṃ tagaracandanī (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.);

Yo ca sīlavataṃ gandho, vāti devesu uttamo.

Faint is this fragrance of tagara and sandalwood; but the fragrance of the virtuous, which is supreme, wafts even among the gods.

57.

57.

Tesaṃ sampannasīlānaṃ, appamādavihārinaṃ;

Sammadaññā vimuttānaṃ, māro maggaṃ na vindati.

Of those accomplished in virtue, who dwell in heedfulness, and are liberated through perfect knowledge, Māra does not find the path.

58.

58.

Yathā saṅkāraṭhānasmiṃ saṅkāradhānasmiṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), ujjhitasmiṃ mahāpathe;

Padumaṃ tattha jāyetha, sucigandhaṃ manoramaṃ.

Just as on a heap of rubbish cast upon the highway, a lotus might grow, of pure fragrance and delightful,

59.

59.

Evaṃ [P 0.0009] saṅkārabhūtesu, andhabhūte andhībhūte (ka.) puthujjane;

Atirocati paññāya, sammāsambuddhasāvako.

Even so, among the rubbish-like, blind common folk, the disciple of the Perfectly Enlightened One outshines them with wisdom.

Pupphavaggo catuttho niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on Flowers, the Fourth, is concluded.

5. Bālavaggo

5. The Chapter on the Fool

60.

60.

Dīghā [M 0.0022][V 0.0021][T 0.0023] jāgarato ratti, dīghaṃ santassa yojanaṃ;

Dīgho bālānaṃ saṃsāro, saddhammaṃ avijānataṃ.

Long is the night for the wakeful; long is a league for the weary; long is saṃsāra for fools who do not know the true Dhamma.

61.

61.

Carañce nādhigaccheyya, seyyaṃ sadisamattano;

Ekacariyaṃ ekacariyaṃ (ka.) daḷhaṃ kayirā, natthi bāle sahāyatā.

If, while wandering, one does not find another better than or equal to oneself, one should firmly pursue a solitary course; there is no companionship with a fool.

62.

62.

Puttā matthi dhanammatthi puttamatthi dhanamatthi (ka.), iti bālo vihaññati;

Attā hi attāpi (?) attano natthi, kuto puttā kuto dhanaṃ.

“I have sons, I have wealth”—thus the fool is afflicted. One is not even the owner of oneself; how then sons? How then wealth?

63.

63.

Yo bālo maññati bālyaṃ, paṇḍito vāpi tena so;

Bālo ca paṇḍitamānī, sa ve “bālo”ti vuccati.

The fool who knows his foolishness is wise at least to that extent; but the fool who thinks himself wise is truly called a fool.

64.

64.

Yāvajīvampi ce bālo, paṇḍitaṃ payirupāsati;

Na so dhammaṃ vijānāti, dabbī sūparasaṃ yathā.

Even if a fool attends upon a wise person for his whole life, he does not understand the Dhamma, just as a ladle does not know the taste of the soup.

65.

65.

Muhuttamapi [P 0.0010] ce viññū, paṇḍitaṃ payirupāsati;

Khippaṃ dhammaṃ vijānāti, jivhā sūparasaṃ yathā.

If an intelligent person attends upon a wise person even for a moment, he quickly understands the Dhamma, just as the tongue knows the taste of the soup.

66.

66.

Caranti bālā dummedhā, amitteneva attanā;

Karontā pāpakaṃ kammaṃ, yaṃ hoti kaṭukapphalaṃ.

Fools of little wisdom wander about with themselves as their own enemy, committing evil deeds that bear bitter fruit.

67.

67.

Na [M 0.0023] taṃ kammaṃ kataṃ sādhu, yaṃ katvā anutappati;

Yassa assumukho rodaṃ, vipākaṃ paṭisevati.

That deed is not well done which, having been done, one regrets, and the result of which one reaps with a tearful face, weeping.

68.

68.

Tañca [V 0.0022] kammaṃ kataṃ sādhu, yaṃ katvā nānutappati;

Yassa patīto sumano, vipākaṃ paṭisevati.

But that deed is well done which, having been done, one does not regret, and the result of which one reaps with a joyful and happy mind.

69.

69.

Madhuvā [T 0.0024] madhuṃ vā (dī. ni. ṭīkā 1) maññati bālo, yāva pāpaṃ na paccati;

Yadā ca paccati pāpaṃ, bālo atha bālo (sī. syā.) atha (?) dukkhaṃ nigacchati.

The fool thinks it is like honey, so long as the evil has not ripened; but when the evil ripens, the fool comes to suffering.

70.

70.

Māse māse kusaggena, bālo bhuñjeyya bhojanaṃ;

Na so saṅkhātadhammānaṃ saṅkhatadhammānaṃ (sī. pī. ka.), kalaṃ agghati soḷasiṃ.

Month after month, though a fool might eat his food with the tip of a kusa grass blade, he is not worth a sixteenth part of those who have fully understood the Dhamma.

71.

71.

Na hi pāpaṃ kataṃ kammaṃ, sajju khīraṃva muccati;

Ḍahantaṃ bālamanveti, bhasmacchannova bhasmāchannova (sī. pī. ka.) pāvako.

An evil deed that has been done does not curdle immediately, like milk; burning, it follows the fool, like a fire covered with ashes.

72.

72.

Yāvadeva anatthāya, ñattaṃ ñātaṃ (?) bālassa jāyati;

Hanti bālassa sukkaṃsaṃ, muddhamassa vipātayaṃ.

A fool's knowledge arises only for his own ruin; it destroys his bright share of merit and splits his head.

73.

73.

Asantaṃ [P 0.0011] bhāvanamiccheyya asantaṃ bhāvamiccheyya (syā.), asantabhāvanamiccheyya (ka.), purekkhārañca bhikkhusu;

Āvāsesu ca issariyaṃ, pūjā parakulesu ca.

The fool would desire a reputation he does not deserve, precedence among bhikkhus, authority in the monasteries, and honors from other families.

74.

74.

Mameva [M 0.0024] kata maññantu, gihīpabbajitā ubho;

Mamevātivasā assu, kiccākiccesu kismici;

Iti bālassa saṅkappo, icchā māno ca vaḍḍhati.

“Let both householders and the ordained think that this was done by me alone. Let them be subject to me alone in any matter, great or small.” Such is the fool's intention; his desire and conceit increase.

75.

75.

Aññā hi lābhūpanisā, aññā nibbānagāminī;

Evametaṃ abhiññāya, bhikkhu buddhassa sāvako;

Sakkāraṃ nābhinandeyya, vivekamanubrūhaye.

One is the path that leads to worldly gain, another is the path that leads to Nibbāna. Having understood this, the bhikkhu, the disciple of the Buddha, should not delight in honor, but should cultivate seclusion.

Bālavaggo pañcamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Fool, the Fifth, is concluded.

6. Paṇḍitavaggo

6. The Chapter on the Wise

76.

76.

Nidhīnaṃva [V 0.0023][T 0.0025] pavattāraṃ, yaṃ passe vajjadassinaṃ;

Niggayhavādiṃ medhāviṃ, tādisaṃ paṇḍitaṃ bhaje;

Tādisaṃ bhajamānassa, seyyo hoti na pāpiyo.

Should one see an intelligent person who points out one's faults and speaks in rebuke, one should associate with such a wise person as one would with a revealer of hidden treasures. For one who associates with such a person, it is better, not worse.

77.

77.

Ovadeyyānusāseyya, asabbhā ca nivāraye;

Satañhi so piyo hoti, asataṃ hoti appiyo.

He should advise, he should instruct, and he should restrain one from what is unseemly. Such a person is dear to the good; he is disliked by the wicked.

78.

78.

Na bhaje pāpake mitte, na bhaje purisādhame;

Bhajetha mitte kalyāṇe, bhajetha purisuttame.

Do not associate with evil friends; do not associate with base people. Associate with virtuous friends; associate with the supreme among men.

79.

79.

Dhammapīti [P 0.0012] sukhaṃ seti, vippasannena cetasā;

Ariyappavedite dhamme, sadā ramati paṇḍito.

He who drinks of the Dhamma lives happily with a serene mind; the wise one ever delights in the Dhamma proclaimed by the Noble Ones.

80.

80.

Udakañhi [M 0.0025] nayanti nettikā, usukārā namayanti damayanti (ka.) tejanaṃ;

Dāruṃ namayanti tacchakā, attānaṃ damayanti paṇḍitā.

Irrigators guide the water, fletchers straighten the arrow-shaft, carpenters shape the wood; the wise tame themselves.

81.

81.

Selo yathā ekaghano ekagghano (ka.), vātena na samīrati;

Evaṃ nindāpasaṃsāsu, na samiñjanti paṇḍitā.

As a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, so the wise are not moved by blame or praise.

82.

82.

Yathāpi rahado gambhīro, vippasanno anāvilo;

Evaṃ dhammāni sutvāna, vippasīdanti paṇḍitā.

Just as a deep lake is clear and still, so do the wise become serene upon hearing the teachings of the Dhamma.

83.

83.

Sabbattha ve sappurisā cajanti, na [T 0.0026] kāmakāmā lapayanti santo;

Sukhena phuṭṭhā atha vā dukhena, na uccāvacaṃ noccāvacaṃ (sī. aṭṭha.) paṇḍitā dassayanti.

The virtuous indeed renounce all things; the good do not speak with longing for sensual pleasures. Whether touched by pleasure or by pain, the wise show neither elation nor dejection.

84.

84.

Na [V 0.0024] attahetu na parassa hetu, na puttamicche na dhanaṃ na raṭṭhaṃ;

Na iccheyya nayicche (pī.), nicche (?) adhammena samiddhimattano, sa sīlavā paññavā dhammiko siyā.

Neither for his own sake nor for another's should one desire a son, wealth, or a kingdom. He should not wish for his own success by unrighteous means. Such a person is virtuous, wise, and righteous.

85.

85.

Appakā te manussesu, ye janā pāragāmino;

Athāyaṃ itarā pajā, tīramevānudhāvati.

Few among men are those who cross to the farther shore; the rest of mankind only runs up and down this shore.

86.

86.

Ye [P 0.0013] ca kho sammadakkhāte, dhamme dhammānuvattino;

Te janā pāramessanti, maccudheyyaṃ suduttaraṃ.

But those who live in accordance with the Dhamma, so well-expounded, will cross over the realm of Death, which is so difficult to traverse, and reach the farther shore.

87.

87.

Kaṇhaṃ [M 0.0026] dhammaṃ vippahāya, sukkaṃ bhāvetha paṇḍito;

Okā anokamāgamma, viveke yattha dūramaṃ.

Abandoning the dark states, the wise one should cultivate the bright. Having gone from home to homelessness, he should seek delight in seclusion, which is hard to enjoy.

88.

88.

Tatrābhiratimiccheyya, hitvā kāme akiñcano;

Pariyodapeyya pariyodāpeyya (?) attānaṃ, cittaklesehi paṇḍito.

Therein he should seek great delight. Having abandoned sensual pleasures and possessing nothing, the wise one should purify himself from the defilements of the mind.

89.

89.

Yesaṃ sambodhiyaṅgesu, sammā cittaṃ subhāvitaṃ;

Ādānapaṭinissagge, anupādāya ye ratā;

Khīṇāsavā jutimanto, te loke parinibbutā.

Those whose minds are well-developed in the factors of enlightenment, who delight in the relinquishing of grasping without clinging—they, the radiant ones whose taints are destroyed, are fully extinguished in the world.

Paṇḍitavaggo chaṭṭho niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Wise, the Sixth, is concluded.

7. Arahantavaggo

7. The Chapter on the Arahant

90.

90.

Gataddhino [T 0.0027] visokassa, vippamuttassa sabbadhi;

Sabbaganthappahīnassa, pariḷāho na vijjati.

For one who has completed the journey, who is sorrowless, wholly freed in every way, and who has cast off all fetters, no fever of distress exists.

91.

91.

Uyyuñjanti [V 0.0025] satīmanto, na nikete ramanti te;

Haṃsāva pallalaṃ hitvā, okamokaṃ jahanti te.

The mindful exert themselves; they do not delight in an abode. Like swans that leave their lake, they abandon every attachment.

92.

92.

Yesaṃ [P 0.0014] sannicayo natthi, ye pariññātabhojanā;

Suññato animitto ca, vimokkho yesaṃ gocaro;

Ākāse va sakuntānaṃ sakuṇānaṃ (ka.), gati tesaṃ durannayā.

Those who have no accumulation, who have full understanding of food, and whose sphere is the liberation of emptiness and signlessness—their path is as hard to trace as that of birds in the sky.

93.

93.

Yassāsavā [M 0.0027] parikkhīṇā, āhāre ca anissito;

Suññato animitto ca, vimokkho yassa gocaro;

Ākāse va sakuntānaṃ, padaṃ tassa durannayaṃ.

He whose taints are destroyed, who is not attached to food, and whose sphere is the liberation of emptiness and signlessness—his track is as hard to trace as that of birds in the sky.

94.

94.

Yassindriyāni samathaṅgatāni samathaṃ gatāni (sī. pī.), assā yathā sārathinā sudantā;

Pahīnamānassa anāsavassa, devāpi tassa pihayanti tādino.

He whose faculties are pacified, like horses well-tamed by a charioteer, who has abandoned conceit and is free from taints—even the gods hold such a steadfast one dear.

95.

95.

Pathavisamo no virujjhati, indakhilupamo indakhīlūpamo (sī. syā. ka.) tādi subbato;

Rahadova apetakaddamo, saṃsārā na bhavanti tādino.

Like the earth, he is not resentful; like a city pillar, the steadfast one is of virtuous conduct. Like a lake free from mud, for such a one there are no more cycles of rebirth.

96.

96.

Santaṃ [T 0.0028] tassa manaṃ hoti, santā vācā ca kamma ca;

Sammadaññā vimuttassa, upasantassa tādino.

Peaceful is his mind, peaceful are his speech and action; thus is the peace of one who is freed through right understanding and is perfectly tranquil.

97.

97.

Assaddho akataññū ca, sandhicchedo ca yo naro;

Hatāvakāso vantāso, sa ve uttamaporiso.

The man who is not a blind believer, who knows the Uncreated, who has severed the links of rebirth, who has destroyed all opportunities for further existence, and who has cast off all desire—he is indeed the supreme person.

98.

98.

Gāme vā yadi vāraññe, ninne vā yadi vā thale;

Yattha arahanto viharanti, taṃ bhūmirāmaṇeyyakaṃ.

Whether in a village or in the forest, in a valley or on high ground, wherever Arahants dwell, that land is delightful.

99.

99.

Ramaṇīyāni [M 0.0028][P 0.0015] araññāni, yattha na ramatī jano;

Vītarāgā ramissanti, na te kāmagavesino.

Delightful are the forests where the worldly find no delight. There the passionless will find delight, for they are not seekers of sensual pleasures.

Arahantavaggo sattamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Arahant, the Seventh, is concluded.

8. Sahassavaggo

8. The Chapter on Thousands

100.

100.

Sahassamapi [V 0.0026] ce vācā, anatthapadasaṃhitā;

Ekaṃ atthapadaṃ seyyo, yaṃ sutvā upasammati.

Better than a thousand words composed of unbeneficial phrases is one beneficial word, hearing which one is pacified.

101.

101.

Sahassamapi ce gāthā, anatthapadasaṃhitā;

Ekaṃ gāthāpadaṃ seyyo, yaṃ sutvā upasammati.

Better than a thousand verses composed of unbeneficial phrases is one verse, hearing which one is pacified.

102.

102.

Yo ca gāthā sataṃ bhāse, anatthapadasaṃhitā anatthapadasañhitaṃ (ka.) visesanaṃ hetaṃ gāthātipadassa;

Ekaṃ dhammapadaṃ seyyo, yaṃ sutvā upasammati.

Better than reciting a hundred verses composed of unbeneficial phrases is one word of the Dhamma, hearing which one is pacified.

103.

103.

Yo sahassaṃ sahassena, saṅgāme mānuse jine;

Ekañca jeyyamattānaṃ attānaṃ (sī. pī.), sa ve saṅgāmajuttamo.

Though one may conquer a thousand times a thousand men in battle, he who conquers himself alone is the supreme victor among all conquerors.

104.

104.

Attā [T 0.0029] have jitaṃ seyyo, yā cāyaṃ itarā pajā;

Attadantassa posassa, niccaṃ saññatacārino.

Victory over oneself is indeed better than the conquest of other people. For a person who has tamed himself and is always restrained in conduct,

105.

105.

Neva devo na gandhabbo, na māro saha brahmunā;

Jitaṃ apajitaṃ kayirā, tathārūpassa jantuno.

neither a deva nor a gandhabba, nor Māra together with Brahmā, can turn the victory of such a being into defeat.

106.

106.

Māse [M 0.0029][P 0.0016] māse sahassena, yo yajetha sataṃ samaṃ;

Ekañca bhāvitattānaṃ, muhuttamapi pūjaye;

Sāyeva pūjanā seyyo, yañce vassasataṃ hutaṃ.

Month after month, for a hundred years, one might offer sacrifices costing a thousand each; but if for just a moment one honors a person who has developed themselves, that homage is superior to a hundred years of sacrifice.

107.

107.

Yo ca vassasataṃ jantu, aggiṃ paricare vane;

Ekañca bhāvitattānaṃ, muhuttamapi pūjaye;

Sāyeva pūjanā seyyo, yañce vassasataṃ hutaṃ.

Though a person should tend the sacred fire in the forest for a hundred years, yet if for just a moment one honors a person who has developed themselves, that homage is superior to a hundred years of fire-worship.

108.

108.

Yaṃ [V 0.0027] kiñci yiṭṭhaṃ va hutaṃ va yiṭṭhañca hutañca (ka.) loke, saṃvaccharaṃ yajetha puññapekkho;

Sabbampi taṃ na catubhāgameti, abhivādanā ujjugatesu seyyo.

Whatever sacrifice or oblation a person seeking merit might offer in the world for a whole year, all of that does not equal a quarter of the merit gained from paying reverence to the upright ones.

109.

109.

Abhivādanasīlissa, niccaṃ vuḍḍhāpacāyino vaddhāpacāyino (sī. pī.);

Cattāro dhammā vaḍḍhanti, āyu vaṇṇo sukhaṃ balaṃ.

For one who is habitually respectful and always honors the elders, four qualities increase: longevity, beauty, happiness, and strength.

110.

110.

Yo ca vassasataṃ jīve, dussīlo asamāhito;

Ekāhaṃ jīvitaṃ seyyo, sīlavantassa jhāyino.

Though one should live a hundred years, immoral and lacking concentration, better is a single day's life for one who is virtuous and practices meditation.

111.

111.

Yo ca vassasataṃ jīve, duppañño asamāhito;

Ekāhaṃ jīvitaṃ seyyo, paññavantassa jhāyino.

Though one should live a hundred years, lacking wisdom and concentration, better is a single day's life for one who is wise and practices meditation.

112.

112.

Yo [T 0.0030] ca vassasataṃ jīve, kusīto hīnavīriyo;

Ekāhaṃ jīvitaṃ seyyo, vīriyamārabhato daḷhaṃ.

Though one should live a hundred years, lazy and lacking in energy, better is a single day's life for one who firmly exerts effort.

113.

113.

Yo [M 0.0030] ca vassasataṃ jīve, apassaṃ udayabbayaṃ;

Ekāhaṃ jīvitaṃ seyyo, passato udayabbayaṃ.

Though one should live a hundred years without seeing the arising and passing away of the five aggregates, better is a single day's life for one who sees their arising and passing away.

114.

114.

Yo [P 0.0017] ca vassasataṃ jīve, apassaṃ amataṃ padaṃ;

Ekāhaṃ jīvitaṃ seyyo, passato amataṃ padaṃ.

Though one should live a hundred years without seeing the Deathless state, Nibbāna, better is a single day's life for one who sees the Deathless state.

115.

115.

Yo ca vassasataṃ jīve, apassaṃ dhammamuttamaṃ;

Ekāhaṃ jīvitaṃ seyyo, passato dhammamuttamaṃ.

Though one should live a hundred years without seeing the supreme Dhamma, better is a single day's life for one who sees the supreme Dhamma.

Sahassavaggo aṭṭhamo niṭṭhito.

The Eighth Chapter, The Thousands, is concluded.

9. Pāpavaggo

9. The Chapter on Evil

116.

116.

Abhittharetha [V 0.0028] kalyāṇe, pāpā cittaṃ nivāraye;

Dandhañhi karoto puññaṃ, pāpasmiṃ ramatī mano.

One should hasten to do good and restrain the mind from evil. For the mind of one who is slow in performing merit delights in evil.

117.

117.

Pāpañce puriso kayirā, na naṃ na taṃ (sī. pī.) kayirā punappunaṃ;

Na tamhi chandaṃ kayirātha, dukkho pāpassa uccayo.

If a person should do evil, let him not do it again and again; let him not set his heart on it. Painful is the accumulation of evil.

118.

118.

Puññañce puriso kayirā, kayirā naṃ kayirāthetaṃ (sī. syā.), kayirāthenaṃ (pī.) punappunaṃ;

Tamhi chandaṃ kayirātha, sukho puññassa uccayo.

If a person should do good, let him do it again and again; let him set his heart on it. Happy is the accumulation of good.

119.

119.

Pāpopi [M 0.0031] passati bhadraṃ, yāva pāpaṃ na paccati;

Yadā ca paccati pāpaṃ, atha pāpo pāpāni atha pāpāni (?) passati.

Even an evil-doer sees good, as long as the evil has not ripened; but when the evil ripens, then the evil-doer sees evil.

120.

120.

Bhadropi [T 0.0031][P 0.0018] passati pāpaṃ, yāva bhadraṃ na paccati;

Yadā ca paccati bhadraṃ, atha bhadro bhadrāni atha bhadrāni (?) passati.

Even a good person sees evil, as long as the good has not ripened; but when the good ripens, then the good person sees good things.

121.

121.

Māvamaññetha māppamaññetha (sī. syā. pī.) pāpassa, na mantaṃ na maṃ taṃ (sī. pī.), na mattaṃ (syā.) āgamissati;

Udabindunipātena, udakumbhopi pūrati;

Bālo pūrati pūrati bālo (sī. ka.), āpūrati bālo (syā.) pāpassa, thokaṃ thokampi thoka thokampi (sī. pī.) ācinaṃ.

Do not think lightly of evil, saying, 'It will not come to me.' By the falling of water drops, a water pot is filled; the fool fills himself with evil, accumulating it little by little.

122.

122.

Māvamaññetha puññassa, na mantaṃ āgamissati;

Udabindunipātena, udakumbhopi pūrati;

Dhīro pūrati puññassa, thokaṃ thokampi ācinaṃ.

Do not think lightly of merit, saying, 'It will not come to me.' By the falling of water drops, a water pot is filled; the wise person fills himself with merit, accumulating it little by little.

123.

123.

Vāṇijova bhayaṃ maggaṃ, appasattho mahaddhano;

Visaṃ jīvitukāmova, pāpāni parivajjaye.

As a merchant with a small caravan and great wealth avoids a perilous road, and as one who desires to live avoids poison, so should one shun evil deeds.

124.

124.

Pāṇimhi [V 0.0029] ce vaṇo nāssa, hareyya pāṇinā visaṃ;

Nābbaṇaṃ visamanveti, natthi pāpaṃ akubbato.

If there is no wound on the hand, one may carry poison with the hand. Poison does not affect one who is unwounded; there is no evil for one who does no evil.

125.

125.

Yo [M 0.0032] appaduṭṭhassa narassa dussati, suddhassa posassa anaṅgaṇassa;

Tameva bālaṃ pacceti pāpaṃ, sukhumo rajo paṭivātaṃva khitto.

Whoever offends a harmless, pure, and faultless person—evil returns to that very fool, like fine dust thrown against the wind.

126.

126.

Gabbhameke uppajjanti, nirayaṃ pāpakammino;

Saggaṃ sugatino yanti, parinibbanti anāsavā.

Some are born in a womb; evil-doers arise in hell; the virtuous go to heaven; the taintless attain Parinibbāna.

127.

127.

Na [P 0.0019] antalikkhe na samuddamajjhe, na pabbatānaṃ vivaraṃ pavissa pavisaṃ (syā.);

Na [T 0.0032] vijjatī na vijjati (ka. sī. pī. ka.) so jagatippadeso, yatthaṭṭhito yatraṭṭhito (syā.) mucceyya pāpakammā.

Not in the sky, nor in the middle of the sea, nor by entering a mountain cleft, is there any place on earth where, abiding, one might be freed from an evil deed.

128.

128.

Na antalikkhe na samuddamajjhe, na pabbatānaṃ vivaraṃ pavissa;

Na vijjatī so jagatippadeso, yatthaṭṭhitaṃ yatraṭṭhitaṃ (syā.) nappasaheyya maccu.

Not in the sky, nor in the middle of the sea, nor by entering a mountain cleft, is there any place on earth where, abiding, one would not be overcome by death.

Pāpavaggo navamo niṭṭhito.

The Ninth Chapter, Evil, is concluded.

10. Daṇḍavaggo

10. The Chapter on the Rod

129.

129.

Sabbe tasanti daṇḍassa, sabbe bhāyanti maccuno;

Attānaṃ upamaṃ katvā, na haneyya na ghātaye.

All tremble at the rod; all fear death. Having made oneself the example, one should neither kill nor cause to kill.

130.

130.

Sabbe [M 0.0033] tasanti daṇḍassa, sabbesaṃ jīvitaṃ piyaṃ;

Attānaṃ upamaṃ katvā, na haneyya na ghātaye.

All tremble at the rod; to all, life is dear. Having made oneself the example, one should neither kill nor cause to kill.

131.

131.

Sukhakāmāni [V 0.0030] bhūtāni, yo daṇḍena vihiṃsati;

Attano sukhamesāno, pecca so na labhate sukhaṃ.

Beings desire happiness; whoever, seeking one's own happiness, harms them with a rod, does not find happiness after death.

132.

132.

Sukhakāmāni [P 0.0020] bhūtāni, yo daṇḍena na hiṃsati;

Attano sukhamesāno, pecca so labhate sukhaṃ.

Beings desire happiness; whoever, seeking one's own happiness, does not harm them with a rod, finds happiness after death.

133.

133.

Māvoca pharusaṃ kañci, vuttā paṭivadeyyu taṃ paṭivadeyyuṃ taṃ (ka.);

Dukkhā hi sārambhakathā, paṭidaṇḍā phuseyyu taṃ phuseyyuṃ taṃ (ka.).

Speak harshly to no one; those spoken to may answer you back. Contentious speech is indeed painful, and retribution may reach you.

134.

134.

Sace [T 0.0033] neresi attānaṃ, kaṃso upahato yathā;

Esa pattosi nibbānaṃ, sārambho te na vijjati.

If you keep yourself as still as a broken gong, you have already reached Nibbana; no contention is found in you.

135.

135.

Yathā daṇḍena gopālo, gāvo pājeti gocaraṃ;

Evaṃ jarā ca maccu ca, āyuṃ pājenti pāṇinaṃ.

As a cowherd with a rod drives cattle to pasture, so do aging and death drive the lives of beings.

136.

136.

Atha pāpāni kammāni, karaṃ bālo na bujjhati;

Sehi kammehi dummedho, aggidaḍḍhova tappati.

When a fool commits evil deeds, he does not realize it; the dull-witted one is tormented by his own actions, as if burned by fire.

137.

137.

Yo daṇḍena adaṇḍesu, appaduṭṭhesu dussati;

Dasannamaññataraṃ ṭhānaṃ, khippameva nigacchati.

Whoever with a rod harms the harmless who are without fault, quickly comes to one of ten states of suffering.

138.

138.

Vedanaṃ [M 0.0034] pharusaṃ jāniṃ, sarīrassa ca bhedanaṃ sarīrassa pabhedanaṃ (syā.);

Garukaṃ vāpi ābādhaṃ, cittakkhepañca cittakkhepaṃ va (sī. syā. pī.) pāpuṇe.

He may reach harsh pain, loss of wealth, or injury to the body; or grave illness, or derangement of mind.

139.

139.

Rājato vā upasaggaṃ upassaggaṃ (sī. pī.), abbhakkhānañca abbhakkhānaṃ va (sī. pī.) dāruṇaṃ;

Parikkhayañca parikkhayaṃ va (sī. syā. pī.) ñātīnaṃ, bhogānañca bhogānaṃ va (sī. syā. pī.) pabhaṅguraṃ pabhaṅgunaṃ (ka.).

Or trouble from the king, or a terrible accusation; or loss of relatives, or the destruction of wealth.

140.

140.

Atha vāssa agārāni, aggi ḍahati ḍayhati (ka.) pāvako;

Kāyassa bhedā duppañño, nirayaṃ sopapajjati so upapajjati (sī. syā.).

Or else a blazing fire will burn his houses; and upon the breakup of the body, that fool is reborn in hell.

141.

141.

Na [V 0.0031][P 0.0021] naggacariyā na jaṭā na paṅkā, nānāsakā thaṇḍilasāyikā vā;

Rajojallaṃ ukkuṭikappadhānaṃ, sodhenti maccaṃ avitiṇṇakaṅkhaṃ.

Neither nakedness, nor matted hair, nor mud, nor fasting, nor lying on the bare ground, nor dust and grime, nor striving in a squatting posture, can purify a mortal who has not overcome doubt.

142.

142.

Sabbesu [T 0.0034] bhūtesu nidhāya daṇḍaṃ, so brāhmaṇo so samaṇo sa bhikkhu.

Even if one is adorned, if one lives in peace—calm, tamed, established in the Path, leading the holy life, having laid down the rod toward all beings—one is a brahmin, one is a recluse, one is a monk.

143.

143.

Yo niddaṃ nindaṃ (sī. pī.) saṃ. ni. 1.18 apabodheti apabodhati (sī. syā. pī.), asso bhadro kasāmiva.

Rarely is a person found in this world who, restrained by shame, avoids censure as a fine horse avoids the whip.

Sampannavijjācaraṇā patissatā, jahissatha pahassatha (sī. syā. pī.) dukkhamidaṃ anappakaṃ.

Like a fine horse touched by the whip, be ardent and filled with urgency. Through faith, virtue, energy, concentration, and discernment of the Dhamma, endowed with knowledge and conduct, and being mindful, you shall abandon this immeasurable suffering.

145.

Dāruṃ namayanti tacchakā, attānaṃ damayanti subbatā.

Irrigators guide the water; fletchers straighten the arrow; carpenters shape the wood; the virtuous tame themselves.

Daṇḍavaggo dasamo niṭṭhito.

The Tenth Chapter on the Rod is finished.

11. Chapter 11: Old Age

146.

146.

Ko [P 0.0022][T 0.0035] nu hāso kinnu hāso (ka.) kimānando, niccaṃ pajjalite sati;

Andhakārena onaddhā, padīpaṃ na gavesatha.

What laughter, what joy, when the world is ever ablaze? Shrouded in darkness, why do you not seek a light?

147.

147.

Passa cittakataṃ bimbaṃ, arukāyaṃ samussitaṃ;

Āturaṃ bahusaṅkappaṃ, yassa natthi dhuvaṃ ṭhiti.

Behold this adorned image, a mass of sores, a body propped up, diseased, and full of many thoughts, which has no permanence or stability.

148.

148.

Parijiṇṇamidaṃ [V 0.0032] rūpaṃ, roganīḷaṃ roganiḍḍhaṃ (sī. pī.), roganiddhaṃ (syā.) pabhaṅguraṃ;

Bhijjati pūtisandeho, maraṇantañhi jīvitaṃ.

This form is worn out, a nest of diseases, and fragile. This foul mass breaks apart, for life ends in death.

149.

149.

Yānimāni [M 0.0036] apatthāni yānimāni apatthāni (sī. syā. pī.), yānimāni’paviddhāni (?), alābūneva alāpūneva (sī. syā. pī.) sārade;

Kāpotakāni aṭṭhīni, tāni disvāna kā rati.

These dove-colored bones, cast away like gourds in autumn—what delight is there in seeing them?

150.

150.

Aṭṭhīnaṃ nagaraṃ kataṃ, maṃsalohitalepanaṃ;

Yattha jarā ca maccu ca, māno makkho ca ohito.

A city is made of bones, plastered with flesh and blood, wherein old age and death, pride and detraction are stored.

151.

151.

Jīranti ve rājarathā sucittā, atho sarīrampi jaraṃ upeti;

Satañca dhammo na jaraṃ upeti, santo have sabbhi pavedayanti.

Even well-decorated royal chariots wear out, and the body too reaches old age. But the Dhamma of the virtuous does not grow old; thus do the virtuous make it known to one another.

152.

152.

Appassutāyaṃ puriso, balibaddhova balivaddova (sī. syā. pī.) jīrati;

Maṃsāni tassa vaḍḍhanti, paññā tassa na vaḍḍhati.

A person of little learning grows old like an ox; his flesh increases, but his wisdom does not.

153.

153.

Anekajātisaṃsāraṃ[P 0.0023], sandhāvissaṃ anibbisaṃ;

Gahakāraṃ gahakārakaṃ (sī. syā. pī.) gavesanto, dukkhā jāti punappunaṃ.

Through a round of many births I have wandered without rest, seeking the builder of the house. Painful is birth again and again.

154.

154.

Sabbā [T 0.0036] te phāsukā bhaggā, gahakūṭaṃ visaṅkhataṃ;

Visaṅkhāragataṃ cittaṃ, taṇhānaṃ khayamajjhagā.

O house-builder, you are seen! You shall not build the house again. All your rafters are broken, the ridgepole is shattered. My mind has reached the unconditioned; I have attained the destruction of craving.

155.

Jiṇṇakoñcāva jhāyanti, khīṇamaccheva pallale.

Not having lived the holy life, not having gained wealth in their youth, they pine away like old herons in a pond without fish.

156.

156.

Senti cāpātikhīṇāva, purāṇāni anutthunaṃ.

Not having lived the holy life, not having gained wealth in their youth, they lie like spent arrows shot from a bow, mourning for the past.

Jarāvaggo ekādasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on Old Age, the Eleventh, is finished.

12. Attavaggo

12. 12. The Chapter on the Self

157.

157.

Attānañce [V 0.0033] piyaṃ jaññā, rakkheyya naṃ surakkhitaṃ;

Tiṇṇaṃ aññataraṃ yāmaṃ, paṭijaggeyya paṇḍito.

If one knows oneself to be dear, one should guard oneself well. A wise person should keep watch during one of the three watches [of the night or stages of life].

158.

158.

Attānameva paṭhamaṃ, patirūpe nivesaye;

Athaññamanusāseyya, na kilisseyya paṇḍito.

One should first establish oneself in what is proper; then one may instruct others. The wise person should not be defiled.

159.

159.

Attānaṃ [P 0.0024] ce tathā kayirā, yathāññamanusāsati;

Sudanto vata dametha, attā hi kira duddamo.

If one would act as one instructs others, then, being well-tamed, one may tame others. For the self is indeed difficult to tame.

160.

160.

Attā hi attano nātho, ko hi nātho paro siyā;

Attanā hi sudantena, nāthaṃ labhati dullabhaṃ.

One is truly one’s own refuge; what other refuge could there be? With oneself well-tamed, one obtains a refuge that is difficult to find.

161.

161.

Attanā hi kataṃ pāpaṃ, attajaṃ attasambhavaṃ;

Abhimatthati abhimantati (sī. pī.) dummedhaṃ, vajiraṃ vasmamayaṃ vajiraṃva’mhamayaṃ (syā. ka.) maṇiṃ.

The evil done by oneself, born of oneself, and produced by oneself, crushes the fool, as a diamond grinds a stone-born gem.

162.

162.

Yassa [T 0.0037] accantadussīlyaṃ, māluvā sālamivotthataṃ;

Karoti so tathattānaṃ, yathā naṃ icchatī diso.

One whose extreme immorality spreads like a māluvā creeper over a sāla tree, makes of himself just what his enemy wishes him to be.

163.

163.

Sukarāni [M 0.0038] asādhūni, attano ahitāni ca;

Yaṃ ve hitañca sādhuñca, taṃ ve paramadukkaraṃ.

Easy to do are things that are bad and harmful to oneself; but what is beneficial and good is indeed exceedingly difficult to do.

164.

164.

Yo sāsanaṃ arahataṃ, ariyānaṃ dhammajīvinaṃ;

Paṭikkosati dummedho, diṭṭhiṃ nissāya pāpikaṃ;

Phalāni kaṭṭhakasseva, attaghātāya attaghaññāya (sī. syā. pī.) phallati.

The fool who, relying on evil views, scorns the teaching of the Arahants, the Noble Ones who live according to Dhamma, bears fruit for his own destruction, like the fruit of the kaṭṭhaka reed.

165.

165.

Attanā [V 0.0034] hi attanāva (sī. syā. pī.) kataṃ pāpaṃ, attanā saṃkilissati;

Attanā akataṃ pāpaṃ, attanāva visujjhati;

Suddhī asuddhi paccattaṃ, nāñño aññaṃ nāññamañño(sī.) visodhaye.

By oneself is evil done; by oneself is one defiled. By oneself is evil left undone; by oneself is one purified. Purity and impurity are personal; no one can purify another.

166.

166.

Attadatthaṃ [P 0.0025] paratthena, bahunāpi na hāpaye;

Attadatthamabhiññāya, sadatthapasuto siyā.

Let one not neglect one’s own welfare for the sake of another’s, however great. Having clearly understood one’s own welfare, let one be intent on one’s own goal.

Attavaggo dvādasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Self, the Twelfth, is finished.

13. Lokavaggo

13. 13. The Chapter on the World

167.

167.

Hīnaṃ dhammaṃ na seveyya, pamādena na saṃvase;

Micchādiṭṭhiṃ na seveyya, na siyā lokavaḍḍhano.

One should not follow a base practice; one should not live in heedlessness. One should not follow a wrong view; one should not be one who prolongs the world.

168.

168.

Dhammacārī sukhaṃ seti, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca.

Rouse yourself! Do not be heedless! Practice the Dhamma well. The Dhamma-farer dwells happily in this world and the next.

169.

169.

Dhammaṃ [M 0.0039][T 0.0038] care sucaritaṃ, na naṃ duccaritaṃ care;

Dhammacārī sukhaṃ seti, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca.

Practice the Dhamma well; do not practice it poorly. The Dhamma-farer dwells happily in this world and the next.

170.

170.

Yathā pubbuḷakaṃ pubbuḷakaṃ (sī. pī.) passe, yathā passe marīcikaṃ;

Evaṃ lokaṃ avekkhantaṃ, maccurājā na passati.

As one would look upon a bubble, as one would look upon a mirage—the King of Death does not see one who looks upon the world in this way.

171.

171.

Etha passathimaṃ lokaṃ, cittaṃ rājarathūpamaṃ;

Yattha bālā visīdanti, natthi saṅgo vijānataṃ.

Come, behold this world, like a decorated royal chariot, wherein fools flounder, but for the wise there is no attachment.

172.

172.

Yo [V 0.0035][P 0.0026] ca pubbe pamajjitvā, pacchā so nappamajjati;

Somaṃ lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhā muttova candimā.

Whoever, having been heedless before, later is not heedless, illuminates this world like the moon freed from a cloud.

173.

173.

Yassa pāpaṃ kataṃ kammaṃ, kusalena pidhīyati pitīyati (sī. syā. pī.);

Somaṃ lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhā muttova candimā.

He whose evil deed is covered by a wholesome one illuminates this world like the moon freed from a cloud.

174.

174.

Andhabhūto andhībhūto (ka.) ayaṃ loko, tanukettha vipassati;

Sakuṇo jālamuttova, appo saggāya gacchati.

This world is blind; few here see with insight. Like a bird freed from a net, few go to heaven.

175.

175.

Haṃsādiccapathe yanti, ākāse yanti iddhiyā;

Nīyanti dhīrā lokamhā, jetvā māraṃ savāhiniṃ savāhanaṃ (syā. ka.).

Swans travel on the path of the sun; they travel through the sky by psychic power. The wise are led out from the world, having conquered Māra and his army.

176.

176.

Ekaṃ dhammaṃ atītassa, musāvādissa jantuno;

Vitiṇṇaparalokassa, natthi pāpaṃ akāriyaṃ.

For the person who has transgressed one law [of Truth], who speaks falsely, and who has rejected the world beyond, there is no evil that he will not do.

177.

177.

Na [M 0.0040] ve kadariyā devalokaṃ vajanti, bālā have nappasaṃsanti dānaṃ;

Dhīro ca dānaṃ anumodamāno, teneva [T 0.0039] so hoti sukhī parattha.

Misers certainly do not go to the world of the devas; fools indeed do not praise giving. But the wise person rejoices in giving, and thereby becomes happy hereafter.

178.

178.

Pathabyā ekarajjena, saggassa gamanena vā;

Sabbalokādhipaccena, sotāpattiphalaṃ varaṃ.

Better than sole sovereignty over the earth, better than going to heaven, better than lordship over all the worlds, is the fruit of stream-entry.

Lokavaggo terasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the World, the Thirteenth, is finished.

14. Buddhavaggo

14. 14. The Chapter on the Buddha

179.

179.

Yassa [V 0.0036][P 0.0027] jitaṃ nāvajīyati, jitaṃ yassa jitamassa (sī. syā. pī.), jitaṃ massa (ka.) no yāti koci loke;

Taṃ buddhamanantagocaraṃ, apadaṃ kena padena nessatha.

Whose victory is not turned into defeat, whose victory no one in the world follows; by what track will you lead that Buddha of infinite range, the trackless?

180.

180.

Yassa jālinī visattikā, taṇhā natthi kuhiñci netave;

Taṃ buddhamanantagocaraṃ, apadaṃ kena padena nessatha.

In whom there is no entangling, clinging craving to lead him anywhere; by what track will you lead that Buddha of infinite range, the trackless?

181.

181.

Ye jhānapasutā dhīrā, nekkhammūpasame ratā;

Devāpi tesaṃ pihayanti, sambuddhānaṃ satīmataṃ.

The wise who are devoted to meditation, who delight in the peace of renunciation—even the gods long for them, the enlightened and mindful.

182.

182.

Kiccho [M 0.0041] manussapaṭilābho, kicchaṃ maccāna jīvitaṃ;

Kicchaṃ saddhammassavanaṃ, kiccho buddhānamuppādo.

Difficult is it to obtain a human birth; difficult is the life of mortals; difficult is the hearing of the True Dhamma; difficult is the arising of Buddhas.

183.

183.

Sabbapāpassa akaraṇaṃ, kusalassa upasampadā kusalassūpasampadā (syā.);

Sacittapariyodapanaṃ [T 0.0040] sacittapariyodāpanaṃ (?), etaṃ buddhāna sāsanaṃ.

The non-doing of all evil, the undertaking of the good, the purification of one's own mind—this is the teaching of the Buddhas.

184.

184.

Khantī paramaṃ tapo titikkhā, nibbānaṃ nibbāṇaṃ (ka. sī. pī.) paramaṃ vadanti buddhā;

Na hi pabbajito parūpaghātī, na ayaṃ nakāro sī. syā. pī. pātthakesu na dissati samaṇo hoti paraṃ viheṭhayanto.

Patient endurance is the highest austerity. The Buddhas say Nibbāna is supreme. He is not a renunciant who harms another; he is not an ascetic who oppresses others.

185.

185.

Anūpavādo anūpaghāto anupavādo anupaghāto (syā. ka.), pātimokkhe ca saṃvaro;

Mattaññutā ca bhattasmiṃ, pantañca sayanāsanaṃ;

Adhicitte ca āyogo, etaṃ buddhāna sāsanaṃ.

Not reviling, not injuring, restraint in the Pātimokkha, moderation in food, a secluded dwelling, and devotion to the higher mind—this is the teaching of the Buddhas.

186.

186.

Na [P 0.0028] kahāpaṇavassena, titti kāmesu vijjati;

Appassādā dukhā kāmā, iti viññāya paṇḍito.

Not even by a shower of gold coins is satisfaction found in sensual pleasures. Sensual pleasures give little satisfaction and are productive of suffering—knowing this, the wise one...

187.

187.

Api [V 0.0037] dibbesu kāmesu, ratiṃ so nādhigacchati;

Taṇhakkhayarato hoti, sammāsambuddhasāvako.

...finds no delight even in celestial pleasures. The disciple of the Perfectly Enlightened One delights in the destruction of craving.

188.

188.

Bahuṃ ve saraṇaṃ yanti, pabbatāni vanāni ca;

Ārāmarukkhacetyāni, manussā bhayatajjitā.

Driven by fear, many people go for refuge to mountains and forests, to parks, sacred trees, and shrines.

189.

189.

Netaṃ kho saraṇaṃ khemaṃ, netaṃ saraṇamuttamaṃ;

Netaṃ saraṇamāgamma, sabbadukkhā pamuccati.

This is not a secure refuge; this is not the supreme refuge. Having gone to such a refuge, one is not released from all suffering.

190.

190.

Yo [M 0.0042] ca buddhañca dhammañca, saṅghañca saraṇaṃ gato;

Cattāri ariyasaccāni, sammappaññāya passati.

But whoever has gone for refuge to the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Sangha, sees with right wisdom the Four Noble Truths:

191.

191.

Dukkhaṃ dukkhasamuppādaṃ, dukkhassa ca atikkamaṃ;

Ariyaṃ caṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ, dukkhūpasamagāminaṃ.

Suffering, the origin of suffering, the overcoming of suffering, and the Noble Eightfold Path leading to the cessation of suffering.

192.

192.

Etaṃ [T 0.0041] kho saraṇaṃ khemaṃ, etaṃ saraṇamuttamaṃ;

Etaṃ saraṇamāgamma, sabbadukkhā pamuccati.

This indeed is the secure refuge; this is the supreme refuge. Having gone to this refuge, one is released from all suffering.

193.

193.

Dullabho purisājañño, na so sabbattha jāyati;

Yattha so jāyati dhīro, taṃ kulaṃ sukhamedhati.

A man of noble breed is rare to find; he is not born everywhere. Wherever such a wise one is born, that family prospers in happiness.

194.

194.

Sukhā saṅghassa sāmaggī, samaggānaṃ tapo sukho.

Happy is the arising of Buddhas; happy is the teaching of the True Dhamma; happy is the harmony of the Sangha; happy is the practice of those in harmony.

195.

195.

Papañcasamatikkante, tiṇṇasokapariddave.

For one who honors those worthy of honor—the Buddhas or their disciples who have transcended the world of obstacles and crossed beyond sorrow and lamentation—

196.

196.

Te [V 0.0038][P 0.0029] tādise pūjayato, nibbute akutobhaye;

Na sakkā puññaṃ saṅkhātuṃ, imettamapi kenaci.

For one who honors such as these, who are emancipated and fearless, the merit cannot be measured by anyone as being 'this much'.

Buddhavaggo cuddasamo niṭṭhito.

The Fourteenth Chapter, the Chapter on the Buddha, is concluded.

15. Sukhavaggo

15. Chapter 15: Happiness

197.

197.

Susukhaṃ [P 0.0030] vata jīvāma, verinesu averino;

Verinesu manussesu, viharāma averino.

Very happily we live, free from hatred among those who hate; among hateful people, we dwell free from hatred.

198.

198.

Susukhaṃ [M 0.0043] vata jīvāma, āturesu anāturā;

Āturesu manussesu, viharāma anāturā.

Very happily we live, free from affliction among those who are afflicted; among afflicted people, we dwell free from affliction.

199.

199.

Susukhaṃ vata jīvāma, ussukesu anussukā;

Ussukesu [T 0.0042] manassesu, viharāma anussukā.

Very happily we live, free from anxiety among those who are anxious; among anxious people, we dwell free from anxiety.

200.

200.

Susukhaṃ vata jīvāma, yesaṃ no natthi kiñcanaṃ;

Pītibhakkhā bhavissāma, devā ābhassarā yathā.

Very happily we live, we who have no impediments. We shall feed on joy, like the Radiant Gods.

201.

201.

Jayaṃ veraṃ pasavati, dukkhaṃ seti parājito;

Upasanto sukhaṃ seti, hitvā jayaparājayaṃ.

Victory breeds hatred; the defeated one dwells in suffering. The peaceful one dwells happily, having abandoned both victory and defeat.

202.

202.

Natthi rāgasamo aggi, natthi dosasamo kali;

Natthi khandhasamā khandhādisā (sī. syā. pī. rūpasiddhiyā sameti) dukkhā, natthi santiparaṃ sukhaṃ.

There is no fire like lust, no crime like hatred. There is no suffering like the aggregates, no happiness higher than peace.

203.

203.

Jighacchāparamā [V 0.0039] rogā, saṅkhāraparamā saṅkārā paramā (bahūsu) dukhā;

Etaṃ ñatvā yathābhūtaṃ, nibbānaṃ paramaṃ sukhaṃ.

Hunger is the greatest illness, conditioned things the greatest suffering. Knowing this as it truly is, Nibbāna is the supreme happiness.

204.

204.

Ārogyaparamā lābhā, santuṭṭhiparamaṃ dhanaṃ;

Vissāsaparamā ñāti vissāsaparamo ñāti (ka. sī.), vissāsaparamā ñātī (sī. aṭṭha.), vissāsā paramā ñāti (ka.), nibbānaṃ paramaṃ nibbāṇaparamaṃ (ka. sī.) sukhaṃ.

Health is the highest gain, contentment the greatest wealth. Trust is the best kinsman, Nibbāna the supreme happiness.

205.

205.

Pavivekarasaṃ [P 0.0031] pitvā pītvā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.), rasaṃ upasamassa ca;

Niddaro hoti nippāpo, dhammapītirasaṃ pivaṃ.

Having tasted the flavor of solitude and the flavor of peace, one becomes free from distress and free from evil, drinking the flavor of joy in the Dhamma.

206.

206.

Sāhu [M 0.0044] dassanamariyānaṃ, sannivāso sadā sukho;

Adassanena bālānaṃ, niccameva sukhī siyā.

It is good to see the noble ones; to live with them is always happy. By not seeing fools, one is always happy.

207.

207.

Bālasaṅgatacārī bālasaṅgaticārī (ka.) hi, dīghamaddhāna socati;

Dukkho bālehi saṃvāso, amitteneva sabbadā;

Dhīro ca sukhasaṃvāso, ñātīnaṃva samāgamo.

He who travels in the company of fools sorrows for a long time. Association with fools is always painful, like association with an enemy; but association with the wise is pleasant, like a meeting with relatives.

208.

208.

Tasmā hi –

Dhīrañca paññañca bahussutañca, dhorayhasīlaṃ [T 0.0043] vatavantamariyaṃ;

Taṃ tādisaṃ sappurisaṃ sumedhaṃ, bhajetha nakkhattapathaṃva candimā tasmā hi dhīraṃ paññañca, bahussutañca dhorayhaṃ; sīlaṃ dhutavatamariyaṃ, taṃ tādisaṃ sappurisaṃ; sumedhaṃ bhajetha nakkhattapathaṃva candimā; (ka.).

Therefore, one should follow the wise, the discerning, the learned, the enduring, the dutiful, and the noble. Follow such a good and wise person, as the moon follows the path of the stars.

Sukhavaggo pannarasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on Happiness, the fifteenth, is concluded.

16. Piyavaggo

16. 16. The Chapter on the Beloved

209.

209.

Ayoge [V 0.0040] yuñjamattānaṃ, yogasmiñca ayojayaṃ;

Atthaṃ hitvā piyaggāhī, pihetattānuyoginaṃ.

Applying oneself to what should not be pursued, and not applying oneself to what should be pursued, abandoning the goal and grasping the pleasant, one envies those who apply themselves.

210.

Piyānaṃ adassanaṃ dukkhaṃ, appiyānañca dassanaṃ.

Do not associate with the dear, nor ever with the undear. Not seeing the dear is suffering, and seeing the undear is also suffering.

211.

211.

Tasmā piyaṃ na kayirātha, piyāpāyo hi pāpako;

Ganthā tesaṃ na vijjanti, yesaṃ natthi piyāppiyaṃ.

Therefore, one should hold nothing dear, for separation from the dear is painful. No bonds exist for those who have neither the dear nor the undear.

212.

212.

Piyato jāyatī soko, piyato jāyatī jāyate (ka.) bhayaṃ;

Piyato vippamuttassa, natthi soko kuto bhayaṃ.

From the dear springs sorrow, from the dear springs fear; for one freed from the dear, there is no sorrow, whence fear?

213.

213.

Pemato [M 0.0045] jāyatī soko, pemato jāyatī bhayaṃ;

Pemato vippamuttassa, natthi soko kuto bhayaṃ.

From affection springs sorrow, from affection springs fear; for one freed from affection, there is no sorrow, whence fear?

214.

214.

Ratiyā jāyatī soko, ratiyā jāyatī bhayaṃ;

Ratiyā vippamuttassa, natthi soko kuto bhayaṃ.

From delight springs sorrow, from delight springs fear; for one freed from delight, there is no sorrow, whence fear?

215.

215.

Kāmato jāyatī soko, kāmato jāyatī bhayaṃ;

Kāmato [T 0.0044] vippamuttassa, natthi soko kuto bhayaṃ.

From sensual pleasure springs sorrow, from sensual pleasure springs fear; for one freed from sensual pleasure, there is no sorrow, whence fear?

216.

216.

Taṇhāya jāyatī jāyate (ka.) soko, taṇhāya jāyatī bhayaṃ;

Taṇhāya vippamuttassa, natthi soko kuto bhayaṃ.

From craving springs sorrow, from craving springs fear; for one freed from craving, there is no sorrow, whence fear?

217.

217.

Sīladassanasampannaṃ[V 0.0041], dhammaṭṭhaṃ saccavedinaṃ;

Attano kamma kubbānaṃ, taṃ jano kurute piyaṃ.

One endowed with virtue and insight, established in the Dhamma, who knows the truth and performs their own duties—such a one the people hold dear.

218.

218.

Chandajāto anakkhāte, manasā ca phuṭo siyā;

Kāmesu ca appaṭibaddhacitto appaṭibandhacitto (ka.), uddhaṃsototi vuccati.

One in whom longing for the Undeclared has arisen, whose mind is touched by it, and whose mind is not bound by sensual pleasures, is called an 'upstream-goer'.

219.

219.

Cirappavāsiṃ purisaṃ, dūrato sotthimāgataṃ;

Ñātimittā suhajjā ca, abhinandanti āgataṃ.

When a person long absent returns safely from afar, relatives, friends, and well-wishers rejoice at their arrival.

220.

220.

Tatheva [P 0.0033] katapuññampi, asmā lokā paraṃ gataṃ;

Puññāni paṭigaṇhanti, piyaṃ ñātīva āgataṃ.

In the same way, when one who has done merit has gone from this world to the next, their merits receive them, as relatives welcome a loved one who has returned.

Piyavaggo soḷasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Beloved, the sixteenth, is concluded.

17. Kodhavaggo

17. 17. The Chapter on Anger

221.

221.

Kodhaṃ [M 0.0046] jahe vippajaheyya mānaṃ, saṃyojanaṃ sabbamatikkameyya;

Taṃ nāmarūpasmimasajjamānaṃ, akiñcanaṃ nānupatanti dukkhā.

One should abandon anger and relinquish conceit; one should overcome all fetters. Sufferings do not befall one who does not cling to name and form, and who is without possessions.

222.

222.

Yo ve uppatitaṃ kodhaṃ, rathaṃ bhantaṃva vāraye dhāraye (sī. syā. pī.);

Tamahaṃ [T 0.0045] sārathiṃ brūmi, rasmiggāho itaro jano.

Whoever restrains rising anger as one would a speeding chariot—that one I call a true charioteer; others merely hold the reins.

223.

223.

Akkodhena jine kodhaṃ, asādhuṃ sādhunā jine;

Jine kadariyaṃ dānena, saccenālikavādinaṃ.

Conquer anger with non-anger; conquer evil with good; conquer the miserly with generosity; conquer the liar with truth.

224.

224.

Saccaṃ bhaṇe na kujjheyya, dajjā appampi dajjā’ppasmimpi (sī. pī.), dajjā appasmi (syā. ka.) yācito;

Etehi tīhi ṭhānehi, gacche devāna santike.

Speak the truth, do not become angry, and give, even if it is a little, when you are asked. By these three principles, one may go to the presence of the gods.

225.

225.

Ahiṃsakā [V 0.0042][P 0.0034] ye munayo ahiṃsakāyā munayo (ka.), niccaṃ kāyena saṃvutā;

Te yanti accutaṃ ṭhānaṃ, yattha gantvā na socare.

Those sages who are harmless, ever restrained in body, go to the deathless state where, having gone, they do not grieve.

226.

226.

Sadā jāgaramānānaṃ, ahorattānusikkhinaṃ;

Nibbānaṃ adhimuttānaṃ, atthaṃ gacchanti āsavā.

For those who are ever vigilant, training day and night, who are intent on Nibbāna, the taints come to an end.

227.

227.

Porāṇametaṃ [M 0.0047] atula, netaṃ ajjatanāmiva;

Nindanti tuṇhimāsīnaṃ, nindanti bahubhāṇinaṃ;

Mitabhāṇimpi nindanti, natthi loke anindito.

This is an ancient matter, Atula, not just of today: they blame one who sits in silence, they blame one who speaks much, and they blame one who speaks in moderation; there is no one in the world who is not blamed.

228.

228.

Na cāhu na ca bhavissati, na cetarahi vijjati;

Ekantaṃ nindito poso, ekantaṃ vā pasaṃsito.

There never was, nor will there ever be, nor is there now, a person who is wholly blamed or wholly praised.

229.

229.

Yaṃ ce viññū pasaṃsanti, anuvicca suve suve;

Acchiddavuttiṃ acchinnavuttiṃ (ka.) medhāviṃ, paññāsīlasamāhitaṃ.

But the one whom the wise praise, having observed day after day—one of flawless conduct, intelligent, endowed with wisdom and virtue—

230.

230.

Nikkhaṃ nekkhaṃ (sī. syā. pī.) jambonadasseva, ko taṃ ninditumarahati;

Devāpi naṃ pasaṃsanti, brahmunāpi pasaṃsito.

Who is worthy to blame such a one, who is like a coin of refined gold? Even the gods praise them; by Brahmā too are they praised.

231.

231.

Kāyappakopaṃ rakkheyya, kāyena saṃvuto siyā;

Kāyaduccaritaṃ hitvā, kāyena sucaritaṃ care.

One should guard against bodily agitation and be restrained in body; having abandoned bodily misconduct, one should practice good conduct with the body.

232.

232.

Vacīpakopaṃ [T 0.0046] rakkheyya, vācāya saṃvuto siyā;

Vacīduccaritaṃ hitvā, vācāya sucaritaṃ care.

One should guard against verbal agitation and be restrained in speech; having abandoned verbal misconduct, one should practice good conduct with speech.

233.

233.

Manopakopaṃ rakkheyya, manasā saṃvuto siyā;

Manoduccaritaṃ hitvā, manasā sucaritaṃ care.

One should guard against mental agitation and be restrained in mind; having abandoned mental misconduct, one should practice good conduct with the mind.

234.

234.

Kāyena [V 0.0043] saṃvutā dhīrā, atho vācāya saṃvutā;

Manasā saṃvutā dhīrā, te ve suparisaṃvutā.

The wise are restrained in body, and also restrained in speech; the wise are restrained in mind—they are indeed perfectly restrained.

Kodhavaggo sattarasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on Anger, the seventeenth, is finished.

18. Malavaggo

18. The Chapter on Stains (Malavagga)

235.

235.

Paṇḍupalāsova [P 0.0035] dānisi, yamapurisāpi ca te taṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) upaṭṭhitā;

Uyyogamukhe ca tiṭṭhasi, pātheyyampi ca te na vijjati.

Like a withered leaf you are now; even the messengers of death have arrived for you. You stand at the threshold of departure, and you have no provisions for the journey.

236.

236.

So [M 0.0048] karohi dīpamattano, khippaṃ vāyama paṇḍito bhava;

Niddhantamalo anaṅgaṇo, dibbaṃ ariyabhūmiṃ upehisi dibbaṃ ariyabhūmimehisi (sī. syā. pī.), dibbamariyabhūmiṃ upehisi (?).

Make an island for yourself, strive quickly, become wise! With impurities removed and being stainless, you will reach the divine land of the Ariyas.

237.

237.

Upanītavayo ca dānisi, sampayātosi [T 0.0047] yamassa santike;

Vāso vāsopi ca (bahūsu) te natthi antarā, pātheyyampi ca te na vijjati.

Your age has now passed; you have set out for the presence of Yama. There is no resting place for you on the way, and you have no provisions for the journey.

238.

238.

So karohi dīpamattano, khippaṃ vāyama paṇḍito bhava;

Niddhantamalo anaṅgaṇo, na punaṃ jātijaraṃ na puna jātijaraṃ (sī. syā.), na puna jātijjaraṃ (ka.) upehisi.

Make an island for yourself! Strive quickly, become wise! With impurities removed and being stainless, you will not come again to birth and old age.

239.

239.

Anupubbena medhāvī, thokaṃ thokaṃ khaṇe khaṇe;

Kammāro rajatasseva, niddhame malamattano.

Gradually, little by little, from moment to moment, let the wise person remove their own impurities, just as a smith removes the dross from silver.

240.

240.

Ayasāva malaṃ samuṭṭhitaṃ samuṭṭhāya (ka.), tatuṭṭhāya taduṭṭhāya (sī. syā. pī.) tameva khādati;

Evaṃ atidhonacārinaṃ, sāni kammāni sakakammāni (sī. pī.) nayanti duggatiṃ.

Just as rust arises from iron and eats away that very iron, so too do their own deeds lead the transgressor to a state of woe.

241.

241.

Asajjhāyamalā [V 0.0044][P 0.0036] mantā, anuṭṭhānamalā gharā;

Malaṃ vaṇṇassa kosajjaṃ, pamādo rakkhato malaṃ.

Non-repetition is the stain of scriptures; non-maintenance is the stain of houses; laziness is the stain of beauty; heedlessness is the stain of a guard.

242.

242.

Malitthiyā duccaritaṃ, maccheraṃ dadato malaṃ;

Malā ve pāpakā dhammā, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca.

Misconduct is the stain of a woman; stinginess is the stain of a giver; truly, evil states are stains in both this world and the next.

243.

243.

Tato malā malataraṃ, avijjā paramaṃ malaṃ;

Etaṃ malaṃ pahantvāna, nimmalā hotha bhikkhavo.

But worse than these stains is ignorance, the greatest stain. Having abandoned this stain, be stainless, O monks!

244.

244.

Sujīvaṃ [T 0.0048] ahirikena, kākasūrena dhaṃsinā;

Pakkhandinā pagabbhena, saṃkiliṭṭhena jīvitaṃ.

Life is easy for the shameless, for one who is as bold as a crow, a backbiter, an intruder, the arrogant, and the corrupt.

245.

245.

Hirīmatā [M 0.0049] ca dujjīvaṃ, niccaṃ sucigavesinā;

Alīnenāppagabbhena, suddhājīvena passatā.

But life is hard for the modest, for one who always seeks purity, who is energetic, unassuming, of pure livelihood, and discerning.

246.

246.

Yo pāṇamatipāteti, musāvādañca bhāsati;

Loke adinnamādiyati, paradārañca gacchati.

Whoever destroys life, speaks falsehood, takes what is not given in the world, and goes to another’s wife,

247.

247.

Surāmerayapānañca, yo naro anuyuñjati;

Idhevameso lokasmiṃ, mūlaṃ khaṇati attano.

and who is addicted to intoxicating drinks—that person, even here in this world, digs up his own root.

248.

248.

Evaṃ bho purisa jānāhi, pāpadhammā asaññatā;

Mā taṃ lobho adhammo ca, ciraṃ dukkhāya randhayuṃ.

Know this, O man: evil qualities are unrestrained. Do not let greed and unrighteousness subject you to long-lasting suffering.

249.

249.

Dadāti ve yathāsaddhaṃ, yathāpasādanaṃ yattha pasādanaṃ (katthaci) jano;

Tattha yo maṅku bhavati tattha ce maṃku yo hoti (sī.), tattha yo maṅkuto hoti (syā.), paresaṃ pānabhojane;

Na so divā vā rattiṃ vā, samādhimadhigacchati.

People give according to their faith and according to their devotion. Whoever is discontented with the food and drink of others does not attain concentration by day or night.

250.

250.

Yassa [V 0.0045][P 0.0037] cetaṃ samucchinnaṃ, mūlaghaccaṃ mūlaghacchaṃ (ka.) samūhataṃ;

Sa ve divā vā rattiṃ vā, samādhimadhigacchati.

But he in whom this is cut off, uprooted, and destroyed, certainly attains concentration by day or night.

251.

251.

Natthi rāgasamo aggi, natthi dosasamo gaho;

Natthi mohasamaṃ jālaṃ, natthi taṇhāsamā nadī.

There is no fire like lust, no grip like hatred, no net like delusion, and no river like craving.

252.

252.

Sudassaṃ vajjamaññesaṃ, attano pana duddasaṃ;

Paresaṃ hi so vajjāni, opunāti ophunāti (ka.) yathā bhusaṃ;

Attano pana chādeti, kaliṃva kitavā saṭho.

The faults of others are easy to see, but one's own are difficult to see. One winnows the faults of others like chaff, but hides one's own, just as a crafty gambler hides a losing throw.

253.

253.

Paravajjānupassissa[M 0.0050], [T 0.0049] niccaṃ ujjhānasaññino;

Āsavā tassa vaḍḍhanti, ārā so āsavakkhayā.

For one who looks for the faults of others and is always finding fault, their taints increase; they are far from the destruction of taints.

254.

254.

Ākāseva padaṃ natthi, samaṇo natthi bāhire;

Papañcābhiratā pajā, nippapañcā tathāgatā.

There is no footprint in the sky; there is no ascetic outside. Mankind delights in mental proliferation; the Tathāgatas are free from proliferation.

255.

255.

Ākāseva padaṃ natthi, samaṇo natthi bāhire;

Saṅkhārā sassatā natthi, natthi buddhānamiñjitaṃ.

There is no footprint in the sky; there is no ascetic outside. There are no eternal conditioned things; there is no wavering for the Buddhas.

Malavaggo aṭṭhārasamo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on Stains, the eighteenth, is finished.

19. Dhammaṭṭhavaggo

19. The Chapter on the Just (Dhammaᙡᙡhavagga)

256.

256.

Na [P 0.0038] tena hoti dhammaṭṭho, yenatthaṃ sāhasā sahasā (sī. syā. ka.) naye;

Yo ca atthaṃ anatthañca, ubho niccheyya paṇḍito.

One is not a judge just because one settles a case hastily. The wise person is one who investigates both what is right and what is wrong.

257.

257.

Asāhasena [V 0.0046] dhammena, samena nayatī pare;

Dhammassa gutto medhāvī, “dhammaṭṭho”ti pavuccati.

The wise one who guides others non-violently, in accordance with the Dhamma and impartiality, guarded by the Dhamma, is called 'one established in Dhamma'.

258.

258.

Na tena paṇḍito hoti, yāvatā bahu bhāsati;

Khemī averī abhayo, “paṇḍito”ti pavuccati.

One is not wise merely because one speaks much. One who is secure, free from hatred, and fearless is called 'wise'.

259.

259.

Na tāvatā dhammadharo, yāvatā bahu bhāsati;

Yo ca appampi sutvāna, dhammaṃ kāyena passati;

Sa ve dhammadharo hoti, yo dhammaṃ nappamajjati.

One is not a bearer of the Dhamma merely because one speaks much. But whoever, having heard even a little, sees the Dhamma with their own eyes and is not heedless of the Dhamma, is indeed a bearer of the Dhamma.

260.

260.

Na [M 0.0051] tena thero so hoti thero hoti (sī. syā.), yenassa palitaṃ siro;

Paripakko [T 0.0050] vayo tassa, “moghajiṇṇo”ti vuccati.

One is not an elder just because one's head is gray. One's age may be ripe, but one is called 'grown old in vain'.

261.

261.

Yamhi saccañca dhammo ca, ahiṃsā saṃyamo damo;

Sa ve vantamalo dhīro, “thero” iti so theroti (syā. ka.) pavuccati.

He in whom there are truth and Dhamma, non-harming, restraint, and self-control—that wise one who has cast out impurities is called an 'elder.'

262.

262.

Na vākkaraṇamattena, vaṇṇapokkharatāya vā;

Sādhurūpo naro hoti, issukī maccharī saṭho.

Not by mere eloquence, nor by a beautiful complexion, does a person who is envious, stingy, and deceitful become one of good character.

263.

263.

Yassa cetaṃ samucchinnaṃ, mūlaghaccaṃ samūhataṃ;

Sa vantadoso medhāvī, “sādhurūpo”ti vuccati.

But he in whom these are utterly cut off, uprooted, and eradicated—that wise one who has cast out faults is called 'one of good character.'

264.

264.

Na muṇḍakena samaṇo, abbato alikaṃ bhaṇaṃ;

Icchālobhasamāpanno, samaṇo kiṃ bhavissati.

Not by a shaven head does one become a recluse, if one is undisciplined and speaks falsehood. How can one filled with desire and greed be a recluse?

265.

265.

Yo [P 0.0039] ca sameti pāpāni, aṇuṃ thūlāni sabbaso;

Samitattā hi pāpānaṃ, “samaṇo”ti pavuccati.

But he who has pacified all evils, both small and great—he is called a 'recluse' because he has pacified all evils.

266.

266.

Na [V 0.0047] tena bhikkhu so hoti, yāvatā bhikkhate pare;

Vissaṃ dhammaṃ samādāya, bhikkhu hoti na tāvatā.

One is not a monk merely by begging from others; by adopting a foul practice, one does not on that account become a monk.

267.

267.

Yodha puññañca pāpañca, bāhetvā brahmacariyavā brahmacariyaṃ (ka.);

Saṅkhāya loke carati, sa ve “bhikkhū”ti vuccati.

He who in this world has set aside both merit and evil, and lives the holy life, faring through the world with understanding—he, indeed, is called a 'monk.'

268.

268.

Na monena munī hoti, mūḷharūpo aviddasu;

Yo ca tulaṃva paggayha, varamādāya paṇḍito.

One does not become a sage by silence if one is deluded and ignorant. But the wise one who, as if holding a scale, takes up what is best,

269.

269.

Pāpāni [M 0.0052] parivajjeti, sa munī tena so muni;

Yo munāti ubho loke, “muni” tena pavuccati.

and avoids evils, is a sage; for that reason he is a sage. He who understands both worlds is, on that account, called a 'sage.'

270.

270.

Na tena ariyo hoti, yena pāṇāni hiṃsati;

Ahiṃsā [T 0.0051] sabbapāṇānaṃ, “ariyo”ti pavuccati.

One is not noble if one harms living beings. Because of non-harming towards all living beings, one is called 'noble.'

271.

271.

Na sīlabbatamattena, bāhusaccena vā pana;

Atha vā samādhilābhena, vivittasayanena vā.

Not merely by virtue and observances, nor by much learning, nor by the attainment of concentration, nor by dwelling in a secluded place,

272.

272.

Phusāmi nekkhammasukhaṃ, aputhujjanasevitaṃ;

Bhikkhu vissāsamāpādi, appatto āsavakkhayaṃ.

thinking, 'I experience the bliss of renunciation not enjoyed by worldlings,' O monk, do not rest content until you have reached the destruction of the taints.

Dhammaṭṭhavaggo ekūnavīsatimo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Just, the nineteenth, is concluded.

20. Maggavaggo

20. The Chapter on the Path

273.

273.

Maggānaṭṭhaṅgiko [V 0.0048][P 0.0040] seṭṭho, saccānaṃ caturo padā;

Virāgo seṭṭho dhammānaṃ, dvipadānañca cakkhumā.

Of all paths, the Eightfold Path is the best; of all truths, the Four Noble Truths; of all things, dispassion is the best; and of all two-footed beings, the One with Vision is the best.

274.

274.

Eseva esova (sī. pī.) maggo natthañño, dassanassa visuddhiyā;

Etañhi tumhe paṭipajjatha, mārassetaṃ pamohanaṃ.

This is the only path; there is no other for the purification of vision. Follow this path, for it confuses Māra.

275.

275.

Etañhi tumhe paṭipannā, dukkhassantaṃ karissatha;

Akkhāto vo akkhāto ve (sī. pī.) mayā maggo, aññāya sallakantanaṃ sallasanthanaṃ (sī. pī.), sallasatthanaṃ (syā.).

By following this path, you will make an end of suffering. I have taught you the path, having myself known the extraction of the dart.

276.

276.

Tumhehi kiccamātappaṃ, akkhātāro tathāgatā;

Paṭipannā pamokkhanti, jhāyino mārabandhanā.

You yourselves must make the effort; the Tathāgatas are only teachers. Those who have entered the path and meditate are released from the bonds of Māra.

277.

277.

“Sabbe [M 0.0053] saṅkhārā aniccā”ti, yadā paññāya passati;

Atha [T 0.0052] nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā.

"All conditioned things are impermanent"—when one sees this with wisdom, one becomes weary of suffering. This is the path to purification.

278.

278.

“Sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā”ti, yadā paññāya passati;

Atha nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā.

"All conditioned things are suffering"—when one sees this with wisdom, one becomes weary of suffering. This is the path to purification.

279.

279.

“Sabbe dhammā anattā”ti, yadā paññāya passati;

Atha nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā.

"All things are not-self"—when one sees this with wisdom, one becomes weary of suffering. This is the path to purification.

280.

280.

Uṭṭhānakālamhi anuṭṭhahāno, yuvā balī ālasiyaṃ upeto;

Saṃsannasaṅkappamano asampannasaṅkappamano (ka.) kusīto, paññāya maggaṃ alaso na vindati.

He who does not rouse himself when it is time to rise, who, though young and strong, is full of sloth, whose mind is filled with sunken thoughts, such a lazy and indolent person does not find the path to wisdom.

281.

281.

Vācānurakkhī [V 0.0049][P 0.0041] manasā susaṃvuto, kāyena ca nākusalaṃ kayirā akusalaṃ na kayirā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.);

Ete tayo kammapathe visodhaye, ārādhaye maggamisippaveditaṃ.

One should be watchful of speech, well-restrained in mind, and do no unwholesome deed with the body. One should purify these three paths of action and fulfill the path taught by the seers.

282.

282.

Yogā ve jāyatī jāyate (katthaci) bhūri, ayogā bhūrisaṅkhayo;

Etaṃ dvedhāpathaṃ ñatvā, bhavāya vibhavāya ca;

Tathāttānaṃ niveseyya, yathā bhūri pavaḍḍhati.

From application wisdom is born; from non-application, wisdom declines. Knowing this twofold path of progress and decline, let one so establish oneself that wisdom may increase.

283.

283.

Vanaṃ [M 0.0054] chindatha mā rukkhaṃ, vanato jāyate bhayaṃ;

Chetvā vanañca vanathañca, nibbanā hotha bhikkhavo.

Cut down the forest of craving, not the tree; from the forest of craving fear is born. Having cut down both the forest and its undergrowth, O monks, be free from the forest.

284.

284.

Yāva hi vanatho na chijjati, aṇumattopi narassa nārisu;

Paṭibaddhamanova paṭibandhamanova (ka.) tāva so, vaccho khīrapakova khīrapānova (pī.) mātari.

For as long as the undergrowth of desire of a man for women is not cut down, even to the slightest degree, so long is his mind bound, like a suckling calf to its mother.

285.

285.

Ucchinda [T 0.0053] sinehamattano kumudaṃ sāradikaṃva pāṇinā;

Santimaggameva brūhaya, nibbānaṃ sugatena desitaṃ.

Cut off your own affection, as one plucks an autumn lily with the hand. Cultivate the very path to peace, Nibbāna, as taught by the Well-Farer.

286.

286.

Idha vassaṃ vasissāmi, idha hemantagimhisu;

Iti bālo vicinteti, antarāyaṃ na bujjhati.

"Here I will dwell during the rains, here in winter and summer," so the fool thinks, not realizing the danger to his life.

287.

287.

Taṃ puttapasusammattaṃ, byāsattamanasaṃ naraṃ;

Suttaṃ gāmaṃ mahoghova, maccu ādāya gacchati.

That man who is infatuated with children and cattle, whose mind is clinging to them, death carries him away as a great flood sweeps away a sleeping village.

288.

288.

Na [P 0.0042] santi puttā tāṇāya, na pitā nāpi bandhavā;

Antakenādhipannassa, natthi ñātīsu tāṇatā.

There are no sons for one's protection, nor a father, nor any relatives. For one who is seized by the End-maker, there is no protection among kin.

289.

289.

Etamatthavasaṃ ñatvā, paṇḍito sīlasaṃvuto;

Nibbānagamanaṃ maggaṃ, khippameva visodhaye.

Having understood the significance of this, the wise one, restrained by virtue, should quickly clear the path leading to Nibbāna.

Maggavaggo vīsatimo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Path, the Twentieth, is concluded.

21. Pakiṇṇakavaggo

21. The Miscellaneous Chapter

290.

290.

Mattāsukhapariccāgā[M 0.0055][V 0.0050], passe ce vipulaṃ sukhaṃ;

Caje mattāsukhaṃ dhīro, sampassaṃ vipulaṃ sukhaṃ.

If by renouncing a limited happiness one might see an abundant happiness, the wise one should renounce the limited happiness, having seen the abundant happiness.

291.

291.

Paradukkhūpadhānena, attano yo attano (syā. pī. ka.) sukhamicchati;

Verasaṃsaggasaṃsaṭṭho, verā so na parimuccati.

He who seeks his own happiness by inflicting suffering on others, being entangled in the bonds of hatred, is not freed from hatred.

292.

292.

Yañhi [T 0.0054] kiccaṃ apaviddhaṃ tadapaviddhaṃ (sī. syā.), akiccaṃ pana kayirati;

Unnaḷānaṃ pamattānaṃ, tesaṃ vaḍḍhanti āsavā.

What should be done is neglected, what should not be done is done; for the arrogant and heedless, their taints increase.

293.

293.

Yesañca susamāraddhā, niccaṃ kāyagatā sati;

Akiccaṃ te na sevanti, kicce sātaccakārino;

Satānaṃ sampajānānaṃ, atthaṃ gacchanti āsavā.

But for those whose mindfulness directed to the body is always well established, who do not pursue what should not be done and are persistent in doing what should be done—for such mindful and clearly comprehending ones, the taints go to destruction.

294.

294.

Mātaraṃ [P 0.0043] pitaraṃ hantvā, rājāno dve ca khattiye;

Raṭṭhaṃ sānucaraṃ hantvā, anīgho yāti brāhmaṇo.

Having slain mother and father, and two warrior kings, and having destroyed the kingdom with its retinue, the brahmin goes untroubled.

295.

295.

Mātaraṃ pitaraṃ hantvā, rājāno dve ca sotthiye;

Veyagghapañcamaṃ hantvā, anīgho yāti brāhmaṇo.

Having slain mother and father, and two brahmin kings, and having slain the tiger as the fifth, the brahmin goes untroubled.

296.

296.

Suppabuddhaṃ pabujjhanti, sadā gotamasāvakā;

Yesaṃ divā ca ratto ca, niccaṃ buddhagatā sati.

The disciples of Gotama are always well-awakened, they whose mindfulness by day and by night is constantly directed to the Buddha.

297.

297.

Suppabuddhaṃ pabujjhanti, sadā gotamasāvakā;

Yesaṃ divā ca ratto ca, niccaṃ dhammagatā sati.

The disciples of Gotama are always well-awakened, they whose mindfulness by day and by night is constantly directed to the Dhamma.

298.

298.

Suppabuddhaṃ [M 0.0056][V 0.0051] pabujjhanti, sadā gotamasāvakā;

Yesaṃ divā ca ratto ca, niccaṃ saṅghagatā sati.

The disciples of Gotama are always well-awakened, they whose mindfulness by day and by night is constantly directed to the Sangha.

299.

299.

Suppabuddhaṃ pabujjhanti, sadā gotamasāvakā;

Yesaṃ divā ca ratto ca, niccaṃ kāyagatā sati.

The disciples of Gotama are always well-awakened, they whose mindfulness by day and by night is constantly directed to the body.

300.

300.

Suppabuddhaṃ pabujjhanti, sadā gotamasāvakā;

Yesaṃ divā ca ratto ca, ahiṃsāya rato mano.

The disciples of Gotama are always well-awakened, they whose minds by day and by night delight in harmlessness.

301.

301.

Suppabuddhaṃ pabujjhanti, sadā gotamasāvakā;

Yesaṃ [T 0.0055] divā ca ratto ca, bhāvanāya rato mano.

The disciples of Gotama are always well-awakened, they whose minds by day and by night delight in mental development.

302.

302.

Duppabbajjaṃ durabhiramaṃ, durāvāsā gharā dukhā;

Dukkhosamānasaṃvāso, dukkhānupatitaddhagū;

Tasmā na caddhagū siyā, na ca tasmā na caddhagū na ca (ka.) dukkhānupatito siyā dukkhānupātito (?).

Difficult is the going forth, difficult to delight in. Difficult and painful is the household life. Painful is association with those unequal. The wayfarer is beset by suffering. Therefore, do not be a wayfarer; do not be one beset by suffering.

303.

303.

Saddho sīlena sampanno, yasobhogasamappito;

Yaṃ yaṃ padesaṃ bhajati, tattha tattheva pūjito.

One who has faith, is accomplished in virtue, and is endowed with fame and wealth, is honored wherever he goes.

304.

304.

Dūre santo pakāsenti, himavantova pabbato;

Asantettha na dissanti, rattiṃ khittā yathā sarā.

The good shine from afar like the Himalaya mountain; but the wicked are not seen here, like arrows shot in the night.

305.

305.

Ekāsanaṃ [P 0.0044] ekaseyyaṃ, eko caramatandito;

Eko damayamattānaṃ, vanante ramito siyā.

Sitting alone, lying down alone, wandering alone and unwearied, one who tames himself alone should find delight in the forest.

Pakiṇṇakavaggo ekavīsatimo niṭṭhito.

The Miscellaneous Chapter, the Twenty-first, is concluded.

22. Nirayavaggo

22. The Chapter on Hell

306.

306.

Abhūtavādī [M 0.0057][V 0.0052] nirayaṃ upeti, yo vāpi yo cāpi (sī. pī. ka.) katvā na karomi cāha na karomīti cāha (syā.);

Ubhopi te pecca samā bhavanti, nihīnakammā manujā parattha.

One who speaks falsely goes to hell, and so does one who, having done a deed, says, 'I did not do it.' Both of them, being people of base actions, become equal in the next world after death.

307.

307.

Kāsāvakaṇṭhā bahavo, pāpadhammā asaññatā;

Pāpā pāpehi kammehi, nirayaṃ te upapajjare.

Many with saffron robes around their necks are of evil character and unrestrained. These evil ones, through their evil deeds, are reborn in hell.

308.

308.

Seyyo [T 0.0056] ayoguḷo bhutto, tatto aggisikhūpamo;

Yañce bhuñjeyya dussīlo, raṭṭhapiṇḍamasaññato.

Better to swallow a hot iron ball, like a flame of fire, than that an immoral and unrestrained person should eat the alms-food of the country.

309.

309.

Cattāri ṭhānāni naro pamatto, āpajjati paradārūpasevī;

Apuññalābhaṃ na nikāmaseyyaṃ, nindaṃ tatīyaṃ nirayaṃ catutthaṃ.

A heedless man who consorts with another's wife incurs four misfortunes: acquisition of demerit; a bed without comfort; thirdly, blame; and fourthly, hell.

310.

310.

Apuññalābho [P 0.0045] ca gatī ca pāpikā, bhītassa bhītāya ratī ca thokikā;

Rājā ca daṇḍaṃ garukaṃ paṇeti, tasmā naro paradāraṃ na seve.

There is acquisition of demerit and an evil destination; brief is the pleasure of the frightened man with the frightened woman; and the king imposes a heavy penalty. Therefore a man should not consort with another’s wife.

311.

311.

Kuso yathā duggahito, hatthamevānukantati;

Sāmaññaṃ dupparāmaṭṭhaṃ, nirayāyupakaḍḍhati.

Just as kusa grass, when wrongly grasped, cuts the hand, so the ascetic life, when wrongly handled, drags one down to hell.

312.

312.

Yaṃ [M 0.0058] kiñci sithilaṃ kammaṃ, saṃkiliṭṭhañca yaṃ vataṃ;

Saṅkassaraṃ brahmacariyaṃ, na taṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.

Any slack action, any defiled observance, a holy life practiced with misgiving—this does not bring a great fruit.

313.

313.

Kayirā ce kayirāthenaṃ kayirā naṃ (ka.), daḷhamenaṃ parakkame;

Sithilo hi paribbājo, bhiyyo ākirate rajaṃ.

If a deed is to be done, one should do it with vigor. A lax renunciant only stirs up more dust.

314.

314.

Akataṃ [V 0.0053] dukkaṭaṃ seyyo, pacchā tappati dukkaṭaṃ;

Katañca sukataṃ seyyo, yaṃ katvā nānutappati.

Better is an evil deed left undone, for an evil deed torments one later. Better is a good deed done, which, having been done, does not lead to remorse.

315.

315.

Nagaraṃ yathā paccantaṃ, guttaṃ santarabāhiraṃ;

Evaṃ [T 0.0057] gopetha attānaṃ, khaṇo vo khaṇo ve (sī. pī. ka.) mā upaccagā;

Khaṇātītā hi socanti, nirayamhi samappitā.

Just as a frontier city is guarded within and without, so should you guard yourself. Do not let the opportune moment pass, for those who miss the moment grieve when consigned to hell.

316.

316.

Alajjitāye lajjanti, lajjitāye na lajjare;

Micchādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti duggatiṃ.

Ashamed of what is not shameful, and not ashamed of what is shameful, beings who adopt wrong views go to a woeful state.

317.

317.

Abhaye bhayadassino, bhaye cābhayadassino;

Micchādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti duggatiṃ.

Seeing fear in what is fearless and seeing no fear in what is fearful, beings who adopt wrong views go to a woeful state.

318.

318.

Avajje [P 0.0046] vajjamatino, vajje cāvajjadassino;

Micchādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti duggatiṃ.

Perceiving fault in what is faultless and seeing no fault in what is faulty, beings who adopt wrong views go to a woeful state.

319.

319.

Vajjañca vajjato ñatvā, avajjañca avajjato;

Sammādiṭṭhisamādānā, sattā gacchanti suggatiṃ.

Having known the faulty as faulty and the faultless as faultless, beings who adopt right view go to a happy state.

Nirayavaggo dvāvīsatimo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on Hell, the twenty-second, is concluded.

23. Nāgavaggo

23. The Chapter on the Elephant

320.

320.

Ahaṃ [M 0.0059] nāgova saṅgāme, cāpato patitaṃ saraṃ;

Ativākyaṃ titikkhissaṃ, dussīlo hi bahujjano.

As an elephant in battle endures an arrow shot from a bow, so shall I endure harsh speech; for many people are immoral.

321.

321.

Dantaṃ [V 0.0054] nayanti samitiṃ, dantaṃ rājābhirūhati;

Danto seṭṭho manussesu, yotivākyaṃ titikkhati.

A tamed beast is led to the assembly; the king mounts a tamed beast. Best among humans is the tamed one who endures harsh speech.

322.

322.

Varamassatarā dantā, ājānīyā ca ājānīyāva (syā.) sindhavā;

Kuñjarā ca kuñjarāva (syā.) mahānāgā, attadanto tato varaṃ.

Excellent are tamed mules, and thoroughbreds of Sindh, and great tusked elephants; but one who has tamed himself is better still.

323.

323.

Na [T 0.0058] hi etehi yānehi, gaccheyya agataṃ disaṃ;

Yathāttanā sudantena, danto dantena gacchati.

For not by these mounts can one go to the unreached land, as a tamed person goes by means of his own well-tamed self.

324.

324.

Dhanapālo dhanapālako (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) nāma kuñjaro, kaṭukabhedano kaṭukappabhedano (sī. syā. pī.) dunnivārayo;

Baddho kabaḷaṃ na bhuñjati, sumarati susarati (ka.) nāgavanassa kuñjaro.

The elephant named Dhanapāla, pungent in rut and hard to restrain, does not eat a morsel when tied up; the elephant longs for the elephant forest.

325.

325.

Middhī [P 0.0047] yadā hoti mahagghaso ca, niddāyitā samparivattasāyī;

Mahāvarāhova nivāpapuṭṭho, punappunaṃ gabbhamupeti mando.

When one is torpid and a glutton, a sleepy-head who lies rolling about like a great hog fattened on slops, the dullard enters the womb again and again.

326.

326.

Idaṃ [M 0.0060] pure cittamacāri cārikaṃ, yenicchakaṃ yatthakāmaṃ yathāsukhaṃ;

Tadajjahaṃ niggahessāmi yoniso, hatthippabhinnaṃ viya aṅkusaggaho.

Formerly this mind wandered as it wished, where it liked, as it pleased. Today I shall restrain it thoroughly, as a mahout with a hook restrains an elephant in rut.

327.

327.

Appamādaratā hotha, sacittamanurakkhatha;

Duggā uddharathattānaṃ, paṅke sannova sattova (sī. pī.) kuñjaro.

Delight in heedfulness; guard your own mind. Lift yourselves out of the mire, as an elephant sunk in the mud.

328.

328.

Sace labhetha nipakaṃ sahāyaṃ, saddhiṃ caraṃ sādhuvihāridhīraṃ;

Abhibhuyya sabbāni parissayāni, careyya tenattamano satīmā.

If you should find a prudent companion to travel with, one who is virtuous and steadfast, then overcoming all dangers, travel with him, gratified and mindful.

329.

329.

No [T 0.0059] ce labhetha nipakaṃ sahāyaṃ, saddhiṃ caraṃ sādhuvihāridhīraṃ;

Rājāva raṭṭhaṃ vijitaṃ pahāya, eko care mātaṅgaraññeva nāgo.

If you do not find a prudent companion to travel with, one who is virtuous and steadfast, fare alone like a king who has renounced his conquered kingdom, like an elephant in the Mātaṅga forest.

330.

330.

Ekassa caritaṃ seyyo, natthi bāle sahāyatā;

Eko care na ca pāpāni kayirā, appossukko mātaṅgaraññeva nāgo.

It is better to fare alone; there is no companionship with a fool. Fare alone and do no evil, with few wants, like an elephant in the Mātaṅga forest.

331.

331.

Atthamhi [V 0.0055] jātamhi sukhā sahāyā, tuṭṭhī sukhā yā itarītarena;

Puññaṃ sukhaṃ jīvitasaṅkhayamhi, sabbassa dukkhassa sukhaṃ pahānaṃ.

Happy are companions when a need arises; happy is contentment with whatever there is. Happy is merit at the end of life; the abandoning of all suffering is happiness.

332.

332.

Sukhā [M 0.0061][P 0.0048] matteyyatā loke, atho petteyyatā sukhā;

Sukhā sāmaññatā loke, atho brahmaññatā sukhā.

Happy in the world is reverence for one's mother; happy, too, is reverence for one's father. Happy in the world is reverence for recluses; happy, too, is reverence for the holy ones.

333.

333.

Sukhaṃ yāva jarā sīlaṃ, sukhā saddhā patiṭṭhitā;

Sukho paññāya paṭilābho, pāpānaṃ akaraṇaṃ sukhaṃ.

Happy is virtue until old age; happy is faith firmly established; happy is the acquisition of wisdom; happy is the non-doing of evil deeds.

Nāgavaggo tevīsatimo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Elephant, the twenty-third, is concluded.

24. Taṇhāvaggo

24. The Chapter on Craving

334.

334.

Manujassa [T 0.0060] pamattacārino, taṇhā vaḍḍhati māluvā viya;

So plavatī plavati (sī. pī.), palavetī (ka.), uplavati (?) hurā huraṃ, phalamicchaṃva vanasmi vānaro.

For a person who lives heedlessly, craving grows like a māluvā creeper. He leaps from one life to another like a monkey in a forest seeking fruit.

335.

335.

Yaṃ esā sahate jammī, taṇhā loke visattikā;

Sokā tassa pavaḍḍhanti, abhivaṭṭhaṃva abhivaḍḍhaṃva (syā.), abhivaṭṭaṃva (pī.), abhivuḍḍhaṃva (ka.) bīraṇaṃ.

Whomever this base, clinging craving overcomes in the world, his sorrows flourish like well-watered bīraṇa grass.

336.

336.

Yo cetaṃ sahate jammiṃ, taṇhaṃ loke duraccayaṃ;

Sokā tamhā papatanti, udabinduva pokkharā.

But whoever in the world overcomes this base craving, so hard to transcend, from him sorrows fall away like water drops from a lotus leaf.

337.

337.

Taṃ vo vadāmi bhaddaṃ vo, yāvantettha samāgatā;

Taṇhāya mūlaṃ khaṇatha, usīratthova bīraṇaṃ;

Mā vo naḷaṃva sotova, māro bhañji punappunaṃ.

This I tell you—blessings to you, all who have gathered here: dig up the root of craving, as one seeking fragrant usīra root digs up bīraṇa grass. Do not let Māra break you again and again, as a river breaks a reed.

338.

338.

Yathāpi [M 0.0062][V 0.0056][P 0.0049] mūle anupaddave daḷhe, chinnopi rukkho punareva rūhati;

Evampi taṇhānusaye anūhate, nibbattatī dukkhamidaṃ punappunaṃ.

Just as a tree, though cut down, grows again if its root is undamaged and firm, so too, if the underlying tendency to craving is not uprooted, this suffering arises again and again.

339.

339.

Yassa chattiṃsati sotā, manāpasavanā bhusā;

Māhā vāhā (sī. syā. pī.) vahanti duddiṭṭhiṃ, saṅkappā rāganissitā.

For one in whom the thirty-six streams flowing toward the agreeable are strong, great floods of passion-based thoughts carry away that person of wrong view.

340.

340.

Savanti [T 0.0061] sabbadhi sotā, latā uppajja ubbhijja (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) tiṭṭhati;

Tañca disvā lataṃ jātaṃ, mūlaṃ paññāya chindatha.

The streams flow everywhere; the creeper of craving springs up and remains. Seeing that creeper has sprung up, cut off its root with wisdom.

341.

341.

Saritāni sinehitāni ca, somanassāni bhavanti jantuno;

Te sātasitā sukhesino, te ve jātijarūpagā narā.

Streams of craving and affections, and joys arise for a person. Dependent on the pleasant and seeking happiness, those people are indeed subject to birth and aging.

342.

342.

Tasiṇāya purakkhatā pajā, parisappanti sasova bandhito bādhito (bahūsu);

Saṃyojanasaṅgasattakā, dukkhamupenti punappunaṃ cirāya.

People beset by craving crawl about like a snared hare. Bound by the sticky bond of fetters, they undergo suffering again and again for a long time.

343.

343.

Tasiṇāya purakkhatā pajā, parisappanti sasova bandhito;

Tasmā tasiṇaṃ vinodaye, ākaṅkhanta bhikkhū ākaṅkhī (sī.), bhikkhu ākaṅkhaṃ (syā.) virāgamattano.

People beset by craving crawl about like a snared hare. Therefore, one who longs for one's own dispassion should dispel craving.

344.

344.

Yo nibbanatho vanādhimutto, vanamutto vanameva dhāvati;

Taṃ puggalametha passatha, mutto bandhanameva dhāvati.

He who, having left the jungle of household life and entered the jungle of practice, though freed from the jungle of household life, runs back to that very jungle. Come, look at that person: though freed from bondage, he runs back to the very bondage.

345.

345.

Na [M 0.0063][P 0.0050] taṃ daḷhaṃ bandhanamāhu dhīrā, yadāyasaṃ dārujapabbajañca dārūjaṃ babbajañca (sī. pī.);

Sārattarattā [T 0.0062] maṇikuṇḍalesu, puttesu dāresu ca yā apekkhā.

The wise do not call that a strong bond which is made of iron, wood, or hemp. But that passionate attachment for jeweled earrings, and the longing for children and wives—

346.

346.

Etaṃ daḷhaṃ bandhanamāhu dhīrā, ohārinaṃ sithilaṃ duppamuñcaṃ;

Etampi chetvāna paribbajanti, anapekkhino kāmasukhaṃ pahāya.

this, the wise say, is a strong bond: dragging one down, seemingly loose yet hard to undo. Having cut this too, they wander forth, without longing, having abandoned sensual pleasures.

347.

347.

Ye rāgarattānupatanti sotaṃ, sayaṃkataṃ makkaṭakova jālaṃ;

Etampi chetvāna vajanti dhīrā, anapekkhino sabbadukkhaṃ pahāya.

Those impassioned by lust fall into the stream of craving they have made themselves, like a spider into the web it has spun. Having cut this too, the wise go forth, without longing, having abandoned all suffering.

348.

348.

Muñca [V 0.0057] pure muñca pacchato, majjhe muñca bhavassa pāragū;

Sabbattha vimuttamānaso, na punaṃ jātijaraṃ upehisi.

Let go of the past, let go of the future, let go of the middle. Having gone to the far shore of existence, with a mind released in every respect, you will not again undergo birth and aging.

349.

349.

Vitakkamathitassa jantuno, tibbarāgassa subhānupassino;

Bhiyyo taṇhā pavaḍḍhati, esa kho daḷhaṃ esa gāḷhaṃ (ka.) karoti bandhanaṃ.

For a person agitated by thoughts, of intense passion, and perceiving the beautiful, craving increases all the more. He indeed makes his bondage strong.

350.

350.

Vitakkūpasame [M 0.0064] ca vitakkūpasameva (ka.) yo rato, asubhaṃ bhāvayate sadā sato;

Esa [T 0.0063] eso (?) kho byanti kāhiti, esa eso (?) checchati mārabandhanaṃ.

But he who delights in the calming of thoughts, ever mindful, develops the perception of the foul. He will surely make an end of craving; he will cut the bond of Mara.

351.

351.

Niṭṭhaṅgato [P 0.0051] asantāsī, vītataṇho anaṅgaṇo;

Acchindi bhavasallāni, antimoyaṃ samussayo.

He who has reached the goal, is fearless, free from craving, and stainless, has cut off the thorns of existence. This is his final body.

352.

352.

Vītataṇho anādāno, niruttipadakovido;

Akkharānaṃ sannipātaṃ, jaññā pubbāparāni ca;

Sa ve “antimasārīro, mahāpañño mahāpuriso”ti vuccati.

He who is free from craving and grasping, skilled in etymology and terms, who knows the arrangement of letters and their sequence—he is truly called one who bears his final body, one of great wisdom, a great man.

353.

353.

Sabbābhibhū sabbavidūhamasmi, sabbesu dhammesu anūpalitto;

Sabbañjaho taṇhakkhaye vimutto, sayaṃ abhiññāya kamuddiseyyaṃ.

I am the all-conqueror, the all-knower, untainted in all things. I have abandoned everything, liberated through the destruction of craving. Having realized this myself through direct knowledge, whom should I point to as my teacher?

354.

354.

Sabbadānaṃ dhammadānaṃ jināti, sabbarasaṃ dhammaraso jināti;

Sabbaratiṃ dhammarati jināti, taṇhakkhayo sabbadukkhaṃ jināti.

The gift of the Dhamma excels all gifts; the taste of the Dhamma excels all tastes; delight in the Dhamma excels all delights. The destruction of craving conquers all suffering.

355.

355.

Hananti bhogā dummedhaṃ, no ca pāragavesino;

Bhogataṇhāya dummedho, hanti aññeva attanaṃ.

Riches destroy the witless, but not those who seek the Far Shore. Through craving for riches, the witless person destroys himself as if destroying others.

356.

356.

Tiṇadosāni khettāni, rāgadosā ayaṃ pajā;

Tasmā hi vītarāgesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.

Fields are ruined by weeds; this generation is ruined by lust. Therefore, what is given to those free from lust bears great fruit.

357.

Tiṇadosāni [M 0.0065][V 0.0058][T 0.0064] khettāni, dosadosā ayaṃ pajā;

Tasmā hi vītadosesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.

Fields are ruined by weeds; this generation is ruined by hatred. Therefore, what is given to those free from hatred bears great fruit.

358.

Tiṇadosāni khettāni, mohadosā ayaṃ pajā;

Tasmā hi vītamohesu, dinnaṃ hoti mahapphalaṃ.

Fields are ruined by weeds; this generation is ruined by delusion. Therefore, what is given to those free from delusion bears great fruit.

(Fields are ruined by weeds; this generation is ruined by desire. Therefore, what is given to those free from desire bears great fruit.)

The Twenty-fourth Chapter on Craving is concluded.

25. Bhikkhuvaggo

25. The Chapter on the Bhikkhu

360.

360.

Cakkhunā saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu sotena saṃvaro;

Ghānena saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu jivhāya saṃvaro.

Good is restraint in the eye, good is restraint in the ear; good is restraint in the nose, good is restraint in the tongue.

361.

361.

Kāyena saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu vācāya saṃvaro;

Manasā saṃvaro sādhu, sādhu sabbattha saṃvaro;

Sabbattha saṃvuto bhikkhu, sabbadukkhā pamuccati.

Good is restraint in the body, good is restraint in speech; good is restraint in the mind, good is restraint in all things. A bhikkhu restrained in all things is freed from all suffering.

362.

362.

Hatthasaṃyato pādasaṃyato, vācāsaṃyato saṃyatuttamo;

Ajjhattarato samāhito, eko santusito tamāhu bhikkhuṃ.

Restrained in hand, restrained in foot, restrained in speech, restrained in the whole body; delighting in meditation, composed, alone, and content—him they call a bhikkhu.

363.

363.

Yo [V 0.0059] mukhasaṃyato bhikkhu, mantabhāṇī anuddhato;

Atthaṃ dhammañca dīpeti, madhuraṃ tassa bhāsitaṃ.

The bhikkhu who is restrained in speech, speaks with wisdom, is not scattered in mind, and explains the meaning and the Dhamma—sweet is his speech.

364.

364.

Dhammārāmo [M 0.0066][P 0.0053][T 0.0065] dhammarato, dhammaṃ anuvicintayaṃ;

Dhammaṃ anussaraṃ bhikkhu, saddhammā na parihāyati.

Delighting in the Dhamma, devoted to the Dhamma, meditating on the Dhamma, the bhikkhu who recollects the Dhamma does not fall away from the true Dhamma.

365.

365.

Salābhaṃ nātimaññeyya, nāññesaṃ pihayaṃ care;

Aññesaṃ pihayaṃ bhikkhu, samādhiṃ nādhigacchati.

One should not despise one's own gains, nor wander about envying the gains of others. A bhikkhu who envies the gains of others does not attain concentration.

366.

366.

Appalābhopi ce bhikkhu, salābhaṃ nātimaññati;

Taṃ ve devā pasaṃsanti, suddhājīviṃ atanditaṃ.

Even if a bhikkhu receives little, if he does not despise his own gains, the gods indeed praise him—one of pure livelihood, untiring.

367.

367.

Sabbaso nāmarūpasmiṃ, yassa natthi mamāyitaṃ;

Asatā ca na socati, sa ve “bhikkhū”ti vuccati.

He for whom there is no sense of ‘mine’ at all in name and form, who does not grieve when they perish—he is truly called a ‘bhikkhu.’

368.

368.

Mettāvihārī yo bhikkhu, pasanno buddhasāsane;

Adhigacche padaṃ santaṃ, saṅkhārūpasamaṃ sukhaṃ.

A bhikkhu who dwells in loving-kindness, serene in the Buddha's Dispensation, attains the peaceful state, the blissful stilling of formations.

369.

369.

Siñca bhikkhu imaṃ nāvaṃ, sittā te lahumessati;

Chetvā rāgañca dosañca, tato nibbānamehisi.

Bail out this boat, O bhikkhu! Bailed out, it will go swiftly. Cutting off lust and hate, then you will go to Nibbāna.

370.

370.

Pañca chinde pañca jahe, pañca cuttari bhāvaye;

Pañca saṅgātigo bhikkhu, “oghatiṇṇo”ti vuccati.

Cut off five, abandon five, and further cultivate five; the bhikkhu who has transcended the five attachments is called 'one who has crossed the flood.'

371.

371.

Jhāya bhikkhu jhāya tuvaṃ bhikkhu (?) mā pamādo mā ca pamādo (sī. syā. pī.), mā te kāmaguṇe ramessu bhamassu (sī. pī.), bhavassu (syā.), ramassu (ka.) cittaṃ;

Mā lohaguḷaṃ gilī pamatto, mā kandi “dukkhamida”nti ḍayhamāno.

Meditate, O bhikkhu, and be not heedless; let not your mind delight in sensual pleasures. Lest, being heedless, you swallow a molten iron ball, and burning, cry out, 'This is suffering!'

372.

372.

Natthi jhānaṃ apaññassa, paññā natthi ajhāyato ajjhāyino (ka.);

Yamhi jhānañca paññā ca, sa ve nibbānasantike.

There is no jhāna for one without wisdom, nor is there wisdom for one who does not practice jhāna; he in whom there is both jhāna and wisdom is indeed near Nibbāna.

373.

373.

Suññāgāraṃ [M 0.0067][V 0.0060][P 0.0054][T 0.0066] paviṭṭhassa, santacittassa bhikkhuno;

Amānusī rati hoti, sammā dhammaṃ vipassato.

For a bhikkhu who has entered an empty dwelling, whose mind is tranquil, there arises a superhuman delight as he rightly discerns the Dhamma.

374.

374.

Yato yato sammasati, khandhānaṃ udayabbayaṃ;

Labhatī labhati (pī.), labhate (ka.) pītipāmojjaṃ, amataṃ taṃ vijānataṃ.

Whenever he comprehends the rise and fall of the aggregates, he obtains joy and gladness; that, for those who know, is the deathless.

375.

375.

Tatrāyamādi bhavati, idha paññassa bhikkhuno;

Indriyagutti santuṭṭhi, pātimokkhe ca saṃvaro.

Here, for a wise bhikkhu, this is the beginning: guarding the senses, contentment, and restraint in accordance with the Pātimokkha.

376.

376.

Mitte bhajassu kalyāṇe, suddhājīve atandite;

Paṭisanthāravutyassa paṭisandhāravutyassa (ka.), ācārakusalo siyā;

Tato pāmojjabahulo, dukkhassantaṃ karissati.

Associate with noble friends, pure in livelihood and diligent. One should be courteous in conduct and skilled in right behavior; then, full of gladness, one will make an end of suffering.

377.

377.

Vassikā viya pupphāni, maddavāni majjavāni (ka. ṭīkā) paccavāni (ka. aṭṭha.) pamuñcati;

Evaṃ rāgañca dosañca, vippamuñcetha bhikkhavo.

As the jasmine sheds its withered flowers, even so, O bhikkhus, should you shed lust and hatred.

378.

378.

Santakāyo santavāco, santavā susamāhito santamano susamāhito (syā. pī.), santamano samāhito (ka.);

Vantalokāmiso bhikkhu, “upasanto”ti vuccati.

Calm in body, calm in speech, calm in mind and well-composed, the bhikkhu who has relinquished worldly allurements is called 'one who is at peace.'

379.

379.

Attanā codayattānaṃ, paṭimaṃsetha attanā paṭimāse attamattanā (sī. pī.), paṭimaṃse tamattanā (syā.);

So attagutto satimā, sukhaṃ bhikkhu vihāhisi.

By yourself, admonish yourself; by yourself, examine yourself. Thus self-guarded and mindful, O bhikkhu, you will dwell in happiness.

380.

380.

Attā hi attano nātho, (ko hi nātho paro siyā) ( ) videsapotthakesu natthi

Attā hi attano gati;

Tasmā saṃyamamattānaṃ saṃyamaya’ttānaṃ (sī. pī.), assaṃ bhadraṃva vāṇijo.

One is indeed one's own protector, for who else could the protector be? One is indeed one's own refuge. Therefore, restrain yourself as a merchant restrains a fine horse.

381.

381.

Pāmojjabahulo [M 0.0068] bhikkhu, pasanno buddhasāsane;

Adhigacche padaṃ santaṃ, saṅkhārūpasamaṃ sukhaṃ.

The bhikkhu, full of gladness, serene in the Buddha's Dispensation, will attain the peaceful state, the bliss of the stilling of formations.

382.

382.

Yo [V 0.0061][P 0.0055] have daharo bhikkhu, yuñjati buddhasāsane;

Somaṃ [T 0.0067] so imaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) lokaṃ pabhāseti, abbhā muttova candimā.

Indeed, a young bhikkhu who applies himself to the Buddha's Dispensation illuminates this world like the moon freed from a cloud.

Bhikkhuvaggo pañcavīsatimo niṭṭhito.

The Chapter on the Bhikkhu, the Twenty-Fifth, is concluded.

26. Brāhmaṇavaggo

26. The Chapter on the Brahmin

383.

383.

Chinda sotaṃ parakkamma, kāme panuda brāhmaṇa;

Saṅkhārānaṃ khayaṃ ñatvā, akataññūsi brāhmaṇa.

Striving, O brahmin, cut off the stream; dispel sensual desires. Knowing the destruction of formations, you are a knower of the Uncreated, O brahmin.

384.

384.

Yadā dvayesu dhammesu, pāragū hoti brāhmaṇo;

Athassa sabbe saṃyogā, atthaṃ gacchanti jānato.

When a brahmin has gone to the far shore in the two phenomena, then for that one who knows, all fetters come to an end.

385.

385.

Yassa pāraṃ apāraṃ vā, pārāpāraṃ na vijjati;

Vītaddaraṃ visaṃyuttaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

For whom the near shore, the far shore, or both, does not exist; who is free from distress and unbound—him I call a brahmin.

386.

386.

Jhāyiṃ virajamāsīnaṃ, katakiccamanāsavaṃ;

Uttamatthamanuppattaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

Meditating, stainless, and seated in solitude, his duty done and free from taints, having attained the highest goal—him I call a brahmin.

387.

387.

Divā [M 0.0069] tapati ādicco, rattimābhāti candimā;

Sannaddho khattiyo tapati, jhāyī tapati brāhmaṇo;

Atha sabbamahorattiṃ sabbamahorattaṃ (?), buddho tapati tejasā.

By day the sun shines, by night the moon is radiant; the warrior shines in his armor, the brahmin shines in meditation; but the Buddha shines with radiance all day and all night.

388.

388.

Bāhitapāpoti [P 0.0056] brāhmaṇo, samacariyā samaṇoti vuccati;

Pabbājayamattano malaṃ, tasmā “pabbajito”ti vuccati.

Because he has banished evil, he is a 'brahmin'; because he lives in serenity, he is called a 'samaṇa'; because he has sent forth his own impurities, he is called a 'pabbajita.'

389.

389.

Na [V 0.0062][T 0.0068] brāhmaṇassa pahareyya, nāssa muñcetha brāhmaṇo;

Dhī dhi (syā. byākaraṇesu) brāhmaṇassa hantāraṃ, tato dhī yassa yo + assa = yassa muñcati.

One should not strike a brahmin, nor should a brahmin retaliate against him. Shame on one who strikes a brahmin, and more shame on one who retaliates.

390.

390.

Na brāhmaṇassetadakiñci seyyo, yadā nisedho manaso piyehi;

Yato yato hiṃsamano nivattati, tato tato sammatimeva dukkhaṃ.

For a brahmin, there is no greater good than when the mind is restrained from things that are dear. To the extent that the mind of ill will turns back, to that extent suffering is allayed.

391.

391.

Yassa kāyena vācāya, manasā natthi dukkaṭaṃ;

Saṃvutaṃ tīhi ṭhānehi, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who does no wrong by body, speech, or mind, being restrained in these three ways—him I call a brahmin.

392.

392.

Yamhā dhammaṃ vijāneyya, sammāsambuddhadesitaṃ;

Sakkaccaṃ taṃ namasseyya, aggihuttaṃva brāhmaṇo.

From whomever one might learn the Dhamma taught by the Perfectly Enlightened One, one should revere him with respect, as a brahmin reveres the sacrificial fire.

393.

393.

Na jaṭāhi na gottena, na jaccā hoti brāhmaṇo;

Yamhi saccañca dhammo ca, so sucī so ca brāhmaṇo.

Not by matted hair, nor by clan, nor by birth does one become a brahmin. He in whom there is truth and righteousness—he is pure, and he is a brahmin.

394.

394.

Kiṃ [M 0.0070] te jaṭāhi dummedha, kiṃ te ajinasāṭiyā;

Abbhantaraṃ te gahanaṃ, bāhiraṃ parimajjasi.

What use is matted hair to you, O fool? What use is a garment of antelope skin? Within you is a jungle, while you polish the outside.

395.

395.

Paṃsukūladharaṃ jantuṃ, kisaṃ dhamanisanthataṃ;

Ekaṃ vanasmiṃ jhāyantaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

The person who wears rag-robes, who is lean, with veins showing, who meditates alone in the forest—him I call a brahmin.

396.

396.

Na [P 0.0057] cāhaṃ brāhmaṇaṃ brūmi, yonijaṃ mattisambhavaṃ;

Bhovādi nāma so hoti, sace hoti sakiñcano;

Akiñcanaṃ anādānaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

I do not call him a brahmin merely because of his birth or being born from a mother. If he has defilements, he is called a 'Bhovādi'. Him who is without defilements and without grasping—I call a brahmin.

397.

397.

Sabbasaṃyojanaṃ chetvā, yo ve na paritassati;

Saṅgātigaṃ [T 0.0069] visaṃyuttaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who has cut all fetters and does not tremble, who has gone beyond attachment and is detached from defilements—him I call a brahmin.

398.

398.

Chetvā [V 0.0063] naddhiṃ nandhiṃ (ka. sī.), nandiṃ (pī.) varattañca, sandānaṃ sandāmaṃ (sī.) sahanukkamaṃ;

Ukkhittapalighaṃ buddhaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who has cut the strap (of anger) and the thong (of craving), the rope (of views) with its latent tendencies, and who has lifted the crossbar (of ignorance), the enlightened one—him I call a brahmin.

399.

399.

Akkosaṃ vadhabandhañca, aduṭṭho yo titikkhati;

Khantībalaṃ balānīkaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who, without anger, endures abuse, assault, and imprisonment; whose strength is patience and whose army is that strength—him I call a brahmin.

400.

400.

Akkodhanaṃ vatavantaṃ, sīlavantaṃ anussadaṃ;

Dantaṃ antimasārīraṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who is free from anger, observant of ascetic practices, virtuous, without craving, tamed, and bearing his final body—him I call a brahmin.

401.

401.

Vāri [M 0.0071] pokkharapatteva, āraggeriva sāsapo;

Yo na limpati lippati (sī. pī.) kāmesu, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

Like water on a lotus leaf, like a mustard seed on the point of an awl, he who is not smeared by sensual pleasures—him I call a brahmin.

402.

402.

Yo dukkhassa pajānāti, idheva khayamattano;

Pannabhāraṃ visaṃyuttaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who understands, here in this very life, the end of his own suffering; who has laid down the burden of the aggregates and is detached from defilements—him I call a brahmin.

403.

403.

Gambhīrapaññaṃ medhāviṃ, maggāmaggassa kovidaṃ;

Uttamatthamanuppattaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He of deep wisdom, the intelligent one, skilled in the right and wrong paths, who has reached the highest goal—him I call a brahmin.

404.

404.

Asaṃsaṭṭhaṃ [P 0.0058] gahaṭṭhehi, anāgārehi cūbhayaṃ;

Anokasārimappicchaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who is unmingled with both householders and the homeless, who wanders without a home and has few wants—him I call a brahmin.

405.

405.

Nidhāya daṇḍaṃ bhūtesu, tasesu thāvaresu ca;

Yo na hanti na ghāteti, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who has laid aside the rod toward all beings, both the trembling and the firm, and who neither kills nor causes others to kill—him I call a brahmin.

406.

406.

Aviruddhaṃ viruddhesu, attadaṇḍesu nibbutaṃ;

Sādānesu anādānaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who is unopposed among the opposed, at peace among those who have taken up weapons, and ungrasping among those who grasp—him I call a brahmin.

407.

407.

Yassa rāgo ca doso ca, māno makkho ca pātito;

Sāsaporiva [T 0.0070] āraggā āragge (ka.), tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He from whom lust, hatred, conceit, and detraction have fallen away, like a mustard seed from the point of an awl—him I call a brahmin.

408.

408.

Akakkasaṃ [M 0.0072][V 0.0064] viññāpaniṃ, giraṃ saccamudīraye;

Yāya nābhisaje kañci kiñci (ka.), tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who utters speech that is gentle, instructive, and true, by which he offends no one—him I call a brahmin.

409.

409.

Yodha dīghaṃ va rassaṃ vā, aṇuṃ thūlaṃ subhāsubhaṃ;

Loke adinnaṃ nādiyati nādeti (ma. ni. 2.459), tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who in this world does not take what is not given, whether long or short, small or large, good or bad—him I call a brahmin.

410.

410.

Āsā yassa na vijjanti, asmiṃ loke paramhi ca;

Nirāsāsaṃ nirāsayaṃ (sī. syā. pī.), nirāsakaṃ (?) visaṃyuttaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He for whom there are no yearnings for this world or the next, who is free from yearning and detached from defilements—him I call a brahmin.

411.

411.

Yassālayā na vijjanti, aññāya akathaṃkathī;

Amatogadhamanuppattaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He for whom there are no attachments, who through direct knowledge is free from doubt, and who has reached the depth of the Deathless—him I call a brahmin.

412.

412.

Yodha puññañca pāpañca, ubho saṅgamupaccagā;

Asokaṃ virajaṃ suddhaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who in this world has gone beyond attachment to both merit and demerit, who is sorrowless, stainless, and pure—him I call a brahmin.

413.

413.

Candaṃva vimalaṃ suddhaṃ, vippasannamanāvilaṃ;

Nandībhavaparikkhīṇaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who is like the moon—stainless, pure, serene, and clear—in whom delight in existence is exhausted—him I call a brahmin.

414.

414.

Yomaṃ [P 0.0059] yo imaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) palipathaṃ duggaṃ, saṃsāraṃ mohamaccagā;

Tiṇṇo pāragato pāragato (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) jhāyī, anejo akathaṃkathī;

Anupādāya nibbuto, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who has traversed this difficult path, this saṃsāra and delusion; who has crossed over to the farther shore, is meditative, free from craving, without doubt, and at peace through non-clinging—him I call a brahmin.

415.

415.

Yodha [M 0.0073] kāme pahantvāna pahatvāna (sī. pī.), anāgāro paribbaje;

Kāmabhavaparikkhīṇaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ idaṃ gāthādvayaṃ videsapotthakesu sakideva dassitaṃ.

He who in this world, having abandoned sensual pleasures, wanders as a homeless one, in whom sensual existence is exhausted—him I call a brahmin.

416.

416.

Yodha taṇhaṃ pahantvāna, anāgāro paribbaje;

Taṇhābhavaparikkhīṇaṃ[T 0.0071], tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who in this world, having abandoned craving, wanders as a homeless one, in whom craving for existence is exhausted—him I call a brahmin.

417.

417.

Hitvā [V 0.0065] mānusakaṃ yogaṃ, dibbaṃ yogaṃ upaccagā;

Sabbayogavisaṃyuttaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

Having abandoned the human yoke and gone beyond the divine yoke, he who is detached from all yokes—him I call a brahmin.

418.

418.

Hitvā ratiñca aratiñca, sītibhūtaṃ nirūpadhiṃ;

Sabbalokābhibhuṃ vīraṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

Having abandoned delight and aversion, he who has become cool and without acquisitions, a hero who has conquered the whole world—him I call a brahmin.

419.

419.

Cutiṃ yo vedi sattānaṃ, upapattiñca sabbaso;

Asattaṃ sugataṃ buddhaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He who knows in every way the passing away and rebirth of beings, who is unattached, the Well-farer, the Awakened—him I call a brahmin.

420.

420.

Yassa gatiṃ na jānanti, devā gandhabbamānusā;

Khīṇāsavaṃ arahantaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He whose destination is not known by gods, gandhabbas, or humans, whose taints are destroyed, the Arahant—him I call a brahmin.

421.

421.

Yassa [M 0.0074] pure ca pacchā ca, majjhe ca natthi kiñcanaṃ;

Akiñcanaṃ anādānaṃ, tamahaṃ brūmi brāhmaṇaṃ.

He for whom there is nothing before, after, or in the middle; who is without defilements and non-grasping—him I call a brahmin.

The bull-like, the noble, the heroic, the great sage, the victorious, the craving-free, the cleansed, the enlightened—him I call a brahmin.

Atho jātikkhayaṃ patto, abhiññāvosito muni;

He who knows his former abodes, who sees the heavens and the states of misery, and has reached the end of birth; the sage who has perfected direct knowledge and has completed the holy life—him I call a brahmin.

Summary of the Chapters of the Dhammapada

The Twin Verses, Heedfulness, Mind, Flowers, the Fool, and the Wise. The Arahant, the Thousands, Evil, Punishment, Old Age, Self and the World. The Buddha, Happiness, Affection, Anger, Impurity, the Just, and the Path. Miscellaneous, Hell, the Elephant, Craving, the Monk, and the Brahmin.

Summary of the Verses

In the Twin Verses, there are twenty verses. In Heedfulness and the World, there are twelve verses. In Affection, there are eleven verses, and in Mind and Old Age. In Flowers, the Fool, and the Thousands; in the Buddha, the Path, and Miscellaneous. There are sixteen in the Wise and Anger; in Hell and the Elephant, fourteen. In the Arahants, ten verses; in Evil and Happiness, thirteen. Seventeen in Punishment and the Just; in Impurity, twenty-one. In the Craving Chapter, twenty-seven; in the Monk Chapter, twenty-three. In the Brahmin Chapter, forty-one. Total: four hundred and twenty-three.

Miscellaneous, Hell, the Elephant, Craving, the Bhikkhu, and the Brahmin; these twenty-six chapters were taught by the Kinsman of the Sun.
