Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

In the Sutta Piṭaka

Dīghanikāye

The Dīgha Nikāya, the Collection of Long Discourses

Sīlakkhandhavaggasutta

The Sīlakkhandhavagga, the Section on the Aggregates of Morality

Saṃgāyanassa pucchā vissajjanā

Questions and Answers on the Recitation

Pucchā – paṭhamamahādhammasaṃgītikāle [M 0.0001] āvuso dhammasaṃgāhakā mahākassapādayo mahātheravarā paṭhamaṃ vinayaṃ saṃgāyitvā tadanantaraṃ kaṃ nāma pāvacanaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Question: Friend, at the time of the First Great Dhamma Council, the compilers of the Dhamma, the foremost great elders led by Mahākassapa, having first recited the Vinaya, what teaching did they recite next?

Vissajjanā – paṭhamamahādhammasaṃgītiyaṃ bhante dhammasaṃgāhakā mahākassapādayo mahātheravarā paṭhamaṃ vinayaṃ saṃgāyitvā tadanantaraṃ dhammaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Answer: Venerable sir, at the First Great Dhamma Council, the compilers of the Dhamma, the foremost great elders led by Mahākassapa, having first recited the Vinaya, then recited the Dhamma.

Pucchā – dhammo [M 0.0002] nāma āvuso suttantābhidhammavasena duvidho, tattha kataraṃ dhammaṃ paṭhamaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Question: Friend, the Dhamma is twofold by way of Suttanta and Abhidhamma. Which Dhamma did they recite first?

Vissajjanā – dvīsu bhante dhammesu suttantābhidhammapiṭakesu paṭhamaṃ suttantaṃ piṭakaṃ dhammaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Answer: Venerable sir, among the two Dhammas, the Suttanta and Abhidhamma Piṭakas, they first recited the Suttanta Piṭaka.

Pucchā – suttantapiṭake [M 0.0003]pi āvuso dīghamajjhimasaṃyuttaaṅguttarakhuddakanikāyavasena pañca nikāyā, tesu paṭhamaṃ kataraṃ nikāyaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Question: Friend, in the Sutta Piṭaka there are also five Nikāyas by way of the Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara, and Khuddaka Nikāyas. Among these, which Nikāya did they recite first?

Vissajjanā – pañcasu bhante nikāyesu paṭhamaṃ dīghanikāyaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Answer: Venerable sir, among the five Nikāyas, they first recited the Dīgha Nikāya.

Pucchā – dīghanikāyepi āvuso tayo vaggā catuttiṃsā ca suttāni, tesu kataraṃ vaggaṃ katarañca suttaṃ paṭhamaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Question: Friend, in the Dīgha Nikāya there are also three sections and thirty-four suttas. Among these, which section and which sutta did they first recite?

Vissajjanā – dīghanikāyepi bhante tīsu vaggesu paṭhamaṃ sīlakkhandhavaggaṃ, catuttiṃsatiyā ca suttesu paṭhamaṃ brahmajālasuttaṃ saṃgāyiṃsu.

Answer: Venerable sir, in the Dīgha Nikāya, among the three sections, they first recited the Sīlakkhandha Vagga. And among the thirty-four suttas, they first recited the Brahmajāla Sutta.

Brahmajālasutta

The Brahmajāla Sutta

Pucchā – sādhu [M 0.0004] sādhu āvuso mayampi dāni tatoyeva paṭṭhāya saṃgāyituṃ pubbakiccāni samārabhāma…, tenāvuso bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena brahmajālasuttaṃ kattha bhāsitaṃ.

Question: Good, good, friend. Let us now begin the recitation starting from just there, undertaking the preliminary tasks... Friend, where was the Brahmajāla Sutta spoken by the Blessed One, the Knower, the Seer, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One?

Vissajjanā – antarā ca bhante rājagahaṃ antarā ca nāḷandaṃ ambalaṭṭhikāyaṃ rājāgārake bhāsitaṃ.

Answer: Venerable sir, it was spoken at the royal rest house in Ambalaṭṭhikā, between Rājagaha and Nāḷandā.

Pucchā – kaṃ [M 0.0005] āvuso ārabbha bhāsitaṃ.

Question: Friend, concerning whom was this spoken?

Vissajjanā – suppiyañca bhante paribbājakaṃ brahmadattañca māṇavaṃ ārabbha bhāsitaṃ.

Answer: Venerable sir, it was spoken concerning the wanderer Suppiya and the brahmin student Brahmadatta.

Pucchā – kismiṃ āvuso vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Question: Friend, on what subject matter was it spoken?

Vissajjanā – vaṇṇāvaṇṇe bhante, suppiyo hi bhante anekapariyāyena buddhassa avaṇṇaṃ bhāsati dhammassa avaṇṇaṃ bhāsati saṅghassa avaṇṇaṃ bhāsati, suppiyassa pana paribbājakassa antevāsī brahmadatto māṇavo anekapariyāyena buddhassa vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati dhammassa vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati saṅghassa vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Answer: Venerable sir, it was spoken regarding praise and dispraise. For Suppiya the wanderer, venerable sir, spoke in many ways in dispraise of the Buddha, in dispraise of the Dhamma, and in dispraise of the Saṅgha. But Brahmadatta, the brahmin student, Suppiya’s disciple, spoke in many ways in praise of the Buddha, in praise of the Dhamma, and in praise of the Saṅgha. It was spoken on that matter, venerable sir.

Anusandhe

Connections

Pucchā – kati [M 0.0007] āvuso tattha anusandhayo.

Question: Friend, how many connections are there in that?

Vissajjanā – tayo bhante tattha anusandhayo, eko avaṇṇānusandhi, dve vaṇṇānusandhiyo.

Answer: Venerable sir, there are three connections there: one is a connection of dispraise, and two are connections of praise.

Paṭhama anusandhe

First Connection

Pucchā – tatthāvuso paṭhame anusandhimhi kathaṃ bhagavatā bhāsitaṃ, taṃ saṃkhepato kathehi.

Question: Friend, how was it spoken by the Blessed One in the first connection? Explain that briefly.

Vissajjanā – paṭhame bhante anusandhimhi avaṇṇe manopadosaṃ nivāretvā, tattha ca ādīnavaṃ dassetvā, tattha paṭipajjitabbākāro bhagavatā bhāsito.

Answer: Venerable sir, in the first connection, having restrained mental displeasure regarding dispraise, and having shown the danger in it, the Blessed One spoke of the proper way to proceed in that regard.

Mamaṃ [M 0.0008] vā bhikkhave pare avaṇṇaṃ bhāseyyuṃ, dhammassa vā avaṇṇaṃ bhāseyyuṃ saṅghassa vā avaṇṇaṃ bhāseyyuṃ, tatra tumhehi na āghāto appaccayo na cetaso anabhiraddhi karaṇīyā…

“Monks, if others should speak in dispraise of me, or of the Dhamma, or of the Saṅgha, you should not harbor anger, displeasure, or unhappiness of mind…”

Dutiya anusandhe

Second Connection

Pucchā – sādhu [M 0.0009] sādhu āvuso, sādhu kho bhagavā attanā paramukkaṃ sāgatakhantīguṇasamannāgato attano sāvakabhūte amhepi tattha samādāpesi, attanā ca lokadhammesu aniñjanasabhāvo amhākampi tathattāya ovādamadāsi, dutiyepanāvuso anusandhimhi kathaṃ bhagavatā bhāsitaṃ, tampi saṃkhepato pakāsehi.

Question: Good, good, friend! It is indeed good that the Blessed One, himself endowed with the highest qualities of patient acceptance, encouraged us, his disciples, in that regard. And being himself of a nature unmoved by worldly conditions, he gave us advice to attain the same state. Now, friend, in the second connection, how was it spoken by the Blessed One? Explain that briefly as well.

Vissajjanā – dutiye pana bhante anusandhimhi vaṇṇe cetaso uppilāvitattaṃ nisedhetvā, tattha ca ādīnavaṃ dassetvā, tattha ca paṭipajjanākāraṃ dassetvā, puthujjanassa vaṇṇabhūmibhūtāni tīṇi sīlāni vitthārato bhagavatā bhāsitāni.

Answer: Venerable sir, in the second connection, having restrained excessive elation of mind toward praise, and having shown the danger in it and the proper way to proceed, the Blessed One spoke in detail of the three kinds of virtuous conduct that are the grounds for praise among ordinary people.

Mamaṃ [M 0.0010] vā bhikkhave pare vaṇṇaṃ bhāseyyuṃ, dhammassa vā vaṇṇaṃ bhāseyyuṃ, saṅghassa vā vaṇṇaṃ bhāseyyuṃ, tatra tumhehi na ānando na somanassaṃ na cetaso abhiraddhi karaṇīyā –

Monks, if others should speak in praise of me, or of the Dhamma, or of the Saṅgha, you should not on that account feel delight, joy, or mental elation.

Tatiya anusandhe

The Third Connection

Pucchā – sādhu [M 0.0011] sādhu āvuso, sādhu kho bhagavā attanā paramukkaṃsagatasuparisuddhasīlasamannāgato, taṃ attano suparisuddhasīlaṃ dassetvā parepi tattha niyojesi, tatiye panāvuso anusandhimhi kathaṃ bhagavatā bhāsitaṃ, taṃ saṃkhepato pakāsehi.

Question: Good, good, friend! It is indeed good that the Blessed One, being himself endowed with the highest degree of supremely pure virtue, demonstrated his own supremely pure virtue and enjoined others in it. But, friend, how was it spoken by the Blessed One in the third connection? Please explain that briefly.

Vissajjanā – tatiye pana bhante anusandhimhi dvāsaṭṭhi diṭṭhiyo sabbaññuta ñāṇena vitthārato vibhajitvā, tāsañca cha phassāyatanapadaṭṭhānabhāvaṃ vibhāvetvā, micchādiṭṭhigatikādhiṭṭhā nañca vaṭṭaṃ kathetvā, yuttayogabhikkhuadhiṭṭhānañca vivaṭṭaṃ kathetvā, micchādiṭṭhigatikassa desanājālato avimuttabhāvaṃ [M 0.0012] desanājālato vimuttassa natthikabhāvañca vibhāvetvā, attano ca katthaci apariyāpannabhāvaṃ dassetvā, upādisesanibbānadhātuṃ pāpetvā desanā bhagavatā niṭṭhāpitā.

Reply: Venerable sir, in the third connection, the Blessed One, with his all-knowing wisdom, analyzed the sixty-two views in detail, and having made clear their basis in the six sense-spheres, having explained the round of existence which is the basis for one with a wrong-view destination and the unwinding of existence which is the basis for a monk devoted to the practice, having made clear the state of non-liberation from the net of teachings for one with a wrong-view destination and the non-nihilism of one liberated from the net of teachings, and having shown his own non-inclusion anywhere, the Blessed One concluded the teaching by leading to the Nibbāna-element with residue remaining.

Ucchinnabhāvanettiko [M 0.0013] bhikkhave tathāgatassa kāyo tiṭṭhati, yāvassa kāyo ṭhassati tāva naṃ dakkhanti devamanussā, kāyassa bhedā uddhaṃ jīvitapariyādānā na naṃ dakkhanti devamanussā.

The body of the Tathāgata, monks, remains, with the conduit to becoming severed. As long as his body remains, gods and humans will see him. With the breakup of the body, after the end of life, gods and humans will no longer see him.

Suttanidesanā

Exposition of the Sutta

Pucchā – sādhu [M 0.0014] sādhu āvuso, sādhu kho bhagavā attanā dvāsaṭṭhi diṭṭhiyo ca cha phassāyatanāni ca dvādasa paṭiccasamuppādaṅgāni ca sabbaso parijānitvā tesaṃ parijānanatthāya paresampi tathattāya dhammaṃ pakāseti, suttañca nāma āvuso catunnaṃ suttanikkhepānaṃ aññataravaseneva nikkhittaṃ, tasmā tesu idaṃ suttaṃ katarena suttanikkhepena bhagavatā nikkhittaṃ.

Question: Good, good, friend! It is indeed good that the Blessed One, having fully understood for himself the sixty-two views, the six sense-bases, and the twelve links of dependent origination, teaches the Dhamma so that others too may attain that same state of full understanding. And, friend, a sutta is classified by way of one of the four sutta classifications. Therefore, among these, by which sutta classification was this sutta classified by the Blessed One?

Vissajjanā – catūsu bhante suttanikkhepesu aṭṭhuppattinikkhepana idaṃ suttaṃ nikkhittaṃ.

Reply: Venerable sir, among the four sutta classifications, this sutta is classified by way of the occasion of its arising.

Pucchā – kassa [M 0.0015] āvuso vacanaṃ.

Question: Whose word is this, friend?

Vissajjanā – bhagavato bhante vacanaṃ arahato sammāsambuddhassa.

Reply: Venerable sir, it is the word of the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One.

Pucchā – kenāvuso [M 0.0016] ābhataṃ.

Question: By whom, friend, was it brought?

Vissajjanā – paramparāya bhante ābhataṃ.

Reply: Venerable sir, it was brought down by tradition.

Pucchā – atthi nu kho āvuso ettha koci viraddhadosā, yena pacchimā janā micchāatthaṃ gaṇheyyuṃ.

Question: Is there, friend, any fault of error here, whereby later generations might grasp a wrong meaning?

Vissajjanā – natthi bhante.

Reply: There is not, venerable sir.

Sāmaññaphalasutta

The Sutta on the Fruits of the Ascetic Life

Pucchā – tenāvuso [M 0.0017] bhagavatā…pe… sammāsambuddhena sāmaññaphalasuttaṃ kattha bhāsitaṃ.

Question: Then, friend, where was the Sutta on the Fruits of the Ascetic Life spoken by the Blessed One… the Perfectly Enlightened One?

Vissajjanā – rājagahe bhante jīvakassa komārabhaccassa ambavane bhāsitaṃ.

Reply: Venerable sir, it was spoken at Rājagaha in Jīvaka Komārabhacca’s mango grove.

Pucchā – kenāvuso saddhiṃ bhagavatā bhāsitaṃ.

Question: With whom, friend, was it spoken by the Blessed One?

Vissajjanā – raññā bhante māgadhena ajātasattunā vedehiputtena saddhiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Reply: Venerable sir, it was spoken with the King of Magadha, Ajātasattu, son of the Videhan queen.

Pucchā – taṃ [M 0.0018] panāvuso suttaṃ catunnaṃ suttanikkhepānaṃ katarena suttanikkhepena bhagavatā nikkhittaṃ.

Question: Now, friend, by which of the four sutta classifications was that sutta classified by the Blessed One?

Vissajjanā – catunnaṃ bhante suttanikkhepānaṃ pucchāvasikena suttanikkhepena nikkhitaṃ bhagavatā.

Reply: Venerable sir, of the four sutta classifications, it was classified by the Blessed One under the question-based classification.

Ajātasattu

Ajātasattu

Pucchā – sā panāvuso pucchā kenākārena samuppannā.

Question: But how, friend, did that question arise?

Vissajjanā – rājā bhante māgadho ajāsattu vedehiputto tadahuposathe pannarase komudiyā cātumāsiniyā puṇṇāya puṇṇamāya rattiyā yena bhagavā tenupasaṃkami, upasaṃkamitvā bhagavantaṃ diṭṭheva dhamme saṃdiṭṭhikaṃ sāmaññaphalaṃ pucchi, evaṃ kho sā bhante pucchā uppannā.

Reply: Venerable sir, the King of Magadha, Ajātasattu, son of the Videhan queen, on that Uposatha day, the fifteenth, on the full-moon night of the four-monthly Komudī festival, approached the Blessed One. Having approached, he asked the Blessed One about the visible fruit of the ascetic life in this very life. Thus, venerable sir, did that question arise.

Rammaṇīyā [M 0.0019] vata bho dosinā ratti, abhirūpā vata bho dosinā ratti, dassanīyā vata bho dosinā ratti, pāsādikā vata bho dosinā ratti, lakkhaññā vata bho dosinā ratti….

How delightful, sirs, is this moonlit night! How beautiful, sirs, is this moonlit night! How lovely to see, sirs, is this moonlit night! How pleasing, sirs, is this moonlit night! How auspicious, sirs, is this moonlit night!…

Kaṃ nukhvajja samaṇaṃ vā brāhmaṇaṃ vā payirupāseyyāma….

What ascetic or brahmin should we attend upon today…?

Yaṃ [M 0.0020] no payirupasato cittaṃ pasīdeyya….

…attending upon whom our minds might become serene?

Charājīvaka

Jīvaka

Tvaṃ pana samma jīvaka kiṃ tuṇhīsi –

But you, friend Jīvaka, why are you silent?

Ayaṃ [M 0.0021] deva amhākaṃ bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho amhākaṃ ambavane viharati.

Your Majesty, this Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Enlightened One, is dwelling in our mango grove.

Taṃ kho pana bhagavantaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato “itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaropurisadammasārathi satthādevamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā”ti.

And this good report concerning the Blessed One has spread far and wide: “Thus is the Blessed One: an Arahant, a Perfectly Enlightened One, accomplished in knowledge and conduct, the Well-Gone One, knower of the world, the unsurpassed trainer of persons to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, the Enlightened One, the Blessed One.”

Taṃ devo bhagavantaṃ payirupāsatu, appevanāma devassa bhagavantaṃ payirupāsato cittaṃ pasīdeyya.

May Your Majesty attend upon the Blessed One; perhaps by attending upon the Blessed One, Your Majesty's mind may become serene.

Tena hi samma jīvaka hatthiyānāni kappāpehi.

Very well, friend Jīvaka, have the elephant mounts prepared.

Kappitāni [M 0.0022] kho te deva hatthiyānāni, yassa dāni kālaṃ maññasi.

The elephant mounts have been prepared, Your Majesty. Now is the time for what you deem fit.

Ajātasattu

Ajātasattu

Kicci maṃ samma jīvaka na vañcesi, kacci maṃ samma jīvaka na palambhesi….

Surely you are not deceiving me, friend Jīvaka? Surely you are not tricking me, friend Jīvaka?…

Mā bhāyi mahārāja, mā bhāyi mahārāja….

Do not be afraid, great king, do not be afraid, great king….

Na taṃ deva vañcemi, na taṃ deva palambhāmi….

I am not deceiving you, Your Majesty, I am not tricking you….

Ajātasatta

Ajātasatta

Eso [M 0.0023] mahārāja bhagavā, eso mahārāja bhagavā majjhimaṃ thambhaṃ nissāya puratthābhimukho nisinno purakkhato bhikkhusaṅghassa….

That, great king, is the Blessed One. That, great king, is the Blessed One, seated facing east, leaning against the central pillar, before the assembly of monks….

Iminā me upasamena udayabhaddo kumāro samannāgato hotu…

May Prince Udayabhadda be endowed with such serenity as this…

Agamā [M 0.0024] kho tvaṃ mahārāja yathā pemaṃ.

You have come, great king, as befits your affection.

Puccheyyāmahaṃ bhante bhagavantaṃ kiñcideva desaṃ sace me bhagavā okāsaṃ karoti pañhassa veyyākaraṇāya.

Venerable sir, I would like to ask the Blessed One a certain question, if the Blessed One would grant me the opportunity for an answer to my question.

Puccha mahārāja yadākaṅkhasi.

Ask, great king, whatever you wish.

Yathā [M 0.0025] nu kho imāni bhante puthusippāyatanāni, seyyathidaṃ, hatthārohā assārohā rathikā dhanuggahā celakā calakā piṇḍadāyakā (peyyāla) gaṇakā muddikā, aññānipi evaṃ gatikāni puthusippāyatanāni, te tena sippena sandiṭṭhikaṃ sippaphalaṃ jīvanti, te tena attānaṃ sukhenti pīṇenti, mātāpitaro sukhenti, pīṇenti, puttadāraṃ sukhenti pīṇenti, mittāmacce sukhenti pīṇenti samaṇabrāhmaṇesu uddhaggikaṃ dakkhiṇaṃ patiṭṭhapenti sovaggikaṃ sukhavipākaṃ saggasaṃvattanikaṃ, sakkā nu kho bhante evameva diṭṭheva dhamme sandiṭṭhikaṃ sāmaññaphalaṃ paññapetuṃ.

Just as, venerable sir, these various trades exist—such as elephant riders, horse riders, charioteers, archers, chamberlains, adjutants, commissariat officers, (and so on), accountants, seal-makers, and other such various trades—they live by the visible fruits of their trades. Through that, they make themselves happy and pleased; they make their parents happy and pleased; they make their children and wives happy and pleased; they make their friends and associates happy and pleased. They establish for ascetics and brahmins a sublime offering, a heavenly one, resulting in happiness, leading to heaven. Venerable sir, is it possible in the same way to point out a visible fruit of the ascetic life here and now?

Pucchā – tadā [M 0.0026] āvuso kathaṃ bhagavatā rājā ajātasattu vedehi putto paṭipucchito kathañca tena bhagavato ārocitaṃ.

Question: Then, friend, how was King Ajātasattu Vedehiputta questioned by the Blessed One, and how did he report to the Blessed One?

Vissajjanā – “abhijānāsi no tvaṃ mahārāja imaṃ pañhaṃ aññe samaṇabrāhmaṇe pucchitāti, abhijānāmahaṃ bhante aññe samaṇabrāhmaṇe pucchitāti, yathākathaṃ pana te mahārāja byākariṃsu, sace te agaru, bhāsassūti na kho me bhante garu, yatthassa bhagavā nisinno bhavantarūpo vāti, tenehi mahārāja bhāsassū”ti, evaṃ kho bhante tadā bhagavatā rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehi putto paṭipucchito, evaṃ kho bhante tena tadā bhagavato ārocitaṃ.

Reply: "Do you recall, great king, having asked this question of other ascetics and brahmins?" "I do recall, venerable sir, having asked other ascetics and brahmins." "How then, great king, did they answer you? If it is not burdensome to you, please speak." "It is not burdensome, venerable sir, where the Blessed One is seated, or one like him." "Then, great king, please speak." Thus indeed, venerable sir, the Magadhan king Ajātasattu, son of the Videhī, was questioned by the Blessed One at that time. Thus indeed, venerable sir, it was reported by him to the Blessed One at that time.

Pūraṇakassapa ayūvāda

Pūraṇa Kassapa's Doctrine of Non-Action

Pucchā – kathañcāvuso [M 0.0028] pūraṇakassapassa santike pucchāvissajjanā ahosi.

Question: And how, friend, did the question and answer occur in the presence of Pūraṇa Kassapa?

Vissajjanā – ekamidaṃ bhante samayaṃ rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto yena pūraṇo kassapo tenupasaṅkami, upasaṅkamitvā pūraṇaṃ kassapaṃ sandiṭṭhikaṃ sāmaññaphalaṃ pucchi, atha kho bhante pūraṇo kassapo karoto kho mahārāja kārayato, chindato chedāpayato, pacato pācāpayato, socayato socāpayato (peyyāla) karoto na karīyati pāpaṃ tyādinā rañño māgadhassa ajātasattussa vedehiputtassa saṃdiṭṭhi kaṃ sāmaññaphalaṃ puṭṭho samāno akiriyaṃ byākāsi, evaṃ kho bhante pūraṇassa kassapassa santike pucchāvissajjanā ahosi.

Reply: Once, venerable sir, King Ajātasattu of Magadha, the son of the Videhī, approached Pūraṇa Kassapa. Having approached, he asked Pūraṇa Kassapa about the visible fruit of the ascetic life. Then, venerable sir, Pūraṇa Kassapa, when asked by King Ajātasattu of Magadha, the son of the Videhī, about the visible fruit of the ascetic life, declared the doctrine of non-action, saying: "Great king, for one who acts or causes others to act, who cuts or causes others to cut, who cooks or causes others to cook, who causes sorrow or causes others to sorrow (and so on), no evil is done." Thus indeed, venerable sir, the question and reply occurred in the presence of Pūraṇa Kassapa.

Attadīpa dhammadesanā

Pucchā – attadīpadhammadesanā [M 0.0147] panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kaṃ ārabbha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitā.

The Question – But where, friend, concerning whom, and on what occasion was the teaching on being an island to oneself spoken by the Blessed One?

Vissajjanā – vesāliyaṃ bhante veḷuvagāmake āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ ārabbha bhāsitā, āyasmā bhante ānando bhagavato gilānavuṭṭhitassa aciravuṭṭhitassa gelaññā bhagavato gelaññena attano khedapattakāraṇaṃ ārocesi, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitā.

The Response – Venerable sir, it was spoken concerning the Venerable Ānanda at Veḷuvagāmaka in Vesālī. Venerable sir, the Venerable Ānanda informed the Blessed One, who had recently recovered from illness, that the Blessed One’s illness had been the cause of his own distress. On that occasion, venerable sir, it was spoken.

“Tasmātihānanda [M 0.0152] attadīpā viharatha attasaraṇā anaññasaraṇā”.

"Therefore, Ānanda, dwell with yourselves as an island, with yourselves as a refuge, with no other refuge."

Pucchā – bhagavatā āvuso purima dutiyadivase pucchitavissajjitakkamena attadīpadhammadesanañca aññāni ca dhammadesanāni kathetvā pariniṭṭhita sabbabuddhakiccena āyusaṅkhāro kattha ossaṭṭho.

The Question – Friend, after the Blessed One, on the previous day and the day before that, in accordance with the question-and-answer method, had delivered the teaching on being an island to oneself and other Dhamma teachings, and having completed all the Buddha's duties, where was the life-sustaining force relinquished?

Vissajjanā – vesāliyaṃ bhante cāpāle cetiye mārena pāpimatā yācito bhagavā satena sampajānena āyusaṅkhāro ossaṭṭho.

The Reply – Venerable sir, at the Cāpāla Shrine in Vesālī, the Blessed One, entreated by Māra the Evil One, relinquished the life-sustaining force with mindfulness and full awareness.

“Parinibbātu [M 0.0157] dāni bhante bhagavā, parinibbātu sugato, parinibbāna kālo dāni bhante bhagavato.

“Now, venerable sir, let the Blessed One attain final Nibbāna; let the Well-gone One attain final Nibbāna. Now, venerable sir, is the time for the Blessed One’s final Nibbāna.”

Apposukko tvaṃ pāpima hoti, na ciraṃ tathāgatassa parinibbānaṃ bhavissati, ito tiṇṇaṃ māsānaṃ accayena tathāgato parinibbāyissati.

“Be at ease, Evil One! The Tathāgata's final Nibbāna will not be long; in three months the Tathāgata will attain final Nibbāna.”

Saṃvega

Pucchā – catusaṃvejanīyakathā [M 0.0163] panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kaṃ ārabbha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitā.

The Question – But where, friend, concerning whom, and on what occasion, was the discourse on the four inspiring places spoken by the Blessed One?

Vissajjanā – kusinārāyaṃ bhante āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ ārabbha bhāsitā, āyasmā bhante ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca “pubbe bhante disāsu vassaṃvuṭṭhā bhikkhū āgacchanti tathāgataṃ dassanāya, te mayaṃ labhāma manobhāvanīye bhikkhū dassanāya, labhāma payirupāsanāya. Bhagavato pana mayaṃ bhante accayena na labhissāma manobhāvanīye bhikkhū dassanāya, na labhissāma payirupāsanāyā”ti, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitā.

The Reply – At Kusinārā, venerable sir, it was spoken concerning the Venerable Ānanda. The Venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One: “Formerly, venerable sir, monks who had spent the rains in various regions would come to see the Tathāgata. But after the Blessed One’s passing, venerable sir, we will no longer be able to see and attend upon monks who are pleasing to the mind.” Venerable sir, it was spoken on that occasion.

Yehi [M 0.0165] keci ānanda cetiyacārikaṃ āhiṇḍantā pasannacittā kālaṃ karissanti, sabbete kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjissanti hu –

Pucchā – kathañcāvuso [M 0.0166] bhagavā bhikkhūnaṃ mātugāmesu paṭipajjitabbavattaṃ kathesi.

The Question – Friend, how did the Blessed One teach the monks about the proper conduct towards women?

Vissajjanā – adassanaṃ ānandāti ca anālāpo ānandāti ca sati ānanda upaṭṭhabbetabbāti ca evaṃ kho bhante bhagavā mātugāmesu paṭipajjitabbākāraṃ kathesi.

The Reply – “Not seeing, Ānanda,” and “No conversation, Ānanda,” and “Mindfulness should be established, Ānanda.” Thus, indeed, venerable sir, the Blessed One spoke about how to conduct oneself towards women.

Mahāsudassanasutta

The Discourse on the Great King of Glory

Pucchā – mahāsudassanasuttaṃ [M 0.0167] panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kaṃ ārabbha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Vissajjanā – tissaṃyeva bhante kusinārāyaṃ āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ ārabbha bhāsitaṃ, āyasmā bhante ānando bhagavantaṃ etadavoca “mā bhante bhagavā imasmiṃ khuddakanagarake

The Reply – Venerable sir, it was spoken right in Kusinārā, concerning the Venerable Ānanda. Venerable sir, the Venerable Ānanda said this to the Blessed One: “Venerable sir, may the Blessed One not attain final Nibbāna in this small town,

Ujjaṅgalanagarake [M 0.0168] sākhānagarake parinibbāyi. Santi bhante aññāni mahānagarāni. Seyyathidaṃ, campā, rājagahaṃ, sāvatthī, sāketaṃ, kosambī, bārāṇasī, ettha bhagavā parinibbāyatu, ettha bahū khattiyamahāsālā brāhmaṇa mahāsālā gahapati mahāsālā tathāgate abhippasannā. Parinibbānasutta

Pucchā – pacchime [M 0.0174] panāvuso kāle bhagavā āyasmato ānandassa kīdisaṃ vacanaṃ kathetvā, kathañca bhikkhū pavāretvā, kīdisañca bhikkhūnaṃ vacanaṃ āmantetvā, kathañca anupādisesāya nibbānadhātuyā parinibbāyi.

The Question – Friend, at the final time, what words did the Blessed One speak to the Venerable Ānanda, how did he invite the bhikkhus to ask questions, with what words did he address the bhikkhus, and how did he attain final Nibbāna with the Nibbāna-element without remainder?

Vissajjanā – bhagavā bhante pacchime kāle āyasmato ānandassa siyā kho panānanda tumhākaṃ evamassa atītasatthukaṃ pāvacanaṃ natthi no satthāti evamādikaṃ vacanaṃ kathetvā, siyā kho pana bhikkhave ekabhikkhussāpi kaṅkhā vā vimati vā buddhe vā dhamme vā saṅghe vā magge vā paṭipadāya vāti evamādinā bhikkhū pavāretvā, handadāni bhikkhave āmantayāmi vo, vayadhammā saṅkhārā appamādena sampādethāti pacchimañca ovādavacanaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ āmantetvā, nava anupubbasamāpattiyo anulomaṃ paṭilomaṃ samāpajjitvā, catutthajjhānā vuṭṭhahitvā samanantarā anupādisesāya nibbānadhātuyā parinibbāyi.

The Reply – Venerable sir, in his last moments the Blessed One said to the Venerable Ānanda: “Ānanda, it may occur to you: ‘The Teacher’s instruction has ceased, now we have no Teacher!’ Do not think thus, Ānanda. Whatever Dhamma and Discipline I have taught and laid down for you, that shall be your Teacher after my passing.” And having invited the bhikkhus to ask questions, saying: “Bhikkhus, if even a single bhikkhu has any doubt or perplexity concerning the Buddha, the Dhamma, the Saṅgha, the Path, or the practice, ask now.” After addressing the bhikkhus with his final exhortation: “Come now, bhikkhus, I exhort you: All conditioned things are subject to decay. Strive on with diligence!” Then, having entered and emerged from the nine successive attainments in forward and reverse order, he rose from the fourth jhāna and immediately attained final Nibbāna with the Nibbāna-element without remainder.

Nāvuso [M 0.0178] ānanda bhagavā parinibbuto, saññāvedayita nirodhaṃ samāpanno.

Reply: Venerable sir, the chieftain Pāyāsi approached the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa and said this: 'I, Master Kassapa, hold this doctrine, this view: "There is no other world, there are no spontaneously arisen beings, there is no fruit or result of good and bad deeds."' Thus, venerable sir, the chieftain Pāyāsi declared his wrong view in the presence of the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa. And the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, presenting the simile of the moon and sun as direct evidence, dispelled that evil view of the chieftain Pāyāsi.

Good, good, friend! It is good, friend, that the Venerable Kumārakassapa, having directly shown the other world to the chieftain Pāyāsi—who held wrong view and had turned away from the true Dhamma—dispelled that wrong view.

Good, good, friend! It is good indeed, friend, that the Venerable Kumārakassapa, having thoroughly refuted the arisen opposing doctrine with reasoned argument, established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Question: Then again, friend, what third line of reasoning did the chieftain Pāyāsi present to establish his own doctrine? And how did the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, having thoroughly refuted that wrong doctrine with reasoned argument, establish the doctrine of the Dhamma?

And the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, with the simile of the man fallen into a cesspool, having thoroughly refuted that wrong doctrine with reasoned argument, established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Sādhu [M 0.0199] sādhu āvuso, sādhu kho āvuso yaṃ āyasmā kumārakassapo uppannaṃ parappavādaṃ sahadhammena suniggahaṃ niggahetvā dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Good, good, friend! It is indeed good, friend, that the Venerable Kumārakassapa, having thoroughly refuted the opposing doctrine that had arisen with reasoned argument, established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Āyasmā ca kumārakassapo tāvatiṃsadevopamāya ca jaccandhopamāya cāti dvīhi upamāhi taṃ pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ sahadhammena suniggahitaṃ niggahetvā, dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Reply: Venerable sir, when the chieftain Pāyāsi was being established in the doctrine of the Dhamma by the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, who had thoroughly refuted his wrong doctrine with the simile of a man plunged into a cesspool, he, for a fourth or fifth time, presented as a line of reasoning his own friends, companions, relatives, and kin who had undertaken the five precepts, and thereby established his own wrong doctrine. And the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, with two similes—that of the Tāvatiṃsa gods and that of a man blind from birth—having thoroughly refuted that evil view with reasoned argument, established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Sādhu [M 0.0204] sādhu āvuso, sādhu kho āvuso yaṃ kumārakassapo pāyāsissa rājaññassa dve upamāyo dassetvā uppannaṃ pāpakaṃ micchāvādaṃ sahadhammena saniggahaṃ niggahetvā dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Good, good, friend! It is indeed good, friend, that the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, having presented two similes to the chieftain Pāyāsi, thoroughly refuted the evil wrong doctrine that had arisen with reasoned argument, and established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Pucchā – atha panāvuso pāyāsirājañño chaṭṭhaṃpi kīdisaṃ sādhakapariyāyaṃ dassetvā attano vādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi, kathañcāyasmā kumārakassapo sahadhammena taṃ micchāvādaṃ suniggahaṃ niggahetvā dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Question: But then, friend, what sixth line of reasoning did the chieftain Pāyāsi present to establish his own doctrine? And how did the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, having thoroughly refuted that wrong doctrine with reasoned argument, establish the doctrine of the Dhamma?

Reply: Sixthly, venerable sir, the chieftain Pāyāsi, presenting as a line of reasoning virtuous ascetics and brahmins of good character who desire to live and do not desire to die,

Good, good, friend! It is good indeed, friend, that the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, having presented the simile of the pregnant woman, rejected that wrong doctrine and established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Question: But then, friend, what seventh method of proof did the chieftain Pāyāsi demonstrate to establish his own doctrine? And how did the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa thoroughly refute that wrong doctrine with reasoned argument and establish the doctrine of the Dhamma?

Vissajjanā – sattamaṃ pana bhante pāyāsirājañño kubbhiyaṃ pakkhipitvā māritapurisaṃ sādhakapariyāyaṃ dassetvā attano vādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi, āyasmāca kumārakassapo supinakūpamāya taṃ micchāvādaṃ sahadhammena suniggahitaṃ niggahetvā dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Reply: The seventh, venerable sir, was when the chieftain Pāyāsi, having put a man he had killed into a jar, demonstrated a method of proof to establish his own doctrine. And the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, using the simile of a dream, thoroughly refuted that wrong doctrine with sound Dhamma and established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Sādhu [M 0.0210] sādhu āvuso, sādhu kho āvuso yaṃ āyasmā kumārakassapo supinakūpamaṃ dassetvā uppannaṃ micchāvādaṃ sahadhammena suniggahaṃ niggahetvā dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Pucchā – atha [M 0.0211] panāvuso pāyāsirājañño aṭṭhamampi navamampi kathetabbaṃ kathetvā dasamaṃ kīdisaṃ sādhakapariyāyaṃ dassetvā attano vādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi, kathañcāyasmā kumārakassapo sahadhammena taṃ micchāvādaṃ paṭinissajjāpesi.

Vissajjanā – aṭṭhamampi bhante navamampi pāyāsirājañño yaṃ vā taṃ vā pariyāyaṃ dassetvā attano vādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi, dasamaṃ pana bhante pāyāsirājañño chaviādīni chinditvā māritapurisaṃ sādhakapariyāyaṃ dassetvā attano vādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi, āyasmā ca kumārakassapo aggikajaṭilopamāya taṃ micchāvādaṃ sahadhammena suniggahitaṃ niggahetvā dhammavādaṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

Reply: In the eighth and ninth instances, venerable sir, the chieftain Pāyāsi demonstrated some method or other to establish his own doctrine. But in the tenth instance, venerable sir, the chieftain Pāyāsi, having killed a man by cutting his skin and so forth, demonstrated a method of proof to establish his own doctrine. And the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, using the simile of the fire-worshipping ascetic, thoroughly refuted that wrong doctrine with sound Dhamma and established the doctrine of the Dhamma.

Paṭinissajjetaṃ [M 0.0213] rājañña pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ. Paṭinissajjetaṃ rājañña pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ.

Abandon this evil view, chieftain. Abandon this evil view, chieftain.

Good, good, friend! Good, indeed, friend, that the Venerable Kumāra Kassapa, having shown a fitting simile, made him abandon that evil wrong view, which would be for his welfare and happiness for a long time.

Pucchā – āyasmatā āvuso kumārakassapena purimadivase pucchitavissajjitākārena pāyāsissa rājaññassa aggikajaṭilopamaṃ dassetvā tasmiṃ pāpake diṭṭhigate paṭinissajjāpite so tāva therassa vacanaṃ anādiyitvā kīdisañca paccanīkakathaṃ kathesi, kathañca thero karuṇāsītalahadayo hutvā aparampi upamaṃ dassetvā taṃ pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ paṭinissajjāpesi.

Vissajjanā – pāyāsi bhante rājañño āyasmatā kumārakassapena aggikajaṭilopamaṃ dassetvā taṃ pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ paṭinissajjāpite kiñcāpi bhavaṃ kassapo evamāha, atha kho nevāhaṃ sakkomi idaṃ pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ paṭinissajjitunti evamādikaṃ paccanīkakathaṃ kathesi, thero ca bhante kumārakassapo dve satthavāhopamaṃ dassetvā taṃ pāpakaṃ micchāvādaṃ paṭinissajjāpesi.

Excellent, excellent, friend! It is indeed excellent, friend, that the Venerable Kumārakassapa, by presenting even the simile of the two caravan leaders to the chieftain Pāyāsi, had him abandon that evil view. Pucchā – evaṃ [M 0.0219] panāvuso āyasmatā kumārakassapena yathā vuttāhi bahūhi upamāhi ca aparāhi gūthabhārikaakkhadhuttakopamāhi ca tasmiṃ pāpake diṭṭhigate vissajjāpite so tāva therassa vacanaṃ anādiyitvāva pacchimapaṭikkhepavasena kīdisaṃ paccanīkakathaṃ kathesi, kathañca thero karuṇāsītalahadayo hutvā pacchimampi upamaṃ dassetvā taṃ pāpakaṃ diṭṭhigataṃ paṭinissajjāpesi.

Pucchā – udumbarikasuttaṃ [M 0.0232] panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kaṃ ārabbha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Question – But friend, where was the Udumbarika Sutta spoken by the Blessed One, concerning whom, and on what occasion?

Vissajjanā – rājagahe bhante nigrodhaṃ paribbājakaṃ ārabbha bhāsitaṃ, nigrodho bhante paribbājako bhagavato parammukhā bhagavantaṃyeva ārabbha anekavihitaṃ abhūtakathaṃ kathesi, tasmiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Reply – Venerable sir, it was spoken at Rājagaha, concerning the wanderer Nigrodha. Venerable sir, the wanderer Nigrodha, in the Blessed One's absence, spoke various kinds of untrue words concerning the Blessed One himself. It was spoken on that occasion.

Reply – Venerable sir, it was spoken at Sāvatthī, in the Eastern Park, in Migāra's Mother's Mansion, concerning Vāseṭṭha, who had gone forth. Venerable sir, the brahmins revile and abuse Vāseṭṭha and Bhāradvāja, who have gone forth, with their own kind of abuse,

Paribhāsāya [M 0.0233] paripuṇṇāya no aparipuṇṇāya, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

...with full abuse, not incomplete. Venerable sir, it was spoken on that occasion.

Pāsādikasutta

The Pāsādika Sutta

Pucchā – pāsādikasuttaṃ panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kaṃ ārabbha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Question – But friend, where was the Pāsādika Sutta spoken by the Blessed One, concerning whom, and on what occasion?

Vissajjanā – sakkesu bhante vedhaññānāma sakyānaṃ ambavane pāsāde cundaṃ samaṇuddesaṃ ārabbha bhāsitaṃ, cundo bhante samaṇuddeso pāvāyaṃ nigaṇṭhassa nāṭaputtassa kālaṃ kiriyāya bhinnānaṃ nigaṇṭhānaṃ dvedhikajātānaṃ bhaṇḍanajātānaṃ kalahajātānaṃ vivādāpannānaṃ aññamaññaṃ mukhasattīhi vitujjanakāraṇaṃ āyasmato ānandassa ārocesi, āyasmā ca bhante ānando bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesi, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Reply – Venerable sir, it was spoken among the Sakyans, in the Sakyans' mango grove named Vedhañña, in the palace, concerning the novice Cunda. Venerable sir, the novice Cunda reported to Venerable Ānanda that in Pāvā, after the death of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta, the Nigaṇṭhas had split, become divided into two factions, were quarreling, disputing, engaged in conflict, and wounding each other with verbal darts. Venerable sir, Venerable Ānanda then reported this matter to the Blessed One. Venerable sir, it was spoken on that occasion.

Siṅgālasutta

The Siṅgāla Sutta

Pucchā – siṅgālasuttaṃ panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kaṃ ārabbha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Question – But friend, where was the Siṅgāla Sutta spoken by the Blessed One, concerning whom, and on what occasion?

Vissajjanā – rājagahe bhante siṅgālaṃ gahapatiputtaṃ ārabbha bhāsitaṃ, siṅgālo bhante gahapatiputto kālasseva uṭṭhāya rājagahā nikkhamitvā allavattho allakeso pañjaliko puthudisā namassati puratthimaṃ disaṃ dakkhiṇaṃ disaṃ pacchimaṃ disaṃ uttaraṃ disaṃ heṭṭhimaṃ disaṃ uparimaṃ disaṃ, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Venerable sir, Siṅgāla, the householder’s son, rising early in the morning, having left Rājagaha, with wet clothes and wet hair, with joined palms, paid homage to the various directions—the eastern direction, the southern direction, the western direction, the northern direction, the downward direction, and the upward direction. Venerable sir, it was spoken on that occasion.

Āṭānāṭiyasutta

The Āṭānāṭiya Sutta

Pucchā – āṭānāṭiyasuttaṃ [M 0.0235] panāvuso bhagavatā kattha kismiṃ vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Question – But friend, where was the Āṭānāṭiya Sutta spoken by the Blessed One, and on what occasion?

Vissajjanā – rājagahe [M 0.0236] bhante gijjhakūṭe pabbate bhāsitaṃ, bhagavati bhante rājagahe viharati cattāro mahārājāno catuddisaṃ rakkhaṃ ṭhapetvā catuddisaṃ gumbaṃ ṭhapetvā catuddisaṃ ovaraṇaṃ ṭhapetvā kevalakappaṃ gijjhakūṭapabbataṃ obhāsetvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṃsu, upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṃ abhivādetvā ekamantaṃ nisīdiṃsu, ekamantaṃ nisinno kho bhante vessavaṇo mahārājā āṭānāṭiyaṃ rakkhaṃ bhagavato ārocesi bhikkhūnaṃ bhikkhunīnaṃ upāsakānaṃ upāsikānaṃ phāsuvihārāya, tasmiṃ bhante vatthusmiṃ bhāsitaṃ.

Reply – Venerable sir, it was spoken at Rājagaha, on Vulture Peak Mountain. Venerable sir, while the Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagaha, the four great kings, having established a guard in the four directions, having established a troop in the four directions, and having established a screen in the four directions, illuminated the entire Vulture Peak Mountain and approached the Blessed One. Having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, Venerable sir, the great king Vessavaṇa announced the Āṭānāṭiya protection to the Blessed One for the comfortable dwelling of bhikkhus, bhikkhunis, laymen, and laywomen. Venerable sir, it was spoken on that occasion.
