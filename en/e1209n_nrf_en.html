Telakaṭāhagāthā [O 0.0001]

The Telakaṭāha Verses

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

1.

1.

Laṃkissaro jayatu vāraṇarājagāmī

Bhogindabhoga rucirāyata pīṇa bāhu,

Sādhupacāranirato guṇasannivāso

Dhamme ṭhito vīgatakodhamadāvalepo;

Victorious is Laṃkissaro, who goes like an elephant king, whose arms, like a serpent king's coils, are beautiful, broad, and strong; devoted to good conduct, a dwelling place of virtues, established in the Dhamma, devoid of anger, conceit, and stain.

2.

2.

Yo sabbalokamahito karuṇādhivāso

Mokkhākaro ravikulambara puṇṇa cando,

Ñeyyodadhiṃ suvipulaṃ sakalaṃ vibuddho

Lokuttamaṃ namatha taṃ sirasā munindaṃ;

He who is honored by all the world, an abode of compassion, the maker of liberation, the full moon in the sky of the solar dynasty; having fully awakened to the vast and extensive ocean of the knowable, the supreme one in the world—bow your head to that lord of sages.

3.

3.

Sopānamālamamalaṃ tidasālayassa

Saṃsāra sāgarasamuttaraṇāya setuṃ,

Sabbāgatībhaya vivajjita khema maggaṃ

Dhammaṃ namassatha sadā muninā paṇītaṃ;

The staircase, pure and stainless, to the abode of the gods, the bridge for crossing the ocean of saṃsāra, the safe path, devoid of the fear of all destinations—always pay homage to the Dhamma, well-proclaimed by the Sage.

4. [O 0.0002]

4.

Deyyaṃ tadappamapi yattha pasanna cittā

Datvā narā phalamuḷārataraṃ labhante,

Taṃ sabbadā dasabalenapi suppasatthaṃ

Saṅghaṃ namassatha sadāmitapuññakhettaṃ;

Wherein people, having given even a small gift with a serene heart, obtain a most abundant fruit; always pay homage to that Saṅgha, ever praised even by the Ten-Powered One, the boundless field of merit.

5.

5.

Tejo balena mahatā ratanattayassa

Lokattayaṃ samadhigacchati yena mokkhaṃ,

Rakkhā na catthi ca samā ratanattayassa

Tasmā sadā bhajatha taṃ ratanattayaṃ bho;

By the great power of the Triple Gem, through which one attains liberation from the three worlds, there is no protection equal to the Triple Gem. Therefore, O friends, always revere that Triple Gem.

6.

6.

Laṃkissaro parahitekarato nirāso

Rattimpi jāgararato karuṇādhivāso,

Lokaṃ vibodhayati lokahitāya kāmaṃ

Dhammaṃ samācaratha jāgariyānuyuttā;

Laṃkissaro, devoted to the welfare of others, without longing, awake even at night, an abode of compassion, awakens the world for the world's welfare. Practice the Dhamma, constantly devoted to wakefulness.

7. [O 0.0003]

7.

Sattopakāra niratā kusale sahāyā

Bho dullabhā bhuvi narā vihatappamādā,

Laṃkādhipaṃ guṇadhanaṃ kusale sahāyaṃ

Āgamma saṃcaratha dhammamalaṃ pamādaṃ;

O friends, rare in the world are people free from negligence, devoted to the welfare of beings, companions in the wholesome. Having approached the lord of Laṅkā, a treasure of virtues, a companion in good, go about practicing the immaculate Dhamma; have done with negligence.

8.

8.

Dhammo tiloka saraṇo paramo rasānaṃ

Dhammo mahaggharatano ratanesu loke,

Dhammo bhave tibhavadukkha vināsahetu

Dhammaṃ samācaratha jāgariyānuyuttā;

The Dhamma is the refuge of the three worlds, supreme among essences; the Dhamma is the most precious jewel among jewels in the world; the Dhamma is the cause for the destruction of suffering in the three realms of existence. Practice the Dhamma, devoted to wakefulness.

9.

9.

Niddaṃ vinodayatha bhāvayathappameyyaṃ

Dukkhaṃ aniccampi ceha anattatañca,

Dehe ratiṃ jahatha ja jajjarabhājanābho

Dhammaṃ samācaratha jāgariyānuyuttā;

Dispel sleepiness; cultivate the immeasurable: suffering, impermanence, and also non-self. Abandon delight in the body, which is like a decaying vessel. Practice the Dhamma, devoted to wakefulness.

10. [O 0.0004]

10.

Okāsa majja mama natthi suve karissaṃ

Dhammaṃ itīhalasatā kusalappayoge,

Nā’laṃ tiyaddhasu tathā bhuvanattaye ca

Kāmaṃ na catthi manujo maraṇā pamutto;

“Today I have no opportunity; tomorrow I will practice the Dhamma.” Thus, due to idleness in applying wholesome effort, [they neglect]. The three stages of life do not suffice, nor similarly the span of the three worlds. Truly, there is no human being who is freed from death.

11.

11.

Khitto yathā nabhasi kenacideva leḍḍu

Bhūmiṃ samāpatti bhāratayā khaṇena,

Jātattameva khalu kāraṇamekamatra

Lokaṃ sadā nanu dhuvaṃ maraṇāya gantuṃ;

As a clod of earth thrown into the sky by someone quickly falls to the ground due to its weight, so birth itself is indeed the single cause here for the world to always, surely, and certainly go to death.

12.

12.

Kāmaṃ narassa patato girimuddhanāto

Majjhe na kiñci bhayanissaraṇāya hetu,

Kāmaṃ vajanti maraṇaṃ tibhavesu sattā

Bhoge ratiṃ pajahathāpi ca jīvite ca;

For a person falling from a mountain peak, there is indeed nothing in between to cause an escape from fear. Likewise, beings in the three realms go to death. Forsake delight in pleasures and also in life!

13. [O 0.0005]

13.

Kāmaṃ patanti mahiyā khalu vassadhārā

Vijjullatā vitatamegha mukhā pamuttā,

Evaṃ narā maraṇabhīma papātamajjhe

Kāmaṃ patanti nahi koci bhavesu nicco;

Indeed, streams of rain and flashes of lightning, released from the mouths of spreading clouds, fall upon the earth. So too do people fall into the dreadful precipice of death—for no one in all existences is permanent.

14.

14.

Velātaṭe paṭutaroru taraṃgamālā

Nāsaṃ vajanti satataṃ salilālayassa,

Nāsaṃ tathā samupayanti narāmarānaṃ

Pāṇāni dāruṇatare maraṇodadhimhi;

On the ocean shore, a succession of great and powerful waves constantly goes to destruction. Likewise, the lives of humans and gods reach destruction in the more terrible ocean of death.

15.

15.

Ruddhopi so rathavarassagajādhipehi

Yodhehi cāpi sabalehi ca sāyudhehi,

Lokaṃ vivaṃciya sadā maraṇūsabho so

Kāmaṃ nihanti bhuvanattaya sāli daṇḍaṃ;

Even though obstructed by the best of chariots, by lords of horses and elephants, and by warriors with their forces and their weapons, that bull of Death, ever deceiving the world, indeed strikes down the rice-stalks of the three worlds.

16. [O 0.0006]

16.

Bho mārutena mahatā vihato padīpo

Khippaṃ vināsa mukhameti mahappabhopi,

Loke tathā maraṇacaṇḍa samīraṇena

Khippaṃ vinassati narāyumahā padīpo;

O friends, a lamp struck by a mighty wind, though of great radiance, quickly meets its end. Even so, in this world, by death's fierce wind, the great lamp of human life is swiftly extinguished.

17

17

Rāmajjunappabhūti bhūpati puṃgavā ca

Sūrā pure raṇamukhe vijitāri saṅghā,

Tepīha caṇḍa maraṇogha nimuggadehā

Nāsaṃ gatā jagati ke maraṇā pamuttā?

Rāma, Arjuna, and other foremost kings, heroes of old who conquered hosts of enemies in the face of battle—even they, with bodies submerged in the fierce flood of death, have gone to destruction. Who in the world is freed from death?

18.

18.

Lakkhī ca sāgarapaṭā sadharādharā ca

Sampattiyo ca vividhā api rūpasobhā,

Sabbā ca tā api ca mittasutā ca dārā

Ke cāpi kaṃ anugatā maraṇaṃ vajantaṃ?

Fortune, the ocean-girdled earth with its mountains, various riches and beauty of form, all these, as well as friends, children, and wives—who among them follows one who is going to death?

19. [O 0.0007]

19.

Brahmāsurāsuragaṇā ca mahānubhāvā

Gandhabbakinnaramahoragarakkhasā ca,

Te cā pare ca maraṇaggisikhāya sabbe

Ante patanti salabhā iva khīṇapuññā;

The mighty Brahmās, Suras, and hosts of Asuras, the Gandharvas, Kinnaras, Mahoragas, and Rākṣasas, they and others too—all fall at the end into the flame of death, like moths with their merit exhausted.

20.

20.

Ye sāriputtapamukhā munisāvakā ca

Suddhā sadāsavanudā paramiddhipattā,

Te cāpi maccuvaḷabhā mukha sannimuggā

Dīpānivānalahatā khayataṃ upetā;

Those disciples of the Sage headed by Sāriputta, who were pure, ever dispelling the cankers, and had attained supreme psychic power—they too, plunged into the jaws of death, have come to dissolution, like lamps struck by a flame.

21.

21.

Buddhāpi buddhakamalāmalacārunettā

Battiṃsalakkhaṇa virājita rūpasobhā,

Sabbāsacakkhayakarāpi ca lokanāthā

Sammadditā maraṇamattamahāgajena;

Even the Buddhas, with eyes like a blossomed, pure, and lovely lotus, with the beauty of their forms resplendent with the thirty-two marks, though they were lords of the world capable of destroying all cankers, were crushed by Death, the great, intoxicated elephant.

22. [O 0.0008]

22.

Rogāturesu karuṇā na jarāturesu

Khiḍḍāparesu sukumārakumārakesu,

Lokaṃ sadā hanati maccu mahāgajindo

Davānalo vanamivāvarataṃ asesaṃ;

There is no compassion for the sick or for the aging, nor for playful, tender youths. Death, the king of elephants, always strikes down the world, just as a forest fire incessantly consumes the entire forest without remainder.

23.

23.

Āpuṇṇatā na salile na jalāsayassa

Kaṭṭhassa cāpi bahutā na hutāsanassa,

Bhutvāna so tibhūvanampi tathā asesaṃ

Bho niddayo na khalu pītimupeti maccu;

Just as a reservoir is never full with water, nor a fire with an abundance of wood, so too, O friends, even having consumed the entire triple world, merciless Death truly finds no satisfaction.

24.

24.

Bho moha mohitatayā vivaso adhañño

Loko patatyapipi maccumukhe subhīme,

Bhoge ratiṃ samupayāti nihīnapañño

Dolā taraṅgacapale supinopameyye;

O friends, deluded by delusion, helpless and unfortunate, the world falls into the very dreadful mouth of death. The one of inferior wisdom finds delight in pleasures that are as fickle as a swing or a wave, and comparable to a dream.

25. [O 0.0009]

25.

Ekopi maccurabhihantumalaṃ tilokaṃ

Kiṃ niddayā api jarāmaraṇānuyāyī,

Ko vā kareyya vibhasuvesu ca jīvitāsaṃ

Jāto naro supina saṃgama sannibhesu;

One death alone is enough to overwhelm the triple world. Why are you heedless, pursued as you are by old age and death? What person, once born, would place their hope in life and in possessions, which are like a meeting in a dream?

26.

26.

Niccāturaṃ jagadidaṃ sabhayaṃ sasokaṃ

Disvā ca kodhamadamohajarābhibhūtaṃ,

Ubbegamattamapi yassa na vijjatī ce

So dāruṇona maraṇaṃ vata taṃ dhiratthu!

Bho bho na passatha jarāsidharañhi maccu

Māhaññamānamakhilaṃ satataṃ tilokaṃ,

Kiṃ niddayā nayatha vītabhayā tiyāmaṃ

Dhammaṃ sadā’savanudaṃ caratha’ppamattā;

Having seen this world to be ever afflicted, full of fear and sorrow, and overwhelmed by anger, pride, delusion, and old age—if for someone not even a trace of agitation arises, indeed, he is the cruel one, not Death! Shame on him! Ho! Ho! Do you not see Death, wielding the sword of old age, constantly striking down the entire threefold world? Why do you pass the three watches of the night heedlessly, without fear? Practice the Dhamma that removes the cankers, ever diligent!

28. [O 0.0010]

28.

Bhāvetha bho maraṇamāravivajjanāya

Loke sadā maraṇa saññamimaṃ yatattā,

Evañhi bhāvanaratassa narassa tassa

Taṇhā pahīyati sarīragatā asesā;

Cultivate, O friends, this mindfulness of death in the world, always, with a controlled mind, for the sake of avoiding Māra, who is death. Indeed, for that person who delights in such cultivation, all bodily craving is utterly abandoned.

29.

29.

Rūpaṃ jarā piyataraṃ malinīkaroti

Sabbaṃ balaṃ harati attani ghorarogo,

Nānūpabhoga parirakkhita mattabhāvaṃ

Bho maccu saṃharati kiṃ phalamattabhāve?

Old age blemishes even the most cherished beauty; a dreadful disease in the body steals all strength. O friends, Death carries away this very self, though protected by various enjoyments. What then is the use of this existence?

30.

30.

Kammānilāpahatarogataraṃgabhaṃge

Saṃsāra sāgara mukhe vitate vipannā,

Mā māpamādamakarittha karotha mokkhaṃ

Dukkhodayo nanu pamādamayaṃ narānaṃ;

In the wide and perilous mouth of the ocean of saṃsāra, where waves of disease, struck by the wind of karma, break, do not be heedless; strive for liberation. For indeed, the arising of suffering for men is due to heedlessness.

31. [O 0.0011]

31.

Bhogā ca mittasutaporisa bandhavā ca

Nārī ca jīvitasamā api khettavatthu,

Sabbāni tāni paralokamito vajantaṃ

Nānubbajanti kusalākusalaṃva loke;

Wealth, friends, sons, servants, and relatives, and a wife dear as life itself, fields and property—all these do not follow one who departs from here to the next world; only one's good and evil deeds follow in this world.

32.

32.

Bho vijjucaṃcalatare bhavasāgaramhi

Khittā purā katamahāpavanena tena,

Kāmaṃ vibhijjati khaṇena sarīranāvā

Hatthe karotha paramaṃ guṇahatthasāraṃ;

O friends, in the ocean of becoming, more fickle than lightning, tossed by the great wind of past deeds, the boat of the body indeed shatters in an instant. Take into your hand the supreme essence of virtue.

33.

33.

Niccaṃ vibhijjatiha āmaka bhājanaṃva

Saṃrakkhitopi bahudhā iha attabhāvo,

Dhammaṃ samācaratha saggapatippatiṭṭhaṃ

Dhammo suciṇṇamihameva phalaṃ dadāti;

Just as a raw earthen pot constantly breaks, so too this body, though guarded in many ways, breaks here. Practice the Dhamma, the foundation for heaven; Dhamma well-practiced yields its fruit right here in this world.

34. [O 0.0012]

34.

Rantvā sadā piyatare divi devarajje

Namhā cavanti vibudhā api khīṇapuññā,

Sabbaṃ sukhaṃ divi bhuvīha viyoganiṭṭhaṃ

Ko paññavā bhavasukhesu ratiṃ kareyya?

Having always enjoyed the very dear divine kingdom in heaven, even the gods fall from there when their merit is exhausted. All happiness, whether in heaven or here on earth, culminates in separation; what wise person, then, would take delight in the pleasures of existence?

35.

35.

Buddho sasāvakagaṇo jagadekanātho

Tārāvalīparivutopi ca puṇṇacando,

Indopi devamakuṭaṃkita pādakañjo

Ko pheṇapiṇḍa-na-samo tibhavesu jāto?

The Buddha with his assembly of disciples, the sole lord of the world; the full moon surrounded by a garland of stars; and even Indra, whose lotus feet are marked by the crowns of the gods—who, born in the three realms, is not like a lump of foam?

36.

36.

Līlāvataṃsamapi yobbana rūpasobhaṃ

Attūpamaṃ piyajanena ca sampayogaṃ,

Disvāpi vijjucapalaṃ kurute pamādaṃ

Bho mohamohitajano bhavarāgaratto;

The beauty of youthful form, like a playful ornament, and union with a beloved person, comparable to oneself—seeing these to be as fickle as lightning, yet one is heedless. O, the person deluded by delusion, impassioned with the lust for existence!

37. [O 0.0013]

37.

Putto pitā bhavati mātu patīha putto

Nārī kadāci jananī ca pitā ca putto,

Evaṃ sadā viparivattati jīvaloko

Citte sadāticapale khalu jātiraṅge;

A son becomes a father; a son here becomes his mother's husband. A woman sometimes becomes a mother, a father, or a son. Thus the world of living beings ever revolves, indeed, in the arena of birth, which is as fickle as thought.

38.

38.

Rantvā pure vividhaphullalatākulehi

Devāpi nandanavane surasundarīhi,

Te ve’kadā vitatakaṇṭakasaṃkaṭesu

Bho koṭisimbalivanesu phusanti dukkhaṃ;

O friends, even the gods, having formerly enjoyed themselves in the Nandana grove with celestial beauties, amidst various blossoming creepers, at another time indeed experience suffering in the Koṭisimbali forests, which are perilous and overgrown with thorns.

39.

39.

Bhutvā sudhannamapi kañcanabhājanesu

Sagge pure suravarā paramiddhipattā,

Te cāpi pajjalitalohagulaṃ gilanti

Kāmaṃ kadāci narakālaya vāsabhūtā;

Chiefs of the gods, having attained supreme power and having formerly feasted on exquisite food from golden vessels in celestial cities, even they, at other times, when they have become inhabitants of the abodes of hell, must indeed swallow blazing balls of iron.

40. [O 0.0014]

40.

Bhutvā narissaravarā ca mahiṃ asesaṃ

Devādhipā ca divi dibbasukhaṃ surammaṃ,

Vāsaṃ kadāci khurasañcitabhūtalesu

Te vā mahārathagaṇānugatā divīha;

Supreme kings of men who ruled the entire earth, and lords of gods followed by hosts of great chariots here in heaven enjoying delightful divine bliss, may at times come to dwell on ground strewn with razors.

41.

41.

Devaṅganā lalitabhinnataraṅgamāle

Raṅge mahissarajaṭāmakuṭānuyāte,

Rantvā pure suravarā pamadāsahāyā

Te cāpi ghorataravetaraṇiṃ patanti;

Chiefs of the gods, having formerly reveled with their female companions in celestial cities, on a stage graced by divine maidens with playful, wave-like movements, a stage associated with the matted locks and crown of the Great Lord—they too fall into the most terrifying Vetaraṇī river.

42.

42.

Phullāni pallavalatāphalasaṃkulāni

Rammāni nandanavanāni manoramāni,

Dibbaccharālalitapuṇṇadarīmukhāni

Kelāsamerusikharāni ca yanti nāsaṃ;

The delightful and charming Nandana forests, filled with blossoms, sprouts, creepers, and fruits; and the peaks of Kelāsa and Meru, whose cave mouths are full of playful divine nymphs—these too go to destruction.

43. [O 0.0015]

43.

Dolā’nilā’nalataraṃgasamā hi bhogā

Vijjuppabhāticapalāni ca jīvitāni,

Māyāmarīcijalasomasamaṃ sarīraṃ

Ko jīvite ca vibhave ca kareyya rāgaṃ?

For enjoyments are like a swing, wind, fire, and waves; and lives are as exceedingly fickle as a flash of lightning. The body is like an illusion, a mirage, a water bubble, or the moon—who would create a passion for life and wealth?

44.

44.

Kiṃ dukkhamatthi na bhavesu ca dāruṇesu

Sattopi tassa vividhassa na bhājano ko,

Jāto yathā maraṇarogajarābhibhūto

Ko sajjano bhavaratiṃ pihayeyya’bālo?

What suffering is not found in dreadful existences? What being is not a partaker of its varied forms? Since one who is born is overcome by death, disease, and old age, what good person, not being a fool, would long for delight in existence?

45.

45.

Ke vāpi pajjalitalohagulaṃ gilanti

Sakkā kathañcidapi pāṇitalena bhīmaṃ,

Dukkhodayaṃ asucinissavanaṃ anantaṃ

Ko kāmayetha khalu dehamimaṃ abālo?

Who indeed would swallow a blazing ball of iron? Who could possibly hold that dreadful thing in the palm of their hand? Similarly, what person who is not a fool would desire this body—the origin of suffering, endlessly oozing impurity?

46. [O 0.0016]

46.

Loke na maccusamamatthi bhayaṃ narānaṃ

Na vyādhidukkhasamamatthi ca kiṃci dukkhaṃ,

Evaṃ virūpakaraṇaṃ na jarāsamānaṃ

Mohena bho ratimupeti tathāpi dehe;

In the world, for humans, there is no fear equal to death; and no suffering whatsoever is equal to the pain of disease. Similarly, there is no disfigurement equal to old age. Yet, O friends, through delusion one finds delight in the body.

47.

47.

Nissārato nalakalīkadalīsamānaṃ

Attānameva parihaññati attahetu,

Sampositopi kusahāya ivākataññū

Kāyo na yassa anugacchati kālakerā;

For the sake of this self, which is essenceless like a reed stalk or a plantain tree, one harms oneself. Though nourished, the body, like an ungrateful, bad companion, does not follow one at the time of death.

48.

48.

Taṃ pheṇapiṇḍasadisaṃ visasūlakappaṃ

Toyā’nilā’nalamahīuragādhivāsaṃ,

Jiṇṇālayaṃva paridubbalamattabhāvaṃ

Disvā naro kathamupeti ratiṃ sapañño?

That body, like a lump of foam, like a poisoned stake, an abode of water, wind, fire, earth, and snakes, like a dilapidated house, an exceedingly frail existence—seeing this, how could a wise person find delight?

49. [O 0.0017]

49.

Āyukkhayaṃ samupayāti khaṇe khaṇepi

Anveti maccu hananāya jarāsipāṇī,

Kālaṃ tathā na parivattati taṃ atītaṃ

Dukkhaṃ idaṃ nanu bhavesu acintanīyaṃ?

The lifespan wastes away moment by moment; Death follows, with the sword of old age in hand, in order to strike. Time that has passed does not turn back. Is this suffering in these existences not indeed inconceivable?

50.

50.

Appāyukassa maraṇaṃ sulabhaṃ bhavesu

Dīghāyukassa ca jarā vyasanaṃ ca’nekaṃ,

Evaṃ bhave ubhayatopi ca dukkhameva

Dhammaṃ samācaratha dukkhavināsanāya;

For the short-lived, death is easily obtained in these existences; and for the long-lived, old age and many misfortunes. Thus, in existence, in both cases, there is only suffering. Practice the Dhamma for the destruction of suffering.

51.

51.

Dukkhagginā sumahatā paripīḷitesu

Lokattayassa vasato bhavavārakesu,

Sabbattatā sucaritassa pamādakālo

Bho bho na hoti paramaṃ kusalaṃ ciṇātha;

For those dwelling in the cycles of existence within the triple world, afflicted by the great fire of suffering, O friends, this is no time for negligence in good conduct. Accumulate supreme merit!

52. [O 0.0018]

52.

Appaṃ sukhaṃ jalalavaṃ viya bho tiṇagge

Dukkhantu sāgarajalaṃ viya sabbaloke,

Saṃkappanā tadapi hoti sabhāvato hi

Sabbaṃ tilokamapi kevaladukkhameva;

O friends, happiness is small, like a drop of water on a blade of grass; but suffering is like the water of the ocean throughout the world. Indeed, even that happiness is by its nature a mere fabrication. The entire triple world is nothing but suffering.

53.

53.

Kāyo na yassa anugacchati kāyahetu

Bālo anekavidhamācaratīha dukkhaṃ,

Kāyo sadā kali malākalilañhi loke

Kāye rato’navarataṃ vyasanaṃ pareti

For the sake of the body, which does not follow his will, the fool performs various evil deeds in this world. The body is indeed always mired in strife and stain; one who delights in the body constantly goes to misfortune.

54.

54.

Mīḷhākaraṃ kalimalākaramāmagandhaṃ

Sūḷāsisallavisapannagarogabhūtaṃ,

Dehaṃ vipassatha jarāmaraṇādhivāsaṃ

Tucchaṃ sadā vigatasāramimaṃ vinindyaṃ;

Contemplate this despicable body: a mine of excrement, a mine of strife and stain, of foul odor; a ground for stakes, swords, darts, poison, snakes, and disease; a dwelling for old age and death; always empty and devoid of essence.

55. [O 0.0019]

55.

Dukkhaṃ aniccamasubhaṃ vata attabhāvaṃ

Mā saṃkilesaya na vijjati jātu nicco,

Ambho na vijjati hi appamapīha sāraṃ

Sāraṃ samācaratha dhammamalaṃ pamādaṃ;

This existence is indeed suffering, impermanent, and impure. Do not defile it; nothing permanent is ever found. Alas, not even a little essence is found here. Practice the essence, the Dhamma! Enough of heedlessness!

In these five aggregates and six sense-spheres, which are like an illusion, a mirage, a plantain trunk, a reed, a mass of foam, a wave on the Ganges, or a water bubble—indeed, no self is found. What wise person would not say so?

Dīpaccimālamiva taṃ khaṇabhaṅgabhūtaṃ

Attāti dubbalatarantu vadeyya dehaṃ;

If a barren woman's son, holding the yoke of a chariot made of a hare's horn, were to run for a very long time—who then would call this exceedingly weak body, which is as momentary as the flame of a lamp, 'self'?

Attāni sārarahitaṃ kadalīsamānaṃ

Mohā bhaṇeyya khalu dehamimaṃ anattaṃ;

Just as a fool, with a vessel made of a water bubble, might drink mirage-water up to his throat, so too, through delusion, one might indeed call 'self' this body, which is not-self, devoid of essence, and like a plantain tree.

Attānameva parihaññati attahetu

Attā na vijjati kadācidapīha dehe;

Whoever desires a dwelling in the flower of an udumbara tree or in mirage water, that fool only courts weariness. He harms himself for his own sake; no self is ever found here in the body.

Suññesu kiñcidapi nopalabheyya sāraṃ;

As a person, while thoroughly peeling a banana tree, would not find even a little essence, as he might wish, so too in these five aggregates and these six sense bases—all empty—one would not find any essence whatsoever.

Deho sabhāvarahito khaṇabhaṃgayutto,

Ko attahetu aparo bhuvi vijjatīha?

Without a thread, there is nothing whatsoever here that exists as a fabric. Without a body, no being whatsoever exists here indeed. The body is devoid of inherent nature, endowed with momentariness. What other self is to be found here on earth?

Dehe pareti parikappanayā hi rāgaṃ;

Seeing a mirage of water from afar, O good people, a foolish deer runs towards it, perceiving it as water. Thus, towards a body devoid of inherent nature and established through perversion, one is drawn to passion through conceptualization.

Bhāvetha bhāvanaratā vigatappamādā

Sabbāsavappahananāya anattasaññaṃ;

In the body, devoid of inherent nature and established by conceptualization, no self is found, just as lightning is not found in the sky. Those delighting in development, free from negligence, should develop the perception of not-self for the destruction of all taints.

Nānāsucīhi paripuṇṇaghaṭaṃ yatheva;

This body, smeared with saliva, excrement, blood, tears, and fat, mired in defilement and stain, without essence—beings always carry this repulsive thing, just like a pot filled with various impurities.

Kiṃ bho vipassatha guṇaṃ kimu attabhāve?

Having bathed in all the water of the four oceans, and applied even supreme fragrance as great as Mount Meru, a person never attains purity. What virtue, O good people, do you see in this existence?

Deho sa eva navadhā paribhinnagaṇḍo

Dehaṃ vinā bhayakaraṃ na susānamatthi;

This very body is a repository of various impurities; this very body is the ground for execution, imprisonment, and disease; this very body is a festering wound with nine openings. Apart from the body, there is no terrible charnel ground.

If the internal portion of urine and excrement, having come out from the body, were to overflow, mother and father would indeed become pitiless and bereft of love. What then of relatives, sons, and wives?

This body with its nine openings, a house for a host of worms, mired in flesh, bone, sweat, and blood, and foul-smelling—those who nourish it, having committed various evils here, are deluded and subject to death. Alas, it is so!

In this body, comparable to a boil, a dwelling place of manifold diseases, always filled with blood, urine, and excrement—whoever delights in this, which is food for dogs and jackals, that person of childish intellect will indeed grieve hereafter.

Āsīvisālayanibho sabhayo sadukkho

Deho sadā savati loṇaghaṭova bhinno;

O good people, like a lump of foam, it is void of essence; like a cesspool, it is always of repulsive smell; like a viper’s lair, it is full of fear and suffering; the body always oozes like a broken salt-pot.

Taṃ nindanīyamiha jātu na hoti loke;

Just as a lotus born on earth is not despicable, O good people, though amidst mud and impure water, similarly, one born for the welfare of others, even if embodied, is never despicable here in the world.

Kāmaṃ tadeva nanu hoti paropakāraṃ;

The body is a special thing, quite full with its thirty-two parts, just as it is for the multitude of men and women in the world. What fruit is there in bodies for the wise? Surely, it is for the benefit of others.

Nevertheless, even by a very wise person, the body should be protected for a very long time in every way. One should practice the Dhamma for a very long time while living, for in the Dhamma, O good people, there is a wish-granting jewel.

Chāyāva yāti paraloka mito vajantaṃ;

As with medicine well-infused in milk, its medicinal power shines forth through its essence, so too the Dhamma, well-practiced here in the world, follows one like a shadow when going from here to the next world.

Sambhūsitā iva bhavanti phalena tena;

As the shadow of a well-adorned body, O good people, appears in accordance with it in a lovely, spotless mirror, just so, having done supreme meritorious deeds, they are as if adorned by that fruit in the next world.

Similarly, in the body, an abode of various sufferings, those subject to delusion and negligence, deluded by the perception of pleasure, are like one licking honey from a razor's sharp edge; the one of deficient wisdom encounters intense suffering.

Delighting in existence through conceptual thought and passion, the one of little wisdom always attains suffering. For the fool who perceives pleasure here in this world, surely, upon reflection, it becomes but bitter fruit.

Though one may obtain all enjoyments, wealth, grain, and special gains, and, O good people, be like the banner of the god of love in form, whoever obtains death unwillingly even in youth, that person is indeed a taker of others' lives hereafter.

He becomes a beggar with a broken bowl in hand, shaven-headed and threatening with hundreds of words of reproach, always begging at the houses of enemies, clothed in rags—that person who is a stealer of others' wealth and a deceiver of others' minds.

A woman is never again freed from the state of womanhood. That man who transgresses with another's wife, which should not be done, always becomes a woman in the next world, and always encounters terrible and numerous misfortunes.

Wretched, with foul-smelling mouth, dull, and unwise, he always becomes mute and unpleasant to behold; and, having become a human, he obtains immeasurable suffering—that witless being who speaks false speech.

The mad become devoid of the virtue of shame, always wretched and devoted to misfortune and sorrow, born in various existences with deformed bodies. Having drunk liquor as if it were halāhala poison, they are of deluded wisdom.

Whatever evil deeds one has committed here, having obtained dreadful, immeasurable suffering in the hells for no few crores and nayutas of years, one also obtains here various and numerous misfortunes.

In all the three worlds, there is nothing equal to the Triple Gem, the peace-bringer for the world. By its great power, having conquered all evil, may I always be one who has attained all happiness.

In all the three worlds, may all beings—friends, neutrals, enemies, and relatives, all of them—be forever free from disease and fear, without sorrow. Having attained all happiness, may they be joyful.

The body is like a broken pot filled with excrement; the body is always a dwelling place of defilement and misery. The world is afflicted in the body, thinking 'it is all happiness'; the body is always a dwelling place of death, disease, and old age.

Death does not regard beings as 'young,' 'old,' or 'child,' but simply afflicts them. Thus, whether I am standing, lying down, or walking, I am certainly going to the mouth of death; indeed, it is so.

Thus, as this body is constituted of faults, you should always hold this in your hearts, with minds directed to that. O you who delight in development, constantly, with controlled selves, cultivate loving-kindness, protection, the foul, and mindfulness of death.

Having performed meritorious deeds such as giving, which result in future happiness, that immeasurable fruit, in its entirety, should always be given for the welfare and happiness of others. O friends, is not that very thing the essence grasped in hand?

Without a cause, nothing whatsoever exists in the world, like sound born from the striking of hands. Thus, distinct because of the division into cause and effect, the world arises, perishes, and endures.

Sattā sadā paṭilabhanti anādikāle;

Indeed, just as ignorance is the cause of kamma, O friends, through kamma one attains distinction of birth. And conditioned by birth, beings always receive aging, death, and other suffering from time without beginning.

When kamma does not arise here due to the destruction of delusion, and with the exhaustion of kamma there is no birth in future existences; with the exhaustion of birth here, aging, death, and other suffering are all exhausted, like a lamp by the wind.

Whoever constantly sees here the Dhamma-body of the Sage, that person sees the Buddha—so he declared. To see the Buddha, the stainless Dhamma, and the Lord of the Three Worlds, seek to discern the truth of Dhamma, O friends!

O friends, this triad of faults is like a well-sharpened dart plunged deep within the heart, smeared with the stain of various evils, a vessel of diverse misfortunes, and impure—remove it with the hook of wisdom.
