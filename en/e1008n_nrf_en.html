Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Cāṇakyanītipāḷi

Cāṇakya Nīti Pāli

1.

1.

Nānāsattho [M 0.0001] ddhataṃ vakkhe,

Rāja nīti samuccayaṃ;

Sabba bīja midaṃ satthaṃ,

Cāṇakyaṃ sārasaṅgahaṃ.

I shall expound a collection of royal policies, extracted from various treatises; this teaching, the seed of all, is a compendium of essence by Cāṇakya.

2.

2.

Mūlasuttaṃ pavakkhāmi,

Cāṇakyena yathoditaṃ;

Yassa viññāna mattena,

Mūḷho bhavati paṇḍito.

I shall proclaim the fundamental aphorism, as declared by Cāṇakya; by the mere understanding of which, even a fool becomes wise.

3.

3.

Viduttaṃ [M 0.0002] narapaccañca,

Nevatulyaṃ kudācanaṃ;

Sadese pujjate rājā,

Vidū sabbattha pujjate.

A wise man and a king are never equal at any time. A king is honored in his own country, but a wise man is honored everywhere.

4.

4.

Paṇḍite [M 0.0003] ca guṇā sabbe,

Mūḷhe dosā hi kevalaṃ;

Tasmā mūḷhasahassesu,

Pañño eko visisyate.

All virtues are found in the wise, while only faults are found in the foolish. Therefore, among thousands of fools, one wise person excels.

5.

5.

Mātariva [M 0.0004] paradāresu,

Paradabbesu leṭṭuva;

Attaniva sabbabhūtesu,

Yo passati sapaṇḍito.

He who regards others' wives as his mother, others' wealth as a clod of earth, and all beings as himself—he is a wise person.

6.

6.

Kiṃkulena visālena,

Guṇahīno tu yonaro;

Akulinopi satthañño,

Devatāhipi pujjate.

Of what use is a high lineage to a person devoid of virtue? Even if not of high lineage, one who knows the scriptures is honored even by the gods.

7.

7.

Rūpayobbanasampannā[M 0.0005],

Visālakulasambhavā;

Vijjāhīnā nasobhante,

Niggandhā iva kiṃsukā.

Endowed with beauty and youth, born in a distinguished family, those without knowledge do not shine, like scentless kiṃsuka flowers.

8.

8.

Tārānaṃ [M 0.0006] bhūsaṇaṃ cando,

Nārīnaṃ bhūsaṇaṃ pati;

Puthabyā bhūsaṇaṃ rājā,

Vijjā sabbassa bhūsaṇaṃ.

The moon is the ornament of the stars, a husband is the ornament of women, a king is the ornament of the earth, knowledge is the ornament of all.

9.

9.

Mātā sattu pitā verī,

Yena bālo napāṭṭhito;

Na sobhate sabhāmajjhe,

Haṃsamajjhe bakoyathā.

A mother is an enemy, a father a foe, by whom a child is not taught; he does not shine in the midst of an assembly, like a heron among swans.

10.

10.

Varameko [M 0.0007] guṇī putto,

Na ca mūḷhasatehipi;

Eko cando tamo hanti,

Na ca tāragaṇehipi.

Better is one virtuous son than even a hundred fools; one moon dispels darkness, not even a host of stars.

11.

11.

Lālaye [M 0.0008] pañcavassāni,

Dasavassāni tālaye;

Patte tu soḷase vasse,

Puttaṃ mittaṃva ācare.

For five years, one should cherish a child; for ten years, one should discipline them; when they reach the sixteenth year, one should treat a son as a friend.

12.

12.

Lālane [M 0.0009] bahavo dosā,

Tālane bahavo guṇā;

Tasmā puttañca sissañca,

Tālaye na tu lālaye.

In pampering there are many faults, in disciplining many virtues; therefore, both a son and a disciple should be disciplined, not pampered.

13.

13.

Ekenāpi [M 0.0010] suvakkhena,

Pupphitena sugandhinā;

Vāsitassa vanaṃ sabbaṃ,

Suputtena kulaṃyathā.

Even by a single good tree, blooming and fragrant, the whole forest is perfumed; just so, a family by a good son.

14.

14.

Ekassāpi [M 0.0011] kuvakkhassa,

Koṭaraṭṭhena agginā;

Dayhate hi vanaṃ sabbaṃ,

Kuputtena kulaṃ yathā.

Even by a single bad tree, with fire residing in its hollow, the whole forest is burnt; just as a family by a bad son.

15.

15.

Dūrato sobhate mūḷho,

Lambamāna paṭāvuto;

Tāvañca sobhate mūḷho,

Yāva kiñci nabhāsate.

From afar a fool shines, covered with hanging cloth; and that fool shines only so long as he says nothing at all.

16.

16.

Visato [M 0.0012] amataṃ gāyhaṃ,

Amejjhāapi kañcanaṃ;

Nīcato uttamā vijjā,

Thīratnaṃ dukkulāapi.

Nectar should be taken from poison; gold, even from impurity; the highest knowledge, from the lowly; a jewel of a woman, even from a humble family.

17.

17.

Ussave [M 0.0013] byasaneceva,

Dubbhikkhe sattuviggahe;

Rājadvāre susāneca,

Yo tiṭṭhati sabandhavo.

In festivity and in disaster, in famine and in conflict with an enemy, at the king's court and in the cemetery—he who stands by is a true kinsman.

18.

18.

Parokkhe kiccahantāraṃ,

Paccakkhe piyavādinaṃ;

Vajjaye tādisaṃ mittaṃ,

Visakumbhaṃ payomukhaṃ.

One who destroys your work in your absence and speaks sweetly in your presence—avoid such a friend, who is like a pot of poison with milk on its surface.

19.

19.

Sakiṃ [M 0.0014] duṭṭhañca mittaṃ yo,

Puna sandhātu micchati;

Samaccu mupagaṇhāti,

Gabbha massatarī yathā.

He who wishes to reconcile again with a friend once corrupted, embraces death, just as a she-mule does with a fetus.

20.

20.

Na [M 0.0015] vissase avissatthaṃ,

Mittañcāpi na vissase;

Kadāci kupitaṃ mittaṃ,

Sabbadosaṃ pakāsaye.

Do not trust the untrustworthy, nor even trust a friend; for an angry friend may sometimes reveal all your faults.

21.

21.

Jāniyā [M 0.0016] pesane bhacce,

Bandhave byasanāgame;

Mittañcā padikāleca,

Bhariyañca vibhavakkhaye.

One should know servants by an errand, relatives when misfortune comes, a friend in times of adversity, and a wife when wealth is dwindling.

22.

22.

Upakāraggahitena,

Sattunāsattumuddhare;

Pādalaggaṃ karaṭṭhena,

Kaṇṭakeneva kaṇṭakaṃ.

One should remove an enemy by means of an enemy won over by a favor; what is stuck to the foot, by what is held in the hand; a thorn, by a thorn.

23.

23.

Na [M 0.0017] mittaṃ koci kassaci,

Na koci ripu kassaci;

Kāraṇena hi ñāyati,

Mittāni ca ripū tathā.

No one is anyone's friend, no one is anyone's enemy; for by their cause are they known as friends and foes alike.

24.

24.

Dujjano [M 0.0018] piyavādī ca,

Netaṃ vissāsakāraṇaṃ;

Madhu tiṭṭhati jivhagge,

Hadaye tu halāhalaṃ.

A wicked person who is a sweet-talker—this is no cause for trust; honey is on the tip of his tongue, but deadly poison is in his heart.

25.

25.

Dujjano parihantabbo,

Vijjāyā laṅkatopi saṃ;

Maṇinā bhūsito sappo,

Kime so nabhayaṃ karo.

A wicked person should be avoided, even if adorned with knowledge; a snake adorned with a jewel, is it not fearsome?

26.

26.

Sappo [M 0.0019] kūro khalo kūro,

Sappā kūrataro khalo;

Manto sadhivaso sappo,

Khalo kena nivāyyate.

A snake is cruel, a rogue is cruel; a rogue is more cruel than a snake. A snake can be subdued by mantras and herbs, but by what is a rogue restrained?

27.

27.

Nakhīnañca [M 0.0020] nadīnañca,

Siṅgīnaṃ satthapāṇinaṃ;

Vissāso nevakātabbo,

Thīsu rājakulesu ca.

Trust should never be placed in those with claws, nor in rivers, nor in those with horns, nor in armed men, nor in women, nor in royal families.

28.

28.

Hatthī [M 0.0021] hatthasahassena,

Satahatthena vājino;

Siṅgino dasahatthena,

Ṭhānacāgena dujjano.

An elephant is to be avoided by a thousand cubits, a horse by a hundred cubits, a horned beast by ten cubits, and a wicked person by abandoning that place.

29.

29.

Āpadatthaṃ [M 0.0022] dhanaṃ rakkhe,

Dāraṃ rakkhe dhanehipi;

Attānaṃ satataṃ rakkhe,

Dārehipi dhanehipi.

One should guard wealth for the sake of security; one should protect one's wife even with wealth; but one should always protect oneself, even at the cost of wife and wealth.

30.

30.

Paradāraṃ paradabbaṃ,

Parivādaṃ parassa ca;

Parihāsaṃ guruṭṭhāne,

Cāpalyañca vivajjaye.

Another's wife, another's property, and slander of another; jesting in the presence of a teacher, and fickleness—these one should avoid.

31.

31.

Caje [M 0.0023] ekaṃ kulassatthe,

Gāmassatthe kulaṃ caje;

Gāma janapadassatthe,

Attatthe pathaviṃ caje.

For the sake of the family, one may forsake an individual; for the sake of the village, one may forsake a family; for the sake of the country, one may forsake a village; for the sake of the Self, one may forsake the world.

32.

32.

Calatyekena [M 0.0024] pādena,

Tiṭṭha tyekena buddhimā;

Nāsamikkhya paraṃ ṭhānaṃ,

Pubbamāyatanaṃ caje.

The wise man moves with one foot and stands with the other; without having secured a new position, one should not abandon the former one.

33.

33.

Luddha matthena gaṇheyya,

Thaddha mañjalī kammunā;

Mūḷhaṃ chando nuvattena,

Tathā tathena paṇḍitaṃ.

One should win over the greedy with wealth, the stubborn with respectful salutation, the foolish by indulging his whim, and the wise with truth.

34.

34.

Atthanāsaṃ [M 0.0025] manotāpaṃ,

Gehe duccaritāni ca;

Vañcanañca pamāṇañca,

Matimā na pakāsaye.

Loss of wealth, mental anguish, misconduct in the home, deception, and insult—these a wise person should not make public.

35.

35.

Dhanadhaññappayogesu[M 0.0026],

Tathā vijjāgamesu ca;

Āhāre byavahāre ca,

Cattalajjo sadā bhave.

In dealings with wealth and grain, and likewise in the acquisition of knowledge; in matters of food and business, one should always be without shame.

36.

36.

Dhanino sotthiyo rājā,

Nadī vejjo tu pañcamo;

Pañca yatra navijjante,

Tatra vāsaṃ nakāraye.

A wealthy person, a learned Brahmin, a king, a river, and fifthly, a physician; where these five are not to be found, one should not make one's dwelling there.

37.

37.

Yasmiṃdese [M 0.0027] na sammānaṃ,

Na pīti naca bandhavā;

Na ca vijjāgamo koci,

Taṃdesaṃ parivajjaye.

In a country where there is no respect, no affection, and no relatives, nor any acquisition of knowledge, that country one should avoid.

38.

38.

Manasā [M 0.0028] cintitaṃ kammaṃ,

Vacasā napakāsaye;

Aññalakkhita kāriyassa,

Yato siddhi najāyate.

A deed contemplated in the mind, one should not reveal by speech; for of a task observed by another, success is not born.

39.

39.

Kudesañca [M 0.0029] kuvuttiñca,

Kubhariyaṃ kunadiṃ tathā;

Kudabbañca kubhojjañca,

Vajjaye tu vicakkhaṇo.

A bad country, a bad livelihood, a bad wife, and likewise a bad river; bad property and bad food—these a discerning person should avoid.

40.

40.

Iṇasesoggi seso ca,

Byādhiseso tatheva ca;

Puna ca vaḍḍhate yasmā,

Tasmā sesaṃ nakāraye.

A remnant of debt, a remnant of fire, and likewise a remnant of disease; since they grow again, one should therefore leave no remnant.

41.

41.

Cintā [M 0.0030] jaro manussānaṃ,

Vatthānaṃ ātapo jaro;

Asobhagyaṃ jaro thīnaṃ,

Assānaṃ methunaṃ jaro.

Worry is the aging of mankind, the sun is the aging of clothes; lack of fortune is the aging of women, sexual union is the aging of horses.

42.

42.

Atthi [M 0.0031] putto vase yassa,

Bhacco bhariyā tatheva ca;

Abhāve pyatisantoso,

Saggaṭṭho so mahītale.

He whose son is under his control, and likewise his servant and wife, and who is greatly content even in their absence—he is established in heaven while on earth.

43.

43.

Duṭṭhā bhariyā saṭhaṃ mittaṃ,

Bhacco cuttaradāyako;

Sa sappeca gahe vāso,

Maccureva nasaṃsayo.

A wicked wife, a deceitful friend, a servant who answers back, and living in a house with a snake—this is death itself, without a doubt.

44.

44.

Mātā [M 0.0032] yassa gehe natthi,

Bhariyācā piyavādinī;

Araññaṃ tena gantabbaṃ,

Yathā raññaṃ tathāgahaṃ.

He in whose home there is no mother, and whose wife does not speak kindly; he should go to the forest, for his home is as a forest.

45.

45.

Iṇakattā [M 0.0033] pitā sattu,

Mātā ca byabhicārinī;

Bhariyā rūpavatī sattu,

Putto sattu apaṇḍito.

A father who is a debtor is an enemy, and a mother who is unchaste; a beautiful wife is an enemy, and a son who is a fool is an enemy.

46.

46.

Kokilānaṃ [M 0.0034] saro rūpaṃ,

Nārī rūpaṃ patibbatā;

Vijjā rūpaṃ kurūpānaṃ,

Khamā rūpaṃ tapassinaṃ.

The beauty of cuckoos is their voice, the beauty of a woman is her fidelity; the beauty of the ugly is knowledge, the beauty of ascetics is patience.

47.

47.

Avijjaṃ jīvanaṃ suññaṃ,

Disā suññā abandhavā;

Puttahīnaṃ gahaṃ suññaṃ,

Sabbasuññā daliddatā.

Life without knowledge is empty; for one without relatives, the directions are empty; a home without a son is empty; poverty is utter emptiness.

48.

48.

Adātā[M 0.0035] vaṃsadosena,

Kammadosā daliddatā;

Ummādā mātudosena,

Pitudosena mūḷhatā.

One is not a giver due to a fault of lineage; poverty is due to a fault of kamma; madness is due to a fault of the mother; foolishness is due to a fault of the father.

49.

49.

Guru [M 0.0036] aggi dvijādīnaṃ,

Vaṇṇānaṃ brāhmaṇo guru;

Pati reko gurutthīnaṃ,

Sabbassābhyāgato guru.

Fire is the guru of the twice-born; the Brahmin is the guru of the castes; the husband alone is the guru of women; a guest is the guru of all.

50.

50.

Atidabbe [M 0.0037] hatā laṅkā,

Atimāne ca koravā;

Atidāne balībaddho,

Sabbamaccanta gahitaṃ.

Lankā was destroyed by excessive pride, and the Kauravas by excessive arrogance; Bali was bound by excessive giving; everything in excess is blameworthy.

51.

51.

Vatthahīno [M 0.0038] tvalaṅkāro,

Ghatahīnañca bhojanaṃ;

Thanahīnā ca yānārī,

Vijjāhīnañca jīvanaṃ.

Worthless is adornment without clothes, and food without ghee; worthless is a woman without breasts, and a life without knowledge.

52.

52.

Bhojjaṃ bhojanasatti ca,

Ratisatti varā thiyo;

Vibhavo dānasatti ca,

Nāppassa tapaso phalaṃ.

Food and the power to digest, the power for pleasure and excellent women, wealth and the power to give—this is the fruit of no small austerity.

53.

53.

Puttappayojanā [M 0.0039] dārā,

Putto piṇḍappayojano;

Hitappayojanaṃ mittaṃ,

Dhanaṃ sabbappayojanaṃ.

Wives are for the purpose of sons, a son is for the purpose of offerings; a friend is for the purpose of benefit, wealth is for all purposes.

54.

54.

Dullabhaṃ [M 0.0040] pākatikaṃ vākyaṃ,

Dullabho khemakaro suto;

Dullabhā sadisī jāyā,

Dullabho sajano piyo.

Rare is natural speech, rare is a son who brings security; rare is a like-minded wife, rare is a beloved kinsman.

55.

55.

Selesele [M 0.0041] namāṇikkaṃ,

Muttikaṃ na gajegaje;

Sādhavo na hi sabbatra,

Candanaṃ na vanevane.

Not on every mountain is there a gem, nor a pearl on every elephant; good people are not found everywhere, nor is sandalwood in every forest.

56.

56.

Asoco [M 0.0042] niddhano pañño,

Asoco paṇḍitabandhavo;

Asocā vidhavā nārī,

Puttanatta patiṭṭhitā.

A wise person, though poor, is without sorrow; one with a wise relative is without sorrow; a widow established with sons and grandsons is without sorrow.

57.

57.

Avijjo puriso soco,

Socaṃ methuna mappajaṃ;

Nirāhārā pajā socā,

Socaṃ rajja marājakaṃ.

A man without knowledge is to be lamented; lamented is childless sexual union; lamented are people without food; lamented is a kingdom without a king.

58.

58.

Kulehi [M 0.0043] saha sampakkaṃ,

Paṇḍitehi ca mittataṃ;

Ñātībhi ca samaṃ melaṃ,

Kubbāno navinassati.

He who maintains contact with good families, friendship with the wise, and association with his kinsmen, does not perish.

59.

59.

Kaṭṭhā [M 0.0044] vutti parādhinā,

Kaṭṭho vāso nirāsayo;

Niddhano byavasāyo ca,

Sabbakaṭṭhā daliddatā.

A livelihood dependent on others is wretched, a dwelling without support is wretched, and an endeavor without wealth is wretched; poverty is the greatest wretchedness of all.

60.

60.

Takkarassa kuto dhammo,

Dujjanassa kuto khamā;

Vesiyā ca kuto sneho,

Kuto saccañca kāminaṃ.

Whence is Dhamma for the evil-doer? Whence is patience for the wicked? Whence is affection in a prostitute? And whence is truth in the lustful?

61.

61.

Pesitassa [M 0.0045] kuto mānaṃ,

Kopanassa kuto sukhaṃ;

Thīnaṃ kuto satittañca,

Kuto mettī khalassa ca.

Whence is honor for a messenger? Whence is happiness for the angry? Whence is satisfaction for women? And whence is loving-kindness for the rogue?

62.

62.

Dubbalassa [M 0.0046] balaṃ rājā,

Bālānaṃ rodanaṃ balaṃ;

Balaṃmūḷhassa monittaṃ,

Corānaṃ atathaṃ balaṃ.

The king is the strength of the weak; crying is the strength of children; silence is the strength of the foolish; falsehood is the strength of thieves.

63.

63.

Yo [M 0.0047] dhuvāni pariccajja,

Adhuvaṃ parisevati;

Dhuvāni tassa nassanti,

Adhuvaṃ naṭṭhameva ca.

He who abandons the permanent and pursues the impermanent, his permanent things are destroyed, and the impermanent is also lost.

64.

64.

Sukkaṃ maṃsaṃ thiyo vuddhā,

Bālakka taruṇaṃ dadhi;

Pabhāte methunaṃ niddā,

Sajju pāṇaharāni cha;

Dried meat, an old woman, a young woman, fresh yogurt, intercourse at dawn, and sleep at dawn—these six are swift takers of life.

65.

65.

Sajju [M 0.0048] maṃsaṃ navannañca,

Bālā thī khīrabhojanaṃ;

Ghatamuṇhodakañceva,

Sajju pāṇakarāni cha.

Fresh meat and new grain, a young woman and milk-food, ghee and warm water—these six are swift givers of life.

66.

66.

Sīhādekaṃ [M 0.0049] bakādekaṃ,

Cha sunā tīṇi gadrabhā;

Vāyasā catu sikkhetha,

Cattāri kukkuṭādapi.

One should learn one thing from the lion, one from the heron, six from the dog, three from the donkey, four from the crow, and also four from the rooster.

67.

67.

Pabhūtamappakiccaṃ [M 0.0050] vā,

Yonaro kattumicchati;

Saṃyatanena kattabbaṃ,

Sīhādekaṃ pakittitaṃ.

Whether a task is great or small, whatever a person wishes to accomplish, it should be done with full effort; this is declared as the one quality to be learned from the lion.

68.

68.

Sabbindriyāni saṃyama,

Bakova patito jano;

Kāladesopapannāni,

Sabbakiccāni sādhaye.

Restraining all the senses, like a heron waiting patiently, a person should accomplish all tasks appropriate to time and place.

69.

69.

Bahvāsī [M 0.0051] sāppasantuṭṭho,

Suniddo sīghacetano;

Pabhubhatto ca sūro ca,

Ñātabbā cha sunā guṇā.

Eating much, being content with little, sleeping soundly, being quickly alert, devotion to one's master, and bravery—these six qualities of a dog should be known.

70.

70.

Avissāmaṃ [M 0.0052] vahe bhāraṃ,

Sītuṇhañca navindati;

Sa santoso tathā niccaṃ,

Tīṇi sikkhetha gadrabhā.

To carry one's burden without respite, to be indifferent to cold and heat, and to be ever content—these three things should be learned from the donkey.

71.

71.

Guḷhamethunadhammañca,

Kālekāle ca saṅgahaṃ;

Appamādamanālasyaṃ,

Catu sikkhetha vāyasā.

Secretive mating, gathering provisions in due season, heedfulness, and lack of idleness—these four should be learned from the crow.

72.

72.

Yuddhañca [M 0.0053] pātaruṭṭhānaṃ,

Bhojanaṃ saha bandhuhi;

Thiyaṃ āpadaggataṃ rakkhe,

Catu sikkhetha kukkuṭā.

Fighting, rising at dawn, eating with kinsmen, and protecting a female in distress—these four should be learned from the rooster.

73.

73.

Kotibhāro [M 0.0054] samatthānaṃ,

Kiṃdūraṃ byavasāyinaṃ;

Ko videso savijjānaṃ,

Ko paro piyavādinaṃ.

What burden is there for the capable? What distance for the industrious? What foreign land for the learned? Who is a stranger to one who speaks kindly?

74.

74.

Bhayassa kathito pantho,

Indriyānamasaṃyamo;

Tajjayo sampadāmaggo,

Yeniṭṭhaṃ tena gamyate.

Lack of restraint of the senses is said to be the path of fear; victory over them is the path to accomplishment, by which one's goal is attained.

75.

75.

Na [M 0.0055] ca vijjāsamo bandhu,

Na ca byādhisamo ripu;

Nacāpaccasamo sneho,

Na ca devā paraṃ balaṃ.

There is no kinsman like knowledge, no enemy like disease, no affection like that for one's own child, and no power greater than that of the gods.

76.

76.

Samuddāvaraṇā [M 0.0056] bhūmi,

Pākārāvaraṇaṃ gahaṃ;

Narindāvaraṇā desā,

Cārittāvaraṇā thiyo.

The earth is enclosed by the ocean; a house is enclosed by its walls; countries are protected by their king; women are protected by their virtue.

77.

77.

Ghatakumbhasamā nārī,

Tattaṅgāra samo pumā;

Tasmā ghatañca aggiñca,

Nekatra thapaye budho.

A woman is like a pot of ghee, a man is like a glowing coal; therefore, a wise person should not place ghee and fire together.

78.

78.

Āhāro [M 0.0057] dviguṇo thīnaṃ,

Chaguṇo byavasāyo ca,

A woman's appetite is twofold, her intelligence fourfold, her enterprise sixfold, and her lust is held to be eightfold.

Raṇā paccāgataṃ sūraṃ,

Sassañca gehamāgataṃ.

One should praise well-digested food, a wife whose youth has passed, a hero returned from battle, and the harvest brought into the granary.

Santuṭṭhāiva pāthivā;

Salajjā gaṇikā naṭṭhā,

Nillajjā ca kulitthiyo.

Unsatisfied brahmins are ruined, while contented kings are ruined; a modest courtesan is ruined, and a shameless woman of good family is ruined.

Mūḷhaputto ca paṇḍito;

Adhanena dhanaṃ pāpya,

Tiṇaṃva maññate janaṃ.

A king of low birth, a wise man with a foolish son, and a poor man who has acquired wealth—these consider other people as mere grass.

Yassatthi vipulaṃ dhanaṃ;

Sasino tulyavaṃsopi,

Niddhano paribhūyate.

Even a man who has slain a brahmin is honored if he has abundant wealth; but one of a lineage equal to the moon, if poor, is despised.

Parahatthagataṃ dhanaṃ;

Kiccakāle samuppanne,

Na sāvijjā na taddhanaṃ.

Knowledge that is confined to books and wealth that is in another's hands—when the time of need arises, that is not true knowledge, nor is that true wealth.

Padmānaṃ sisirā bhayaṃ;

Pabbatānaṃ vajīramhā,

Sādhūnaṃ dujjanā bhayaṃ.

For trees, the fear is from the wind; for lotuses, the fear is from the frost; for mountains, from the thunderbolt; for the virtuous, the fear is from the wicked.

Yaso sagganivāso ca,

Vipulo ca dhanāgamo.

When a wise person is employed, there are three benefits for the king: fame, a dwelling in heaven, and a great influx of wealth.

When a fool is appointed, three faults befall the king: dishonor, loss of wealth, and likewise, a journey to hell.

Pacchādyante guṇā sabbe,

Meghehiva divākaro.

By large gatherings of fools, with their mutually beast-like conduct, all virtues are obscured, like the sun by clouds.

Puttassa vinayo natthi,

For one whose field is by the riverbank, whose wife is dear to another, and whose son has no discipline—for him, death is without a doubt.

Silā tarati pānīyaṃ,

Gītaṃ gāyati vānaro.

The impossible should not be spoken, even if it is seen directly; a stone floating on water, a monkey singing a song.

Bhariyā bhattu piyā yassa,

For a farmer, there is always abundance; for the healthy, there is always happiness; for him whose wife is dear to her husband, his home is a constant festival.

Yācanā mānanāsāya,

Negligence leads to the destruction of work; poverty, to the destruction of wisdom; begging, to the destruction of honor; feasting, to the destruction of family.

Phalacchāyā samanvito;

Yadi devā phalaṃ natthi,

Chāyā kena nivāraye.

A great tree, endowed with fruit and shade, should be resorted to. If by fate there is no fruit, who can prevent its shade?

Dutīye najjitaṃ dhanaṃ;

Tatīye najjitaṃ puññaṃ,

Catutthe kiṃkarissati.

In the first stage of life, knowledge was not acquired; in the second, wealth was not acquired; in the third, merit was not acquired—what will one do in the fourth?

Parahatthagataṃ dhanaṃ;

Kiccaṃ thīgocaraṃ yassa,

Sabbaṃ taṃ viphalaṃ bhave.

Trees on a riverbank, wealth that has passed into another's hands, and a matter left to the management of women—all this becomes fruitless.

Kudesamāsajja [M 0.0069] kutotthasañcayo,

Kuputtamāsajja kuto jalañjalī;

Kugehiniṃ pāpya gahe kuto sukhaṃ,

Kusissamajjhāpayato kuto yaso.

Having resorted to a bad country, whence the accumulation of wealth? Having resorted to a bad son, whence the offering of water? Having obtained a bad wife, whence happiness in the home? For one teaching a bad student, whence fame?

Sāmā thīciṭṭhakālayaṃ;

Sītakāle bhave uṇhaṃ,

Gimhakāle ca sītalaṃ.

Well water, the shade of a banyan tree, a Sāmā woman, and a brick house—in the cold season they are warm, and in the hot season, cool.

Visaṃ thīpi aññāsattā,

Walking about at night is poison; the favor of a king is poison; a woman attached to another is poison; a neglected illness is poison.

Visaṃ goṭṭhī daliddassa,

Vuddhassa taruṇī visaṃ.

Poorly learned knowledge is poison; food during indigestion is poison; an assembly is poison to a poor person; a young wife is poison to an old man.

Appabījaṃ hataṃ khettaṃ,

Bhaccadosā hato pabhū.

A path is ruined by nightfall; a fallen woman is ruined; a field with little seed is ruined; a master is ruined by the faults of his servants.

Hatā rūpavatī vañjhā,

Hataṃ senamanāyakaṃ.

Faith in one who is not a learned Brahmin is ruined; a sacrifice without a sacrificial gift is ruined; a beautiful but barren woman is ruined; an army without a leader is ruined.

One who knows the essence of the Vedas and their auxiliaries, who is devoted to recitation and oblations, and is endowed with words of blessing—such is the royal priest.

One who knows the meaning of gestures and expressions, who is strong and of pleasing appearance, always vigilant and skillful—such a one is called a gatekeeper.
