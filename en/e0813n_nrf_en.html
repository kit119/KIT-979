Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Bālāvatāra

An Introduction for Beginners

Paṇāma

Homage

[Ka]

[Ka]

Sabbaṃ niruttipathapāragataṃ sabuddhaṃ,

Buddhaṃ tilokatilakaṃ hatapāpadhammaṃ;

Dhammaṃ vimuttisukhadaṃ vihatāghasaṃghaṃ,

Saṃghaṃ ca niccamabhivandiya dakkhiṇeyyaṃ.

Having always revered the Buddha—who has gone beyond all paths of language, the perfectly self-awakened, the crest-jewel of the three worlds, who has destroyed evil states; the Dhamma—which gives the bliss of liberation and has dispelled the mass of defilements; and the Sangha—worthy of offerings:

[Kha]

[Kha]

Vuddhippattosmi muddho mama varagaravo sīlapaññādisobhe,

Candādicceva suddhe varajinaṭhapite sāsanābbhe patīte;

Nissāyevā tipemā paṇamiya sirasā niccamesaṃ saritvā,

Pādambhoje guṇagge hataduritamalo ānubhāvena tassā.

I have attained old age and am dull-witted, yet my reverence is great for the splendors of virtue, wisdom, and the like, pure as the moon and sun, renowned in the sky of the excellent Conqueror's Dispensation. Relying on them with intense devotion, ever remembering them and bowing my head to the lotus feet of those foremost in virtue, may the stain of evil be destroyed by their power.

[Ga]

[Ga]

Porāṇa sīhaḷa padattha vinicchayañca,

Sabbampi māgadhaniruttinayaṃ pasatthaṃ;

Aññañca nekavidha sakkata saddasatthaṃ,

Pāramparābhata matañca nisamma sammā.

Having carefully considered the ancient Sinhala analysis of word-meaning, the entire praised method of the Magadhan language, various other kinds of Sanskrit grammatical treatises, and the views handed down by tradition;

[Gha]

[Gha]

Bālāvatāra varamāgadha saddasatthe,

Dubbodha nekapadaattha vinicchayena;

Atthāya ādhunika bālaparamparāya,

Bālāvatāra varagaṇṭhipadaṃ karissaṃ.

For the benefit of the modern lineage of beginners, I shall compose this excellent gloss on the Bālāvatāra, by analyzing the meaning of the many difficult words in that excellent Magadhan grammar, which is hard to understand.

[Ṅa]

[Ṅa]

Niccaṃ ye mettha bālā varahadayayutā suṭṭhu nikkhittanettā,

Puṇṇe nānānayānaṃ suratarusadise dhīrapāsaṃsiye ve;

Gambhīraṃ duttaraṃ te jinavacanudadhiṃ tiṇṇathāmā bhaveyyuṃ,

Laddhopāyā ca candaṃ taditarapacuraṃ sakkataṃ sotukāmā.

May those beginners here, who are possessed of noble hearts and well-restrained eyes, who are like the full moon and wish-fulfilling trees, praised by the wise, gain the strength to cross the deep and difficult-to-cross ocean of the Victor's word. And having obtained the means, may they become eager to hear the beautiful teaching and its abundant praises.

[Ca]

[Ca]

Ganthanipphattiyā sesa – dukkarattaṃ yathāvato;

Jānanti kusalā dhīrā, nekasatthantarādisu;

Ganthesu guṇadosampi, teyeva vidurā sadā.

Only the wise and skilled, who are versed in many different treatises, truly understand the great difficulty of completing a book; they alone always perceive both the merits and faults in such works.

[Cha]

[Cha]

Tasmā ettha pamādādi – dosaleso bhave yadi;

Porāṇāceraladdhīhi, vilomaṃ vā bhaveyya ce.

Therefore, if there should be any trace of a fault here, such as negligence, or if it should be contrary to the doctrines received from the ancient teachers,

[Ja]

[Ja]

Ganthantaraṃ vigāhetvā, vicāretvā punappunaṃ;

Yuttimeva ca gaṇhantu, hutvā vīmaṃsabuddhikāti.

Let them examine other texts and reflect again and again, and having become discerning, let them accept only what is proper.

Saññā

Terminology

2. Akkharāpādayo ekacattālīsaṃ.

2. The letters, beginning with 'a', are forty-one.

Akkharāpi [M 0.0006] akārādayo ekacattālīsaṃ suttantopakārā. Taṃ yathā-a ā-i ī-u ū-e o, ka kha ga gha ṅa, ca cha ja jha ña, ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa, ta tha da dha na, pa pha ba bha ma, ya ra la va sa ha ḷa aṃ-iti.

The letters, beginning with 'a', are forty-one, and are useful for the Suttas. They are as follows: a, ā, i, ī, u, ū, e, o; ka, kha, ga, gha, ṅa; ca, cha, ja, jha, ña; ṭa, ṭha, ḍa, ḍha, ṇa; ta, tha, da, dha, na; pa, pha, ba, bha, ma; ya, ra, la, va, sa, ha, ḷa, aṃ.

3. Tatthodantāsarā aṭṭha.

3. Among them, eight are vowels.

Tattha [M 0.0008] akkharesu okārantā aṭṭha sarā nāma. Tattheti vattate.

Among those letters, the eight ending with 'o' are called vowels. The word 'among them' is carried over from the rule.

4. Lahumattā tayo rassā.

4. Three, having a light measure, are short.

Tattha [M 0.0009] saresu lahumattā a, i, u iti tayo rassā.

Among those vowels, the three with a light measure—a, i, u—are the short vowels.

5. Aññe dīghā.

5. The others are long.

Tattha [M 0.0010] saresu rassehaññe dīghā.

Among those vowels, those other than the short ones are long.

Saṃyogato pubbe eo rassā ivoccante, anantarā byañjanā saṃyogo. Ettha, seyyo, oṭṭho, sotthi.

Before a conjunct consonant, 'e' and 'o' are pronounced as if short. A conjunct consonant is a sequence of consonants without an intervening vowel. For example: seyyo, oṭṭho, sotthi.

6. Sesā byañjanā.

6. The remaining are consonants.

Sare [M 0.0012] ṭhapetvā sesā kādayo niggahītantā byañjanā.

Excluding the vowels, the remaining letters from 'ka' to the niggahīta are consonants.

7. Vaggā pañcapañcaso mantā.

7. The consonants up to 'm' are in groups of five.

Byañjanānaṃ [M 0.0013] kādayo makārantā pañcapañcaso akkharavanto vaggā.

Among the consonants, those from 'ka' to 'm' are the 'groups' (vaggas), each consisting of five letters.

8. Vaggānaṃ [M 0.0016] paṭhamadutiyā so cāghoso. Ḷantāññe ghosā. Ghosāghosasaññā ca “parasamaññā payoge”ti saṅgahītā. Evaṃ liṅga, sabbanāma, pada, upasagga, nipāta, taddhita, ākhyāta, kammappavacanīyādisaññā ca.

8. Of the groups, the first and second letters, and 's', are voiceless. The others are voiced. The designations 'voiced' and 'voiceless' are included under 'conventional terms in usage.' Similarly, this applies to designations for gender, pronoun, word, prefix, particle, derivative, verb, postposition, and so on.

9. Aṃiti niggahītaṃ.

9. The sound 'aṃ' is the niggahīta.

Aṃiti [M 0.0018] akārato paraṃ yo bindu sūyate, taṃ niggahītaṃ nāma.

The dot that is heard after the letter 'a' is called the niggahīta.

Bindu [M 0.0019] cūḷāmaṇākāro, niggahītanti vuccate.

The dot, shaped like a crest-jewel, is called niggahīta.

Kevalassā ppayogattā, akāro sannidhīyate.

Because it is not used by itself, the letter 'a' is supplied.

10. A, kavagga, hā kaṇḍajā, i, cavagga, yā tālujā, u, pavaggā oṭṭhajā, ṭavagga, ra, ḷā muddhajā, tavagga, la, sā dantajā, e kaṇṭhatālujo, o kaṇṭhoṭṭhajo, vo dantoṭṭhajo.

10. 'A', the 'ka' group, and 'ha' are guttural; 'i', the 'ca' group, and 'ya' are palatal; 'u', the 'pa' group are labial; the 'ṭa' group, 'ra', and 'ḷa' are cerebral; the 'ta' group, 'la', and 'sa' are dental; 'e' is guttural-palatal; 'o' is guttural-labial; 'va' is dental-labial.

Sarasandhi

Vowel Sandhi

11. Loka [M 0.0021] aggoityasmiṃ – “pubbamadhoṭhita massaraṃ sarena viyojaye”ti pubbabyañjanaṃ sarato puthakkātabbaṃ.

11. In the case of loka aggo: according to the rule 'Separate the preceding consonant below from its vowel,' the preceding consonant is to be separated from its vowel.

Sarā sare lopaṃ.

A preceding vowel is elided when a vowel follows.

Anantare sare pare sarā lopaṃ papponti.

When a vowel immediately follows, a preceding vowel is elided.

“Nare [M 0.0022] paraṃ yutte”ti assaro byañjano parakkharaṃ netabbo lokaggo.

According to the rule 'The following letter should be joined to the vowelless one,' the vowelless consonant is to be joined to the following letter, resulting in lokaggo.

Saretyasmiṃ [M 0.0024] opasilesiko kāsasattamī, tato vaṇṇakālabyavadhāne kāriyaṃ na hoti. Yathā-maṃ ahā sīti, “pamādamanuyuñjantī”tyādigāthāyaṃ ‘janā appamāda’nti ca. Evaṃ sabbasandhīsu.

In the rule, the locative case in 'sare' indicates immediate proximity. Therefore, the operation does not occur when there is an interval. For example, in maṃ ahāsi, and in the verse beginning 'pamādamanuyuñjanti,' between janā and appamāda. This applies to all sandhi rules.

Anantaraṃ parassa sarassa lopaṃ vakkhati, tasmānena pubbassa lopo ñāyati, teneva sattamīniddiṭṭhassa paratāpi gamyate.

He will later state the elision of the following vowel; therefore, by the present rule, the elision of the preceding vowel is to be understood. By that same token, it is understood that the word in the locative case refers to what follows.

12. Saretyadhikāro. [M 0.0026] Pana ime pana imetīha-sarā lopaṃ itveva.

12. The governing rule is 'when a vowel follows.' In pana ime, the rule 'a preceding vowel is elided' applies.

Vā paro asarūpo.

Optionally, a following dissimilar vowel is elided.

Asamānarūpāsaramhā paro saro vā lupyate, paname, panime.

A vowel that follows a dissimilar vowel is optionally elided. For example: paname, panime.

13. Bandhussa [M 0.0027] iva, na upetītīdha –

13. In the cases bandhussa iva and na upeti:

Kvacāsavaṇṇaṃ lutte.

Sometimes, upon elision, a dissimilar vowel is substituted.

Sare lutte parasarassa kvaci asavaṇṇo hotīti i u iccetesaṃ ṭhānāsannā e o. Bandhusseva. Nopeti.

When a vowel is elided, the following vowel sometimes becomes a dissimilar vowel. Thus, 'e' and 'o' replace 'i' and 'u' respectively, due to proximity of articulation. For example: bandhusseva, nopeti.

14. Tatra [M 0.0029] ayaṃ, yāni idha, bahu upakāraṃ, saddhā idha, tathā upamantyetasmiṃ –

14. In the cases tatra ayaṃ, yāni idha, bahu upakāraṃ, saddhā idha, and tathā upamaṃ:

Dīghaṃ.

Lengthening.

Sare lutte paro saro kvaci ṭhānāsannaṃ dīghaṃ yāti. Tatrāyaṃ, yānīdha, bahūpakāraṃ, saddhīdha, tathūpamaṃ.

When a vowel is elided, the following vowel is sometimes lengthened. For example: tatrāyaṃ, yānīdha, bahūpakāraṃ, saddhīdha, tathūpamaṃ.

15. Kiṃsu idhetyatra –

15. In the case of kiṃsu idha—

Pubbo ca.

And the preceding.

Sare lutte pubbo ca kvaci dīghaṃ yāti. Kiṃ sūdha.

When a following vowel is elided, the preceding vowel is sometimes lengthened. For example: kiṃ sūdha.

16. Te [M 0.0030] ajja, te ahaṃtettha –

16. In the cases of te ajja, te ahaṃ, and te ettha—

Yamedantassādeso.

Substitution of 'y' for a final 'e'.

Sare pare antassa ekārassa kvaci yo ādeso hoti, tyajja, “dīgha”nti byañjane pare kvaci dīgho, tyāhaṃ.

When a vowel follows, a final 'e' is sometimes substituted by 'y'; for example, tyajja. By the rule 'dīghaṃ', it is sometimes lengthened before a consonant, as in tyāhaṃ.

Kvacīti kiṃ. Nettha.

Why 'sometimes'? For example, in nettha.

17. So [M 0.0033] assa, anu etityettha –

17. In the cases of so assa and anu eti—

Vamodudantānaṃ.

Substitution of 'v' for final 'o' and 'u'.

Sare pare anto kārukārānaṃ kvaci vo ādeso hoti. Svassa, anveti.

When a vowel follows, a final 'o' or 'u' is sometimes substituted by 'v'. For example: svassa, anveti.

Kvacīti kiṃ. Tayassu, sametāyasmā.

Why 'sometimes'? For example, in tayassu, sametāyasmā.

18. Idha [M 0.0034] ahaṃ tīdha –

18. In the case of idha ahaṃ—

Do dhassa ca.

And substitution of 'd' for 'dh'.

Sare pare dhassa kvaci do hoti. Dīghe – idāhaṃ. Kvacīti kiṃ. Idheva. Cakārena byañjanepi, idha bhikkhave.

When a vowel follows, 'dh' sometimes becomes 'd'. With lengthening: idāhaṃ. Why 'sometimes'? For example, in idheva. By the word 'ca', this also applies before a consonant; for example, idha, bhikkhave.

19. Pati antuṃ, vutti assetīha –

19. In the cases of paṭi antaṃ and vutti assa—

Ivaṇṇo yannavā.

The vowel 'i' optionally becomes 'y'.

Sare pare ivaṇṇassa yo navā hoti. Kata yakārassa tissa “sabbo cantī”ti kvaci cādese “paradvebhāvo ṭhāne”ti sarato parabyañjanassa ṭhānāsannavasā dvittaṃ. Paccantaṃ, vutyassa.

When a vowel follows, the vowel 'i' is optionally substituted by 'y'. For the 't' that now precedes this 'y', 'c' is sometimes substituted by the rule 'sabbo caṃ ti'. Then, by the rule 'paradvebhāvo ṭhāne', the consonant following the vowel is doubled according to its place of articulation. For example: paccantaṃ, vutyassa.

Navāti kiṃ. Paṭaggi,

Why 'optionally'? For example, paṭaggi.

Ettha “kvaci paṭi patisse”ti patissa paṭi, vaṇṇaggahaṇaṃ sabbattha rassadīgha saṅgahaṇatthaṃ.

Here, by the rule 'kvaci paṭi patissa,' 'paṭi' is for 'pati.' The term 'vowel' is used to include both short and long vowels everywhere.

20. Yathā [M 0.0036] evetīha –

20. In the case of yathā eva—

Evādissa ri pubbo ca rasso.

The beginning of 'eva' becomes 'ri', and the preceding vowel is shortened.

Sarato parassa evassādiekāro rittaṃ navā yāti. Pubbo ca ṭhānāsannaṃ rassaṃ. Yathariva. Yatheva.

When following a vowel, the initial 'e' of 'eva' is optionally substituted by 'ri', and the preceding vowel is shortened. For example: yathariva. The alternative is yatheva.

21. Na [M 0.0037] imassa, ti aṅgikaṃ, lahu essati, attha atthaṃ, ito āyati, tasmā iha, sabbhi eva, cha abhiññā, putha eva, pā evatīha vā tveva –

21. In the cases of na imassa, ti aṅgikaṃ, lahu essati, attha atthaṃ, ito āyati, tasmā iha, sabbhi eva, cha abhiññā, puthu eva, and pā eva, the following rules apply optionally—

Ya va ma da na ta ra ḷā cāgamā.

'y, v, m, d, n, t, r, ḷ' are intrusive consonants.

Sare pare yādayo āgamā vā honti, cakārena go ca. Nayimassa, tivaṅgikaṃ, lahumessati, attadatthaṃ, itonāyati, tasmātiha, sabbhireva, chaḷabhiñā, puthageva, “rassa”nti byañjane pare kvaci rasso. Pageva.

When a vowel follows, the consonants 'y', etc., are optionally inserted; and by the word 'ca', 'g' is also included. For example: nayimassa, tivaṅgikaṃ, lahumessati, attadatthaṃ, itonāyati, tasmātiha, sabbhireva, chaḷabhiññā, puthageva. By the rule 'rassaṃ,' the vowel is sometimes shortened before a consonant, as in pageva.

Vāti kiṃ. Cha abhiññā, putha eva, pā eva.

Why 'optionally'? For example, in cha abhiññā, puthu eva, pā eva.

Ettha “sare kvacī”ti sarānaṃ pakati hoti, sassarūpameva, na vikārotyattho.

Here, by the rule 'sare kvaci,' the vowels sometimes remain in their natural state, meaning they retain their own form without alteration.

22. Abhi [M 0.0039] uggatotyatra –

22. In the case of abhi uggato—

“Abbho abhī”ti abhissa abbho. Abbhuggato.

By the rule 'abbho abhi,' 'abhi' becomes 'abbho'. For example: abbhuggato.

23. Byañjanetyadhikāro. Kvacītveva. So bhikkhu, kacci nu tvaṃ, jānema tantīha –

23. Here follows the section on consonants. And only sometimes. [As in the examples:] so bhikkhu, kacci nu tvaṃ, jānema taṃ iha—

Lopañca tatrākāro.

And elision, and in that place the letter a.

Byañjane pare sarānaṃ kvaci lopohoti, tatra lutte ṭhāne akārāgamo, cakārena okārukārāpi. Sabhikkhu kaccino tvaṃ, jānemu taṃ.

When a consonant follows, there is sometimes elision of a vowel; in the place of the elided vowel, the letter a is inserted. By the word ca, the letters o and u are also inserted. [The results are:] sabhikkhu, kaccino tvaṃ, jānemu taṃ.

Kvacīti kiṃ, somuni.

Why 'sometimes'? [As in] so muni.

24. Ughoso[M 0.0040], ākhātantīha – dvebhāve ṭhāne itveva.

24. [In the case of] udghoso and ākhātaṃ here—only in the place of a pair.

Vagge ghosāghosānaṃ tatiyapaṭhamā.

Of the voiced and unvoiced consonants in a class, the third and first [are substituted respectively].

Vagge ghosāghosānaṃ catutthadutiyānaṃ tabbagge tatiyapaṭhamā honti yathāsaṅkhyaṃ yutte ṭhāne, ugghoso, rasse akkhātaṃ.

In a class, for the fourth and second voiced and unvoiced consonants, the third and first consonants of that class are substituted respectively in the appropriate place. [Examples:] ugghoso; when a short vowel precedes, akkhātaṃ.

25. Para [M 0.0041] sahassaṃ, atippakhotīha – “kvaci o byañjane”ti okārāgamo. Parosahassaṃ. Gāgame ca, atippagokho.

25. [In] para sahassaṃ and atippakho here—by the rule 'sometimes o before a consonant,' there is insertion of o, [giving] parosahassaṃ. And with the insertion of g, [it becomes] atippagokho.

26. Ava naddhātyatra – “o avasse”ti kvaci avassa o. Onaddhā.

26. Here in ava naddha—by the rule 'o for ava necessarily,' ava sometimes becomes o. [Result:] onaddhā.

Kvacīti kiṃ. Avasussatu.

Why 'sometimes'? [As in] avasussatu.

27. Niggahītantyadhikāro[M 0.0042]. Kiṃ kato, saṃ jāto, saṃ ṭhito, taṃ dhanaṃ, taṃ mittantiha –

27. Here follows the section on the Niggahīta. [As in the examples:] kiṃ kato, saṃ jāto, saṃ ṭhito, taṃ dhanaṃ, taṃ mittaṃ here—

Vaggantaṃ vā vagge.

Optionally the nasal of the class when a class-consonant follows.

Vaggabyañjane pare bindussa tabbagganto vā hoti. Kiṅkato, sañjāto, saṇṭhito, tandhanaṃ, tammittaṃ.

When a class-consonant follows, the bindu optionally becomes the nasal of that class. [Examples:] kiṅkato, sañjāto, saṇṭhito, tandhanaṃ, tammittaṃ.

Vāti kiṃ. Na taṃ kammaṃ.

Why 'optionally'? [As in] na taṃ kammaṃ.

Vākāreneva le lo ca. Pulliṅgaṃ.

And by the word vā, also before l, [the niggahīta becomes l]. [Example:] pulliṅgaṃ.

28. Vātyadhikāro[M 0.0043]. Evaṃ assa, etaṃ avocetīha –

28. Here follows the section on optional rules. [As in:] evaṃ assa, etaṃ avoca here—

Madā sare.

m and d before a vowel.

Sare pare binduno ma dā vā honti. Evamassa, etadavoca.

When a vowel follows, the bindu optionally becomes m or d. [Examples:] evamassa, etadavoca.

Vāti kiṃ. Maṃ ajini.

Why 'optionally'? [As in] maṃ ajini.

29. Taṃ eva, taṃ hītīha –

29. [In] taṃ eva and taṃ hi here—

Eheñaṃ.

ñ for ṃ before e and hi.

Ekāre, he ca pare binduno ño vā hoti. Dvitte – taññeva, tameva. Tañhi, taṃ hi.

When e or hi follows, the bindu optionally becomes ñ. With doubling: taññeva, tameva. [Also:] tañhi, taṃ hi.

30. Saṃyogotīha –

30. [In] saṃyogo here—

Saye ca.

And with y.

Yakāre pare tena saha binduno ño vā hoti. Dvitte – saññogo, saṃyogo.

When y follows, the bindu together with it optionally becomes ñ. With doubling: saññogo, saṃyogo.

31. Cakkhu aniccaṃ, ava sirotīha - āgamo, kvacitveva.

31. [In] cakkhu aniccaṃ and ava siro here—insertion, and only sometimes.

Niggahītañca.

And of a niggahīta.

Sare, byañjane vā pare kvaci bindvāgamo hoti. Cakkhuṃaniccaṃ, avaṃsiro.

When a vowel or a consonant follows, there is sometimes the insertion of a bindu. Examples: `cakkhuṃaniccaṃ`, `avaṃsiro`.

32. Vidūnaṃ [M 0.0044] aggaṃ, tāsaṃ ahaṃtīha –

32. In `vidūnaṃ aggaṃ` and `tāsaṃ ahaṃ` here—

“Kvaci lopaṃ”ti sare bindulopo, vidūnaggaṃ. Dīghetāsāhaṃ.

By the rule 'sometimes elision,' there is elision of the bindu before a vowel, giving `vidūnaggaṃ`. With lengthening: `tāsāhaṃ`.

33. Buddhānaṃ sāsanaṃ, saṃ rāgotīha –

33. In `buddhānaṃ sāsanaṃ` and `saṃ rāgo` here—

“Byañjane ce”ti bindulopo, buddhānasāsanaṃ. Dīghesārāgo.

By the rule 'and before a consonant,' there is elision of the bindu, giving `buddhānasāsanaṃ`. With lengthening: `sārāgo`.

34. Bījaṃ ivetīha –

34. In `bījaṃ iva` here—

Paro vā saro.

Or the following vowel.

Binduto paro saro vā lupyate, bījaṃva.

A vowel following a bindu is optionally elided. Example: `bījaṃva`.

35. Evaṃ assetīha –

35. In `evaṃ assa` here—

Byañjano ca visaññogo.

And the consonant is disjoined.

Binduto pare sare lutte saṃyogo byañjano vinaṭṭhasaṃyogo hotīti pubbasalopo. Evaṃsa.

36. Anupadiṭṭhānaṃ vuttayogato.

38. Vaṇṇānaṃ [M 0.0047] bahuttaṃ, viparītatā ca.

38. And the addition and transposition of letters.

In the cases of `sarati`, `iti eva`, `sā itthī`, `busaṃ eva`, `bahu ābādho`, `adhi abhavi`, `sukhaṃ`, `dukkhaṃ`, `jīvo` here—

There is insertion of `m` before `s`, and `u` for `a`, giving `sumarati`.

Issa vo, itveva.

`i` becomes `v`, giving `itveva`.

Paralope ākārassa o, sotthī.

With elision of the following vowel, `ā` becomes `o`, giving `sotthī`.

Mādese[M 0.0048], pubbadīghe ca ekārassa i. Busāmiva.

With the substitution of `m`, and with lengthening of the preceding vowel, `e` becomes `i`, giving `busāmiva`.

Vādese havakāravipariyayo. Bahvābādho.

With the substitution of `v`, there is transposition of the letters `h` and `v`. Example: `bahvābādho`.

Adhissa kvaci addho, dīghe-addhābhavi.

Sometimes `adhi` becomes `addh`; with lengthening: `addhābhavi`.

Binduno, okārassa ca e. Sukhe, dukkhe, jīve.

40. Sare, byañjane vā pare binduno kvaci mo. Mama abhāsi, buddhama saraṇaṃ, pubbe mo paraṃ na netabbo ayuttattā.

41. Binduto parasarāna maññassaratāpi.

Issa u, akārassa ca e, bindulopādo. Evumaṃ, kehaṃ.

`i` becomes `u`, and `a` becomes `e`, with elision of the bindu, etc. [Examples:] `evumaṃ` (from `evaṃ imaṃ`), `kehaṃ` (from `kiṃ ahaṃ`).

42. Vākyasukhuccāraṇatthaṃ, chandahānitthañca vaṇṇalopopi.

42. Elision of letters also occurs for the sake of easy utterance and to avoid spoiling the meter.

[In] `paṭisaṅkhāya yoniso` here—there is elision of the preceding `y`, [giving] `paṭisaṅkhāyoniso`.

43. In `alābūni` and so on, there is elision of the vowel `a`. [As in the saying:] `lābūni sīdanti, silā plavanti` (Gourds sink, stones float).

44. Vutyabhedāya vikāropi.

44. For the sake of the meter, alteration also occurs.

Akaramhase tetyādo sakāre garuno ekārassa iminā lahuakāro, akaramhasa te kiccaṃ.

In `akaramhase te` and so on, the heavy vowel `e` before `s` becomes a light `a` by this rule. [Example:] `akaramhasa te kiccaṃ`.

45. Akkharaniyamo [M 0.0052] chandaṃ, garulahuniyamo bhave vutti,

45. The regulation of syllables is meter (`chanda`); the regulation of heavy and light syllables is prosody (`vutti`).

Dīgho, saṃyogādipubbo rasso ca garu, lahu tu rasso. Yathā- ā, assa, aṃ, a.

A long syllable is heavy; a short syllable is also heavy when followed by a consonant cluster or a niggahīta. A short syllable standing alone is light. For example: `ā` (heavy), `assa` (heavy), `aṃ` (heavy), `a` (light).

That which is pure, established in its own form, and has not attained another state such as object or agent, is called the meaning of the nominal stem. To merely designate it, the first inflection is used. When it is unspecified, to denote one, the singular ‘si’ is used. That by which it is expressed is a ‘vacana’ (number). The expression of a single thing is the singular number (ekavacana). Similarly for the plural number (bahuvacana).

By the rule 'sassaṃ,' the ending 'sa' optionally becomes 'aṃ'.

“Amhassa mamaṃ savibhattissa se”ti se amhassa mamaṃ ca. Tava, tuyhaṃ, tumhaṃ, mama, mayhaṃ, amhaṃ, mamaṃ.

By the rule 'amhassa mamaṃ savibhattissa se,' before 'sa', 'amha' with its ending also becomes 'mamaṃ'. The forms are: tava, tuyhaṃ, tumhaṃ; mama, mayhaṃ, amhaṃ, mamaṃ.

“Tumhamhehi namākaṃ”ti naṃvacanassa ākaṃ. Tumhākaṃ, amhākaṃ. Smānāva.

By the rule 'tumhamhehi naṃ ākaṃ,' the ending 'naṃ' is substituted by 'ākaṃ'. The forms are: tumhākaṃ, amhākaṃ.

169. “Tumhāmhānaṃ tayi mayī”ti smimhi tayi mayi honti. Tve kate - tvayi, tayi, mayi, tumhesu, amhesu. Liṅgattaye samaṃ.

169. By the rule 'tumhāmhānaṃ tayi mayi,' before the ending 'smiṃ', the substitutes are 'tayi' and 'mayi'. When the change to 'tv' is made, the form is 'tvayi'. The forms are: tvayi, tayi; mayi. The plural forms are: tumhesu, amhesu. They are the same in all three genders.

170. Navātveva.

170. It is indeed optional.

Padato dutiyā catutthī chaṭṭhīsu vono.

After a word in a sentence, in the accusative, dative, and genitive cases, the enclitic forms 'vo' and 'no' are used.

Atthajjotakā vaṇṇā padaṃ, dutiyā catutthī chaṭṭhī bahuvacanesu paresu padasmā paresaṃ savibhattīnaṃ tumhāmhānaṃ vonokārā navā honti.

The letters that illuminate the meaning constitute a word. After a word, when the plural forms of the second, fourth, and sixth cases follow, 'vo' and 'no' are optional substitutes for the inflected forms of 'tumha' and 'amha'.

Rakkhatu [M 0.0114] vo, passatu no, dadāti vo, dadāhi no, saddhā vo, satthā no.

May he protect you; may he see us; he gives to you; give to us; your faith; our teacher.

Navāti kiṃ, eso amhākaṃ satthā.

Why 'optional'? [To allow for:] 'This is our teacher' (eso amhākaṃ satthā).

171. Padatotyadhikāro.

171. The governing rule is 'after a word'.

“Temekavacanesu ce”ti catutthīchaṭṭhekavacanesu te me honti. Dadāmi te, dadāhi me, idaṃ te, ayaṃ me.

By the rule 'And 'te' and 'me' in the singular cases,' in the singular of the fourth and sixth cases, the substitutes 'te' and 'me' occur. [Examples:] I give to you; give to me; this is yours; this is mine.

“Naamhī”ti [M 0.0115] amhi nisedho. Passetha taṃ, ajini maṃ.

By 'Not in the accusative singular,' there is a prohibition for the accusative singular. [Examples:] May you see thee (passetha taṃ); he conquered me (ajini maṃ).

“Vā tatiye ce”ti tatiyekavacane te me vā honti. Kataṃ te tayā vā. Kataṃ me mayā vā.

By 'And optionally in the third,' in the third case singular, 'te' and 'me' occur optionally. For example: done by you (kataṃ te or kataṃ tayā); done by me (kataṃ me or kataṃ mayā).

“Bahuvacanesu vono”ti tatiyābahuvacanesu vo no honti. Bahuvacane paṭhame yomhi ca. Kataṃ vo, kataṃ no, gāmaṃ vo gaccheyyātha, gāmaṃ no gaccheyyāma.

By 'In the plural, `vo` and `no`,' the substitutes `vo` and `no` occur in the third case plural, and also in the first (nominative) and second (accusative) case plural. [Examples:] Done by you (kataṃ vo); done by us (kataṃ no); you should go to the village (gāmaṃ vo gaccheyyātha); we should go to the village (gāmaṃ no gaccheyyāma).

Saṅkhyā

Numerals

172. Saṅkhyā vuccate.

172. The numerals are now explained.

Ekasaddo sabbanāmesu vutto.

The word 'eka' has been explained among the pronouns.

173. Dvādayo [M 0.0117] aṭṭhārasantā bahuvacanantā.

173. The numerals from 'dvi' (two) to 'aṭṭhārasa' (eighteen) are always in the plural.

Savibhattissa, itthipumanapuṃsakasaṅkhyanti cādhikāro.

The governing rules are 'of the inflected stem,' 'feminine, masculine, and neuter,' and 'numeral'.

“Yosu dvinnaṃ dve ce”ti dvissa dve hoti. Dve, dve, dvībhi, dvīhi.

By the rule 'And for `dvi`, `dve` in the nominative/accusative plural,' the form of `dvi` becomes `dve`. [Examples:] `dve` (nom.), `dve` (acc.), `dvībhi`/`dvīhi` (instr.).

No ca dvādito namhi.

And there is no vowel lengthening for `dvi` and the following numerals before the genitive plural ending.

Namhi dvādito nakārāgamo hoti. Dvinnaṃ, dvīsu, liṅgattaye samaṃ.

When the genitive plural ending `naṃ` follows, there is an augment of the letter `n` after `dvi` and the following numerals. [Examples:] `dvinnaṃ` (gen.), `dvīsu` (loc.). It is the same in the three genders.

174. Ticatunnaṃ tisso catasso tayo cattāro tīṇi cattāri.

174. For 'ti' (three) and 'catu' (four), the nominative/accusative forms are `tisso` and `catasso` (fem.), `tayo` and `cattāro` (masc.), and `tīṇi` and `cattāri` (neut.).

Yosu itthipumanapuṃsakesu savibhattīnaṃ ticatunnaṃ tisso catasso ādayo honti. Tayo, tayo, tībhi, tīhi, tinnaṃ.

In the nominative/accusative plural, for the inflected numerals 'ti' and 'catu' in the feminine, masculine, and neuter genders, the forms `tisso`, `catasso`, etc., occur. [Masculine examples:] `tayo` (nom./acc.), `tībhi`/`tīhi` (instr.), `tinnaṃ` (gen.).

“Iṇṇamiṇṇannaṃ tīhi saṅkhyāhī”ti tisaddato naṃiccassa iṇṇaṃ iṇṇannaṃ ca. Tiṇṇaṃ, tiṇṇannaṃ, tīsu.

By the rule '`iṇṇaṃ` and `iṇṇannaṃ` from the numeral `ti`,' after the word 'ti,' the genitive plural ending `naṃ` is replaced by `iṇṇaṃ` and `iṇṇannaṃ`. [Examples:] `tiṇṇaṃ`, `tiṇṇannaṃ` (gen.); `tīsu` (loc.).

175. Itthiyaṃ-tisso[M 0.0118], tisso, tībhi, tīhi.

175. In the feminine: `tisso` (nom./acc.), `tībhi`/`tīhi` (instr.).

“No cā”do cakārena namhi ssaṃāgamo, vaggantetissanaṃ, tīsu.

By the `ca` in a previous rule, there is an augment of `ssa` before `naṃ`; and the final consonant of the varga is elided, forming `tissannaṃ`. The locative is `tīsu`.

176. Napuṃsake-tīṇi, tīṇi, tīhi.

176. In the neuter: `tīṇi` (nom./acc.), `tībhi`/`tīhi` (instr.).

177. Cattāro.

177. The numeral 'four'.

“Osare ce”tīha cakārena yosu ussa uro. “Tato yonamotū”tīha tukārena yonaṃ o. Caturo. Evaṃ dutiyā. Catūhi, catunnaṃ, catūsu.

Here, by the `ca` in the rule '`Osare ce`', `u` is replaced by `uro` before the nominative/accusative plural ending. By the `tu` in the rule '`Tato yonam o tu`', the `yo` ending is replaced by `o`, forming `caturo`. The accusative is similar. Other forms are: `catūhi` (instr.), `catunnaṃ` (gen.), `catūsu` (loc.).

178. Itthiyaṃ[M 0.0119], catasso, catasso, catūhi. Pubbeva ssaṃāgamo. Yadādinā ussa attaṃ. Catassannaṃ, catūsu.

178. In the feminine: `catasso` (nom./acc.), `catūhi` (instr.). The augment `ssa` is added as before. By the rule beginning with `Yadā...`, `u` is replaced by `a`, forming `catassannaṃ` (gen.). The locative is `catūsu`.

179. Napuṃsake, cattāri, cattāri.

179. In the neuter: `cattāri` (nom./acc.).

180. “Pañcādīna makāro”ti yosu savibhattissa pañcādyantassa attaṃ. Pañca, pañca.

180. By the rule 'For `pañca` and the following, `a`,' before the nominative/accusative plural endings, the final vowel of the inflected stem for `pañca` and the following numerals becomes `a`. [Examples:] `pañca` (nom./acc.).

“Pañcādīna mattaṃ”ti sunaṃhisu pañcādyantassa attaṃ. Edīghānamapavādoyaṃ. Pañcahi, pañcannaṃ, pañcasu. Liṅgattaye samaṃ.

By the rule 'For `pañca` and the following, shortness,' before the endings `su`, `naṃ`, and `hi`, the final vowel of `pañca` and the following numerals becomes short `a`. This is an exception to the rules of `e`-sandhi and vowel lengthening. [Examples:] `pañcahi` (instr.), `pañcannaṃ` (gen.), `pañcasu` (loc.). It is the same in the three genders.

181. Evaṃ cha satta aṭṭha nava dasādayo aṭṭhārasantā.

181. Similarly for `cha` (six), `satta` (seven), `aṭṭha` (eight), `nava` (nine), `dasa` (ten), and the other numerals up to `aṭṭhārasa` (eighteen).

182. Vīsatyādayo [M 0.0120] ānavutiyā itthiliṅgā ekavacanantā, vīsati rattīva. Evaṃ tiṃsati.

182. The numerals from `vīsati` (twenty) up to `navuti` (ninety) are feminine in gender and singular in number. `Vīsati` is declined like `ratti`. Similarly for `tiṃsati` (thirty).

Cattālīsaṃ paññāsaṃ saddehi parāsaṃ sabbāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ “sabbāsamā”dotīha ādisaddena lopo, saṭṭhi vīsatī va. Evaṃ sattati asīti navuti.

For the words `cattālīsaṃ` (forty) and `paññāsaṃ` (fifty), all subsequent case endings are elided by the word `ādi` in the rule beginning '`Sabbāsamādo`'. `Saṭṭhi` (sixty) is declined like `vīsati`. Similarly for `sattati` (seventy), `asīti` (eighty), and `navuti` (ninety).

Sataṃ napuṃsakamekavacanantaṃ. Evaṃ sahassādi.

`Sataṃ` (one hundred) is neuter and singular. Similarly for `sahassa` (thousand) and the higher numerals.

Koṭi vīsatīva.

`Koṭi` (a crore) is declined like `vīsati`.

183. Rāsibhede [M 0.0122] tu sabbattha bahuvacanampi. Yathā-dvevīsatiyo buddhadantā. Tisso vīsatiyo dinaghaṭikā. Evamaññatra.

183. But when denoting distinct groups, the plural is also used everywhere. For example: `dve vīsatiyo buddhadantā` (two twenties of the Buddha's teeth); `tisso vīsatiyo dinaghaṭikā` (three twenties of day-measures). And so in other cases.

Eseso etantippasiddhi, lokassa hoti yatthatthesu.

The convention for `esa`, `eso`, and `etaṃ` occurs in the world in their various meanings.

Thīpumanapuṃsakānityuccante, tānimāni lokenātthā.

Feminine, masculine, and neuter are thus spoken of; these are the meanings in the world.

Aliṅga

Indeclinables

184. Aliṅgā vuccante.

184. The indeclinables are now explained.

Kvaci to pañcamyatthe.

Sometimes the suffix `to` is used in the sense of the fifth (ablative) case.

Liṅgato pañcamyatthe kvaci toppaccayo hoti.

After a nominal stem, the suffix `to` sometimes occurs in the sense of the fifth (ablative) case.

“Tvādayo vibhattisaññāyo”ti toppabhutidānyantānaṃ vibhattisaññā. Tasmā tadantānampi vibhatyantattā padattaṃ siddhanti na puna vibhatti. Corasmā corato. Evaṃ pitito, ettha “pitādīna masimhi” tyatrāsimhiggahaṇena tomhi pitādīnaṃ ussa i.

By the rule '`Tvādayo vibhattisaññāyo`,' the suffixes from `to` up to `dāni` are designated as inflections. Therefore, since words ending in them are considered to end in an inflection, they attain the status of a word and are not inflected again. For example: `corasmā` or `corato` (from the thief). Similarly `pitito` (from the father); here, in the rule '`pitādīnamasimhi`', by the mention of `asimhi`, the `u` of `pitu` and other stems becomes `i` before `to`.

Imassi thandānihatodhesu ca.

And `i` is the substitute for the stem `ima` before the suffixes `tha`, `ṃ`, `dāni`, `ha`, `to`, and `dha`.

Thaṃādīsu paresu imassa i hoti. Ito.

When the suffixes beginning with `thaṃ` follow, `i` is the substitute for `ima`. For example: `ito` (from this).

“Sabbassetassākāro vā”ti tothesvetassa attaṃ vā, ato, etto. Pakkhe- “saralopā”dinā akāralopo.

By the rule '`Sabbassetassākāro vā`,' `a` is the optional substitute for `eta` before `to` and `tha`, resulting in `ato`. In the alternative, `etto` is formed, where the `a` of `eta` is elided by the rule beginning '`Saralopo`'.

“Tratothesu [M 0.0126] ce”ti kissa ku. Kuto.

By the rule '`Tratothesu ce`,' `ku` is the substitute for `kiṃ`. For example: `kuto` (from where?).

“Kvaci to”ti suttadvidhākaraṇena sattamyatthe ca to hoti, ādismiṃ, ādito.

By splitting the sutta '`Kvaci to`' into two, the suffix `to` also occurs in the sense of the seventh (locative) case. For example: `ādismiṃ` or `ādito` (at the beginning).

185. “Tratha [M 0.0127] sattamiyā sabbanāmehī”ti sattamyatthe trathappaccayā honti. Sabbasmiṃ, sabbatra, sabbattha, dvittaṃ. Evaṃ atra, attha. Ettha “tre nicca”nti pubbe etassa a. Kutra, kuttha.

185. By the rule '`Tratha sattamiyā sabbanāmehī`,' the suffixes `tra` and `tha` occur in the sense of the locative. For example: `sabbasmiṃ`, `sabbatra`, or `sabbattha` (everywhere), with doubling. Similarly `atra` and `attha` (here); in these cases, by the rule '`tre niccaṃ`', `a` is the substitute for the preceding `eta`. Also `ettha` (here). And `kutra` or `kuttha` (where?).

“Sesesu ce”ti kādese-kattha.

By the rule '`Sesesu ce`,' with the substitution of `ka` for `kiṃ`, the form is `kattha` (where?).

186. “Kismā vo ce”ti vappaccayo. “Kissa ka vece”ti ko, kakārākāralopo. Kva.

186. By the rule '`Kismā vo ce`,' the suffix `va` is added. By the rule '`Kissa ka vece`,' `ka` is substituted for `kiṃ`, and then the `a` of `ka` is elided, resulting in `kva` (where?).

187. “Hiṃ haṃ hiñcana”nti kasmā hiṃ ādipaccayā. “Ku hiṃ haṃsu ce”ti kissa ku. Cakārena hiñcanaṃ dācanaṃsu ca. Kuhiṃ, kuhaṃ, kuhiñcanaṃ.

187. By the rule '`Hiṃ haṃ hiñcanaṃ`,' the suffixes beginning with `hiṃ` are added after `kiṃ`. By the rule '`Ku hiṃ haṃsu ce`,' `ku` is substituted for `kiṃ`. By the `ca` in that rule, this also applies before `hiñcanaṃ` and `dācanaṃ`. Examples: `kuhiṃ` (where?), `kuhaṃ` (where?), `kuhiñcanaṃ` (wherever?).

188. “Tamhā ce”ti hiṃhaṃ. Tahiṃ, tahaṃ.

188. By the rule '`Tamhā ce`,' the suffixes `hiṃ` and `haṃ` are added. Examples: `tahiṃ`, `tahaṃ` (there).

189. “Yato hiṃ”ti hiṃ. Yahiṃ.

189. By the rule '`Yato hiṃ`,' the suffix `hiṃ` is added. Example: `yahiṃ` (where, wherein).

190. “Imasmā [M 0.0128] hadhā ce”ti hadhā. Iha, idha.

190. By the rule '`Imasmā hadhā ce`,' the suffixes `ha` and `dhā` are added. Examples: `iha`, `idha` (here).

191. “Sabbato dhī”ti dhi. Sabbadhi.

191. By the rule '`Sabbato dhī`,' the suffix `dhi` is added. Example: `sabbadhi` (everywhere).

192. Kāletyadhikāro.

192. The governing rule is 'in the sense of time'.

“Kiṃsabbaññekayakuhi dādācana”nti kiṃ ādito dā, dācanaṃ ca. Kasmiṃ kāle kadā, kudācanaṃ.

By the rule '`Kiṃsabbaññekayakuhi dādācanaṃ`,' the suffixes `dā` and `dācanaṃ` are added after `kiṃ`, etc. Examples: `kadā` (when?); `kudācanaṃ` (sometime).

“Sabbassa so dāmhi vā”ti sabbassa so vā. Sadā, sabbadā.

By the rule '`Sabbassa so dāmhi vā`,' `sa` is the optional substitute for `sabba`. Examples: `sadā` or `sabbadā` (always).

193. “Tamhā dāni ce”ti dāni, dā ca. Tadāni, tadā.

193. By the rule '`Tamhā dāni ce`,' the suffixes `dāni` and `dā` are added. Examples: `tadāni`, `tadā` (then).

194. Yadādinā imasaddā, samānāparehi ca yathāsaṅkhyaṃ jja jjuppaccayā, ima, samānānaṃ a, sā ca. Ajja, sajju, aparajju.

194. By the rule beginning with `Yadā`, the suffix `jja` is added after `ima`, and `jju` is added after `samāna` and `apara`. `a` is the substitute for `ima`, and `sa` for `samāna`. Examples: `ajja` (today), `sajju` (on the same day), `aparajju` (on another day).

195. “Imasmā [M 0.0129] rahidhunādāni ce”ti rahyādippaccayā. “Eta rahimhī”ti imassa eto. Etarahi.

195. By the rule '`Imasmā rahidhunādāni ce`,' the suffixes beginning with `rahi` are added. By the rule '`Eta rahimhi`,' `eta` is the substitute for `ima`, resulting in `etarahi` (now, at present).

“A dhunāmhi ce”ti imassa a. Adhunā, idāni.

By the rule 'A dhunāmhi ce', `a` is the substitute for `ima`. Examples: `adhunā` (now), `idāni` (now).

196. Lopaṃ itveva.

196. The governing rule is 'elision'.

Sabbāsamāvusopasagganipātādīhi ca.

And elision of all inflections after vocatives, prefixes, particles, and so on.

Etehi parā sabbā vibhattī lupyante. Tvaṃ āvuso, tumhe āvuso.

All inflections following these are elided. For example: `tvaṃ āvuso` (friend, you); `tumhe āvuso` (friends, you).

Upasagganipāta

Prefixes and Particles

197. Upasagganipātā vuccante.

197. The prefixes and particles will now be spoken of.

Pa [M 0.0130] parā ni nī u du saṃ vi ava anu pari adhi abhi pati su ā ati api apa upa ete vīsatyupasaggā.

Pa, parā, ni, nī, u, du, saṃ, vi, ava, anu, pari, adhi, abhi, pati, su, ā, ati, api, apa, upa—these are the twenty prefixes.

Ca na va vā mā hi dhi ci ku tu nu ce re he sve ve vo kho no to yaṃ naṃ taṃ kiṃ handa kira eva kīva yāva tāva vata vatha atha aṅga iṅgha taggha āma nāma nūna puna pana āha saha sakkā labbhā heṭṭhā ārā dūrā divā navā vinā nānā addhā mudhā micchā pacchā āvi sakkhi sacci sacchi bahi yadi iti kinti atthi sotthi khalu nanu kimu assu yagghe sace have suve are pure namo tiro adho atho aho raho hīyo bhīyo anto pāto sudaṃ kallaṃ evaṃ dhuvaṃ alaṃ halaṃ sayaṃ sāyaṃ samaṃ sāmaṃ kāmaṃ pāraṃ oraṃ ciraṃ huraṃ ahaṃ sahaṃ uccaṃ nīcaṃ sakiṃ saddhiṃ, athavā antarā ārakā bāhirā bahiddhā yāvatā tāvatā samantā sāmantā āmantā sammukhā carahi tarahi sampati āyati upari yāvade tāvade tiriyaṃ sanikaṃ sasakkaṃ ettāvatā parammukhā kittāvatā etarahi aññadatthu seyyathidaṃ appevanāma bhīyosomattāya iccādayo nipātā.

Ca, na, va, vā, mā, hi, dhi, ci, ku, tu, nu, ce, re, he, sve, ve, vo, kho, no, to, yaṃ, naṃ, taṃ, kiṃ, handa, kira, eva, kīva, yāva, tāva, vata, vatha, atha, aṅga, iṅgha, taggha, āma, nāma, nūna, puna, pana, āha, saha, sakkā, labbhā, heṭṭhā, ārā, dūrā, divā, navā, vinā, nānā, addhā, mudhā, micchā, pacchā, āvi, sakkhi, sacci, sacchi, bahi, yadi, iti, kinti, atthi, sotthi, khalu, nanu, kimu, assu, yagghe, sace, have, suve, are, pure, namo, tiro, adho, atho, aho, raho, hīyo, bhīyo, anto, pāto, sudaṃ, kallaṃ, evaṃ, dhuvaṃ, alaṃ, halaṃ, sayaṃ, sāyaṃ, samaṃ, sāmaṃ, kāmaṃ, pāraṃ, oraṃ, ciraṃ, huraṃ, ahaṃ, sahaṃ, uccaṃ, nīcaṃ, sakiṃ, saddhiṃ; and also: antarā, ārakā, bāhirā, bahiddhā, yāvatā, tāvatā, samantā, sāmantā, āmantā, sammukhā, carahi, tarahi, sampati, āyati, upari, yāvade, tāvade, tiriyaṃ, sanikaṃ, sasakkaṃ, ettāvatā, parammukhā, kittāvatā, etarahi, aññadatthu, seyyathidaṃ, appevanāma, bhīyosomattāya—these and others are particles.

198. Sadisā [M 0.0134] ye tiliṅgesu, sabbāsu ca vibhattīsu.

198. Those that are the same in the three genders and in all the cases,

Vacanesu ca sabbesu, te nipātāti kittitā.

and in all the numbers, they are declared to be particles.

Yathā [M 0.0135] – uccaṃ rukkho, latā, gharaṃ vā, uccaṃ rukkho. He rukkha, rukkhaṃ, rukkhena, rukkhassa, rukkhasmā, rukkhe vā iccādi. Uccaṃ rukkho, rukkhā vā iccādi. Evaṃ latā, gharāni.

For example: a high tree, a creeper, or a house. The particle `uccaṃ` (high) remains unchanged with `rukkho` (tree) in all cases: O tree, a tree, by a tree, of a tree, from a tree, or in a tree, and so on. And in both numbers: a high tree, or high trees, and so on. Similarly for `latā` (creeper) and `gharāni` (houses).

199.

Ubhayesu vibhatyatta -

Kriyadesa samaya disāguṇatthehi;

Sabbāpi yathāyogaṃ,

Vibhattiyoññehi tuppaṭhamā.

With both kinds of indeclinables, case endings are employed in the senses of action, place, time, direction, and qualities, all according to what is appropriate. With others, however, the case endings are other than the first.

Taṃ yathā – adhiantosaddehi sattamī. Sayaṃsaddā tatiyā, chaṭṭhī ca. Namosaddā paṭhamā, dutiyā ca. Pāraṃsaddā sattamī. Divāsaddā paṭhamā, dutiyā, sattamī ca. Heṭṭhāsaddā sattamī. Uccaṃsaddā sabbāpi. Pasaddā ca casaddā ca paṭhamā, hesaddā ālapane paṭhamā. Tathāññehipi.

For example: with the words `adhi` and `anto`, the seventh case is used. With the word `sayaṃ`, the third and sixth cases. With the word `namo`, the first and second cases. With the word `pāraṃ`, the seventh case. With the word `divā`, the first, second, and seventh cases. With the word `heṭṭhā`, the seventh case. With the word `uccaṃ`, all cases. With the words `pa` and `ca`, the first case; with the word `he` in address, the first case. Similarly with others.

200. Upasaggā [M 0.0136] sabbepi saddantarena saha payujjante. Nipātā tu keci visumpi. Yathā – pahāro, paharati, sā ca so ca bhāsati vā karoti vā, sotthityādi.

200. All prefixes are used in conjunction with another word. Some particles, however, are also used separately. For example: `pahāro` (a blow), `paharati` (he strikes); `sā ca so ca` (she and he); `bhāsati vā karoti vā` (speaks or acts); `sotthi` (well-being), and so on.

201. Ekekaliṅgaṃ [M 0.0137] dviliṅgaṃ, tiliṅgaṃ cāpyaliṅgikaṃ.

201. Some are of one gender, some of two genders, some of three genders, and some are without gender.

Catudheti nāmaṃ nāmaṃ, namatyatthanti kittitaṃ.

Thus the noun is fourfold. It is called a 'noun' (nāma) because it is said to incline (namati) towards a meaning.

Nāmikaṃ.

The Section on Nouns

3. Samāsakaṇḍa

3. The Chapter on Compounds

Samāsalakkhaṇādi

The Characteristics of Compounds, etc.

202. Nāmānaṃ [M 0.0138] samāso yuttatthotyadhikāro. Samāsoti bhinnatthānaṃ padāna mekatthatā. Yuttatthoti aññamaññasambandhattho.

202. The compounding of nouns has a connected meaning: this is the governing rule. Compounding is the unification of meaning of words with different meanings into a single meaning. Connected meaning is a meaning of mutual relation.

Vibhāsātyadhikātabbaṃ [M 0.0142] vākyatthaṃ.

The governing rule 'optionally' should be supplied, with reference to the meaning of the analytical phrase.

Kammadhārayasamāsa

Appositional Compounds

203. “Mahanto ca so vīro cā”ti vākye –

203. In the phrase 'Mahanto ca so vīro ca' (He is great and he is a hero)—

Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo.

When two words are in apposition, it is a Kammadhāraya compound.

Bhinnappavattinimittā [M 0.0143] saddā ekasmiṃ vatthuni pavattā tulyādhikaraṇā, visesanavisesassabhūtā samānādhikaraṇā dve padā yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso kammadhārayo

Words that have different grounds for application but which refer to a single entity are in apposition. When two such words, which are a qualifier and a qualified and are in the same case, are compounded, that compound is a Kammadhāraya.

Nāma[M 0.0144], idha vā samāsasuttāni saññādvārena samāsavidhāyakāni.

Indeed, the rules for compounds here prescribe compounding by means of a technical term (saññā).

Aggahitavisesanā [M 0.0146] buddhi visessamhi na uppajjatīti visesanaṃ pubbaṃ hoti, samāseneva tulyādhikaraṇattassa vuttattā tappakāsanatthaṃ payuttā samāsato atirittā ca so iccete “vuttaṭṭhānamappayogo”ti ñāyā nappayujjante. Evamaññatra.

Since understanding of the qualified does not arise without grasping the qualifier, the qualifier is placed first. And since the appositional relationship is expressed by the compound itself, the words `ca` (and) and `so` (he), which are used to clarify it and are external to the compound, are not employed, according to the maxim, 'What is already expressed should not be stated.' Similarly elsewhere.

Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca.

And the elision of their case endings.

Tesaṃ yuttatthānaṃ samāsānaṃ pubbuttarapadānaṃ vibhattī lupyante, cakārena kvaci na.

Of those compounds with a connected meaning, the case endings of the first and subsequent members are elided; but owing to the word `ca` in the rule, sometimes they are not.

Tato mahanta vīra iti ca rūpappasaṅge –

Then, when the form `mahanta vīra` would result—

Pakati cassa sarantassa.

And the original form of that which ends in a vowel is restored.

Vibhattīsu [M 0.0147] luttāsu sarantassa pubbabhūtassa, parabhūtassa ca assa samāsapadassa pakati hotītīha luttākārā punānīyante.

When the case endings have been elided, the original form of a compound member ending in a vowel, whether it is the first or the subsequent member, is restored. Thus, here, the elided '-a' vowels are brought back.

Tato mahanta vīraiti ṭhite –

Then, when the form is 'mahanta vīra'—

“Mahataṃ [M 0.0149] mahā tulyādhikaraṇe pade”ti mahantassa mahā.

By the rule 'mahā for mahat when an appositional word follows,' mahanta becomes mahā.

Taddhita samāsa kitakā nāmaṃvātavetunādīsu ca.

Derivatives with taddhita suffixes, compounds, and derivatives with kitaka suffixes are treated as nouns, except for those ending in suffixes such as -tave and -tuṃ.

Taddhitādayo nāmaṃ iva daṭṭhabbā taveppabhutipaccaye vajjetvā.

Taddhita derivatives and the others should be regarded as nouns, with the exception of suffixes such as -tave.

Tato [M 0.0150] vatticchāya syādi. Mahāvīro, mahāvīrāiccādi.

Then, by application of the declensional endings beginning with si, the forms Mahāvīro, Mahāvīrā, etc., are derived.

204. Kammadhārayo [M 0.0152] dvando ca, tappuriso ca lābhino.

204. The Kammadhāraya, the Dvanda, and the Tappurisa compounds take

Tayo parapade liṅgaṃ, bahubbīhi padantare.

the gender of the final member, whereas a Bahubbīhi takes it from another word to which it refers.

205. Rattā ca sā paṭī cāti rattapaṭī, mahantī ca sā saddhā cāti mahāsaddhā. Ettha “kammadhārayasaññe ce”ti pubbapade pumeva kate āīpaccayānaṃ nivutti.

205. Rattā ca sā paṭī cāti rattapaṭī (a red cloth); mahantī ca sā saddhā cāti mahāsaddhā (great faith). Here, when the first member is treated as masculine by the rule concerning the Kammadhāraya designation, the feminine suffixes -ā and -ī are dropped.

206. Nīlañca taṃ uppalañcāti nīluppalaṃ, satthīva satthi, satthi ca sā sāmā cāti satthisāmā. Mukhameva cando mukhacando.

206. Nīlaṃ ca taṃ uppalaṃ ca iti nīluppalaṃ (a blue lotus); satthi ca sā sāmā ca iti satthisāmā (a dark sword); mukhameva cando iti mukhacando (a moon-like face).

Visesanavisessānaṃ [M 0.0154] yathecchattā kvaci visesanaṃ paraṃ hoti, khattiyabhūtoiccādi, icchā ca yathātanti.

Because there is freedom of choice regarding the position of the qualifier and the qualified, sometimes the qualifier is placed second, as in khattiyabhūto (one who has become a khattiya), etc. And this choice is in accordance with the grammatical system.

Ubhe tappurisa samāsa

Both are Tappurisa compounds.

207. Nasaddā si, tassa lopo. Na suro asuro.

207. From the word 'na' comes the nominative singular ending 'si', which is elided. 'Na suro' becomes 'asuro' (not a god, an asura).

Ettha kammadhāraye kate – “ubhe tappurisā”ti tappurisasaññā. “Attannassa tappurise”ti nassa a. Na asso anasso. Ettha “sare ana”ti nassa ana.

Here, when a Kammadhāraya compound is formed, by the rule 'ubhe tappurisā,' it receives the designation 'Tappurisa.' By the rule 'attannassa tappurise,' 'na' becomes 'a.' 'Na asso' becomes 'anasso' (not a horse, a non-horse). Here, by the rule 'sare ana,' 'na' becomes 'ana.'

208. “Nāmānaṃ [M 0.0155] samāso”ti sutte dvidhākate ayuttatthānampi kvaci samāso. Na puna geyyā apunageyyā gāthetyādi. Ettha geyyena sambandho na-saddo ayuttatthenāpi punena yogavibhāgabalā samasyate.

208. By splitting the rule 'Nāmānaṃ samāso' (Compounding of Nouns), a compound is sometimes formed even with words of incongruous meaning. For example, 'apunageyyā gāthā' (a verse not to be sung again). Here, the word 'na,' though connected in meaning with 'geyyā,' is compounded with 'puna,' which is incongruous, by the force of splitting the rule (yogavibhāga).

Digusamāsa

The Digu Compound

209. Tayo [M 0.0156] lokā samāhaṭā tilokaṃ.

209. 'Three worlds gathered together' becomes 'tilokaṃ' (the three worlds).

Ettha “saṅkhyāpubbo digū”ti kammadhārayassa digusaññā. ‘Digussekattaṃ”ti ekattaṃ, napuṃsakattañca.

Here, by the rule 'saṅkhyāpubbo digū,' a Kammadhāraya compound receives the designation 'Digu.' By the rule 'Digussekattaṃ,' it becomes singular and neuter.

Suddhatappurisasamāsa

The Pure Tappurisa Compound

210. Tappurisā [M 0.0157] tveva.

210. The Tappurisa Compounds Proper

Amādayo parapadehi.

Words ending in 'aṃ' and so on are compounded with subsequent words.

Dutiyantādayo parapadehi nāmehi yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso tappuriso nāma.

When words ending in the second case ending and so on are compounded with subsequent nouns, that compound is called a 'Tappurisa'.

Gāmaṃ [M 0.0158] gato gāmagato.

'Gāmaṃ gato' (gone to the village) becomes 'gāmagato'.

“Passa vāsiṭṭha gāmaṃ, gato tisso sāvatthiṃ”tya trāyuttatthatāya na samāso. Tathā ññatra ñeyyaṃ.

In the sentence 'Passa vāsiṭṭha gāmaṃ, gato tisso sāvatthiṃ' (See, Vāsiṭṭha, the village; Tissa has gone to Sāvatthī), there is no compounding due to the lack of semantic connection. This should be understood similarly in other cases.

211. Raññā [M 0.0159] hato rājahato.

211. 'Raññā hato' (killed by the king) becomes 'rājahato'.

Kiccantehi bhīyo adhikatthavacane.

Compounding with words ending in kicca suffixes often occurs when an additional meaning is expressed.

Tabba, anīya, ṇya, teyya, riccappaccayā kiccā. Thutinindatthamajjhāropitatthaṃ vacanaṃ adhikatthavacanaṃ. Soṇaleyyo kūpoiccādi. Soṇehi yathā liyhate, tathā puṇṇattā thuti. Tehi ucchiṭṭhattā nindā ca.

The suffixes -tabba, -anīya, -ṇya, -teyya, and -ricca are the kicca (gerundive) suffixes. A statement in which a meaning of praise or blame is superimposed is a 'statement with an additional meaning.' For example, 'soṇaleyyo kūpo' (a well to be licked by dogs). Just as it is licked by dogs, there is praise on account of its fullness; and there is blame on account of it being their leavings.

Dadhinā upasittaṃ bhojanaṃ dadhibhojanaṃ, samāsapadeneva upasittakriyāya kathanā natthetthāyuttatthatā. Upasittasaddāppayogo pubbeva.

'Dadhinā upasittaṃ bhojanaṃ' (food sprinkled with curd) becomes 'dadhibhojanaṃ.' Since the action of sprinkling is expressed by the compound word itself, there is no incongruity here. The non-use of the word 'upasitta' has already been established.

212. Karaṇe [M 0.0160] tu-asinā kalaho asikalaho.

212. In the instrumental case: 'asinā kalaho' (a quarrel with a sword) becomes 'asikalaho'.

213. Buddhassa deyyaṃ buddhaddeyyaṃ, parassapadaṃ, ettha vibhatyalopo. Evaṃ attanopadamiccādi.

213. 'Buddhassa deyyaṃ' (to be given to the Buddha) becomes 'buddhaddeyyaṃ.' Here there is elision of the case ending. Similarly with 'parassapadaṃ,' 'attanopadaṃ,' and so on.

214. Corasmā [M 0.0161] bhayaṃ corabhayaṃ. Evaṃ baddhanamuttoccādi.

214. 'Corasmā bhayaṃ' (fear from a thief) becomes 'corabhayaṃ.' Similarly, 'bandhanamutto' (freed from bondage), and so on.

215. Rañño putto rājaputto.

215. 'Rañño putto' (son of the king) becomes 'rājaputto'.

“Brāhmaṇassa kaṇhā dantā” iccatra dantāpekkhā chaṭṭhīti kaṇhena sambandhābhāvā na samāso. Yadā tu kaṇhā ca te dantā ceti kammadhārayo, tadā chaṭṭhī kaṇhadantāpekkhāti brāhmaṇakaṇhadantāti samāso hoteva.

In the phrase 'Brāhmaṇassa kaṇhā dantā' (The brahmin's teeth are black), the genitive ('brāhmaṇassa') is dependent on 'dantā' (teeth); because there is no connection with 'kaṇhā' (black), no compound is formed. But when there is the kammadhāraya compound 'kaṇhadantā' (from 'kaṇhā ca te dantā'), then the genitive is dependent on 'kaṇhadantā,' and thus the compound 'brāhmaṇakaṇhadantā' (the brahmin's black teeth) can indeed be formed.

216. “Rañño [M 0.0162] māgadhassa dhana” ntyatra raññoti chaṭṭhī dhana mapekkhate, na māgadhaṃ. Rājā eva māgadhasaddena vuccateti bhedābhāvā sambandhābhāvoti tulyādhikaraṇena māgadhena saha rājā na samasyate. Dviṭṭho hi sambandho.

216. In the phrase 'Rañño māgadhassa dhanaṃ' (the wealth of the king of Magadha), the genitive 'rañño' is syntactically dependent on 'dhanaṃ' (wealth), not on 'māgadhaṃ.' Since 'rājā' and 'māgadha' refer to the same entity, they are in apposition (tulyādhikaraṇa). Because 'rañño' is dependent on another word ('dhanaṃ'), it cannot be compounded with 'māgadha.' For the syntactic connection is divided.

Rañño [M 0.0163] asso puriso ce” tya tra rañño asso, rañño puriso ti ca paccekaṃ sambandhato sāpekkhatā atthīti na samāso. “Asso ca puriso cā”ti dvande kate tu rājassapurisāti hoteva, aññānapekkhattā.

In the phrase 'Rañño asso puriso ca' (the king's horse and man), because 'rañño' is connected separately to both 'asso' and 'puriso,' there is syntactic dependency, and thus no compound is formed. However, when a dvanda compound 'assapuriso' is formed from 'asso ca puriso ca,' the compound 'rājassapuriso' (the king's horse-and-man) does occur, as there is no longer any external dependency.

“Rañño garuputto” iccatra rājāpekkhinopi garuno

In 'Rañño garuputto' (the son of the king's teacher), although 'garu' (the teacher) is dependent on 'rāja' (the king),

Puttena [M 0.0164] saha samāso, gamakattā. Gamakattampi samāsassa nibandhanaṃ. Tattha garuno puttoti viggaho, evamaññatra.

a compound is formed with 'putta' because of intelligibility. Intelligibility is also a basis for compounding. Here the analysis is 'garuno putto' (teacher's son); it is similar in other cases.

217. Rūpe saññā rūpasaññā.

217. 'Rūpe saññā' (perception in regard to form) becomes 'rūpasaññā'.

Kvaci nindāyaṃ - kūpe maṇḍūko viya kūpamaṇḍūko. Evaṃ nagarakāko iccādi. Atropamāya nindā gamyate.

Sometimes in the sense of censure: 'kūpe maṇḍūko viya' (like a frog in a well) becomes 'kūpamaṇḍūko' (a frog in a well). Similarly, 'nagarakāko' (a city crow), and so on. Here, censure is understood through the simile.

Antevāsiko tyādo vibhattyalopo.

In 'antevāsiko' (a resident pupil), and so on, there is elision of the case ending.

Bahubbīhisamāsa

The Bahubbīhi Compound

218. Aññapadatthesu bahubbīhi.

218. A bahubbīhi compound is formed when the meaning refers to another word.

Appaṭhamantāna [M 0.0165] maññesaṃ padānaṃ atthesu dve vā bahūni vā nāmāni yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso bahubbīhi nāma.

When two or more nouns, which are not in the nominative case, are compounded to denote the meaning of another word, that compound is called a bahubbīhi.

Āgatā samaṇā yaṃ sā āgatasamaṇo, vihāro.

'Āgatā samaṇā yaṃ so vihāro' (the monastery to which ascetics have come) becomes 'āgatasamaṇo vihāro'.

219. Jitāni [M 0.0166] indriyāni yena so jitindriyo, bhagavā. Āhito aggi yena so āhitaggi. Agyāhito vātyādo yathecchaṃ visesanassa paratā.

219. 'Jitāni indriyāni yena so jitindriyo, bhagavā' (He by whom the faculties have been conquered is 'one with conquered faculties,' the Blessed One). 'Āhito aggi yena so āhitaggi' (He by whom the fire has been placed is 'one who has placed the fire'). Or 'aggim āhito,' and so on; the qualifier may optionally be placed after.

220. Karaṇe tu-chinno rukkho yena so chinnarukkho, pharasu.

220. In the instrumental case: 'Chinno rukkho yena so pharasu' (the axe by which the tree was cut) becomes 'chinnarukkho pharasu'.

221. Dinno suṅko yassa so dinnasuṅko, rājā.

221. 'Dinno suṅko yassa so rājā' (the king to whom the toll has been given) becomes 'dinnasuṅko rājā'.

222. Niggatā janā yasmā so niggatajano, gāmo.

222. 'Niggatā janā yasmā so gāmo' (the village from which the people have departed) becomes 'niggatajano gāmo'.

223. Dasa [M 0.0167] balāni yassa so dasabalo, bhagavā. Natthi samo yassa so asamo. Ettha “attannassā”ti yogavibhāgena nassa a.

223. 'Dasa balāni yassa so dasabalo, bhagavā' (He whose are the ten powers is 'one of ten powers,' the Blessed One). 'Natthi samo yassa so asamo' (He who has no equal is 'unequalled'). Here, by splitting the rule 'attannassā,' 'na' becomes 'a'.

Pahūtā jivhā yassa so pahūtajivho, mahantī paññā yassa so mahāpañño. Dvīsu “itthiyambhāsitapumitthīpumāva ce”ti pumbhāvātidesā pubbuttarapadesu āīppaccayānamabhāvo.

'Pahūtā jivhā yassa so pahūtajivho' (he whose tongue is abundant). 'Mahantī paññā yassa so mahāpañño' (he whose wisdom is great). In these two cases, by the rule 'itthiyambhāsitapumitthīpumāva ce,' the masculine form is adopted, and therefore the feminine suffixes -ā and -ī are absent in the prior and subsequent members of the compound.

224. “Kvaci [M 0.0169] samāsantagatānamakāranto”ti antassa attaṃ. Kāraggahaṇena ā i ca. Itthiyamivaṇṇantā, tvantehi ca kappaccayopi. Yathā - visālaṃ akkhi yassa so visālakkho, paccakkhadhammā, silopo. Sobhano gandho yassa so [M 0.0171] sugandhi. Bahukantiko, bahunadiko, samuddo. Ettha yadādinā rasso. Bahukattuko. Mattā bahavo mātaṅgā yasmiṃ taṃ mattabahumātaṅgaṃ, vanaṃ.

224. By the rule 'Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto,' the final vowel becomes 'a.' By the inclusion of 'kāra,' this also applies to 'ā' and 'ī.' In the feminine, for words ending in 'ī' and 'ū,' and for those ending in '-tu,' the suffix '-ka' is also added. For example: 'visālaṃ akkhi yassa so' becomes 'visālakkho' (one with a large eye); 'paccakkhadhammā' (one for whom the Dhamma is directly visible), with elision of 'si.' 'Sobhano gandho yassa so' becomes 'sugandhi' (one with a pleasant scent). The ocean is 'bahukantiko' (having many wives) or 'bahunadiko' (having many rivers); here, by the rule 'yadādinā,' there is shortening. 'Bahukattuko' (having many doers). 'Mattā bahavo mātaṅgā yasmiṃ taṃ vanaṃ' becomes 'mattabahumātaṅgaṃ vanaṃ' (the forest in which there are many intoxicated elephants).

Tulyādhikaraṇo.

Appositional Bahubbīhi

225. Suvaṇṇassa [M 0.0172] viya vaṇṇo yassa so suvaṇṇavaṇṇo. Vajiraṃ pāṇimhi yassa so vajirapāṇi. Urasi lomāni yassa so urasilomo. Ettha vibhatyalopo.

225. 'Suvaṇṇassa viya vaṇṇo yassa so' becomes 'suvaṇṇavaṇṇo' (one whose complexion is like gold). 'Vajiraṃ pāṇimhi yassa so' becomes 'vajirapāṇi' (one with a thunderbolt in his hand). 'Urasi lomāni yassa so' becomes 'urasilomo' (one with hair on his chest). In these cases, there is elision of the case endings.

“Atthesū”ti bahuttaggahaṇena kvaci paṭhamantānampi. Saha hetunā yo vattate so sahetuko, “yadā” dinā sahassa so.

By the use of the plural in 'atthesu' (in the rule 'Aññapadatthesu...'), sometimes even words ending in the nominative case are compounded. For example, 'Saha hetunā yo vattati so' becomes 'sahetuko' (that which is with a cause). By the rule 'yadādinā,' 'saha' becomes 'sa'.

226. Satta [M 0.0173] vā aṭṭha vā sattaṭṭha, māsā, etthaññapadattho vā saddassattho. Dakkhiṇassā ca pubbassā ca disāya yaṃ antarālaṃ, sā dakkhiṇapubbā, disā.

226. 'Satta vā aṭṭha vā' becomes 'sattaṭṭha', as in 'sattaṭṭha māsā' (seven or eight months). Here, the meaning of the other word is the meaning of the word 'vā' (or). 'Dakkhiṇassā ca pubbassā ca disāya yaṃ antarālaṃ sā disā' becomes 'dakkhiṇapubbā disā' (the southeastern direction).

Bhinnādhikaraṇo.

Vyadhikaraṇa Bahubbīhi

Appaṭhamantānanti kiṃ, desito buddhena yo dhammo.

Why is the term 'appaṭhamantānaṃ' (not ending in the nominative) stated in the rule? To prevent compounding in a phrase such as 'desito buddhena yo dhammo' (the Dhamma that was taught by the Buddha).

Dvandasamāsa

The Dvanda Compound

227. Nāmānaṃ samuccayo dvando.

227. A conjunction of nouns is a dvanda.

Samuccayo[M 0.0177], ti piṇḍīkaraṇaṃ ekavibhattikānaṃ nāmānaṃ yo samuccayo, so dvando nāma, idaṃ suttaṃ bahuvacanavisayaṃ.

‘Conjunction’ means aggregation. That conjunction of nouns having the same case ending is called a dvanda. This rule pertains to the plural.

Cando ca sūriyo ca candasūriyā. Tiṭṭhanti tyādi-

Cando ca sūriyo ca becomes candasūriyā. For example: tiṭṭhanti (they stand), and so on.

Kriyāsambandhasāmaññato [M 0.0178] atthetthāpekatthatā, evaṃ naranāriyo, akkharapadāni.

Because of the common connection with the action, there is a single intended meaning. Thus: naranāriyo (men and women), akkharapadāni (letters and words).

228. Tathā dvande pāṇi turiya yogga senaṅga khuddajantuka vividha viruddha visabhāgatthādīnañca.

228. Likewise, in a dvanda, there is compounding of terms for the limbs of living beings, musical instruments, parts of a vehicle, divisions of an army, small creatures, various opposites, things of dissimilar nature, and so on.

Vividhenākārena viruddhā vividhaviruddhā, sabhāgā sadisā, vividhā ca te sabhāgā ceti visabhāgā. Yathā digusamāse, tathā dvande pāṇyaṅgatthādīnaṃ ekattaṃ, napuṃsakattañca hoti.

‘Various opposites’ are things opposed in various ways. Sabhāgā means similar. Visabhāgā refers to things that are diverse and yet of a similar nature. Just as in a digu compound, so too in a dvanda, terms for the limbs of living beings, etc., take the singular and the neuter gender.

Cakkhusotaṃ, gītavāditaṃ, yuganaṅgalaṃ, hatthassaṃ, asicammaṃ, ḍaṃsamakasaṃ, kokālūkaṃ.

Cakkhusotaṃ: eye and ear. Gītavāditaṃ: song and music. Yuganaṅgalaṃ: yoke and plough. Hatthassaṃ: elephants and horses. Asicammaṃ: sword and shield. Ḍaṃsamakasaṃ: gadflies and mosquitoes. Kokālūkaṃ: crow and owl.

Nāmarūpaṃ[M 0.0182], nāmaṃ namanalakkhaṇaṃ, rūpaṃ ruppanalakkhaṇaṃ. Evamete dhammā lakkhaṇato vividhā, paramatthato sabhāgā ca.

Nāmarūpaṃ: name-and-form. Name has the characteristic of inclining; form has the characteristic of being afflicted. Thus these phenomena are diverse in characteristic but of a similar nature in an ultimate sense.

Ādisaddenāññatthāpi[M 0.0183]. Yathā - bhinnaliṅgānaṃ - itthipumaṃ. Yadādinā rasso, dāsidāsaṃ, pattacīvaraṃ. Gaṅgāsoṇaṃ.

By the word ‘etc.’ it applies in other cases too. For example, with words of different gender: itthipumaṃ (woman and man). By the rule beginning ‘ya,’ the vowel is shortened: dāsidāsaṃ (female slave and male slave); pattacīvaraṃ (bowl and robes); Gaṅgāsoṇaṃ (the Ganges and the Sona).

Saṅkhyāparimāṇānaṃ - tikacatukkaṃ.

Of numbers and measures: tikacatukkaṃ (a triad and a tetrad).

Sippīnaṃ - veṇarathakāraṃ.

Of artisans: veṇarathakāraṃ (bamboo-workers and chariot-makers).

Luddakānaṃ - sākuntika māgavikaṃ.

Of hunters: sākuntikamāgavikaṃ (fowlers and deer-hunters).

Appāṇijātīnaṃ - ārasatthi.

Of the class of inanimate things: ārasatthi (awl and knife).

Ekajjhāyanabrāhmaṇānaṃ - kaṭhakālāpaṃ iccādi.

Of brahmins studying the same text: kaṭhakālāpaṃ (the Kaṭhas and the Kālāpas), etc.

229. Vibhāsā rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapadādīnañca.

229. Optionally in the case of trees, grass, cattle, wealth, grain, countries, and so on.

Dvande [M 0.0185] rukkhādīnaṃ ekattaṃ napuṃsakattañca vā hoti.

In a dvanda compound of terms for trees, etc., there is optionally singularity and the neuter gender.

Dhavakhadiraṃ, dhavakhadirā, muñjapabbajaṃ, muñjapabbajā, ajeḷakaṃ, ajeḷakā, hiraññasuvaṇṇaṃ, hiraññasuvaṇṇāni, sāliyavaṃ, sāliyavā.

Dhavakhadiraṃ or dhavakhadirā: Dhava and Khadira trees. Muñjapabbajaṃ or muñjapabbajā: Muñja and Pabbaja grasses. Ajeḷakaṃ or ajeḷakā: goats and sheep. Hiraññasuvaṇṇaṃ or hiraññasuvaṇṇāni: gold and bullion. Sāliyavaṃ or sāliyavā: rice and barley.

Kāsikosalaṃ [M 0.0187] kāsikosalā.

Kāsikosalaṃ or kāsikosalā: the countries of Kāsī and Kosala.

Ādisaddena aññesupi vā. Yathā – niccavirodhīnamaddabbānaṃ - kusalākusalaṃ, kusalākusalāni.

By the word ‘etc.,’ this applies optionally to others too. For example, to abstract terms in constant opposition: kusalākusalaṃ or kusalākusalāni (the wholesome and the unwholesome).

Sakuṇīnaṃ - bakabalākaṃ, bakabalākā.

Of birds: bakabalākaṃ or bakabalākā (herons and cranes).

Byañjanānaṃ - dadhighataṃ, dadhighatāni.

Of condiments: dadhighataṃ or dadhighatāni (curd and ghee).

Disānaṃ - pubbāparaṃ, pubbāparā iccādi.

Of directions: pubbāparaṃ or pubbāparā (east and west), etc.

Abyayībhāvasamāsa

The Avyayībhāva Compound

230. Adhisaddā [M 0.0189] smiṃ, tassa lopo. Adhisaddena tulyādhikaraṇattā itthisaddāpismiṃ. Niccasamāsattā ādhārabhūtāyamitthiyanti padantarena viggaho. Adhi itthiyanti ṭhite –

230. On account of the word `adhi`, the locative ending `-smiṃ` is employed, and it is elided. Because it has the same referent as the word `adhi`, the word `itthi` also takes the locative ending. Since this is an obligatory compound, its analytic phrase with other words is: `ādhārabhūtā ayaṃ itthi` ('this woman who is the basis'). When `adhi itthi` stands:

Upasagganipātapubbako abyayībhāvo.

An avyayībhāva is a compound preceded by a prefix or a particle.

Upasaggādipubbako saddo vibhatyatthādīsu samāso hoti, abyayībhāvasañño ca.

A word preceded by a prefix or the like, when compounded in the sense of a case ending and so on, is designated an avyayībhāva.

“So napuṃsakaliṅgo”ti abyayībhāvo napuṃsakaliṅgo, yadādinā ekavacano ca.

By the rule ‘It is neuter,’ an avyayībhāva is in the neuter gender; and by the rule beginning ‘ya,’ it is in the singular.

“Saro [M 0.0191] rasso napuṃsake”ti rasso.

By the rule ‘A vowel is short in the neuter,’ the final vowel is shortened.

Aññasmā lopo ca.

And elision of the case ending of the other member.

Anakārantā abyayībhāvā parā sabbā vibhattī lujjare. Adhitthi, vibhattīnamattho ādhārādi.

All case endings following an avyayībhāva compound not ending in -a are elided. For example, adhitthi. The meaning of the case ending is location, etc.

Idhādhisaddo ādhārevattate, adhitthiiccetaṃ padaṃ itthiya miccetamatthaṃ vadati.

Here the word `adhi` functions in the sense of location. The word `adhitthi` expresses the meaning ‘concerning a woman’ (locative).

Samīpaṃ [M 0.0193] nagarassa upanagaraṃ. “Aṃvibhattīnamakārantabyayībhāvā”ti vibhattīnaṃ kvaci aṃ.

Samīpaṃ nagarassa is upanagaraṃ (near the city). By the rule ‘After an avyayībhāva ending in -a, aṃ is the substitute for case endings,’ aṃ is sometimes the substitute for case endings.

Kvacīti kiṃ. Upanagare.

Why ‘sometimes’? To allow for forms such as upanagare.

Abhāvo makkhikānaṃ nimmakkhikaṃ rasso. Anupubbo therānaṃ anutheraṃ, anatikkamma sattiṃ yathāsatti.

Abhāvo makkhikānaṃ is nimmakkhikaṃ (absence of flies); the vowel is shortened. Anupubbo therānaṃ is anutheraṃ (in succession of the elders). Anatikkamma sattiṃ is yathāsatti (according to one’s ability).

Ye [M 0.0195] ye buḍḍhā yathābuḍḍhaṃ, vicchāyaṃ.

Ye ye buḍḍhā is yathābuḍḍhaṃ (according to seniority). Vicchāyaṃ (absence of shade).

Yattako paricchedo jīvassa yāvajīvaṃ, avadhāraṇe.

Yattako paricchedo jīvassa is yāvajīvaṃ (for as long as life lasts); this is in the sense of limitation.

Ā pabbatā khettaṃ āpabbataṃ khettaṃ, mariyādāyaṃ, vajjamānā sīmā mariyādā, pabbataṃ vinātyattho.

Ā pabbatā khettaṃ is āpabbataṃ khettaṃ (the field up to the mountain). This is in the sense of a boundary (mariyādā). A boundary that excludes is a mariyādā; the meaning is ‘excluding the mountain’.

Ā jalantā sītaṃ ājalantaṃ sītaṃ, abhividhimhi, gayhamānā sīmā abhividhi, jalantena sahetyattho.

Ā jalantā sītaṃ is ājalantaṃ sītaṃ (the cold up to the burning). This is in the sense of inclusion (abhividhi). A boundary that includes is an abhividhi; the meaning is ‘together with the burning’.

Āsaddayoge “dhātunāmā”dinā apādānavidhāneneva vākyampi siddhaṃ. Tathāññatra.

In connection with the word ā, the uncompounded phrase is also valid by the rule for the ablative case beginning ‘dhātunāma...’. Likewise in other cases.

231. “Uttamo [M 0.0197] vīro pavīro” iccādo pana pubbapadatthappadhānattābhāvābyayībhāvābhāvo kammadhārayoeva. Evaṃ visiṭṭho dhammo abhidhammo. Kucchitaṃ annaṃ kadannaṃ. Etttha [M 0.0198] “kada kussā”ti sare kussa kadādeso.

231. However, in examples such as uttamo vīro: pavīro (the highest hero: a great hero), because the meaning of the first member is not predominant, it is not an avyayībhāva but simply a kammadhāraya. Similarly, visiṭṭho dhammo: abhidhammo (the special teaching: the higher teaching). Kucchitaṃ annaṃ: kadannaṃ (spoiled food). Here, by the rule ‘kada for ku,’ kad is the substitute for ku before a vowel.

Appakaṃ lavaṇaṃ kālavaṇaṃ, ettha “kāppatthesu cā”ti kussa kā, bahuvacanenāññatrāpi kvaci. Kucchito puriso kāpuriso, kupuriso vā, evamasurādi.

Appakaṃ lavaṇaṃ is kālavaṇaṃ (a little salt). Here, by the rule ‘kā in the sense of appa (little),’ kā is the substitute for ku. By the plural in the rule, it applies elsewhere too. Kucchito puriso is kāpuriso or kupuriso (a contemptible man). Similarly asura, etc.

Pubbaparūbhayamaññapadattha - ppadhānābyayībhāva samāso;

Kammadhārayaka tappurisā dve, dvendo ca bahubbīhi ca ñeyyā.

The avyayībhāva, the tappurisa, the dvanda, and the bahubbīhi should be known as the compounds where the meaning of the first member, the final member, both members, and another word, respectively, is predominant.

Samāso.

4. The Section on Taddhita Derivatives

Apaccataddhita

Patronymic Derivatives

232. Vāṇapacce.

232. The suffix ṇa is used optionally in the sense of 'descendant'.

Chaṭṭhantā [M 0.0200] saddā “tassāpacca”miccasmiṃ atthe ṇo vā hoti. Vāti vākyatthaṃ. Ṇenevāpaccatthassa vuttattā apaccasaddāppayogo.

From words ending in the sixth case affix (genitive), the suffix ṇo optionally occurs in the sense of 'descendant of that one.' The word 'vā' is for the sake of the uncompounded phrase. Since the meaning of 'descendant' is already expressed by ṇo, the word 'apacca' is not used.

“Tesaṃ vibhatyā” do tesaṃgahaṇena vibhattilopo. Tathottaratra.

By the rule beginning 'Tesaṃ vibhatyā...', the elision of the case ending occurs because of the word 'tesaṃ' (of them) in the rule. Likewise hereafter.

“Tesaṃ ṇo lopaṃ”ti paccayānaṃ ṇassa lopo.

By the rule 'Tesaṃ ṇo lopaṃ', there is elision of the ṇ of the suffixes.

“Vuddhādisarassa vā saṃyogantassa saṇe ce”ti saṇakāre pare asaṃyogantassādisarassa vuddhi.

By the rule 'Vuddhādisarassa vā saṃyogantassa saṇe ce', when a suffix with the indicator ṇ follows, vuddhi of the initial vowel of a word not ending in a conjunct consonant occurs.

Tassāpaniyame –

In that regard, without a specific rule:

Ayuvaṇṇānañcāyo vuddhi.

For the vowels a, i, and u, the vuddhi forms are ā, e, and o, respectively.

Akārivaṇṇuvaṇṇānaṃ āeovuddhiyo honti, casaddena kvaci na.

For the vowels a, i, and u, ā, e, and o are the vuddhi forms; due to the word 'ca', this sometimes does not happen.

Saralopādi, taddhitattā nāmamiva kate syādi.

Elision of vowels, etc., occurs. Since it is a taddhita derivative, once it is formed like a noun, the nominal suffixes beginning with si are applied.

Taddhitābhidheyyaliṅga–vibhattivacanā [M 0.0202] siyuṃ.

Taddhita derivatives should take the gender, case, and number of that which they denote.

Samūhabhāvajā bhīyo, sakatthe ṇyo napuṃsake.

Derivatives denoting collectivity and state are mostly in the neuter. The suffix ṇya, when used in its own sense, is in the neuter.

Tā tutthiyaṃ nipātā te, dhāmithaṃpaccayantakā.

Those ending in the suffixes tā, tu, tti, yaṃ, as well as indeclinables, and those ending in the suffixes dhā, mi, and thaṃ.

Vasiṭṭhassāpaccaṃ [M 0.0203] poso vāsiṭṭho, itthī vāsiṭṭhī, napuṃsakaṃ vāsiṭṭhaṃ. Vikappavidhānato taddhitena samāsassāccantaṃ bādhāyā bhāvā vasiṭṭhā paccantipi hoti.

A male descendant of Vasiṭṭha is Vāsiṭṭho; a female, Vāsiṭṭhī; and the neuter, Vāsiṭṭhaṃ. Because the rule allows for an alternative, the taddhita derivative does not absolutely preclude the uncompounded phrase; thus, vasiṭṭhassa apaccaṃ also occurs.

Napuṃsakena vāpīti, saddasatthavidū viduṃ.

Or also in the neuter, thus the grammarians know.

233. Vā [M 0.0205] apacceti cādhikāro.

233. And the governing rule 'vā apacce' (optionally in the sense of descendant) continues.

Ṇāyana ṇāna vacchādito.

The suffixes ṇāyana and ṇāna are used after words of the Vacchādi group.

Vacchādito gottagaṇato ṇāyano ṇāno ca vā hoti.

From the Vacchādi group of clans, the suffixes ṇāyana and ṇāna optionally occur.

Apaccaṃ paputtappabhuti gottaṃ. Kaccassāpaccaṃ kaccāyano, kaccāno vā. Saṃyogantattā na vuddhi.

Apaccaṃ means lineage, beginning with a descendant. The descendant of Kacca is Kaccāyano, or Kaccāno. Because the stem ends in a conjunct consonant, vuddhi does not occur.

234. “Ṇeyyo [M 0.0206] kattikādīhī”ti ṇeyyo, vinatāya apaccaṃ venateyyo vinateyyo vā. Na pakkhe vuddhi, ṇeyyoti yogavibhāgena “tassa dīyate” tyatthepi ṇeyyo, dakkhiṇā dīyate yassa so dakkhiṇeyyo.

234. By the rule, “Ṇeyyo kattikādīhī,” the suffix ṇeyyo is applied. The descendant (apaccaṃ) of Vinatā is Venateyyo or Vinateyyo. In the alternative form, there is no vuddhi. By division of the rule (yogavibhāgena), ṇeyyo is also applied in the sense of “to whom it is given.” He to whom a gift (dakkhiṇā) is given is called Dakkhiṇeyyo.

235. Ato ṇi vā.

235. After a stem ending in 'a', the suffix ṇi is optional.

Akārantato [M 0.0207] apacce ṇi vā hoti, puna vāsaddena ṇiko, akārantā anakārantā ca bopi.

After a stem ending in 'a', the suffix ṇi optionally occurs in the sense of 'descendant' (apacca); further, by the word 'vā', the suffix ṇika optionally occurs; and after stems ending in 'a' and not ending in 'a', the suffix bo also optionally occurs.

Dakkhi, sakyaputtiko, maṇḍabbo, bhātubbo. Dvittaṃ.

Dakkhi, Sakyaputtiko, Maṇḍabbo, Bhātubbo. Gemination.

236. “Ṇavo [M 0.0208] pagvādīhī” ti ṇavo. Manuno apaccaṃ māṇavo.

236. By the rule, “Ṇavo pagvādīhī,” the suffix ṇavo is applied. The descendant of Manu is Māṇavo (a youth).

237. “Ṇera [M 0.0209] vidhavādito”ti ṇero, sāmaṇero.

237. By the rule, “Ṇera vidhavādito,” the suffix ṇero is applied, as in Sāmaṇero (a novice monk).

Saṃsaṭṭhādianekatthataddhita

Taddhita Suffixes with Various Meanings, such as 'Mixed With'

238. “Yena [M 0.0210] vā saṃsaṭṭhaṃ tarati carati vahati ṇiko”ti ṇiko. Vākārena nekatthenekapaccayā ca. Ghatena saṃsaṭṭho ghātiko, odano. Uḷūpena taratīti oḷūpiko, uḷūpiko vā, na pakkhe vuddhi.

238. By the rule, “Yena vā saṃsaṭṭhaṃ tarati carati vahati ṇiko,” the suffix ṇiko is applied. And by the word 'vā', there are many meanings and many suffixes. Mixed with ghee is Ghātiko, as in rice (odano). One who crosses by a raft is Oḷūpiko, or Uḷūpiko; in the alternative form, there is no vuddhi.

Sakaṭena caratīti sākaṭiko. Sīsena vahatīti sīsiko, na vuddhi.

He who travels by cart is Sākaṭiko. He who carries with the head is Sīsiko; there is no vuddhi.

Itthiliṅgato eyyako, ṇako ca. Campāyaṃ jāto campeyyako. Evaṃ bārāṇaseyyako. Ṇako – kusinārāyaṃ vasatīti kosinārako. Janapadato ṇako ca – magadhesu vasati, tesaṃ issaro vā māgadhako.

After a feminine stem, the suffixes eyyako and ṇako occur. Born in Campā is Campeyyako. Similarly, Bārāṇaseyyako. Regarding the suffix ṇako: one residing in Kusinārā is Kosinārako. After a country name, the suffix ṇako also occurs: one dwelling in Magadha, or their ruler, is Māgadhako.

Tajjātiyā [M 0.0212] visiṭṭhatthe ājānīyo. Assajātiyā visiṭṭho assājānīyo. Ño - agganti jānitabbaṃ aggaññaṃ, dvittaṃ.

In the sense of 'excellent of that kind', the suffix ājānīyo is applied. Excellent of the horse kind is Assājānīyo. Regarding the suffix ño: what is to be known as foremost is Aggaññaṃ; there is gemination.

239. Tamadhīte tena katādisannidhānaniyogasippabhaṇḍa jīvikatthesu ca.

239. And in the senses of 'he studies that', 'done by that', 'proximity', 'appointment', 'skill', 'merchandise', and 'livelihood'.

Taṃ [M 0.0213] adhīte iccādīsvatthesu ādisaddena hatādīsu ca ṇiko vā hoti. Abhidhammamadhīteti ābhidhammiko, abhidhammiko vā, na pakkhe vuddhi. Vacasā kataṃ kammaṃ vācasikaṃ. Evaṃ mānasikaṃ, ettha –

In the senses of 'he studies that', etc., and by the word 'ādi' (etc.), in senses such as 'killed', etc., the suffix ṇiko optionally occurs. One who studies the Abhidhamma is Ābhidhammiko, or Abhidhammiko; in the alternative form, there is no vuddhi. An action done by speech is Vācasikaṃ. Similarly, Mānasikaṃ. Here—

“Sa [M 0.0214] sare vāgamo”tīhānuvattitādisaddena sāgamo.

By the rule 'Sa sare vāgamo', and by the word 'ādi' which is carried over, there is an augment.

Sarīre sannidhānā vedanā sārīrikā. Dvāre niyutto dovāriko, ettha- “māyūnamāgamo ṭhāne”ti vakārato pubbe okārāgamo.

A feeling present in the body is Sārīrikā. One appointed at the door is Dovāriko. Here, by the rule, “māyūnamāgamo ṭhāne,” there is an augment of 'o' before the letter 'v'.

Sippanti [M 0.0215] gītādikalā, vīṇā assa sippanti veṇiko, atra vīṇeti vīṇāvādanaṃ. Gandho assa bhaṇḍanti gandhiko, mage hantvā jīvatīti māgaviko, vakārāgamo. Jālena hato jāliko, suttena baddho suttiko, cāpo assa āyudhanti cāpiko, vāto assa ābādho atthīti vā vātiko, buddhe pasanno buddhiko, vatthena kītaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ vatthikaṃ.

Sippa means art, such as singing and the like. The lute is his art—thus Veṇiko (a lute-player). Here, 'vīṇā' means lute-playing. Scent is his merchandise—thus Gandhiko (a perfumer). One who lives by killing deer is Māgaviko; there is an augment of 'v'. One killed by a net is Jāliko. One bound by a thread is Suttiko. The bow is his weapon—thus Cāpiko (an archer). Wind is his ailment, or he has it—thus Vātiko. One with faith in the Buddha is Buddhiko. Merchandise bought with cloth is Vatthikaṃ.

Kumbho [M 0.0217] assa parimāṇaṃ, ta marahati, tesaṃ rāsi vā kumbhiko. Akkhena dibbatīti akkhiko, magadhesu vasati, jātoti vā māgadhiko iccādi.

A pot is his measure, he is worthy of it, or it is a heap of them: Kumbhiko. One who plays with dice is Akkhiko. One who lives in Magadha or is born there is Māgadhiko, etc.

240. Ṇa rāgā tena rattaṃ tassedamaññatthesu ca.

240. The suffix ṇa occurs in the senses of 'from passion', 'colored by that', 'this is his', and in other meanings.

Tena [M 0.0218] rattaṃ tyādyatthesu ṇo vā hoti. Kasāvena rattaṃ kāsāvaṃ.

In the senses of 'colored by that', etc., the suffix ṇo optionally occurs. Colored by saffron (kasāvena rattaṃ) is Kāsāvaṃ.

Evaṃ [M 0.0219] nīlaṃ pītamiccādi. Na vuddhi, mahisassa idaṃ māhisaṃ, siṅgaṃ.

Similarly, Nīlaṃ, Pītaṃ, and so forth. There is no vuddhi. This of a buffalo is Māhisaṃ, for example, a horn (siṅgaṃ).

Evaṃ [M 0.0220] rājaporisaṃ, ettha “ayuvaṇṇānañcā” do puna vuddhiggahaṇena uttarapadassa vuddhi. Magadhehi āgato, tatra jāto, tesaṃ issaro, te assa nivāsoti vā māgadho, kattikādīhi yutto kattiko, māso.

Similarly, Rājaporisaṃ. Here, by the rule, “ayuvaṇṇānañcā...” and by the repeated mention of vuddhi, there is vuddhi of the latter member of the compound. One who has come from Magadha, or is born there, or is their ruler, or whose abode is among them, is Māgadho. Connected with the constellation Kattikā, etc., is Kattiko, for example, the month.

Buddho [M 0.0222] assa devatāti buddho. Byākaraṇaṃ avecca adhīteti veyyākaraṇo. Ettha “māyūnamā”dinā yakārato pubbe e āgamo, yassa dvittaṃ. Sagarehi nibbatto sāgaroiccādi.

The Buddha is his deity—thus Buddho. One who has studied and understood grammar is Veyyākaraṇo. Here, by the rule, “māyūnamā...” etc., there is an augment of 'e' before 'y', and the 'y' is doubled. Born of the Sagaras is Sāgaro, etc.

241. Jātādīnamimiyā ca.

241. And after 'jāta', etc., the suffixes ima and iya are applied.

Jātādīsu [M 0.0224] imo iyo ca hoti, casaddena kiyo ca. Pacchā jāto pacchimo, manussajātiyā jāto manussajātiyo. Ante niyutto antimo, antiyo.

In the case of 'jāta' and similar words, the suffixes ima and iya occur, and by the word 'ca', the suffix kiya also occurs. Born later is Pacchimo; born of the human species is Manussajātiyo. Appointed at the end is Antimo or Antiyo.

Evaṃ andhakiyo. Putto assa atthīti puttimo, puttiyo. Evaṃ kappiyo.

Similarly, Andhakiyo. He has a son—thus Puttimo or Puttiyo. Similarly, Kappiyo.

242. “Tadassaṭṭhānamīyo [M 0.0225] ce”ti īyo, cakārena hitādyatthepi, bandhanassa ṭhānaṃ bandhanīyaṃ, caṅkamanassa hitaṃ caṅkamanīyaṃ.

242. By the rule, “Tadassaṭṭhānamīyo ce,” the suffix īyo is applied. And by the word 'ca', it is also applied in the senses of 'beneficial', etc. The place for binding is Bandhanīyaṃ. That which is beneficial for walking is Caṅkamanīyaṃ.

243. “Ālu [M 0.0226] tabbahule”ti ālu. Abhijjhābahulo abhijjhālu.

243. By the rule, “Ālu tabbahule,” the suffix ālu is applied. One who abounds in covetousness is Abhijjhālu.

Visesataddhita

Special Taddhita Suffixes

244. Visese taratamissikiyiṭṭhā.

244. In the sense of distinction, the suffixes tara, tama, issika, iya, and iṭṭha are used.

Atisayatthe tarādayo honti.

The suffixes tara and so on occur in the sense of excellence.

Ayametesaṃ [M 0.0227] atisayena pāpoti pāpataro, pāpatamo, pāpissiko, pāpiyo, pāpiṭṭho vā.

‘This one is more evil than these’ becomes pāpataro, pāpatamo, pāpissiko, pāpiyo, or pāpiṭṭho.

“Vuddhassa [M 0.0228] jo iyiṭṭhesū”ti vuddhassa jādese – “saralopā”do pakatiggahaṇena pakatyabhāvā issa e. Jeyyo, jeṭṭho.

By the rule ‘For vuddha, ja before iya and iṭṭha,’ ja is substituted for vuddha. By ‘elision of a vowel, etc.,’ and because the original form is absent due to its being specified in the rule, the i becomes e. Hence, jeyyo, jeṭṭho.

Evaṃ “pasatthassa so ce”ti sādese seyyo, seṭṭho.

Similarly, by the rule ‘For pasattha, also sa,’ with the substitution of sa, the forms are seyyo, seṭṭho.

Assatthitaddhita

Possessive Derivational Suffixes

245. “Tadassatthīti [M 0.0230] vī ce”ti vī. Medhā assa atthīti medhāvī.

245. By the rule ‘In the sense “he has that,” also vī,’ the suffix vī is used. ‘He has wisdom’ (medhā assa atthi) becomes medhāvī.

246. Evaṃ [M 0.0231] “tapādito sī”ti sī, dvittaṃ, tapassī.

246. Similarly, by ‘After tapas etc., sī,’ the suffix sī is used, with doubling, forming tapassī.

247. “Daṇḍādito [M 0.0232] ika ī”ti iko, ī ca. Daṇḍiko, daṇḍī.

247. By ‘After daṇḍa etc., ika and ī,’ the suffixes ika and ī are used. Hence, daṇḍiko, daṇḍī.

248. “Guṇādito vantū”ti vantu. Guṇavā, paññavā. Yadādinā rasso.

248. By ‘After guṇa etc., vantu,’ the suffix vantu is used. Hence, guṇavā, paññavā. The vowel is shortened by a subsequent rule.

249. “Satyādīhi [M 0.0233] mantū”ti mantu. Satimā, bhānumā.

249. By ‘After sati etc., mantu,’ the suffix mantu is used. Hence, satimā, bhānumā.

250. “Āyussukārāsmantumhī”ti [M 0.0235] ussa asa. Āyasmā.

250. By ‘For āyus and stems ending in u, before mantu,’ us becomes as. Hence, āyasmā.

251. “Saddhādito ṇa” iti ṇo. Saddho.

251. By ‘After saddhā etc., ṇa,’ the suffix ṇa is used. Hence, saddho.

252. “Tappakativacane [M 0.0236] mayo”ti mayo. Suvaṇṇena pakataṃ sovaṇṇamayaṃ, suvaṇṇamayaṃ vā. Pakkhe - yadādinā vuddhi.

252. By ‘In the sense “made of that,” maya,’ the suffix maya is used. ‘Made of gold’ becomes sovaṇṇamayaṃ or suvaṇṇamayaṃ. In one case, vuddhi occurs by a subsequent rule.

Etesamo lope.

Regarding the elision of these.

Vibhattilope manādīnamantassa o hoti. Manomayaṃ.

When the case ending is elided, the final part of manas and so on becomes o. For example, manomayaṃ.

Saṅkhyātaddhita

Numerical Derivational Suffixes

253. Saṅkhyāpūraṇe [M 0.0237] tyadhikāro.

253. The governing rule for ordinal numbers.

“Dvitīhi tiyo”ti tiyo, “tiye dutāpi ce”ti dvitīnaṃ dutā. Dvinnaṃ pūraṇo dutiyo, evaṃ tatiyo.

By ‘After dvi and ti, tiya,’ the suffix tiya is used. By ‘Before tiya, also duta for dvi,’ duta is substituted for dvi. The ordinal of two (dvinnaṃ pūraṇo) is dutiyo; similarly, tatiyo.

254. “Catucchehi thaṭhā”ti thaṭhā. Catuttho, chaṭṭho.

254. By ‘After catu and cha, tha and ṭha,’ the suffixes tha and ṭha are used. Hence, catuttho, chaṭṭho.

255. Tesamaḍḍhūpapadena aḍḍhuḍḍha divaḍḍha diyaḍḍhāḍḍhatiyā.

255. For these, when preceded by aḍḍha, the forms are aḍḍhuḍḍha, divaḍḍha, diyaḍḍha, and aḍḍhatiya.

Catuttha [M 0.0238] dutiya tatiyānaṃ aḍḍhūpapadena saha aḍḍhuḍḍha divaḍḍha diyaḍḍhāḍḍhatiyā honti.

When catuttha, dutiya, and tatiya are compounded with a preceding aḍḍha, they become aḍḍhuḍḍha, divaḍḍha, diyaḍḍha, and aḍḍhatiya respectively.

Aḍḍhena catuttho aḍḍhuḍḍho, aḍḍhena dutiyo divaḍḍho, diyaḍḍho vā, aḍḍhena tatiyo aḍḍhatiyo.

Three and a half (aḍḍhena catuttho) is aḍḍhuḍḍho; one and a half (aḍḍhena dutiyo) is divaḍḍho or diyaḍḍho; two and a half (aḍḍhena tatiyo) is aḍḍhatiyo.

256. “Saṅkhyāpūraṇe [M 0.0239] mo”ti mo, pañcamo. Itthiyaṃ pañcannaṃ pūraṇī pañcamī.

256. By ‘In ordinals, ma,’ the suffix ma is used; hence, pañcamo. In the feminine, the ordinal of five (pañcannaṃ pūraṇī) is pañcamī.

Eko ca dasa cāti dvande kate –

When a dvanda compound is formed from ‘one and ten’—

Dvekaṭṭhānamākāro vā.

For dvi, eka, and aṭṭha, the final vowel optionally becomes ā.

Saṅkhyāne uttarapade dviekaaṭṭhaiccetesa mantassa ā vā hoti. Ekādasa pañcīva. Evaṃ dvādasa.

When a numeral is the final member of a compound, the final vowel of dvi, eka, and aṭṭha optionally becomes ā. For example, ekādasa. Similarly, dvādasa.

Yadādinā tissa teādese “ekādito dassa ra saṅkhyāne”ti dasasadde dassa ro. Terasa.

By a subsequent rule, te is substituted for ti. Then, by the rule ‘In numerals, after eka etc., ra for da,’ the da in the word dasa becomes ra. Hence, terasa.

257. “Catūpapadassa [M 0.0241] lopo tuttarapadādicassa cucopi navā”ti catusadde tussa lopo cassa cu ca. Cuddasa.

257. By the rule ‘For catu as first member, elision of tu...’, in the word catu, tu is elided and ca becomes cu. Hence, cuddasa.

“Dase so niccañce”ti chassa soādese – “ḷa darānaṃ”ti dasasadde dassa ḷo. Soḷasa, aṭṭhārasa.

By the rule ‘Before dasa, so is always the substitute for cha,’ so is substituted for cha. Then by ‘ḷa for da and ra,’ the da in the word dasa becomes ḷa. Hence, soḷasa. Also, aṭṭhārasa.

258. “Vīsati [M 0.0242] dasesu bā dvissa tū”ti dvissa bā. Bāvīsati, ekādasannaṃ pūraṇo ekādasamo.

258. By the rule ‘Before `vīsati` and `dasa`, `bā` for `dvi`...’, `bā` is substituted for `dvi`. Hence, `bāvīsati`. The ordinal of eleven (`ekādasannaṃ pūraṇo`) is `ekādasamo`.

259. “Ekādito dasassī”ti itthiyaṃ ī. Ekādasī iccādi.

259. By the rule ‘After the numbers from eleven onwards, `ī` in the feminine,’ the suffix `ī` is used in the feminine. Hence, `ekādasī`, and so on.

“Dvādito konekatthe ce”ti ko, dve parimāṇāni asseti dvikaṃ. Evaṃ tikādi.

By the rule ‘After `dvi` and so on, `ka` in the sense of a collection, and also...’, the suffix `ka` is used. A collection of two (`dve parimāṇāni assa`) is `dvikaṃ`. Similarly, `tika`, and so on.

260. “Samūhatthe [M 0.0243] kaṇṇā”ti kaṇa ca, ṇo ca. Manussānaṃ samūho mānussako, mānusso vā.

260. By the rule ‘In the sense of a group, `kaṇa` and `ṇa`’, the suffixes `kaṇa` and `ṇa` are used. A group of humans (`manussānaṃ samūho`) becomes `mānussako` or `mānusso`.

Ṇe kate – “jhalānamiyuvā sare vā” tīha vākārena issa ayādese – dvayaṃ, tayaṃ. Evaṃ “gāmajanabandhusahāyādīhi tā”ti tā. Gāmatā, nāgaratā.

When the suffix `ṇa` is applied, by the rule ‘jhalānamiyuvā sare vā,’ here, by the letter `v`, `aya` is substituted for `i`, resulting in `dvayaṃ` and `tayaṃ`. Similarly, by the rule ‘After `gāma`, `jana`, `bandhu`, `sahāya`, etc., `tā`’, the suffix `tā` is used. Hence, `gāmatā`, `nāgaratā`.

Bhāvataddhita

Abstract Derivational Suffixes

261. Ṇyattatā bhāve tu.

261. But in the sense of an abstract state, the suffixes `ṇya`, `tta`, and `tā` are used.

Bhāvatthe ṇyattatā honti. Tusaddena ttano ca. Sakatthādīsupi ṇyo, sakatthe tā ca.

In the sense of an abstract state, the suffixes `ṇya`, `tta`, and `tā` occur. By the word `tu` in the previous rule, `ttana` is also included. The suffix `ṇya` is also used in the sense of ‘one’s own,’ etc., and `tā` is also used in the sense of ‘one’s own’.

262.

All case endings following an avyayībhāva compound not ending in -a are elided. For example, adhitthi. The meaning of the case ending is location, etc.

Hontyasmā [M 0.0245] saddañāṇāni,

Bhāvo sā saddavuttiyā;

Nimittabhūtaṃ nāmañca,

Jāti dabbaṃ kriyā guṇo.

From which knowledge of words arises, that is the state (`bhāva`), by the function of the word. And the name becomes the ground for it, which may be species, substance, action, or quality.

263. Yathā [M 0.0246] – candassa bhāvo candattaṃ. Iha nāmavasā candasaddo candaddabbe vattate, nimittassa rūpānugatañca ñāṇaṃ. Evaṃ manussattanti manussajātivasā. Yadādinā īssa rasse – daṇḍittanti daṇḍaddabbasambandhā. Pācakattanti pacanakriyāsambandhā. Nīlattanti nīlaguṇavasā.

263. For example, the state of the moon (`candassa bhāvo`) is `candattaṃ`. Here, by the power of the name, the word `canda` refers to the substance ‘moon,’ and knowledge conforms to the form of that ground. Similarly, `manussattaṃ` is based on the human species. By a subsequent rule, with shortening of `ī`, `daṇḍittaṃ` is from the connection with the substance ‘staff.’ `Pācakattaṃ` is from the connection with the action of cooking. `Nīlattaṃ` is based on the quality of blueness.

Evaṃ ṇyādīsupi yathāyogaṃ ñeyyaṃ. Ṇyo.

Thus it should be understood appropriately in the case of `ṇya` and the other suffixes as well. The Suffix `Ṇya`.

Avaṇṇo ye lopañca.

The `a`-vowel is elided before `ya`, and also...

Ye [M 0.0247] pare avaṇṇo lupyate, cakārena ikāropi.

When `ya` follows, the `a`-vowel is elided; by the word `ca` in the rule, the vowel `i` is also elided.

Yavataṃ talaṇadakārānaṃ byañjanāni calaña jakāratta”nti yakārayuttānaṃ tādīnaṃ cādayo, kāraggahaṇena sakapabhamādito parayakārassa pubbena saha kvaci pubbarūpañca, dvittaṃ. Paṇḍiccaṃ, kosallaṃ, sāmaññaṃ, sohajjaṃ, porissaṃ, nepakkaṃ, sāruppaṃ, osabbhaṃ, opammaṃ.

By the rule ‘For `t`, `l`, `ṇ`, `d` followed by `y`, the consonants become `c`, `l`, `ñ`, `j`...’, for `t`, etc., joined with `y`, the substitutes are `c`, etc. By the inclusion of `kāra`, sometimes the `y` following `s`, `k`, `p`, `bh`, `m` is assimilated to the preceding consonant, and there is doubling. Examples: `paṇḍiccaṃ`, `kosallaṃ`, `sāmaññaṃ`, `sohajjaṃ`, `porissaṃ`, `nepakkaṃ`, `sāruppaṃ`, `osabbhaṃ`, `opammaṃ`.

Āttañca.

And `ā` is the substitute.

Iuiccetesaṃ ā hoti, rikārāgamo ca ṭhāne.

For `i` and `u`, `ā` is the substitute, and the augment `ri` is inserted in its place.

Saralopādinā ilopo. Isino bhāvo.

By the rule ‘elision of a vowel, etc.,’ the vowel `i` is elided. The state of a seer (`isino bhāvo`)...

Ārissaṃ[M 0.0248]. Evaṃ mudutā, arahatā, ntassa yadādinā lopo.

...becomes `ārissaṃ`. Similarly, `mudutā`, `arahatā`. The ending `-nt` is elided by a subsequent rule.

Puthujjanattanaṃ, akiñcanameva ākiñcaññaṃ, kuṇḍaniyā apaccaṃ koṇḍañño, ettha vuddhādo vākārena saṃyogantassāpi vuddhi.

`Puthujjanattanaṃ`. Nothingness (`akiñcanaṃ`) itself becomes `ākiñcaññaṃ`. The descendant of Kuṇḍanī (`kuṇḍaniyā apaccaṃ`) is `koṇḍañño`. Here, by a rule beginning with `vuddhi`, by the word `vā`, `vuddhi` occurs even for a stem ending in a conjunct consonant.

Padāya [M 0.0250] hitaṃ pajjaṃ, dhanāyaṃ saṃvattanikaṃ dhaññaṃ, satito sambhūtaṃ saccaṃ, ilopo, tīsu na vuddhi. Devo eva devatā.

Beneficial for the foot (`padāya hitaṃ`) is `pajjaṃ`. Conducive to wealth (`dhanāya saṃvattanikaṃ`) is `dhaññaṃ`. Born from mindfulness (`satito sambhūtaṃ`) is `saccaṃ`. There is elision of `i`. In these three cases, there is no `vuddhi`. A god (`devo`) itself is a deity (`devatā`).

264. “Ṇa [M 0.0251] visamādīhī”ti bhāve ṇo. Vesamaṃ. Ujuno bhāvo ajjavaṃ. Ettha ussa ātte parūkārassa yadādinā avo.

264. By the rule ‘`ṇa` after `visama`, etc.,’ the suffix `ṇa` is used in the abstract sense. Hence, `vesamaṃ`. The state of being straight (`ujuno bhāvo`) is `ajjavaṃ`. Here, when the initial `u` is substituted, the following `u` becomes `ava` by a subsequent rule.

265. “Ramaṇīyādito [M 0.0252] kaṇti kaṇa. Mānaññakaṃ.

265. By the rule ‘After `ramaṇīya`, etc., `kaṇ`’, the suffix `kaṇa` is used. Hence, `mānaññakaṃ`.

Abyayataddhita

Indeclinable Derivational Suffixes

266. “Vibhāge [M 0.0254] dhā ce”ti dhā, cakārena soppaccayo ca. Ekena vibhāgena ekadhā, nipātattā silopo. Padavibhāgena padaso.

266. By the rule ‘In the sense of division, `dhā`, and also...’, the suffix `dhā` is used; and by the word `ca`, the suffix `so` also. By one division, `ekadhā`; due to being an indeclinable, the case ending is elided. By division of words, `padaso`.

267. “Sabbanāmehi pakāravacane tu thā”ti thā. Tukārena thattā ca. Sabbo pakāro, sabbena pakārena vā sabbathā. Evaṃ aññathattā.

267. By the rule ‘After pronouns, in the sense of manner, `thā`...’, the suffix `thā` is used. And by the word `tu`, `thattā` is also formed. `Sabbathā` means all kinds, or by all kinds. Similarly, `aññathattā`.

268. “Kimimehi [M 0.0255] tha”nti thaṃ, kādese-kathaṃ. Iādese-itthaṃ, thanti yogavibhāgena thaṃ-bahutthaṃ.

268. By the rule ‘After `kiṃ` and `ima`, `thaṃ`’, the suffix `thaṃ` is used. When `ka` is the substitute for `kiṃ`, it becomes `kathaṃ`. When `i` is the substitute for `ima`, it becomes `itthaṃ`. By splitting the rule at `thaṃ`, the form `bahutthaṃ` is derived.

269. Amalinaṃ malinaṃ karotītyādyatthe-abhūtatabbhāve gamyamāne karabhūyoge sati nāmato yadādinā īppaccayo, malinīkaroti setaṃ. Abhasmano bhasmano karaṇanti bhasmīkaraṇaṃ kaṭṭhassa. Amalino malino bhavatīti malinībhavati seto. Īppaccayantopi nipāto. Abhūtatabbhāveti kiṃ, ghaṭaṃ karoti, ghaṭo bhavati.

269. In the sense of ‘making the unblemished blemished,’ etc., when the state of becoming what was not before is understood, and there is connection with the roots `kar` or `bhū`, the suffix `ī` is applied after a noun stem by a subsequent rule. For example, `malinīkaroti setaṃ` (he makes the white blemished). `Bhasmīkaraṇaṃ kaṭṭhassa` (reducing wood to ashes) means ‘the making of what is not ashes into ashes.’ `Malinībhavati setaṃ` (the white becomes blemished) means ‘the unblemished becomes blemished.’ A word ending with the `ī` suffix is also an indeclinable. Why the condition `abhūtatabbhāve` (in the state of becoming what was not before)? Consider `ghaṭaṃ karoti` (he makes a pot) and `ghaṭo bhavati` (it becomes a pot).

Karabhūyogeti [M 0.0256] kiṃ, amalino malino jāyate.

Why the condition `karabhūyoge` (in connection with `kar` or `bhū`)? Consider `amalino malino jāyate` (the unblemished becomes blemished), which is formed without the `ī` suffix.

Avatthāvatovatthayā, bhūtassaññāya vatthuno.

Of a thing, from a non-state to a state, which is understood as having become different,

Tāyāvatthāya bhavanaṃ, abhūtatabbhavaṃ viduṃ.

the becoming of that state, they know as `abhūtatabbhāva`.
