Dāṭhāvaṃso [O 0.0001]

The Chronicle of the Tooth Relic

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

1.

1.

Visāradaṃ vādapathāti vattinaṃ,

Tilokapajjotamasayhasāhinaṃ;

Asesa ñeyyāvaraṇappahāyinaṃ,

Namāmi satthāramanantagocaraṃ.

I bow to the Teacher of boundless range, expert in the path of discourse, the light of the three worlds, the unassailable conqueror, who has abandoned all obstructions to the knowable.

2. [O 0.0002]

2.

Tiloka nāthappabhavaṃ bhayāpahaṃ,

Visuddhavijjācaraṇehi sevitaṃ;

Papañca saṃyojanabandhanacchidaṃ,

Namāmi dhammaṃ nipuṇaṃ sududdasaṃ.

Originating from the Lord of the Three Worlds, dispeller of fear, served by those with pure knowledge and conduct, cutter of the bonds of proliferation and fetters, I bow to the Dhamma, subtle and extremely hard to see.

3.

3.

Pasādamattena’pi yattha pāṇino,

Phūsanti dukkhakkhayamaccutaṃ padaṃ;

Tamāhuṇeyyaṃ susamāhitindriyaṃ,

Namāmi saṅghaṃ munirājasāvakaṃ.

I bow to the Sangha, disciples of the King of Sages, who are worthy of offerings, with faculties well-composed, through whom living beings, even with mere faith, touch the deathless state, the end of suffering.

4.

4.

Vibhusayaṃ kāḷakanāgaranva yaṃ,

Parakkamo kāruṇiko camūpati;

Gavesamāno jinasāsanassa yo,

Virūḷhimatthañca janassa patthayaṃ.

Adorning the lineage of the city of Kāḷaka, the valiant, compassionate commander, seeking the growth of the Victor's Teaching and desiring the welfare of the people.

5. [O 0.0003]

5.

Sudhāmayūkhāmala paṇḍuvaṃsajaṃ,

Virūḷhasaddhaṃ munirājasāsane;

Piyaṃ vadaṃ nītipathānuvattiniṃ,

Sadā pajānaṃ janikaṃ’va mātaraṃ.

Born of the Pāṇḍu lineage, pure as a moonbeam, with flourishing faith in the Dispensation of the King of Sages; speaking pleasantly, following the path of wise conduct, always like a mother to her people.

6.

6.

Piyaṃ parakkantibhujassa rājino,

Mahesi maccunnatakhuddhisampadaṃ;

Vidhāya līlāvatimicchitatthadaṃ,

Asesa laṅkātalarajjalakkhiyaṃ.

The beloved queen of the king who strives with his arms, the great queen whose wealth of virtues humbled the pride of death; having established Līlāvatī, the granter of desires, in the sovereignty over the entire realm of Laṅkā.

7.

7.

Kumāramārādhitasādhumantinaṃ,

Mahādayaṃ paṇḍunarinda vaṃsajaṃ;

Vidhāya saddhaṃ madhurindanāmakaṃ,

Susikkhītaṃ pāvacane kalāsu ca.

The youth, esteemed as a virtuous and wise counselor, greatly compassionate, born in the lineage of the Pāṇḍu king; he, named Madhurinda, having established faith, was well-trained in the Dispensation and the arts.

8. [O 0.0004]

8.

Narindasuññaṃ suciraṃ tisīhaḷaṃ,

Itippatītaṃ ayasaṃ apānudi;

Ciraṃ paṇītena ca civarādinā,

Susaññate saṃyamino atappayi.

When the three parts of Sinhala were long without a ruler, he dispelled the disgrace that had thus arisen; and for a long time, with excellent robes and other requisites, he satisfied the well-restrained ascetics.

9.

9.

Ciraṭṭhitiṃ pāvacanassa icchatā,

Kataññunā vikkama buddisālinā;

Satīmatā candima bandhukittinā,

Sagāravaṃ tena’bhīyācito ahaṃ.

Desiring the long-standing of the Dispensation, grateful, energetic, and wise, mindful, famed among his kinsmen like the moon—by him I was reverently entreated.

10.

10.

Sadesa bhāsāya kavīhi sīhaḷe,

Katampi vaṃsaṃ jinadantadhātuyā;

Niruttiyā māgadhikāya vuddhiyā,

Karomi dīpantaravāsinaṃ api.

Although a chronicle of the Jina's Tooth Relic has been composed by Sinhala poets in their native tongue, I shall compose it in the Māgadhan language for its promotion and for the benefit of those dwelling on other islands.

11. [O 0.0005]

11.

Jīno’yamiddhe amaravhaye pure,

Kadāci hutvāna sumedhanāmako;

Savedavedaṅgavibhāgakovido,

Mahaddhane vippakulamhi māṇavo.

This Jina, in the prosperous city called Amaravatī, was once a youth named Sumedha; an expert in the divisions of the Vedas and their auxiliaries, a young man from a wealthy Brahmin family.

12.

12.

Ahañhi jātibyasanena pīḷito,

Jarābhibhuto maraṇena otthaṭo;

Sivaṃ padaṃ jātijarādinissaṭaṃ,

Gavesayissaṃ’ti raho vicintiya.

“For I am afflicted by the calamity of birth, overwhelmed by old age, and beset by death; I will seek the blissful state, freed from birth, old age, and the like”—thus he reflected in solitude.

13.

13.

Anekasaṅkhaṃ dhanadhaññasampadaṃ,

Patiṭṭhapetvā kapaṇesi duccajaṃ;

Anappake pemabharānubandhino,

Vihāya mitte ca suteca bandhave.

His immeasurable fortune of wealth and grain, difficult for misers to relinquish, he set aside; his many friends, children, and relatives, bound by the weight of affection, he abandoned.

14. [O 0.0006]

14.

Pahāya kāme nikhile manorame,

Gharābhinikkhamma himācalantike;

Mahīdharaṃ dhammikanāma vissutaṃ,

Upecca nānātarurājibhusitaṃ.

Having abandoned all delightful sensual pleasures, having gone forth from home to the vicinity of the Himalayas, he approached the mountain renowned by the name Dhammika, adorned with rows of various splendid trees.

15.

15.

Manonukūle surarājanimmīte,

Asammigānaṃ agatimhi assame;

Nivattacīro ajinakkhipaṃ vahaṃ,

Jaṭādharo tāpasa vesamaggahī.

In a hermitage pleasing to the mind, created by the king of gods, a place inaccessible to beasts; wearing bark garments, carrying a deer-skin, and with matted hair, he assumed the appearance of an ascetic.

16.

16.

Susaññatattoparivāri tindriyo,

Phalāphalādīhi pavattayaṃ tanuṃ;

Gato abhiñgñāsu ca pāramiṃ vasī,

Tahiṃ samāpatti sukhaṃ avindi so.

With self well-restrained and senses guarded, he sustained his body with various fruits; having attained mastery and perfection in the super-knowledges, there he found the bliss of meditative attainments.

17. [O 0.0007]

17.

Susajjite rammapurādhivāsinā,

Mahājanena’ttamanena añjase;

Pathappadese abhiyantamattano,

Aniṭṭhiteyeva sumedha tāpaso.

While a section of the path was still unfinished, being prepared by the delighted populace of the well-adorned city of Ramma for the one who was approaching, Sumedha the ascetic saw him.

18.

18.

Agādhañeyyodadhīpāradassinaṃ,

Bhavantaguṃ nibbanathaṃ vināyakaṃ;

Anekakhīṇāsavalakkhasevitaṃ,

Kadā ci dīpaṅkarabuddhamaddasa.

Then he saw the Buddha Dīpaṅkara, the leader who sees the further shore of the unfathomable ocean of the knowable, who has gone to the end of becoming, who is free from craving, attended by hundreds of thousands whose taints are destroyed.

19.

19.

Tato sasaṅghassa tilokabhattuno,

Pariccajitvāna tanumpi jīvitaṃ;

Pasārayitvāna jaṭājinādikaṃ,

Vidhāya setuṃ tanumeva pallale.

Then, for the Lord of the three worlds and his Sangha, renouncing even his body and life, he spread out his matted hair, deer-skin, and the like, making a bridge with his own body over the mud.

20. [O 0.0008]

20.

Anakkamitvā kalalaṃ mahādaye,

Sabhikkhuko gaccatu piṭṭhiyā iti;

Adhiṭṭhahitvāna nipannako tahiṃ,

Anātha metaṃ tibhavaṃ samekkhiya.

Having resolved, “May the Greatly Compassionate One with his monks walk over my back, not treading in the mud,” he lay down there, reflecting that this triple world is without a protector.

21.

21.

Dayāya sañcoditamānaso jane,

Bhavaṇṇavā uddharituṃ dukhaddite;

Akāsi sambodhipadassa pattiyā,

Mahābhinīhāramudaggavikkamo.

With his mind impelled by compassion for beings, to rescue those afflicted by suffering from the ocean of becoming, he, with uplifted vigor, made the great resolve for the attainment of the state of complete enlightenment.

22. [O 0.0009]

22.

Atho viditvā vasino tamāsayaṃ,

Adāsi so vyākaraṇaṃ mahāmuni;

Tato puraṃ tamhi tathāgate gate,

Sayaṃvasī sammasi pāramiguṇe.

Then, the Great Sage, the Master, knowing his intention, gave him the prophecy. Thereafter, when the Tathāgata had entered the city, the self-controlled Sumedha reflected on the qualities of the perfections.

23.

23.

Tato ca kappānamalīnavikkamo,

Asaṃkhiye so caturo salakkhake;

Tahiṃ tahiṃ jātisu bodhipācane,

Visuddhasambhāraguṇe apūrayi;

Athābhijāto tusite mahāyaso,

Visuddhasambodhipadopaladdhiyā;

Udikkhamāno samayaṃ dayādhano,

Ciraṃ vibhutiṃ anubhosi sabbaso.

Then, with unfading vigor, for four incalculable and one hundred thousand aeons, in various births, for the ripening of enlightenment, he fulfilled the qualities of pure preparation. Then, born in Tusita, greatly renowned, rich in compassion, awaiting the time for the attainment of the state of pure complete enlightenment, he enjoyed complete prosperity for a long time.

25. [O 0.0010]

25.

Sahassasaṅkhā dasacakkavālato,

Samāgatānekasurādhipādihi;

Udaggudaggehi jinattapattiyā,

Sagāravaṃ so abhigamma yācito.

He was reverently approached and requested by many assembled divine lords and others, numbering in the thousands, from ten world-systems, who, with greatly uplifted hearts, desired his attainment of the state of a Conqueror.

26.

26.

Tato cavitvā kapiḷavhaye pure,

Sadā sato sakyakulekaketuno;

Ahosi suddhodanabhumibhattuno,

Mahādimāyāya mahesiyā suto.

Then, having passed away from there, in the city called Kapila, he was born as the son of King Suddhodana and Queen Mahāmāyā, ever mindful, the sole banner of the Sākya clan.

27. [O 0.0011]

27.

Vijātamatto’va vasundharāya so,

Patiṭṭhahitvāna disā vilokayī;

Tadā ahesuṃ vivaṭaṅganā disā,

Apūjayuṃ tattha ca devamānusā.

As soon as he was born, he stood firmly on the earth and looked to the directions. Then the directions became unobstructed, and gods and humans paid homage there.

28.

28.

Ādhārayuṃ ātapavāraṇādikaṃ,

Adissamānāva nabhambhi devatā;

Padāni so satta ca uttarāmukho,

Upecca nicchārayi vācamāsabhiṃ.

Unseen in the sky, deities held up sunshades and other such things. And he, facing north and taking seven steps, uttered a noble speech.

29.

29.

Yathatthasiddhattakumāranāmako,

Mahabbalo yobbanahāriviggaho;

Ututtayānucchavikesu tīsu so,

Nubhosi pāsādavaresu sampadaṃ.

Named Prince Siddhartha, “He Whose Purpose Is Fulfilled,” of great strength and a form charming in its youth, he enjoyed the prosperity of three choice palaces suited to the three seasons.

30. [O 0.0012]

30.

Kadāci uyyānapathe jarāhataṃ,

Tathāturaṃ kālakatañca saṃyamiṃ;

Kamena disvāna virattamānaso,

Bhavesu so pabbajituṃ akāmayi.

Once, on a garden path, he saw one afflicted by old age, a sick person, a corpse, and an ascetic. Seeing these in order, his mind grew dispassionate, and he desired to go forth from worldly existence.

31.

31.

Sapupphadīpādīkarehi rattiyaṃ,

Purakkhato so tidivādhivāsihi;

Sachannako kantakavājiyānato,

Tato mahākāruṇiko’bhinikkhami.

Then at night, honored by the dwellers of heaven with flower-lamps and other offerings, the Great Compassionate One, accompanied by Channa, went forth from the chariot of the horse Kaṇṭhaka.

32.

32.

Kamena patvāna anomamāpagaṃ,

Sudhotamuttāphalahārisekate;

Patiṭṭhahitvā varamoḷibandhanaṃ,

Sitāsilūnaṃ gagane samukkhipī.

In due course, having reached the Anomā River, he stood upon its sandbanks, charming with well-washed pearls and crystals. Having cut his excellent top-knot with a sharp sword, he cast it into the sky.

33. [O 0.0013]

33.

Paṭiggahetvā tidasānamissaro,

Suvaṇnacaṅgoṭavarena taṃ tadā;

Tiyojanaṃ nīlamaṇīhi cetiyaṃ,

Akāsi cūḷāmaṇimattano pure.

Then the lord of the Thirty Gods, having received it in an excellent golden casket, built in his own city the Cūḷāmaṇi Cetiya, a shrine three leagues high, made with blue gems.

34.

34.

Tato ghaṭīkārasarojayoninā,

Samāhaṭaṃ dhārayī cīvarādikaṃ;

Atho sakaṃ vatthayugaṃ nabhatthale,

Pasatthavesaggahaṇo samukkhipī.

Then he put on the robes and other requisites brought by Ghaṭīkāra, the lotus-born Brahmā. Then, praised for adopting the excellent guise, he cast his own pair of garments into the sky.

35.

35.

Paṭiggahetvāna tamambujāsano,

Mahiddhiko bhattibharena codito;

Sake bhave dvādasayojanaṃ akā,

Maṇīhi nīlādihi dussacetiyaṃ.

Then the mighty, lotus-seated Brahmā, urged by the fullness of his devotion, received it and in his own realm constructed a twelve-league shrine for the garments, adorned with blue and other gems.

36. [O 0.0014]

36.

Susaññatatto satimā jitindriyo,

Vinītaveso rasagedhavajjito;

Chahāyanāneva anomavikkamo,

Mahāpadhānaṃ padahittha dukkaraṃ.

Well-restrained, mindful, with senses subdued, wearing humble attire and devoid of craving for flavors, for six years, the one of unparalleled prowess undertook the great and arduous striving.

37.

37.

Visākhamāsassatha puṇṇamāsiyaṃ,

Upecca mūlaṃ sahajāya bodhiyā;

Tiṇāsane cuddasahatthasammite,

Adhiṭṭhahitvā vīriyaṃ nisajji so.

Then, on the full-moon day of the month of Vesākha, he approached the foot of the Bodhi tree. On a seat of grass measuring fourteen cubits, he sat down, having made a firm resolution with his energy.

38.

38.

Avattharantiṃ vasudhaṃca ambaraṃ,

Virūpavesaggahaṇena bhiṃsanaṃ;

Pakampayanto sadharādharaṃ mahiṃ,

Jino padose’jini māravāhiṇiṃ.

At dusk, the Conqueror vanquished Māra's army, which covered the earth and sky, was terrifying in its assumption of misshapen forms, and made the earth with its mountains tremble.

39. [O 0.0015]

39.

Surāsurabrahmagaṇehi sajjite,

Jagattaye pupphamayagaghikādinā;

Pavattamāne suradundubhissare,

Abujjhibodhiṃ rajanīparikkhaye.

When the three worlds were adorned by hosts of devas, asuras, and Brahmās with flower-canopies and the like, and the sound of celestial drums resounded, he awakened to enlightenment at the end of the night.

40.

40.

Tadā pakampiṃsu saselakānanā,

Sahassasaṃkhā dasalokadhātuyo;

Agañji so loṇapayodhi sādhutaṃ,

Mahāvabhāso bhuvanesu pattharī.

Then ten thousand world-systems, with their mountains and forests, trembled. The salt ocean roared its approval, and a great radiance spread throughout the worlds.

41.

41.

Labhiṃsu andhā vimale vilocane,

Suṇiṃsu sadde badhirāpi jātiyā;

Lapiṃsu mūgā vacanena vaggunā,

Cariṃsu khelaṃ padasā’va paṅgulā.

The blind obtained spotless eyes; the deaf, even those deaf from birth, heard sounds; the mute spoke with eloquent speech; and the crippled walked nimbly on their own feet.

42.

42.

Bhaviṃsu khujjā ujusommaviggahā,

Sikhī’pi nibbāyi avīciādisu;

Apāgamuṃ bandhanato’pi jantavo,

Khudādikaṃ petabhavā apakkamī.

The hunchbacked became straight with graceful forms. Even the fires in Avīci and other hells were extinguished. Beings were freed even from bondage, and hunger and the like departed from the realm of the hungry ghosts.

43. [O 0.0016]

43.

Samiṃsu rogavyasanāni pāṇinaṃ,

Bhayaṃ tiraccānagate na pīḷayī;

Janā ahesuṃ sakhilā piyaṃvadā,

Pavattayuṃ koñcanadaṃ mataṅgajā.

The diseases and calamities of living beings subsided; fear from animals no longer oppressed them. People became friendly and spoke kindly, and elephants trumpeted with the cry of the curlew.

44.

44.

Hayā ca hesiṃsu pahaṭṭhamānasā,

Nadiṃsu sabbā sayameva dundubhī;

Raviṃsu dehābharaṇāni pāṇinaṃ,

Disā pasīdiṃsu samā samantato.

Horses neighed with delighted minds; all kettledrums sounded by themselves; the body ornaments of living beings tinkled; and the directions on all sides became serene and calm.

45.

45.

Pavāyi mando sukhasītamāruto,

Pavassi megho’pi akālasambavo;

Jahiṃsu ākāsagatiṃ vihaṅgamā,

Mahiṃ samubhijjajalaṃ samuṭṭhahī.

A gentle, pleasant, and cool breeze blew; an unseasonal cloud also rained; birds abandoned their flight in the sky; and water welled up, breaking through the earth.

46.

46.

Asandamānā’va ṭhitā savantiyo,

Nabhe virociṃsu asesajotiyo;

Bhavā ahesuṃ vivaṭā samantato,

Janassa nāsuṃ vacanūpapattiyo.

The flowing rivers stood as if unmoving; all the lights in the sky shone forth; the realms of existence were opened up on all sides; and for the people, the power of speech was lost.

47. [O 0.0017]

47.

Samekkhataṃ nāvaraṇā nagādayo,

Pavāyi gandho api dibbasammato;

Dumā ahesuṃ phalapuppha dhārino,

Ahosi channo kamalehi aṇṇavo.

Mountains and the like were no obstruction to sight; a fragrance wafted, one considered divine; trees became bearers of fruit and flowers; and the ocean was covered with lotuses.

48.

48.

Thalesu toyesu ca pupphamānakā,

Vicittapupphā vikasiṃsu sabbathā;

Nirantaraṃ pupphasugandhavuṭṭhiyā,

Ahosi sabbaṃ vasudhambarannaraṃ.

On land and in water, variegated flowers bloomed everywhere; with a continuous shower of fragrant flowers, the entire world of earth, sky, and humanity was covered.

49.

49.

Nisajja pallaṅkavare tahiṃ jino,

Sukhaṃ samāpattivihārasambhavaṃ;

Tato’nubhonto sucirābhipatthitaṃ,

Dināni satteva atikkamāpayī.

Seated there on the excellent cross-legged seat, the Conqueror, experiencing the long-desired bliss born of dwelling in meditative attainment, thus passed seven days.

50. [O 0.0018]

50.

Samuppatitvā gagaṇaṅganaṃ tato,

Padassayitvā yamakaṃ mahāmuni;

Sapāṭihīraṃ tidivādhivāsinaṃ,

Jinattane saṃsayitaṃ nirākari.

Then, having risen up into the expanse of the sky, the Great Sage, by displaying the wondrous Twin Miracle, dispelled the doubt of the dwellers of heaven concerning his attainment of Buddhahood.

51.

51.

Athotaritvāna jayāsanassa so,

Ṭhito’va pubbuttarakaṇṇanissito;

Dināni sattānimisena cakkhunā,

Tamāsanaṃ bodhitaruṃca’pūjayi.

Then, having descended from the seat of victory, he stood near the northeast corner and for seven days, with unblinking eyes, venerated that seat and the Bodhi tree.

52.

52.

Atha’ntarāḷe maṇicaṅkame jino,

Ṭhitappadesassa ca āsanassa ca;

Mahārahe devavarābhinimmite,

Dināni satteva akāsi vaṅkamaṃ.

Then, for seven days, the Conqueror paced upon the magnificent jeweled walkway, created by the best of devas, in the space between the seat and the place where he had stood.

53. [O 0.0019]

53.

Tato disāyaṃ aparāya bodhiyā,

Upāvisitvā ratanālaye jino;

Samantapaṭṭhānanayaṃ vicintayaṃ,

Dināni satteva savītināmayi.

Then, in the western direction from the Bodhi tree, the Conqueror sat in the jeweled abode and, reflecting upon the method of the Conditional Relations, spent seven days.

54.

54.

Viniggato satthusarīrato tadā,

Jutippabandho paṭibandhavajjito;

Pamāṇasuññāsu ca lokadhātusu,

Samantato uddhamadho ca pattharī.

Then from the Teacher’s body, a continuous, unimpeded stream of radiance went forth, spreading above, below, and all around throughout the immeasurable world-systems.

55.

55.

Vaṭassa mūle ajapālasaññino,

Sukhaṃ phusanto pavivekasambhavaṃ;

Vināyako satta vihāsi vāsare,

Anantadassī surarājapūjito.

At the foot of the Banyan tree known as the Goatherd's, the Guide, the All-seeing One, revered by the king of devas, dwelt for seven days, experiencing the bliss born of seclusion.

56. [O 0.0020]

56.

Vihāsi mūle mucalindasākhino,

Nisajja bhogāvalimandirodare;

Vikiṇṇapupphe mucalindabhogino,

Samādhinā vāsarasattakaṃ jino.

The Conqueror dwelt at the foot of the Mucalinda tree, seated for a week of days in meditative absorption within the palace of the coils of the Nāga king Mucalinda, amidst scattered flowers.

57.

57.

Dume’pi rājāyatane samādhinā,

Vihāsi rattindiva sattakaṃ muni;

Sahassanetto atha dantapoṇakaṃ,

Mukhodakañcāpi adāsi satthuno.

The Sage also dwelt in meditation at the Rājāyatana tree for a week of days and nights. Then the Thousand-Eyed One gave a tooth-cleaner and water for the mouth to the Teacher.

58.

58.

Tato mahārājavarehi ābhataṃ,

Silāmayaṃ patta catukkamekakaṃ;

Vidhāya matthaṃ madhupiṇḍikaṃ tahiṃ,

Paṭiggahetvāna savāṇijāhaṭā.

Then, having made the four stone bowls brought by the excellent Great Kings into a single bowl, he accepted into it the honey-cake brought by the merchants.

59. [O 0.0021]

59.

Katannakicco saraṇesu te ubho,

Patiṭṭhapetvāna tapassubhallike;

Adāsi tesaṃ abhipūjituṃ sakaṃ,

Parāmasitvāna siraṃ siroruhe.

Having finished his meal, and having established the two, Tapassu and Bhallika, in the refuges, he touched his head and gave them his own hair for them to worship.

60.

60.

Vaṭassamule ajapālasaññino,

Sahampatībrahmavarena yācito;

Janassa kātuṃ varadhammasaṅgahaṃ,

Agañchi bārāṇasimekako muni.

At the foot of the Banyan tree, known as the Goatherd's, requested by the excellent Brahmā Sahampati to provide for the welfare of the people with the sublime Dhamma, the solitary Sage went to Bārāṇasī.

61. [O 0.0022]

61.

Gantvā so dhammarājā vanamisipatanaṃ saññatānaṃ niketaṃ,

Pallaṅkasmiṃ nisinno tahimavicalitaṭṭhānasampāditamhi;

Āsāḷhe puṇṇamāyaṃ sitaruciruciyā jotite cakkavāḷe,

Devabrahmādikānaṃ duritamalaharaṃ vattayi dhammacakkaṃ.

Having gone to the forest of Isipatana, the abode of the self-controlled, that King of Dhamma sat upon a seat established in an unshakeable state.
