Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Aṅguttaranikāyo

The Numerical Discourses

Ekakanipātapāḷi

The Book of the Ones

1. Rūpādivaggo

1. The Chapter on Forms, etc.

1. Evaṃ [T 1.0001][V 1.0001][P 1.0001][M 1.0001] me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – “bhikkhavo”ti. “Bhadante”ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ. Bhagavā etadavoca –

1. Thus have I heard. At one time the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika's monastery. There the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: “Bhikkhus!” “Venerable sir,” those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One. The Blessed One said this:

“Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekarūpampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, itthirūpaṃ. Itthirūpaṃ, bhikkhave, purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

“Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single form that so overpowers and persists in a man’s mind as this: the form of a woman. The form of a woman, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a man’s mind.” First.

2. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekasaddampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya [V 1.0002] tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, itthisaddo. Itthisaddo, bhikkhave, purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

2. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single sound that so overpowers and persists in a man’s mind as this: the sound of a woman. The sound of a woman, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a man’s mind.” Second.

3. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekagandhampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, itthigandho. Itthigandho, bhikkhave, purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Tatiyaṃ.

3. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single scent that so overpowers and persists in a man’s mind as this: the scent of a woman. The scent of a woman, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a man’s mind.” Third.

4. “Nāhaṃ[T 1.0002][P 1.0002], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekarasampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, itthiraso. Itthiraso, bhikkhave, purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Catutthaṃ.

4. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single taste that so overpowers and persists in a man’s mind as this: the taste of a woman. The taste of a woman, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a man’s mind.” Fourth.

5. “Nāhaṃ[M 1.0002], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekaphoṭṭhabbampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, itthiphoṭṭhabbo. Itthiphoṭṭhabbo, bhikkhave, purisassa cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Pañcamaṃ.

5. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single touch that so overpowers and persists in a man’s mind as this: the touch of a woman. The touch of a woman, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a man’s mind.” Fifth.

6. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekarūpampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, purisarūpaṃ. Purisarūpaṃ, bhikkhave, itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

6. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single form that so overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind as this: the form of a man. The form of a man, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind.” Sixth.

7. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekasaddampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, purisasaddo. Purisasaddo, bhikkhave, itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Sattamaṃ.

7. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single sound that so overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind as this: the sound of a man. The sound of a man, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind.” Seventh.

8. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekagandhampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, purisagandho. Purisagandho, bhikkhave, itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

8. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single scent that so overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind as this: the scent of a man. The scent of a man, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind.” Eighth.

9. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekarasampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya [V 1.0003] tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, purisaraso. Purisaraso, bhikkhave, itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Navamaṃ.

9. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single taste that so overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind as this: the taste of a man. The taste of a man, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind.” Ninth.

10. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekaphoṭṭhabbampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ [T 1.0003] itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, purisaphoṭṭhabbo. Purisaphoṭṭhabbo, bhikkhave, itthiyā cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī”ti. Dasamaṃ.

10. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single touch that so overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind as this: the touch of a man. The touch of a man, bhikkhus, overpowers and persists in a woman’s mind.” Tenth.

Rūpādivaggo paṭhamo.

2. Nīvaraṇappahānavaggo

2. The Chapter on the Abandonment of the Hindrances

11. “Nāhaṃ[V 1.0004][P 1.0003], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppanno vā kāmacchando uppajjati uppanno vā kāmacchando bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, subhanimittaṃ. Subhanimittaṃ, bhikkhave, ayoniso manasi karoto anuppanno ceva kāmacchando uppajjati uppanno ca kāmacchando bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattatī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

11. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single thing by which unarisen sensual desire arises and arisen sensual desire increases and expands, like this, bhikkhus: the sign of the beautiful. For one who gives unwise attention to the sign of the beautiful, bhikkhus, unarisen sensual desire arises, and arisen sensual desire increases and expands.” First.

12. “Nāhaṃ[M 1.0003], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppanno vā byāpādo uppajjati uppanno vā byāpādo bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, paṭighanimittaṃ. Paṭighanimittaṃ, bhikkhave, ayoniso manasi karoto anuppanno ceva byāpādo uppajjati uppanno ca byāpādo bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattatī”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

12. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single thing by which unarisen ill will arises and arisen ill will increases and expands, like this, bhikkhus: the sign of aversion. For one who gives unwise attention to the sign of aversion, bhikkhus, unarisen ill will arises, and arisen ill will increases and expands.” Second.

13. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannaṃ vā thinamiddhaṃ thīnamiddhaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) uppajjati uppannaṃ vā thinamiddhaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, arati tandī tandi (ka.) vijambhitā vijambhikā (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) bhattasammado cetaso ca līnattaṃ. Līnacittassa, bhikkhave, anuppannañceva thinamiddhaṃ [T 1.0004] uppajjati uppannañca thinamiddhaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattatī”ti. Tatiyaṃ.

13. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single thing by which unarisen sloth and torpor arise and arisen sloth and torpor increase and expand, like these, bhikkhus: discontent, lassitude, stretching, drowsiness from food, and sluggishness of mind. For one with a sluggish mind, bhikkhus, unarisen sloth and torpor arise, and arisen sloth and torpor increase and expand.” Third.

14. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannaṃ vā uddhaccakukkuccaṃ uppajjati uppannaṃ vā uddhaccakukkuccaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cetaso avūpasamo. Avūpasantacittassa, bhikkhave, anuppannañceva uddhaccakukkuccaṃ uppajjati uppannañca uddhaccakukkuccaṃ bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattatī”ti. Catutthaṃ.

14. “Bhikkhus, I do not see any other single thing by which unarisen restlessness and remorse arise and arisen restlessness and remorse increase and expand, like this, bhikkhus: non-tranquility of mind. For one with a non-tranquil mind, bhikkhus, unarisen restlessness and remorse arise, and arisen restlessness and remorse increase and expand.” Fourth.

15. “Nāhaṃ[V 1.0005][P 1.0004], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannā vā vicikicchā uppajjati uppannā vā vicikicchā bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, ayonisomanasikāro. Ayoniso, bhikkhave, manasi karoto anuppannā ceva vicikicchā uppajjati uppannā ca vicikicchā bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattatī”ti. Pañcamaṃ.

15. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing by which unarisen doubt arises and arisen doubt increases and expands, namely this, Bhikkhus: unwise attention. For one who gives unwise attention, Bhikkhus, unarisen doubt arises, and arisen doubt increases and expands.” The Fifth.

16. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppanno vā kāmacchando nuppajjati uppanno vā kāmacchando pahīyati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, asubhanimittaṃ. Asubhanimittaṃ, bhikkhave, yoniso manasi karoto anuppanno ceva kāmacchando nuppajjati uppanno ca kāmacchando pahīyatī”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

16. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing by which unarisen sensual desire does not arise and arisen sensual desire is abandoned, namely this, Bhikkhus: the sign of the unattractive. For one who gives wise attention to the sign of the unattractive, Bhikkhus, unarisen sensual desire does not arise, and arisen sensual desire is abandoned.” The Sixth.

17. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppanno vā byāpādo nuppajjati uppanno vā byāpādo pahīyati [T 1.0005] yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, mettā [M 1.0004] cetovimutti. Mettaṃ, bhikkhave, cetovimuttiṃ yoniso manasi karoto anuppanno ceva byāpādo nuppajjati uppanno ca byāpādo pahīyatī”ti. Sattamaṃ.

17. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing by which unarisen ill will does not arise and arisen ill will is abandoned, namely this, Bhikkhus: the liberation of mind through loving-kindness. For one who gives wise attention to the liberation of mind through loving-kindness, Bhikkhus, unarisen ill will does not arise, and arisen ill will is abandoned.” The Seventh.

18. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannaṃ vā thinamiddhaṃ nuppajjati uppannaṃ vā thinamiddhaṃ pahīyati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, ārambhadhātu nikkamadhātu parakkamadhātu. Āraddhavīriyassa, bhikkhave, anuppannañceva thinamiddhaṃ nuppajjati uppannañca thinamiddhaṃ pahīyatī”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

18. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing by which unarisen sloth and torpor do not arise and arisen sloth and torpor are abandoned, namely this, Bhikkhus: the element of initiative, the element of exertion, and the element of striving. For one whose energy is aroused, Bhikkhus, unarisen sloth and torpor do not arise, and arisen sloth and torpor are abandoned.” The Eighth.

19. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannaṃ vā uddhaccakukkuccaṃ nuppajjati uppannaṃ vā uddhaccakukkuccaṃ pahīyati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cetaso vūpasamo. Vūpasantacittassa, bhikkhave, anuppannañceva uddhaccakukkuccaṃ nuppajjati uppannañca uddhaccakukkuccaṃ pahīyatī”ti. Navamaṃ.

19. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing by which unarisen restlessness and remorse do not arise and arisen restlessness and remorse are abandoned, namely this, Bhikkhus: tranquility of mind. For one with a tranquil mind, Bhikkhus, unarisen restlessness and remorse do not arise, and arisen restlessness and remorse are abandoned.” The Ninth.

20. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi [P 1.0005] yena anuppannā vā vicikicchā nuppajjati uppannā vā vicikicchā pahīyati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, yonisomanasikāro[V 1.0006]. Yoniso, bhikkhave, manasi karoto anuppannā ceva vicikicchā nuppajjati uppannā ca vicikicchā pahīyatī”ti. Dasamaṃ.

20. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing by which unarisen doubt does not arise and arisen doubt is abandoned, namely this, Bhikkhus: wise attention. For one who gives wise attention, Bhikkhus, unarisen doubt does not arise, and arisen doubt is abandoned.” The Tenth.

Nīvaraṇappahānavaggo dutiyo.

3. Akammaniyavaggo

3. The Chapter on the Unworkable

21. “Nāhaṃ[V 1.0007], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ abhāvitaṃ [T 1.0006] akammaniyaṃ hoti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ yathayidaṃ cittaṃ (sī. pī.) evamuparipi. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, abhāvitaṃ akammaniyaṃ hotī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

21. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when undeveloped, is so unworkable as the mind. An undeveloped mind, bhikkhus, is unworkable.” The First.

22. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ bhāvitaṃ kammaniyaṃ hoti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ kammaniyaṃ hotī”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

22. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when developed, is so workable as the mind. A developed mind, bhikkhus, is workable.” The Second.

23. “Nāhaṃ[M 1.0005], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ abhāvitaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, abhāvitaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Tatiyaṃ.

23. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when undeveloped, conduces to such great harm as the mind. An undeveloped mind, bhikkhus, conduces to great harm.” The Third.

24. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ bhāvitaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Catutthaṃ.

24. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when developed, conduces to such great benefit as the mind. A developed mind, bhikkhus, conduces to great benefit.” The Fourth.

25. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ abhāvitaṃ apātubhūtaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, abhāvitaṃ apātubhūtaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Pañcamaṃ.

25. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when undeveloped and unmanifested, conduces to such great harm as the mind. The mind, bhikkhus, when undeveloped and unmanifested, conduces to great harm.” The Fifth.

26. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi [P 1.0006] yaṃ evaṃ bhāvitaṃ pātubhūtaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ pātubhūtaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

26. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when developed and manifested, conduces to such great benefit as the mind. The mind, bhikkhus, when developed and manifested, conduces to great benefit.” The Sixth.

27. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ abhāvitaṃ abahulīkataṃ [V 1.0008] mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, abhāvitaṃ abahulīkataṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Sattamaṃ.

27. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when undeveloped and uncultivated, conduces to such great harm as the mind. An undeveloped and uncultivated mind, bhikkhus, conduces to great harm.” The Seventh.

28. “Nāhaṃ[T 1.0007], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ bhāvitaṃ bahulīkataṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ bahulīkataṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

28. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when developed and cultivated, conduces to such great benefit as the mind. The mind, bhikkhus, when developed and cultivated, conduces to great benefit.” The Eighth.

29. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ abhāvitaṃ abahulīkataṃ dukkhādhivahaṃ hoti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, abhāvitaṃ abahulīkataṃ dukkhādhivahaṃ hotī”ti. Navamaṃ.

29. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when undeveloped and uncultivated, brings such suffering as the mind. The mind, bhikkhus, when undeveloped and uncultivated, brings suffering.” The Ninth.

30. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ bhāvitaṃ bahulīkataṃ sukhādhivahaṃ hoti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ bahulīkataṃ sukhādhivahaṃ hotī”ti. Dasamaṃ.

30. “Bhikkhus, I do not perceive even one other thing that, when developed and cultivated, brings such happiness as the mind. The mind, bhikkhus, when developed and cultivated, brings happiness.” The Tenth.

Akammaniyavaggo tatiyo.

The Chapter on the Unworkable, the Third.

4. Adantavaggo

4. The Chapter on the Untamed

31. “Nāhaṃ[V 1.0009][M 1.0006], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ adantaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, adantaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

31. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when untamed, leads to such great harm as the mind. An untamed mind, monks, leads to great harm. First.

32. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ dantaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, dantaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

32. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when tamed, leads to such great benefit as the mind. A tamed mind, monks, leads to great benefit. Second.

33. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi [P 1.0007] yaṃ evaṃ aguttaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, aguttaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Tatiyaṃ.

33. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when unguarded, leads to such great harm as the mind. An unguarded mind, monks, leads to great harm. Third.

34. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ guttaṃ [T 1.0008] mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, guttaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Catutthaṃ.

34. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when guarded, leads to such great benefit as the mind. A guarded mind, monks, leads to great benefit. Fourth.

35. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ arakkhitaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, arakkhitaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Pañcamaṃ.

35. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when unprotected, leads to such great harm as the mind. An unprotected mind, monks, leads to great harm. Fifth.

36. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ rakkhitaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, rakkhitaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

36. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when protected, leads to such great benefit as the mind. A protected mind, monks, leads to great benefit. Sixth.

37. “Nāhaṃ[V 1.0010], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ asaṃvutaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, asaṃvutaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Sattamaṃ.

37. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when unrestrained, leads to such great harm as the mind. An unrestrained mind, monks, leads to great harm. Seventh.

38. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ saṃvutaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, saṃvutaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

38. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when restrained, leads to such great benefit as the mind. A restrained mind, monks, leads to great benefit. Eighth.

39. “Nāhaṃ[M 1.0007], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ adantaṃ aguttaṃ arakkhitaṃ asaṃvutaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, adantaṃ aguttaṃ arakkhitaṃ asaṃvutaṃ mahato anatthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Navamaṃ.

39. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when untamed, unguarded, unprotected, and unrestrained, leads to such great harm as the mind. An untamed, unguarded, unprotected, and unrestrained mind, monks, leads to great harm. Ninth.

40. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ dantaṃ guttaṃ rakkhitaṃ saṃvutaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ[T 1.0009]. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, dantaṃ guttaṃ rakkhitaṃ saṃvutaṃ mahato atthāya saṃvattatī”ti. Dasamaṃ.

40. Monks, I do not see even one other thing that, when tamed, guarded, protected, and restrained, leads to such great benefit as the mind. A tamed, guarded, protected, and restrained mind, monks, leads to great benefit. Tenth.

Adantavaggo catuttho.

The Fourth Chapter: On the Untamed.

5. Paṇihitaacchavaggo

5. The Chapter on the Directed and the Serene

41. “Seyyathāpi[V 1.0011][P 1.0008], bhikkhave, sālisūkaṃ vā yavasūkaṃ vā micchāpaṇihitaṃ hatthena vā pādena vā akkantaṃ hatthaṃ vā pādaṃ vā bhecchati bhijjissati (syā. kaṃ. ka.), bhejjati (sī.) moggallānabyākaraṇaṃ passitabbaṃ lohitaṃ vā uppādessatīti netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Micchāpaṇihitattā, bhikkhave, sūkassa. Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, so vata bhikkhu micchāpaṇihitena cittena avijjaṃ bhecchati, vijjaṃ uppādessati, nibbānaṃ sacchikarissatīti netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Micchāpaṇihitattā, bhikkhave, cittassā”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

41. Monks, just as, if a rice awn or a barley awn is wrongly directed, it is impossible that when pressed on by hand or trodden on by foot it will pierce the hand or the foot or cause blood to arise. For what reason? Because, monks, the awn is wrongly directed. Even so, monks, it is impossible that a monk with a wrongly directed mind will destroy ignorance, cause true knowledge to arise, and realize Nibbāna. For what reason? Because, monks, the mind is wrongly directed. First.

42. “Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, sālisūkaṃ vā yavasūkaṃ vā sammāpaṇihitaṃ hatthena vā pādena vā akkantaṃ hatthaṃ vā pādaṃ vā bhecchati lohitaṃ vā uppādessatīti ṭhānametaṃ vijjati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Sammāpaṇihitattā, bhikkhave, sūkassa. Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, so vata bhikkhu sammāpaṇihitena cittena avijjaṃ bhecchati, vijjaṃ uppādessati, nibbānaṃ sacchikarissatīti ṭhānametaṃ vijjati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Sammāpaṇihitattā, bhikkhave, cittassā”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

42. Monks, just as, if a rice awn or a barley awn is rightly directed, it is possible that when pressed on by hand or trodden on by foot it will pierce the hand or the foot or cause blood to arise. For what reason? Because, monks, the awn is rightly directed. Even so, monks, it is possible that a monk with a rightly directed mind will destroy ignorance, cause true knowledge to arise, and realize Nibbāna. For what reason? Because, monks, the mind is rightly directed. Second.

43. “Idhāhaṃ [M 1.0008] idāhaṃ (sī.), bhikkhave, ekaccaṃ puggalaṃ paduṭṭhacittaṃ evaṃ cetasā ceto paricca pajānāmi – ‘imamhi ce ayaṃ samaye puggalo kālaṃ kareyya, yathābhataṃ nikkhitto evaṃ niraye’. Taṃ kissa hetu? Cittaṃ hissa[T 1.0010], bhikkhave, paduṭṭhaṃ. “Cetopadosahetu pana, bhikkhave, evamidhekacce sattā kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjantī”ti. Tatiyaṃ.

43. Here, monks, having encompassed with my own mind the mind of a certain person, I know distinctly a person with a mind corrupted by hatred thus: ‘If this person were to die at this time, they would be placed in hell as if carried there.’ For what reason? Because, monks, their mind is corrupted by hatred. It is due to the corruption of the mind, monks, that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in the plane of misery, a bad destination, a state of ruin, in hell. Third.

44. “Idhāhaṃ, bhikkhave, ekaccaṃ puggalaṃ pasannacittaṃ evaṃ cetasā ceto paricca pajānāmi – ‘imamhi ce ayaṃ [P 1.0009] samaye puggalo kālaṃ kareyya, yathābhataṃ nikkhitto evaṃ sagge’. Taṃ kissa hetu? Cittaṃ hissa, bhikkhave, pasannaṃ. “Cetopasādahetu pana, bhikkhave, evamidhekacce sattā kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjantī”ti. Catutthaṃ.

44. Here, monks, having encompassed with my own mind the mind of a certain person, I know distinctly a person with a serene mind thus: ‘If this person were to die at this time, they would be placed in heaven as if carried there.’ For what reason? Because, monks, their mind is serene. It is due to the serenity of the mind, monks, that some beings here, with the breakup of the body, after death, are reborn in a good destination, in the heavenly world. Fourth.

45. “Seyyathāpi[V 1.0012], bhikkhave, udakarahado āvilo luḷito kalalībhūto tattha cakkhumā puriso tīre ṭhito na passeyya sippisambukampi sippikasambukampi (ka.) sakkharakaṭhalampi macchagumbampi carantampi tiṭṭhantampi. Taṃ kissa hetu? Āvilattā, bhikkhave, udakassa. Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, so vata bhikkhu āvilena cittena attatthaṃ vā ñassati paratthaṃ vā ñassati ubhayatthaṃ vā ñassati uttariṃ vā manussadhammā alamariyañāṇadassanavisesaṃ sacchikarissatīti netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Āvilattā, bhikkhave, cittassā”ti. Pañcamaṃ.

45. “Monks, just as if there were a pool of water that is turbid, stirred up, and muddy, a man with good eyesight standing on the bank would not see the oysters and snails, the gravel and potsherds, or the schools of fish swimming about or staying still. For what reason? Because, monks, of the water's turbidity. Even so, monks, it is impossible that a monk with a turbid mind will know his own welfare, the welfare of others, or the welfare of both, or that he will realize a state beyond ordinary human states, a special knowledge and vision befitting the noble ones. For what reason? Because, monks, of the mind's turbidity.” The fifth.

46. “Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, udakarahado accho vippasanno anāvilo tattha cakkhumā puriso tīre ṭhito passeyya sippisambukampi sakkharakaṭhalampi macchagumbampi carantampi tiṭṭhantampi. Taṃ kissa hetu? Anāvilattā, bhikkhave, udakassa. Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, so vata bhikkhu anāvilena [T 1.0011] cittena attatthaṃ vā ñassati paratthaṃ vā ñassati ubhayatthaṃ vā ñassati uttariṃ vā manussadhammā alamariyañāṇadassanavisesaṃ sacchikarissatīti ṭhānametaṃ vijjati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Anāvilattā, bhikkhave, cittassā”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

46. “Monks, just as if there were a pool of water that is clear, serene, and unturbid, a man with good eyesight standing on the bank could see the oysters and snails, the gravel and potsherds, and the schools of fish swimming about or staying still. For what reason? Because, monks, of the water's lack of turbidity. Even so, monks, it is possible that a monk with an unturbid mind will know his own welfare, the welfare of others, or the welfare of both, and that he will realize a state beyond ordinary human states, a special knowledge and vision befitting the noble ones. For what reason? Because, monks, of the mind's lack of turbidity.” The sixth.

47. “Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, yāni kānici rukkhajātānaṃ phandano tesaṃ aggamakkhāyati yadidaṃ mudutāya ceva kammaññatāya ca. Evamevaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave[M 1.0009], nāññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ bhāvitaṃ bahulīkataṃ mudu ca hoti kammaññañca yathayidaṃ cittaṃ. Cittaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ bahulīkataṃ mudu ca hoti kammaññañca hotī”ti. Sattamaṃ.

47. “Monks, just as among all kinds of trees, the Phandana tree is declared the foremost on account of its pliancy and workability, even so, monks, I do not see any other single thing that, when developed and cultivated, becomes so pliant and workable as this: the mind. The mind, monks, when developed and cultivated, becomes pliant and workable.” The seventh.

48. “Nāhaṃ[P 1.0010], bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yaṃ evaṃ lahuparivattaṃ yathayidaṃ cittaṃ. Yāvañcidaṃ, bhikkhave, upamāpi na sukarā yāva lahuparivattaṃ citta”nti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

48. “Monks, I do not see any other single thing that changes so quickly as this: the mind. So much so, monks, that it is not easy to give a simile for how quickly the mind changes.” The eighth.

49. “Pabhassaramidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Tañca kho āgantukehi upakkilesehi upakkiliṭṭha”nti. Navamaṃ.

49. “Monks, this mind is luminous. And it is defiled by incoming defilements.” The ninth.

50. “Pabhassaramidaṃ, bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Tañca kho āgantukehi upakkilesehi vippamutta”nti. Dasamaṃ.

50. “Monks, this mind is luminous. And it is freed from incoming defilements.” The tenth.

Paṇihitaacchavaggo pañcamo.

6. Accharāsaṅghātavaggo

6. The Chapter on the Snap of a Finger

51. “Pabhassaramidaṃ[V 1.0013], bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Tañca kho āgantukehi upakkilesehi upakkiliṭṭhaṃ. Taṃ assutavā puthujjano yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti. Tasmā ‘assutavato puthujjanassa cittabhāvanā natthī’ti vadāmī”ti. Paṭhamaṃ.

51. “Monks, this mind is luminous. And it is defiled by adventitious defilements. The uninstructed worldling does not understand this as it really is. Therefore, I say that for the uninstructed worldling there is no development of the mind.” The First.

52. “Pabhassaramidaṃ[T 1.0012], bhikkhave, cittaṃ. Tañca kho āgantukehi upakkilesehi vippamuttaṃ. Taṃ sutavā ariyasāvako yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti. Tasmā ‘sutavato ariyasāvakassa cittabhāvanā atthī’ti vadāmī”ti. Dutiyaṃ.

52. “Monks, this mind is luminous. And it is freed from adventitious defilements. The instructed noble disciple understands this as it truly is. Therefore, I say that for the instructed noble disciple there is development of the mind.” The Second.

53. “Accharāsaṅghātamattampi ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu mettācittaṃ mettaṃ cittaṃ (sī.), mettacittaṃ (syā. kaṃ. pī. ka.) āsevati; ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave – ‘bhikkhu arittajjhāno viharati satthusāsanakaro ovādapatikaro, amoghaṃ raṭṭhapiṇḍaṃ bhuñjati’. Ko pana vādo ye naṃ bahulīkarontī”ti! Tatiyaṃ.

53. “Monks, if a monk cultivates a mind of loving-kindness even for the time it takes to snap his fingers, he is called, monks, ‘a monk who dwells with non-barren jhāna, who acts in accordance with the Teacher’s instruction, who responds to advice, and who eats the country’s almsfood not in vain.’ What need is there to speak of those who cultivate it abundantly!” The Third.

54. “Accharāsaṅghātamattampi [M 1.0010] ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu mettācittaṃ bhāveti; ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave – ‘bhikkhu arittajjhāno viharati satthusāsanakaro ovādapatikaro, amoghaṃ raṭṭhapiṇḍaṃ bhuñjati’. Ko pana vādo ye naṃ bahulīkarontī”ti! Catutthaṃ.

54. “Monks, if a monk develops a mind of loving-kindness even for the time it takes to snap his fingers, he is called, monks, ‘a monk who dwells with non-barren jhāna, who acts in accordance with the Teacher’s instruction, who responds to advice, and who eats the country’s almsfood not in vain.’ What need is there to speak of those who cultivate it abundantly!” The Fourth.

55. “Accharāsaṅghātamattampi [P 1.0011] ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu mettācittaṃ manasi karoti; ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave – ‘bhikkhu arittajjhāno viharati satthusāsanakaro ovādapatikaro amoghaṃ raṭṭhapiṇḍaṃ bhuñjati’. Ko pana vādo ye naṃ bahulīkarontī”ti! Pañcamaṃ.

55. “Monks, if a monk attends to a mind of loving-kindness even for the time it takes to snap his fingers, he is called, monks, ‘a monk who dwells with non-barren jhāna, who acts in accordance with the Teacher’s instruction, who responds to advice, and who eats the country’s almsfood not in vain.’ What need is there to speak of those who cultivate it abundantly!” The Fifth.

56. “Ye keci, bhikkhave, dhammā akusalā akusalabhāgiyā akusalapakkhikā, sabbe te manopubbaṅgamā. Mano tesaṃ dhammānaṃ paṭhamaṃ uppajjati, anvadeva akusalā dhammā”ti. Chaṭṭhaṃ.

56. “Monks, whatever phenomena there are that are unwholesome, partaking of the unwholesome, allied with the unwholesome, all of them are preceded by mind. Mind arises first among these phenomena, and the unwholesome phenomena arise in its wake.” The Sixth.

57. “Ye [V 1.0014] keci, bhikkhave, dhammā kusalā kusalabhāgiyā kusalapakkhikā, sabbe te manopubbaṅgamā. Mano tesaṃ dhammānaṃ paṭhamaṃ uppajjati, anvadeva [T 1.0013] kusalā dhammā”ti. Sattamaṃ.

57. “Monks, whatever phenomena there are that are wholesome, partaking of the wholesome, allied with the wholesome, all of them are preceded by mind. Mind arises first among these phenomena, and the wholesome phenomena arise in its wake.” The Seventh.

58. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannā vā akusalā dhammā uppajjanti uppannā vā kusalā dhammā parihāyanti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, pamādo. Pamattassa, bhikkhave, anuppannā ceva akusalā dhammā uppajjanti uppannā ca kusalā dhammā parihāyantī”ti. Aṭṭhamaṃ.

58. “Monks, I do not perceive any other single state by which unarisen unwholesome states arise and arisen wholesome states decline, namely, monks, heedlessness. For the heedless, monks, unarisen unwholesome states arise and arisen wholesome states decline.” The Eighth.

59. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannā vā kusalā dhammā uppajjanti uppannā vā akusalā dhammā parihāyanti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, appamādo. Appamattassa, bhikkhave, anuppannā ceva kusalā dhammā uppajjanti uppannā ca akusalā dhammā parihāyantī”ti. Navamaṃ.

59. “Monks, I do not perceive any other single state by which unarisen wholesome states arise and arisen unwholesome states decline, namely, monks, heedfulness. For the heedful, monks, unarisen wholesome states arise and arisen unwholesome states decline.” The Ninth.

60. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppannā vā akusalā dhammā uppajjanti uppannā vā kusalā dhammā parihāyanti yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, kosajjaṃ. Kusītassa, bhikkhave, anuppannā ceva akusalā dhammā uppajjanti uppannā ca kusalā dhammā parihāyantī”ti. Dasamaṃ.

60. Bhikkhus, I do not perceive any other single thing on account of which unarisen unwholesome states arise and arisen wholesome states decline, as this, bhikkhus: laziness. For a lazy person, bhikkhus, unarisen unwholesome states arise, and arisen wholesome states decline. Tenth.

Who is that one person? It is one who has right view, who has an undistorted perspective. They cause many people to abandon the bad Dhamma and establish them in the good Dhamma. This, monks, is the one person who, arising in the world, arises for the welfare of many people, for the happiness of many people, for the benefit, welfare, and happiness of the multitude, of gods and humans.

311. “Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekapuggalampi samanupassāmi yo evaṃ bahujanaahitāya paṭipanno bahujanaasukhāya, bahuno janassa anatthāya ahitāya dukkhāya devamanussānaṃ yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, makkhali moghapuriso. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, nadīmukhe khippaṃ khipaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) uḍḍeyya oḍḍeyya (sī.), ujjheyya (ka.) bahūnaṃ macchānaṃ ahitāya dukkhāya anayāya byasanāya; evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave[V 1.0047], makkhali moghapuriso manussakhippaṃ [T 1.0045] maññe loke uppanno bahūnaṃ sattānaṃ ahitāya dukkhāya anayāya byasanāyā”ti.

311. Monks, I do not see even one other person who practices for the non-welfare of many people, for the unhappiness of many people, for the harm, non-welfare, and suffering of the multitude, of gods and humans, like this Makkhali, the worthless man. Just as, monks, if a fish-trap were set at a river mouth, it would be for the non-welfare, suffering, ruin, and disaster of many fish; in the same way, monks, Makkhali, the worthless man, has, I think, arisen in the world like a human fish-trap for the non-welfare, suffering, ruin, and disaster of many beings.

312. “Durakkhāte[P 1.0034], bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo ca samādapeti samādāpeti (?) yañca samādapeti yo ca samādapito tathattāya paṭipajjati sabbe te bahuṃ apuññaṃ pasavanti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Durakkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

312. Monks, in a poorly proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, the one who exhorts, the one who is exhorted, and the one who, having been exhorted, practices for the sake of that state—all of them generate much demerit. For what reason? Because, monks, of the poor proclamation of the Dhamma.

313. “Svākkhāte[M 1.0036], bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo ca samādapeti yañca samādapeti yo ca samādapito tathattāya paṭipajjati sabbe te bahuṃ puññaṃ pasavanti. Taṃ kissa hetu? Svākkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

313. Monks, in a well-proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, the one who exhorts, the one who is exhorted, and the one who, having been exhorted, practices for the sake of that state—all of them generate much merit. For what reason? Because, monks, of the good proclamation of the Dhamma.

314. “Durakkhāte, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye dāyakena mattā jānitabbā, no paṭiggāhakena. Taṃ kissa hetu? Durakkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

314. Monks, in a poorly proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, the measure should be known by the donor, not by the recipient. For what reason? Because, monks, of the poor proclamation of the Dhamma.

315. “Svākkhāte, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye paṭiggāhakena mattā jānitabbā, no dāyakena. Taṃ kissa hetu? Svākkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

315. Monks, in a well-proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, the measure should be known by the recipient, not by the donor. For what reason? Because, monks, of the good proclamation of the Dhamma.

316. “Durakkhāte, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo āraddhavīriyo so dukkhaṃ viharati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Durakkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

316. Monks, in a poorly proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, one who has aroused effort dwells in suffering. For what reason? Because, monks, of the poor proclamation of the Dhamma.

317. “Svākkhāte, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo kusīto so dukkhaṃ viharati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Svākkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

317. Monks, in a well-proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, one who is lazy dwells in suffering. For what reason? Because, monks, of the good proclamation of the Dhamma.

318. “Durakkhāte, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo kusīto so sukhaṃ viharati[T 1.0046]. Taṃ kissa hetu? Durakkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

318. Monks, in a poorly proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, one who is lazy dwells in happiness. For what reason? Because, monks, of the poor proclamation of the Dhamma.

319. “Svākkhāte, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo āraddhavīriyo so sukhaṃ viharati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Svākkhātattā, bhikkhave, dhammassā”ti.

319. Monks, in a well-proclaimed Dhamma and Vinaya, one who has aroused effort dwells in happiness. For what reason? Because, monks, of the good proclamation of the Dhamma.

320. “Seyyathāpi[V 1.0048], bhikkhave, appamattakopi gūtho duggandho hoti; evamevaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, appamattakampi bhavaṃ na vaṇṇemi, antamaso accharāsaṅghātamattampi”.

320. Just as, monks, even a small amount of excrement is foul-smelling; even so, monks, I do not praise even a small amount of existence, not even for the duration of a finger-snap.

321. “Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, appamattakampi muttaṃ duggandhaṃ hoti… appamattakopi kheḷo duggandho hoti… appamattakopi pubbo duggandho hoti… appamattakampi [P 1.0035] lohitaṃ duggandhaṃ hoti; evamevaṃ kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, appamattakampi bhavaṃ na vaṇṇemi, antamaso accharāsaṅghātamattampi”.

321. Just as, monks, even a small amount of urine is foul-smelling… even a small amount of saliva is foul-smelling… even a small amount of pus is foul-smelling… even a small amount of blood is foul-smelling; even so, monks, I do not praise even a small amount of existence, not even for the duration of a finger-snap.

Even so, monks, few are those beings who are reborn in the central districts; but indeed, far more are those beings who are reborn in the border districts, among the barbarians who are unable to understand.

324. Even so, monks, few are those beings who are wise, not stupefied, not slobbering-mutes, and are capable of understanding the meaning of what is well-spoken and ill-spoken; but indeed, far more are those beings who are of little wisdom, stupefied, slobbering-mutes, and are not capable of understanding the meaning of what is well-spoken and ill-spoken.

325. … Evamevaṃ [V 1.0049] kho, bhikkhave, appakā te sattā ye ariyena paññācakkhunā samannāgatā; atha kho eteva sattā bahutarā ye avijjāgatā sammūḷhā.

325. Even so, monks, few are those beings who are endowed with the noble eye of wisdom; but indeed, far more are those beings who are overcome by ignorance and are bewildered by delusion.

326. … Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, appakā te sattā ye labhanti tathāgataṃ dassanāya; atha kho eteva sattā bahutarā ye na labhanti tathāgataṃ dassanāya.

326. Even so, monks, few are those beings who get to see the Tathāgata; but indeed, far more are those beings who do not get to see the Tathāgata.

327. Even so, monks, few are those beings who get to hear the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata; but indeed, far more are those beings who do not get to hear the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata.

328. … Evamevaṃ [M 1.0038] kho, bhikkhave, appakā te sattā ye sutvā dhammaṃ dhārenti; atha kho eteva sattā bahutarā ye sutvā dhammaṃ na dhārenti.

328. Even so, monks, few are those beings who, having heard the Dhamma, bear it in mind; but indeed, far more are those beings who, having heard the Dhamma, do not bear it in mind.

329. … Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, appakā te sattā ye dhātānaṃ dhatānaṃ (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.) dhammānaṃ atthaṃ upaparikkhanti; atha kho eteva sattā bahutarā ye dhātānaṃ dhammānaṃ atthaṃ na upaparikkhanti.

329. Even so, monks, few are those beings who investigate the meaning of the teachings they have borne in mind; but indeed, far more are those beings who do not investigate the meaning of the teachings they have borne in mind.

330. … Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, appakā te sattā ye atthamaññāya dhammamaññāya dhammānudhammaṃ paṭipajjanti; atha kho eteva sattā bahutarā ye atthamaññāya dhammamaññāya dhammānudhammaṃ na paṭipajjanti.

330. Even so, monks, few are those beings who, having understood the meaning and having understood the Dhamma, practice in accordance with the Dhamma; but indeed, far more are those beings who, having understood the meaning and having understood the Dhamma, do not practice in accordance with the Dhamma.

333. Even so, monks, few are those beings who, having made relinquishment their object, attain concentration and attain one-pointedness of mind; but indeed, far more are those beings who, having made relinquishment their object, do not attain concentration and do not attain one-pointedness of mind.

334. Even so, monks, few are those beings who are gainers of the best food and the best tastes; but indeed, far more are those beings who are not gainers of the best food and the best tastes, who sustain themselves by gleaning and with alms received in a potsherd-like bowl.

335. … Evamevaṃ kho, bhikkhave, appakā te sattā ye attharasassa dhammarasassa vimuttirasassa lābhino; atha kho eteva sattā bahutarā ye attharasassa dhammarasassa vimuttirasassa na lābhino. Tasmātiha, bhikkhave[M 1.0039], evaṃ sikkhitabbaṃ – attharasassa dhammarasassa vimuttirasassa lābhino bhavissāmāti. Evañhi vo, bhikkhave, sikkhitabbanti.

335. Even so, monks, few are those beings who are gainers of the taste of the goal, the taste of the Dhamma, and the taste of liberation; but indeed, far more are those beings who are not gainers of the taste of the goal, the taste of the Dhamma, and the taste of liberation. Therefore, monks, you should train yourselves thus: ‘We will become gainers of the taste of the goal, the taste of the Dhamma, and the taste of liberation.’ This, monks, is how you should train yourselves.

20. The Chapter on the Deathless

600. “Amataṃ [T 1.0059][V 1.0061][M 1.0047] te, bhikkhave, na paribhuñjanti ye kāyagatāsatiṃ na paribhuñjanti. Amataṃ te, bhikkhave, paribhuñjanti ye kāyagatāsatiṃ paribhuñjantī”ti.

600. “Monks, they do not partake of the Deathless who do not partake of mindfulness directed to the body. Monks, they partake of the Deathless who partake of mindfulness directed to the body.”

601. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, aparibhuttaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati aparibhuttā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, paribhuttaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati paribhuttā”ti.

601. “Monks, the Deathless is unpartaken by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is unpartaken. Monks, the Deathless is partaken by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is partaken.”

602. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, parihīnaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati parihīnā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, aparihīnaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati aparihīnā”ti.

602. “Monks, the Deathless is lost to those to whom mindfulness directed to the body is lost. Monks, the Deathless is not lost to those to whom mindfulness directed to the body is not lost.”

603. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, viraddhaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati [P 1.0046] viraddhā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, āraddhaṃ aviraddhaṃ (ka.) yesaṃ kāyagatāsati āraddhā”ti.

603. “Monks, the Deathless is missed by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is missed. Monks, the Deathless is accomplished by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is accomplished.”

604. “Amataṃ te, bhikkhave, pamādiṃsu ye kāyagatāsatiṃ pamādiṃsu. Amataṃ te, bhikkhave, na pamādiṃsu ye kāyagatāsatiṃ na pamādiṃsu”.

604. “Monks, they were heedless of the Deathless who were heedless of mindfulness directed to the body. Monks, they were not heedless of the Deathless who were not heedless of mindfulness directed to the body.”

605. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, pamuṭṭhaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati pamuṭṭhā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, appamuṭṭhaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati appamuṭṭhā”ti.

605. “Monks, the Deathless is forgotten by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is forgotten. Monks, the Deathless is not forgotten by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is not forgotten.”

606. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, anāsevitaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati anāsevitā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, āsevitaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati āsevitā”ti.

606. “Monks, the Deathless is not practiced by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is not practiced. Monks, the Deathless is practiced by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is practiced.”

607. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, abhāvitaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati abhāvitā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, bhāvitaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati bhāvitā”ti.

607. “Monks, the Deathless is undeveloped by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is undeveloped. Monks, the Deathless is developed by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is developed.”

608. “Amataṃ [V 1.0062][M 1.0048] tesaṃ, bhikkhave, abahulīkataṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati abahulīkatā[T 1.0060]. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, bahulīkataṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati bahulīkatā”ti.

608. “Monks, the Deathless is not made much of by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is not made much of. Monks, the Deathless is made much of by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is made much of.”

609. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, anabhiññātaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati anabhiññātā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, abhiññātaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati abhiññātā”ti.

609. “Monks, the Deathless is not directly known by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is not directly known. Monks, the Deathless is directly known by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is directly known.”

610. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, apariññātaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati apariññātā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, pariññātaṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati pariññātā”ti.

610. “Monks, the Deathless is not fully understood by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is not fully understood. Monks, the Deathless is fully understood by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is fully understood.”

611. “Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, asacchikataṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati asacchikatā. Amataṃ tesaṃ, bhikkhave, sacchikataṃ yesaṃ kāyagatāsati sacchikatā”ti. (….) (ekakanipātassa suttasahassaṃ samattaṃ.) (sī. syā. kaṃ. pī.)

611. “Monks, the Deathless is unrealized by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is unrealized. Monks, the Deathless is realized by those by whom mindfulness directed to the body is realized.”

(Idamavoca bhagavā. Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṃ abhinandunti.) ( ) etthantare pāṭho sī. syā. kaṃ. pī. potthakesu natthi

The Blessed One said this. Those monks, being pleased, greatly rejoiced in the Blessed One’s words.

Amatavaggo vīsatimo.

The Twentieth Chapter: On the Deathless.

Ekakanipātapāḷi niṭṭhitā.

The Pāḷi of the Book of Ones is concluded.
