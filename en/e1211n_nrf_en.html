Padamañjarī [O 0.0001]

Padamañjarī

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

Niruttikāyodadhipāragaṃ jinaṃ,

Visuddhadhammañca gaṇaṃ anuttaraṃ;

Tidhā namitvā padamañjarī mayā,

Karīyato bālakabuddhivuddhiyāti.

Having paid homage in three ways to the Victor who has crossed the ocean of the body of etymology, to the pure Dhamma, and to the unsurpassed Saṅgha, this Padamañjarī is composed by me for the growth of a child's wisdom.

Atha akārantapulliṅgo buddhasaddo vuccate.

Now the masculine noun 'buddha' ending in 'a' is explained.

Buddho devamanussānaṃ dhammaṃ desesi

The Buddha taught the Dhamma to gods and humans.

Buddhā devamanussānaṃ dhammaṃ adesiṃsu

The Buddhas taught the Dhamma to gods and humans.

Bho buddha tvampikho maṃ pālaya

O Buddha, do you also protect me.

Bho buddhā tumhepi kho maṃ pāletha.

O Buddhas, do you also protect me.

Buddhaṃ bhagavantaṃ sakkaccaṃ vandāmahaṃ

I reverently pay homage to the Blessed Buddha.

Buddhe bhagavante sakkaccaṃ vandāma mayaṃ

We reverently pay homage to the Blessed Buddhas.

Buddhena bhagavatā dhammo desīyate

The Dhamma is taught by the Blessed Buddha.

Buddhehi bhagavantehi dhammo desito

The Dhamma was taught by the Blessed Buddhas.

Buddhena bhagavatā mahājano sucarati

Through the Blessed Buddha, the great populace practices good conduct.

Buddhehi bhagavantehi sivapadaṃ yanti

Through the Blessed Buddhas, they go to the state of bliss.

Buddhassa bhagavato pupphaṃ yajati

He offers a flower to the Blessed Buddha.

Buddhānaṃ bhagavataṃ pupphāni yajati

He offers flowers to the Blessed Buddhas.

Buddhā bhagavatā sivapadaṃ labheyya

From the Blessed Buddha, may one attain the state of bliss.

Buddhehi bhagavantehi pabhā niccharati

Light radiates from the Blessed Buddhas.

Buddhassa bhagavato iddhi kiṃ na kare

What could the psychic power of the Blessed Buddha not accomplish?

Buddhānaṃ bhagavantānaṃ iddhipāṭihāriyāni

The miracles of psychic power of the Blessed Buddhas.

Buddhe bhagavante mahājano pasīdati.

The great populace has faith in the Blessed Buddha.

Buddhesu bhagavantesu manaṃ patiṭṭhitaṃ.

The mind is well-established in the Blessed Buddhas.

Iti paṭhamo pāṭho.

Thus, the first lesson.

Akārantapulliṅgo attasaddo vuccate.

The masculine noun 'atta' ending in 'a' is explained.

Attā saṃsāradukkhaṃ pāpuṇāti

The self attains the suffering of saṃsāra.

Attāno sukhadukkhaṃ pāpuṇanti

The selves attain happiness and suffering.

Bho atta sukhadukkhaṃ anubhosi

O self, you experience happiness and suffering.

Bho attano sukhadukkhaṃ anubhotha

O selves, you experience happiness and suffering.

Attānaṃ passati buddho bhagavā

The Blessed Buddha sees the self.

Attāno [O 0.0002] passati sammā sambuddho

The Perfectly Self-Enlightened One sees the selves.

Attanā sukhadukkhaṃ anubhuttaṃ

Happiness and suffering are experienced by oneself.

Attanehi kammaphalaṃ anubhuttaṃ

The fruit of kamma is experienced by the selves.

Attanā saṃsārasukhaṃ nāma natthi

For oneself, there is no such thing as happiness in saṃsāra.

Attanehi bhavasukhaṃ nāma natthi

For the selves, there is no such thing as happiness in existence.

Attano attāva patiṭṭhā siyā

Let the self indeed be one's own refuge.

Attānaṃ kammameva patiṭṭhā siyā

Let kamma indeed be a refuge for oneself.

Attanāpi mahabbhayaṃ uppajjati

Great fear also arises from oneself.

Attanehi mahabbhayāni jāyanti

Great fears are born from the selves.

Attano mātāpitaro honti

One has one's own mother and father.

Attānaṃ pañcagatiyo honti

For oneself, there are five destinies.

Attani sabbaṃ balaṃ harati rogo

Disease takes away all strength in the self.

Attanesu sabbaṃ balaṃ haranti rogā.

Diseases take away all strength in the selves.

Iti dutiyo pāṭho.

Thus the second lesson.

Akāranta pulliṅgo rājasaddo vuccate.

The masculine word 'rāja', ending in 'a', is stated.

Rājāpi mahājanaṃ toseti

The king also pleases the great multitude.

Rājāno mahājanaṃ tosenti

Kings please the great multitude.

Bho rājā mahājanaṃ pālaya

O king, protect the great multitude.

Bho rājāno mahājanaṃ pāletha

O kings, protect the great multitude.

Rājānaṃ passati mahājano

The great multitude sees the king.

Rājāno passanti mahājanā

The great multitudes see the kings.

Raññā kārīyate mahāpāsādo

A great palace is caused to be made by the king.

Rājūhi kārāpitā mahāvihārā

Great monasteries were caused to be made by kings.

Raññā mahājano sukhaṃ carati

Through the king, the great multitude fares happily.

Rājūhī mahājanā sukhaṃ caranti

Through the kings, the great multitudes fare happily.

Rañño paṇṇākāraṃ deti mahājano

The great multitude gives a gift to the king.

Rājūnaṃ paṇṇākāraṃ denti mahājanā

The great multitudes give a gift to the kings.

Raññā mahabbhayaṃ uppajjati

Great fear arises from the king.

Rājūhi mahabbhayāni uppajjanti

Great fears arise from kings.

Rañño vappamaṅgalaṃ hoti

There is a ploughing ceremony for the king.

Rājūnaṃ sabbābharaṇāni honti

There are all ornaments for the kings.

Raññe mahājano pana pasīdati

The great multitude, however, is pleased with the king.

Rājusu mahājanā pana pasīdanti.

The great multitudes, however, are pleased with the kings.

Iti tatiyo pāṭho.

Thus the third lesson.

Akārantapulliṅgo guṇavattusaddo vuccate.

The masculine word 'guṇavant', ending in 'a', is stated.

Guṇavā puriso sundaraṃ nibbāṇaṃ gato

Guṇavanto [O 0.0003] purisā sundaraṃ nibbāṇaṃ gatā

Virtuous men have gone to beautiful Nibbāna.

O virtuous man, you too give a gift.

O virtuous men, you give a gift.

Guṇavantaṃ purisaṃ dhammaṃ bodheti ācariyo

The teacher instructs the virtuous person in the Dhamma.

Guṇavante purise dhammaṃ pāṭhayati ācariyo

The teacher teaches the virtuous men the Dhamma.

Guṇavantena purisena kārito vihāro

A monastery was caused to be built by a virtuous man.

Guṇavantehi purisehi kāritā vihārā

Monasteries were caused to be built by virtuous men.

Guṇavantena purisena loko sucarati

Through a virtuous man, the world lives well.

Guṇavantehi purisehi lokā sucaranti

Through virtuous men, the worlds live well.

Guṇavato purisassa sakkāraṃ karoti

He pays homage to the virtuous man.

Guṇavataṃ purisānaṃ sakkāraṃ karonti

They pay homage to virtuous men.

Guṇavatā purisasmā bhayaṃ nūppajjati

Fear does not arise from a virtuous person.

Guṇavantehi purisehi bhayāni nūppajjanti

Fears do not arise from virtuous men.

Guṇavato purisassa kittisaddo aṅguggacchati

The fame of a virtuous person spreads abroad.

Guṇavataṃ purisānaṃ guṇaghoso hoti

There is a proclamation of the virtues of virtuous men.

Guṇavante purise me ramati mato

My mind delights in the virtuous man.

Guṇavantesu purisesu sappuriso pasīdati.

A good person is pleased with virtuous men.

Iti catuttho pāṭho.

Thus the fourth lesson.

Bho gacchantā yaññadattā maṃ pāletha

O Yaññadattas, as you go, protect me.

The brahmin asks the going Yaññadatta for a blanket.

Gacchante yaññadatte kambalaṃ yācayati dvijo

The brahmin asks the going Yaññadattas for a blanket.

Gacchatā yaññadattena rukkho pupphāni avacīyate

The tree is being plucked of flowers by the going Yaññadatta.

Gacchantehi yaññadattehi rukkho pupphāni avacito

The tree was plucked of flowers by the going Yaññadattas.

Gacchatā yaññadattena koci maggaṃ jānāti

Through the going Yaññadatta, someone knows the path.

Gacchantehi yaññadattehi kecimaggaṃ jānanti

Through the going Yaññadattas, some know the path.

Gacchato yaññadattassa chattaṃ dhārayate

Gacchataṃ yaññadattānaṃ chattāni dhārayante

Umbrellas are held for the going Yaññadattas.

Gacchatā yaññadattasmā bhayaṃ nūppajjati

Gacchantehi yaññadattehi bhayāni nūppajjanti

No fears arise from the going Yaññadattas.

Gacchato yaññadattassa chatto hoti

The going Yaññadatta has an umbrella.

Gacchataṃ yaññadattānaṃ ābharaṇāni honti

The going Yaññadattas have ornaments.

Gacchante yaññadatte koci pasīdati

Someone is pleased with the going Yaññadatta.

Gacchantesu yaññadattesu keci pasīdanti.

Some are pleased with the going Yaññadattas.

Iti [O 0.0004] pañcamo pāṭho.

Thus, the fifth lesson.

Ikārantapulliṅgo aggisaddo vuccate.

The masculine noun 'aggi', ending in 'i', is explained.

Aggi pana kaṭṭhamaṅgāraṃ karoti

Aggayo kaṭṭhamaṅgāraṃ karonti

Fires make firewood into charcoal.

Bho aggi tvaṃ sītaṃ vinodehi

O fire, dispel the cold.

Bho aggī tumhe sītaṃ vinodetha

O fires, dispel the cold.

Aggiṃ nibbāpeti yo koci

Whoever extinguishes a fire.

Aggayo nibbāpenti ye keci

Those who extinguish fires.

Agginā koci akāro daḍḍho

By fire, a certain house was burnt.

Aggīhi keci agārā daḍḍhā

By fires, some houses were burnt.

Agginā kiñci āgāraṃ jhāpeti

With fire, someone sets a house ablaze.

Aggīhi keci agāre jhāpeti

With fires, some set houses ablaze.

Aggino upādānaṃ dadāti brāhmaṇo

A brahmin gives fuel to the fire.

Aggīnaṃ upādānaṃ dadanti brāhmaṇā

Brahmins give fuel to the fires.

Agginā dhūmo apeti niccaṃ

Smoke always departs from fire.

Aggīhi’dhūmāpi apenti niccaṃ

Also, smokes always depart from fires.

Aggino āloko ca hoti

And from fire there is light.

Aggīnaṃ ālokāpi honti

Fires also have lights.

Aggimhi yo koci pasīdati

Whoever finds delight in fire.

Aggīsu ye keci pasīdanti.

Whoever finds delight in fires.

Iti chaṭṭho pāṭho.

Thus is the sixth lesson.

Ādinā parisuddhena pana bhūyate

Ādinā samaggo saṅgho sucarati

Thus is the seventh lesson.

This person has fields and lands.

Imesaṃ janānañca pahūtadhanadhaññāni

And these people have abundant wealth and grain.

Imamhi janasmiṃ pasīdati yo koci

Whoever has confidence in this person.

Imesu janesu pasīdanti ye keci.

Whoever are pleased with these people.

Iti ekūnavīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus the nineteenth lesson.

Itthiliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

Feminine gender forms are spoken of.

Ayaṃ kaññā pana bahuṃ puññaṃ pasavati

This maiden, however, generates much merit.

Imā kaññāyo bahuṃ puññaṃ pasavanti

These maidens generate much merit.

Imaṃ kaññaṃ puññaṃ kārāpeti mātā

The mother has this maiden perform merit.

Imā kaññāyo puññaṃ kārāpeti mātā

The mother has these maidens perform merit.

Imāya [O 0.0029] kaññāya pana tilāni bhajjīyante

By this maiden, however, sesame seeds are parched.

Imāhi kaññāhi pana dhaññāni bhajjīyante

By these maidens, however, grains are parched.

Imāya kaññāya sukhaṃ pāpuṇāti mātā

Through this maiden, the mother attains happiness.

Imāhi kaññāhi sukhaṃ pāpuṇāti mātā

Through these maidens, the mother attains happiness.

Imissā kaññāya ābharaṇaṃ deti sāmi

The master gives an ornament to this maiden.

Imāsaṃ kaññānaṃ ābharaṇaṃ deti sāmi

The master gives an ornament to these maidens.

Imāya kaññāya pana apeti yo koci

From this maiden, however, whoever departs.

Imāhi kaññāhi pana apeti yo koci

From these maidens, however, whoever departs.

Imissā kaññāya pana ābharaṇāni honti

This maiden, however, has ornaments.

Imāsaṃ kaññānaṃ pana ābharaṇāni honti

These maidens, however, have ornaments.

Imissaṃ kaññāyaṃ pana cittaṃ patiṭṭhitaṃ

In this maiden, however, the mind is established.

Imāsu kaññāsu cittaṃ pana napatiṭṭhitaṃ.

In these maidens, however, the mind is not established.

Iti vīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus the twentieth lesson.

Napuṃsakaliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

Neuter gender forms are spoken of.

Imaṃ cittaṃ pana attano santānaṃ vijānāti

This mind, however, knows its own continuum.

Imāni cittāni attano santānaṃ vijānanti

These minds know their own continuum.

Imaṃ cittaṃ saññamessanti ye keci

Whoever will restrain this mind.

Ime citte saññamessanti ye keci

Those who will restrain these minds.

Iminā cittena sabbopi jano nīyyati

By this mind, all people are led.

Imehi cittehi sabbe janā nīyyanti

By these minds, all people are led.

Iminā cittena yo koci saṃkilissati

By this mind, anyone becomes defiled.

Imehi cittehi yo koci visujjhati

By these minds, anyone is purified.

Imassa cittassa ovādaṃ deti yo koci

Anyone gives instruction to this mind.

Imesaṃ cittānaṃ ovādaṃ denti ye keci

Those who give advice to these minds.

Imasmā cittamhā ārammaṇaṃ uppajjati

From this mind, an object arises.

Imehi cittehi ārammaṇāni uppajjanti

From these minds, objects arise.

Imassa cittassa aniccadhammassa vasaṃ anvagū

He came under the power of the impermanent nature of this mind.

Imesānaṃ cittānaṃ parivitakko udapādi

Reflection arose in these minds.

Asmiṃ citte arakkhite kāyakammaṃ arakkhitaṃ

When this mind is unguarded, bodily action is unguarded.

Imesu cittesu guttesu kāyakammaṃ rakkhitaṃ.

When these minds are guarded, bodily action is protected.

Iti ekavīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus the twenty-first lesson.

Pulliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

Masculine forms are spoken.

Asu mahārājā catuhi saṅgahavatthūhi janaṃ toseti

That great king pleases the people with the four bases of benevolence.

Amū mahārājā catūhisaṅgahavatthūhi janaṃ tosenti

Those great kings please the people with the four bases of benevolence.

Amuṃ mahārājānaṃ sakkaccaṃ upasaṅkamati mahājano

The great multitude respectfully approaches that great king.

Amūyo mahārāje sakkaccaṃ upasaṅkamati mahājano

The great multitude respectfully approaches those great kings.

Amunā [O 0.0030] mahārājenapi mahāpāsādo kārāpīyate

By that great king, a great palace is also caused to be built.

Amūhi mahārājehi mahāvihāropi kārāpito

By those great kings, a great monastery was also caused to be built.

Amunā mahārājena kho pana mahājano sucarati

Under that great king, indeed, the great multitude lives well.

Amūhi mahārājehi kho pana mahājano sucarati

Under those great kings, indeed, the great multitude lives well.

Amussa mahārājassa paṇṇākāraṃ deti mahājano

The populace gives tribute to that great king.

Amūsānaṃ mahārājānaṃ paṇṇākāraṃ denti mahājanā

The great multitudes give tribute to those great kings.

Amusmā mahārājamhā pana mahabbhayaṃ uppajjati

From that great king, however, great fear arises.

Amūhi mahārājehi mahabbhayāni uppajjanti

From those great kings, great fears arise.

Amūssa mahārājassa vappamaṅgalañca hoti

For that great king, there is also a ploughing festival.

Amūsānaṃ mahārājānaṃ vappamaṅgalāni honti

For those great kings, there are ploughing festivals.

Amusmiṃ mahārāje kho pana mahājano pasīdati

However, the great multitude has confidence in that great king.

Amūsu mahārājesu kho pana mahājanā pasīdanti.

However, the people have faith in those great kings.

Iti dvevīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus ends the twenty-second lesson.

Itthiliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

The feminine forms are stated.

Asu upāsikā pana sakkaccaṃ dhammaṃ suṇāti

That female lay follower, however, respectfully listens to the Dhamma.

Amuyo upāsikāyo sakkaccaṃ dhammaṃ suṇanti

Those female lay followers respectfully listen to the Dhamma.

Amuṃ upāsikaṃ puññaṃ kārāpeti saddho

The faithful one causes that female lay follower to make merit.

Amuyo upāsikāyo puññaṃ kārāpeti saddho

The faithful one causes those female lay followers to make merit.

Amuyā upāsikāya bhikkhu bhattaṃ bhojāpīyate

A monk is offered a meal by that female lay follower.

Amūhi upāsikāhi saṅgho bhattaṃ bhojāpito

The Sangha was offered a meal by those female lay followers.

Amuyā upāsikāya yo koci pana sucarati

Because of that female lay follower, however, someone conducts himself well.

Amūhi upāsikāhi ye keci pana sucaranti

Because of those female lay followers, indeed, some people conduct themselves well.

Amussā upāsikāya dānaṃ deti sappuriso

The virtuous person gives a gift to that female lay follower.

Amūsaṃ upāsikānaṃ dānaṃ denti sappurisā

Virtuous people give a gift to those female lay followers.

Amuyā upāsikāyapi yo koci apeti

Also, someone departs from that female lay follower.

Amūhi upāsikāhi ye keci pana apenti

Indeed, some people depart from those female lay followers.

Amussā upāsikāya puttāpi bahavo honti

That female lay follower also has many sons.

Amūsānaṃ upāsikānaṃ parisāpi bahavo

Those female lay followers also have many retinues.

Amussaṃ upāsikāyaṃ yo koci pasīdati

Someone has faith in that female lay follower.

Amūsu upāsikāsu pana ye keci pasīdanti.

Moreover, some people have faith in those female lay followers.

Iti tevīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus ends the twenty-third lesson.

Napuṃsakaliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

The neuter forms are stated.

Aduṃ dhanavaṃ niccaṃ bahuṃ puññaṃ pasavati

That wealthy one constantly generates much merit.

Amūni dhanavantāni bahuṃ puññaṃ pasavanti

Those wealthy ones generate much merit.

Aduṃ dhanavantaṃ puññaṃ kārāpeti saddho

The faithful one causes that wealthy one to make merit.

Amūni dhanavantāni puññaṃ kārāpeti saddho

The faithful one causes those wealthy ones to make merit.

Amunā [O 0.0031] dhanavantena bhikkhu bhattaṃ bhojāpīyate

A monk is offered a meal by that wealthy one.

Amūhi dhanavantehi bhikkhu bhattaṃ bhojāpito

A monk was offered a meal by those wealthy ones.

Amunā dhanavantena mahājanopi sukhījāto

Because of that wealthy one, even the multitude became happy.

Amūhi dhanavattehi mahājanopi sukhījāto

Because of those wealthy ones, even the multitude became happy.

Amuno dhanavantassa suvaṇṇachattaṃ dhārayate

For that wealthy one, a golden umbrella is held.

Amūsaṃ dhanavantānaṃ upatiṭṭheyya ekacco

A certain one might attend upon those wealthy ones.

Amusmā dhanavantamhā lābhasakkāraṃ labheyya

From that wealthy person, one might obtain gain and honor.

Amūhi dhanavantehi lābhasakkārāni labheyyuṃ

From those wealthy ones, they might obtain gains and honors.

Amuno dhanavantassa mahāparivāro atthi

That wealthy man has a large retinue.

Amūsānaṃ dhanavantānaṃ mahāparivārā vijjanti

Great retinues exist for those wealthy ones.

Amumhi dhanavantasmiṃ yo koci pasīdati

Whoever is pleased with that wealthy one.

Amūsu dhanavantesu ye keci pasīdanti.

Whoever are pleased with those wealthy ones.

Iti catuvīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus, the twenty-fourth lesson.

Pulliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

The masculine forms are recited.

Kohi nāma budho loke vasaṃ kodhassa gacchati;

Ke hitvā mānusaṃ dehaṃ dibbaṃ yogaṃ upaccaguṃ.

Who indeed, being wise, in the world falls under the sway of anger? Who, having abandoned the human body, have attained the divine state?

Kaṃsi tvaṃ assu uddissa pabbajito ca āvuso;

Keci puññāni katvāna kittakā tidivaṃ gatā.

For whom, friend, have you gone forth? Some, having performed meritorious deeds—how many have gone to heaven?

Kenāyaṃ pakato satto kuvaṃ sattassa kārako;

Kehidaṃ pakataṃ bimbaṃ kvannu bibbassa kārako.

By whom was this being created? Where is the creator of the being? By what was this image created? Where indeed is the creator of the image?

Kena te tādiso vaṇṇo kena te idha mijjhati;

Kehi me puññakammehi mamaṃ rakkhanti devatā.

By what is your complexion such? By what do you prosper here? By what meritorious deeds of mine do the deities protect me?

Kassa cābhirato satto sabbadukkhā pamuccati;

Kesaṃ divāca rattoca sadā puññaṃ pavaḍḍhati.

A being delighting in whom is freed from all suffering? For whom does merit ever grow, both day and night?

Kasmā naparidevesi evarūpe mahabbhaye;

Kehi nāma ariyehi puthageva jano ayaṃ.

Why do you not lament in such great peril? By what indeed is this person distinguished from the noble ones?

Kassa tvaṃ dhammamaññāya vācaṃ bhasayi īdisaṃ;

Kesaṃ te dhammamaññāya acchiduṃ bhavabhandhanaṃ.

Having understood whose Dhamma, did you utter such a speech? Having understood whose Dhamma did they sever the bonds of existence?

Kamhi kāle tayā vīra patthitā bodhimuttamā;

Kesuddhānesu muninda sāvako paritibbutoti.

At what time, O hero, did you aspire to supreme enlightenment? In what pure states, O lord of sages, is a disciple completely stilled?

Iti pañcavīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus, the twenty-fifth lesson.

Itthiliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

The feminine forms are recited.

Kā ca suphassayaṃ dānaṃ mañcapīṭhādikaṃ adā;

Kāyo nānāvidhaṃ puññaṃ katvāna tidivaṃ gatā.

Who gave a gift pleasant to touch, such as beds and chairs? Who, having done various meritorious deeds, went to heaven?

Kaṃ [O 0.0032] bhāvanañca bhāveti kaṃ sīlaṃ paripālayī;

Kā nāma dāsiyo kamme kārāpayati sāmiko.

Which meditation does one cultivate, and which virtue does one observe? Which female servants by name does the master have perform the work?

Kāya upāsikāyassu dhammo ca sūyate sadā;

Kāhi ca sīlavatīhi dhammo ca sūyate sadā.

For which female lay follower is the Dhamma always heard? And by which virtuous women is the Dhamma always heard?

Kāya vijjāya me putto pāpuṇāti idaṃ sukhaṃ;

Kāhi sikkhāhi me puttā pāpuṇanti idaṃ sukhaṃ.

By what knowledge does my son attain this happiness? By what trainings do my sons attain this happiness?

Kassā upāsikāyassu dānaṃ dadeyya dhanavā;

Kāsaṃ upāsikānañca dānaṃ dadeyya guṇavā.

To which female lay follower would a wealthy person give a gift? And to which female lay followers would a virtuous person give a gift?

Kāya gaṅgāya sabbāca pabhavantīti kunnadī;

Kāhi ca pana nadīhi pabhavanti mahānadī.

From which river do all the small rivers originate? And from which rivers do the great rivers originate?

Kassā kho pana gaṅgāya mahogho hoti sabbadā;

Kāsaṃ kho pana kaññānaṃ ābharaṇā bhavanti ca.

Of which river is there always a great flood? And which maidens possess ornaments?

Kassaṃ nadiṃ pana macchā niccaṃ vicaranti sadā;

Kāsu gaṅgāsu kho macchā niccaṃ vicaranti sadā.

In which river do fish always roam? In which rivers do fish always roam?

Iti chabbīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus, the twenty-sixth lesson.

Napuṃsakaliṅgarūpāni vuccante.

The neuter forms are recited.

Kiṃ te jaṭāhi dummedha kiṃ te ajinasāṭiyā;

Kāni cittāni jāyanti kathaṃ jānema taṃ mayaṃ.

What use are matted locks to you, O fool? What use is your antelope-hide garment? What thoughts arise? How may we know them?

Kiṃ tvaṃ attavasaṃ disvā mama dajjāsimaṃ dhanaṃ;

Kāni puññāni katvāna kittakā tidivaṃ gatā.

Seeing what advantage for yourself would you give me this wealth? Having done what meritorious deeds, how many have gone to heaven?

Kenassu nīyati loko kenassu parikassati;

Kehi me puññakammassa nāntamevañca dissati.

By what indeed is the world led? By what indeed is it dragged along? And by what means is the end of my meritorious deeds thus not seen?

Kenāsi dummano tāta purisaṃ kannu yocasi;

Kehi pupphehi sakkaccaṃ saddho yajati gotamaṃ.

Why are you dejected, dear one? For what man do you indeed lament? With what flowers does the faithful one respectfully worship Gotama?

Kassa so kayirā mettiṃ tamāhu cariyaṃ budhā;

Kesaṃ dānavaraṃ etaṃ dātabbañca sadādarā.

For whom should one cultivate loving-kindness? The wise call that conduct. And to whom should this excellent gift always be given with respect?

Kasmā coro ahu me tvaṃ iti rājā apucchitaṃ;

Kehi nāmapi hetūhi jāyantīti ime janā.

'Why were you a thief to me?' thus the king asked him. By what causes indeed are these people born?

Kassa te dhammamaññāya acchiduṃ bhavabhandhanaṃ;

Kesaṃ majjhagato bhāti candova nabhamajjhago.

Having understood whose Dhamma, did they sever the bond of existence? In the midst of whom does he shine, like the moon in the middle of the sky?

Kasmiṃ me sivayo kuddhā nāhaṃ passāmi dukkaṭaṃ;

Kesu puññesu yojeti sakhīnaṃ so sakhāhitoti.

In what respect are the auspicious ones angry with me? I see no wrongdoing. In what meritorious deeds does that friend engage his friends for their welfare?

Iti [O 0.0033] sattavīsatimo pāṭho.

Thus the twenty-seventh lesson.

Iti padamañjariyā sabbanāmānaṃ.

In you men, some have faith.

Dasassa gaṇanassa dasaguṇitaṃ katvā sataṃ hoti.

Multiplying the number ten by ten, it becomes a hundred.

Satassa dasaguṇitaṃ katvā sahassaṃ hoti.

Multiplying a hundred by ten, it becomes a thousand.

Dasasahassassa dasaguṇitaṃ katvā satasahassaṃ hoti.

Multiplying ten thousand by ten, it becomes a hundred thousand.

Taṃ lakkhanti vuccati, satasahassassa dasaguṇitaṃ katvā dasasatasahassaṃ hoti.

That is called a lakkha; multiplying a hundred thousand by ten, it becomes a million.

Dasasatasahassassa dasaguṇitaṃ katvā koṭi hoti.

Multiplying a million by ten, it becomes a koṭi.

Satasahassānaṃ sataṃ koṭi nāmāti attho.

The meaning is that a hundred hundred-thousands is called a koṭi.

Koṭisatasahassānaṃ sataṃ pakoṭi.

A hundred hundred-thousands of koṭis is a pakoṭi.

Pakoṭisatasahassānaṃ sataṃ koṭippakoṭi.

A hundred hundred-thousands of pakoṭis is a koṭippakoṭi.

Koṭippakoṭi satasahassānaṃ sataṃ nahutaṃ.

A hundred hundred-thousands of koṭippakoṭis is a nahuta.

Nahutasatasahassānaṃ sataṃ ninnahutaṃ

A hundred hundred-thousands of nahutas is a ninnahuta.

Ninnahutasatasahassānaṃ sataṃ akkhohiṇi.

A hundred hundred-thousands of ninnahutas is an akkhohiṇī.

Aparo nayo-ekaṃ dasaṃ sataṃ sahassaṃ

Another method: one, ten, a hundred, a thousand.

Dasasahassaṃ satasahassaṃ dasasatasahassaṃ

Ten thousand, a hundred thousand, a million.

Koṭi pakoṭi koṭippakoṭi nahutaṃ

Koṭi, pakoṭi, koṭippakoṭi, nahuta.

Ninnahutaṃ akkhohiṇīti evaṃ

Ninnahuta, akkhohiṇī—thus.

Ekato paṭṭhāya guṇīyamānā akkhohiṇi

Beginning from one, when multiplied, it is an akkhohiṇī.

Terasama ṭhānaṃ hutvā tiṭṭhati.

It stands as the thirteenth place.

Nava nāgasahassāni nāge nāge sataṃ rathā

Nine thousand elephants; for each elephant, a hundred chariots.

Rathe rathe sataṃ assā asse asse sataṃ narā

For each chariot, a hundred horses; for each horse, a hundred men.

Nare nare sataṃ kaññā eke kissaṃ satitthiyo

For each man, a hundred maidens; for one, why a hundred women?

Esā akkhohiṇī nāma pubbācariyehi bhāsitāti.

This is called an akkhohiṇī, spoken by the ancient teachers.

Akkhohiṇīca bhindūca abbudaṃca nirabbudaṃ

Akkhohiṇī, and bhindū, and abbuda, and nirabbuda.

Ahahaṃ ababañceva aṭaṭaṃca sugandhikaṃ

Ahaha, and Ababa, and Aṭaṭa, and Sugandhika.

Uppalaṃ kumudañceva puṇḍarīkaṃ padumaṃ tathā

Uppala, and Kumuda, Puṇḍarīka, and Paduma likewise.

Kathānaṃ mahākathānaṃ asaṃkheyyanti bhāsito.

Of Kathāna, Mahākathāna, it is said they are countless.

Kamo kaccāyane eso pāliyā so virujjhati

O Kaccāyana, this sequence conflicts with the Pāli.

Pāliyantu kamo evaṃ veditabbo nirabbudā

But in the Pāli, the sequence should be understood thus: Nirabbuda,

Ababaṃ aṭaṭaṃ ahahaṃ kumudaṃca sugandhikaṃ

Ababa, Aṭaṭa, Ahaha, and Kumuda, Sugandhika,

Uppalaṃ puṇḍarīkaṃca padumanti jinobravīti.

Uppala, and Puṇḍarīka, and Paduma—thus spoke the Conqueror.
