Samantakūṭavaṇṇanā [O 0.0001]

A Description of the Samantakūṭa Mountain

Namo tassa bhagavato arahato sammāsambuddhassa

Homage to the Blessed One, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Enlightened One.

1.

1.

Satatavitata kittiṃ dhastakandappadappaṃ

Tibhavahitavidhānaṃ sabbalokekaketuṃ,

Amitamatimanagghaṃ santidaṃ merusāraṃ

Sugatamahamudāraṃ rūpasāraṃ namāmi;

I bow to the Sugata, the noble one, the essence of form, whose fame is ever widespread, who has crushed the pride of the God of Love, who has brought about the welfare of the three realms, the sole banner of all worlds, of immeasurable wisdom, priceless, the bestower of peace, the essence of Meru.

2.

2.

Hataduritatusāraṃ mohapaṅkopatāpaṃ

Manakamalavikāsaṃ jantunaṃ sesakānaṃ,

Kumatikumudanāsaṃ buddhapubbācalaggā

Uditamahamudāraṃ dhammabhānuṃ namāmi;

I bow to the great and noble Dhamma-sun, arisen from the peak of the Buddha's eastern mountain, which has destroyed the frost of evil deeds and the affliction of the mud of delusion, which causes the mind-lotus of other beings to blossom, and which destroys the water-lilies of wrong views.

3.

3.

Sakalavimalasīlaṃ dhutapāpārijālaṃ

Suranaramahanīyaṃ pāhuneyyāhuneyyaṃ,

Ujupathapaṭipannaṃ puññakhettaṃ janānaṃ

Gaṇamahamabhivande sāradaṃ sādarena;

I reverently salute the excellent Sangha, the field of merit for people, pure in all virtue, who have shaken off the net of evil enemies, honored by gods and men, worthy of hospitality and offerings, who have entered upon the straight path.

4.

4.

Iti kaḷita paṇāmā hantva sabbopasagge

Sugatavimala pādambhojasampāta pūtaṃ,

Sumanasikharirājaṃ vaṇṇayissaṃ surānaṃ

Vasati matulametaṃ sādhujantu suṇantu;

Thus, having performed this obeisance and destroyed all obstacles, I shall describe this unparalleled abode of the gods, the king of mountains, Sumana, purified by the touch of the Sugata’s stainless lotus feet. May righteous beings listen.

5.

5.

Savaṇā lapanā ceva satiyā cāpi vandanā,

Yassa sammā sukhī hotī nibbāṇañcādhigacchati;

By hearing of it, reciting it, and paying homage with mindfulness, one becomes truly happy and also attains Nibbāna.

6. [O 0.0002]

6.

Tasmā sappurisehe’daṃ patthentehi tisampadaṃ,

Savaṇīyañhi sādhūhi acikkhittena cetasā;

Therefore, by good people who aspire to the three accomplishments, this should indeed be listened to with an unagitated mind.

7.

7.

Yo lokatilako nātho pūretvā dasa pāramī,

Jātosi tusite kāye santusito’ti vissuto;

The Lord, the crest-jewel of the world, having fulfilled the ten perfections, was born in the Tusita realm, renowned as Santusita.

8.

8.

Ārādhito surādīhi kālo mārisa te ayaṃ,

Tiṇṇo tārayimaṃ lokaṃ bahū apparajakkhakā;

“Implored by the gods and others, this, esteemed one, is your time. You have crossed over; lead this world to cross over, for many have but little dust in their eyes.”

9.

9.

Etadatthāya te vīra pūritā dasa pāramī,

Bāhuṃ pitussa putto’va loko ālambate tuvaṃ;

“For this very purpose, O hero, have the ten perfections been fulfilled by you. Like a son holding his father's arm, the world depends on you.”

10.

10.

Tesaṃ taṃ vacanaṃ sutvā mahāsatto mahāmatī,

Vilokane viloketvā pañcāmala vilocano;

Hearing their words, the great being of great wisdom, with his five pure eyes, made the great surveyings;

11.

11.

Jambudīpaggakamale kaṇṇikā’va manoramā,

Purī kapiḷavatthū’si visāṇā’va sudhāsinaṃ;

In the foremost lotus of Jambudīpa, like its delightful pericarp, was the city of Kapilavatthu, like the horn of the moon.

12.

12.

Siṅgīnikkhamayābhāsa tuṅgasiṅgasamaṅgitā,

Maṇisīhapañjarākiṇṇa pāsādā yattha bhāsare;

Where palaces shine, endowed with towering peaks lustrous as pure gold, crowded with jeweled lion-windows.

13.

13.

Saghane gagane niccaṃ sateratasatāni’va,

Anileritapajjota soṇṇaketu ahū yahiṃ;

Where, in the dense sky, like hundreds of stars, the golden banner, stirred by the wind, was ever a shining lamp.

14.

14.

Janānaṃ nīlanettehi vadanehi tu yā purī,

Madhupālisamonaddha kañjinī sirimāvahe;

That city, with the dark eyes and faces of its people, bore the splendor of a lotus pond covered with rows of bees.

15.

15.

Raṅgatuṅgaturaṅgehi gajjitehi ca yā purī,

Saghosuttuṅgakallola lolasāgarasantibhā;

That city, with the neighing of its spirited, tall horses, had the semblance of the restless ocean with its loud, high waves.

16.

16.

Avhamānā’va devānaṃ puralakhyā bhujā iva,

Mandānileritā tuṅga nānārāgaddhajā yahiṃ;

Where lofty flags of various colors, stirred by the gentle breeze, were like arms, as if calling the gods to the splendors of the city.

17.

17.

Nānāvesadharā yasmiṃ nānābhūsaṇa bhūsitā,

Nānāsampattisaṃyuttā nānāvijjāsu pāragā;

Wherein dwelt people wearing various garbs, adorned with various ornaments, endowed with various riches, and proficient in various sciences;

18. [O 0.0003]

18.

Navayobbana uddāmā rūpena ca manoharā,

Saccesu niratā niccaṃ anakkuṭṭhakulā siyuṃ;

Exuberant in fresh youth, captivating in form, ever delighting in truths, they were of impeccable lineage.

19.

19.

Niccaṃ kīḷā vidaddhāhi naranārīhi sevitā,

Yā purī sādhurūpā’si madhurālāpinīhi ca;

That city was always frequented by playful and clever men and women, and was beautiful with its sweet-speaking people.

20.

20.

Tasmiṃ saddhādayopeta okkākakulaketuko,

Suddhodanavhayo āsī vissuto bhuvanattaye;

In that city, there was one named Suddhodana, the banner of the Okkāka lineage, endowed with faith and other virtues, renowned in the three worlds.

21.

21.

Yassaṅghikamale sabba bhubhujānaṃ mahītale,

Kirīṭamaṇihiṅgāli kīḷantī’va nirantaraṃ;

At his lotus-like feet, the vermilion from the crown jewels of all the kings on earth seemed to play incessantly.

22.

22.

Yassa patthaṭatejena puranetāpi divākaro,

Vahanīca’mbare līlaṃ osadhīpatino divā;

By his spreading radiance, even the sun, the leader of the day, bore in the sky the likeness of the lord of herbs by day.

23.

23.

Yassa dānappavāho tu nānāyācaka jantunaṃ,

Manodadhīsu velantetikkamantova sattataṃ;

His stream of generosity to the various supplicants flowed continuously, as if surpassing the shores of their mind-oceans.

24.

24.

Matiyā suramantīva dhanena dhanado viya,

Rūpena kusumesu’va yo jumhati mahītale;

By wisdom, like the counselor of the gods; by wealth, like Dhanada; by beauty, like the God of Love, he shone upon the earth.

25.

25.

Tassābhisittā rajjena mahāmāyāti vissutā,

Candikā viya candassa girirājā’va kapālito;

His anointed queen, renowned as Mahāmāyā, was like moonlight to the moon, like the king of mountains to the Skull-Bearer.

26.

26.

Lakkhī’va vāsudevassa sītā’va rāmarājino,

Mahesīsi varārohā sundarī sundarādharā;

Like Lakkhī to Vāsudeva, like Sītā to King Rāma, she was his great queen, of noble bearing, beautiful, with lovely lips.

27.

27.

Tassā kucchikaraṇḍamhi anaggharatanaṃ viya,

Khattiṃsāhi nimittehi vimhāpento sadevakaṃ;

In the casket of her womb, like a priceless jewel, with thirty-two signs, astonishing the world with its gods,

28.

28.

Sitambujakaro santo sitebhacchāpako viya,

Katvā padakkhiṇaṃ mātu paṭisandhimagaṇhi so;

The serene one, with hands like white lotuses, like a white elephant calf, having circumambulated his mother, took conception.

29.

29.

Dasekādasamāsena tassevaṃ āsi cetanā,

Passituṃ sakañātīnaṃ gantvāna nagaraṃ tadā;

In the tenth or eleventh month, this intention arose in her: to go to that city to see her own kinsfolk.

30. [O 0.0004]

30.

Nivedetvā tamatthaṃ sā rañño tena susajjite,

Saparicchadā tadā magge gacchanti antarāpathe;

Having informed the king of that matter, she, with her retinue, then set out on the road well-prepared by him, proceeding along the way.

31.

31.

Devatānaṃ manonandakara nandana sannibhaṃ,

Disvāna lumbiniṃ nāma uyyānaṃ mananandanaṃ;

Having seen the park named Lumbinī, a delight to the mind, resembling the Nandana garden that brings joy to the minds of the gods.

32.

32.

Tasmiṃ kīḷitumussāhā pavisitvāna taṃ vanaṃ,

Kīḷanti upagantvāna maṅgalaṃ sālapādapaṃ;

Eager to sport therein, they entered that grove, and having approached the auspicious sāla tree, they sported.

33.

33.

Vilola pallavākiṇṇaṃ suphullakusumonataṃ,

Gahetvā sālasākhaṃ sā surattakara pallavā;

She, with hands like tender red leaves, grasped a sāla branch laden with swaying sprouts and bent low with fully blossomed flowers.

34.

34.

Devehi gahitā’rakkhā mahamāne sadevake,

Janesi tanayaṃ māyā tatraṭṭhā lokalocanaṃ;

Guarded by the gods, and honored by the world with its gods, Māyā, standing there, gave birth to a son, the eye of the world.

35.

35.

Brahmāno lokapālā ca manussā kamato tadā,

Soṇṇajālājinādīhi gaṇhiṃsu jananandanaṃ;

Then Brahmās, the world-protectors, and humans, in that order, received the delight of the people with golden nets, antelope skins, and so forth.

36.

36.

Mahiṃ patiṭṭhito dhīro passitvāna tato disā,

Uttarābhimukho sattapadaṃ gaṇtvā’mbuje ṭhito;

The wise one, established on the earth, having surveyed the directions, took seven steps facing north and stood upon a lotus.

37.

37.

Disantamavaloketvā suphullambujalocano,

Nicchāresāsabhiṃ vācaṃ aggo seṭṭho’tiādinā;

With eyes like fully blossomed lotuses, gazing upon all directions, he uttered a noble speech, beginning with: 'I am the foremost, I am the best.'

38.

38.

Brahmāmaranarādīhi pūjito ca namassito,

Kamena abhivaḍḍhanto juṇhapakkhe sasī yathā;

Worshipped and revered by Brahmās, gods, humans, and others, he grew gradually, like the moon in the bright fortnight.

39.

39.

Brahmūnaṃ chattachāyāya mandārakusumantare,

Sānandāmandadevehi dhutacāmaramajjhago;

Under the shade of the Brahmās' parasols, amidst mandāra flowers, in the midst of chowries waved by greatly joyful gods.

40.

40.

Dibbehi rūpasaddehi gandhehi ca rasehi ca,

Phoṭṭhabbehi ca dibbehi modamāno anekadhā;

Rejoicing in many ways in divine sights, sounds, scents, tastes, and divine tangible objects.

41.

41.

Dibbehi ramaṇīyehi naccehi vāditehi ca,

Padānekasahassehi thūyamānaguṇākaro;

With delightful divine dances and music, he, a mine of virtues, was praised by many thousands of verses.

42. [O 0.0005]

42.

Patvā soḷasamaṃ vassaṃ ñātisaṅghassa majjhago,

Dassetvā’sesasippaṃ taṃ loke vijjati yaṃ tadā;

Having reached his sixteenth year, amidst the assembly of his kinsmen, he displayed all the arts that existed in the world at that time.

43.

43.

Ñātisaṅghaṃ pamodento deve ca manuje’pi ca,

Laddhā yasodharaṃ deviṃ’nukūlaṃ jātijātiyaṃ;

Delighting the assembly of his kinsmen, and gods and humans as well, he obtained the lady Yasodharā, his consort through birth after birth.

44.

44.

Soṇṇadappaṇasaṅkāsa sommānana vibhusitaṃ,

Nīlanīrajasaṅkāsa visālāyata locanaṃ;

Adorned with a gentle face resembling a golden mirror, and with wide, long eyes like blue lotuses.

45.

45.

Siṅgāramandiradvāre dhajopamabhamudvayaṃ,

Hemakāhalasaṅkāsa nāsikaṃ rūpalakkhiyā

With a pair of eyebrows like banners at the gate of a pleasure mansion, and a nose like a golden trumpet, distinguished by beauty.

46.

46.

Nīlavellitadhammilla jimutobhaya koṭiyaṃ,

Niccavijjullatācakka manuññakaṇṇapāsakaṃ;

With blue, wavy locks of hair, the two ends of which were like clouds; with lovely ear-pendants like a circle of ever-shining lightning flashes.

47.

47.

Sātakumbhanibhābhāsa payodharaghaṭadvaṃ,

Suvaṇṇadditaṭāyāta nijjharākārahārakaṃ

With a pair of breasts shining with the lustre of pure gold pots, and necklaces like cascades flowing from the banks of a golden mountain.

48.

48.

Dehadevaddumālamba pārohābhabhujadvayaṃ,

Aṅgulīpallavantambu binducāru nakhāvaliṃ;

With a pair of arms like sprouts hanging from the divine tree of her body; with a row of nails as beautiful as water drops on her sprout-like fingers.

49.

49.

Dehamālālimālābha romarājivirājitaṃ,

Rūpaṇṇavataraṅgāha valittaya vicittakaṃ;

Resplendent with a line of body-hair like a garland of bees on the garland of her body; adorned with three folds like waves on the ocean of her beauty.

50.

50.

Soṇṇarambhāsamāvaṭṭa piṇorudvaya sundaraṃ,

Sannirakalikākāra cārujaṅghā vibhūsitaṃ;

Beautiful with a pair of plump thighs rounded like golden plantains, and adorned with lovely calves shaped like sannīra buds.

51.

51.

Paccakkharūpalakkhiñca līlānicaya sannibhaṃ,

Guṇānamākaraṃ sādhu velaṃva ratisāgare;

She was the very image of beauty, like a collection of graces, an excellent mine of virtues, like the shore of an ocean of delight.

52.

52.

Kanto vasantarājā’va kandappo’va surūpavā,

Sasī’va dassanīyo ca sūriyo viya tejavā;

He was charming like the king of spring, handsome like the god of love, fair to see like the moon, and radiant like the sun.

53.

53.

Acalattena merūva gambhīrenaṇṇavo viya,

Brahmassaro piyaṃvādī pañgñāya ca anūpamo;

In steadfastness like Meru, in depth like the ocean, with a voice like Brahmā's, speaking pleasantly, and incomparable in wisdom.

54. [O 0.0006]

54.

Vasanto so mahāvīro purasmiṃ kapilavhaye,

Disvā nimitte caturo uyyāna gamanañjase;

That great hero, dwelling in the city called Kapilavatthu, saw the four signs on the way to the park.

55.

55.

Pabbajjābhirato nātho gantvā uyyānabhūmiyaṃ,

Suphullacampakāsoka nāgādā’gehi maṇḍitaṃ;

The Lord, intent on renunciation, went to the park grounds, which were adorned with the finest trees, such as blossoming campaka, asoka, and nāga.

56.

56.

Phullapaṅkaja kaḷhāra jalājalaya satākulaṃ,

Mandamandānilāyāta nānāmodehi vāsitaṃ;

A reservoir, teeming with hundreds of water creatures, with blossoming lotuses and water lilies, perfumed by various scents wafted on a gentle breeze.

57.

57.

Sarā saraṃ samāyāta madhubbata nisevitaṃ,

Phalapuppharasuddāma dvija saṅgha nikūjitaṃ;

Lakes joined one another, frequented by bees, abundant with the nectar of fruits and flowers, and resounding with the songs of flocks of birds.

58.

58.

Naccanta mattamorānaṃ niccaṃ maṇḍapasantibhaṃ,

Dibbanta migasagghānaṃ kīḷāmaṇḍapa sannibhaṃ;

Always resembling a pavilion for the dancing of proud peacocks, and like a pleasure pavilion for the sporting herds of deer.

59.

59.

Samīra sisirodāra dhārāsīkara vārihi,

Dhārāmaṇḍapa pantīhi janānandakaraṃ varaṃ;

Excellent, delighting the people with cool, abundant water from streams and sprays, and with rows of water pavilions.

60.

60.

Uyyānavanamāgamma devarājāva nandanaṃ,

Kīḷanto nijaputtassa sutvāna jātasāsanaṃ;

Having approached the park and grove, like the king of gods to the Nandana grove, while playing, he heard the message of his own son's birth.

61.

61.

Nivatto vissakammena sahassakkhova bhūsito,

Puraṃ pavisamānova kisā gotami bhāsitaṃ;

Having returned, adorned as if by Vissakamma, like the thousand-eyed one, while entering the city, he heard the utterance of Kisā Gotamī.

62.

62.

Sutvāna nibbutapadaṃ tadā cittānukūlakaṃ,

Santuṭṭho sānurāgo ca lakkhagghaṃ tārabhāsuraṃ;

Having heard the word of peace, which was then pleasing to his mind, content and filled with fondness, he gave a necklace worth a hundred thousand, shining like the stars.

63.

63.

Hāraṃ tissāya pesetvā gantvāna sakamandiraṃ,

Devarājavilāsena nisīdi pavarāsane;

Having sent the necklace to Tissā, he went to his own palace and sat upon the excellent seat with the splendor of a king of gods.

64.

64.

Athāgamma tadā nekanāṭikā parivārayuṃ,

Vajjanti bheriyo tāsu paggayha kāci nāriyo;

Then many dancing girls came and surrounded him; among them, some women took up and struck the drums.

65.

65.

Nānālayasamākiṇṇaṃ gītaṃ gāyanti kāci’pi,

Dhamanti susiraṃ kāci kāci vādenti tantiyo;

Some sang a song teeming with diverse rhythms; some blew wind instruments, and others played stringed instruments.

66. [O 0.0007]

66.

Cāru bimbādharāramma payodharabharā subhā,

Visālāyata nīlakkhā somasommānanā tadā;

With lovely lips like bimba fruit, fair and bearing heavy breasts, with wide, long, dark eyes, and with pleasant, moon-like faces then.

67.

67.

Naccanti purato tassa bherimaṇḍala majjhagā,

Devakaññāva raṅgamhī rasabhāvanirantaraṃ;

They danced before him in the midst of a circle of drums, like celestial maidens on a stage, continuously filled with aesthetic feeling and emotion.

68.

68.

Disvāna so tamacchariyaṃ viratto visaye tadā,

Urattāḷañca addakkhi sabbantaṃ bheritāḷanaṃ;

Having seen that marvel, he then became dispassionate towards sensual pleasures. He saw the beating of chests and the striking of drums all around.

69.

69.

Paridevaṃ’cupaṭṭhāsi gītaṃ saṃsārasāgare,

Vāyuvega vikāraṃ’va naccaṃ cintesi nāyako;

The song in the ocean of saṃsāra appeared to him as a lamentation; the Leader reflected that the dance was like the changing force of the wind.

70.

70.

’Kadāhaṃ gharamossajja pabbajjāsiri mubbahe?’

Iccevaṃ cintayanto so supantova sayī tahiṃ;

'When shall I abandon the household life and take up the glory of going forth?' Thus reflecting, he lay there as if asleep.

71.

71.

’Yaṃ nissāya mayaṃ ettha naccagītesu vyāvaṭā,

So’yaṃ’dāni supī’ amhe kassadāni karoma taṃ?;

'He for whose sake we are here engaged in dance and song now sleeps; for whom, then, shall we perform?'

72.

72.

Iti cintiya te tattha turiyesu sake sake,

Ālambā sayitā kaññā susaññāññamanissitā;

Thinking thus, the maidens lay down there, each leaning on her own instrument, heedless and unmindful of one another.

73.

73.

Khādanti kāci dantāni kāci lālā vahantiyo,

Kāci rudantiyo tattha vilapanti athā’parā;

Some ground their teeth, some were drooling saliva; some were weeping there, while others lamented.

74.

74.

Kākacchanti’pi semhampi gilanti ca camanti ca,

Karonti nādaṃ nāsāya gharū’ti ca khipanti ca;

Some coughed, swallowed phlegm, and made sipping sounds; some made noises with their noses, and others snorted loudly.

75.

75.

Muttayanti tadā kāci rahassaṃ vicaranti ca,

Duggandhaṃ vāti taṃ ṭhānaṃ susānaṃ āmakaṃ yathā;

Some then urinated, and their private parts were exposed. That place gave off a foul smell, like a fresh charnel ground.

76.

76.

Pabuddho aḍḍharattamhi gate tattha nisīdiya,

Addakkhi tāsaṃ nekāni vikārāni tahiṃ tadā;

Having awakened when midnight had passed, he sat down there and then saw their many disfigurements.

77.

77.

Tassevaṃ pekkhamānassa bhave saṃviggacetaso,

Ādittaṃva upaṭṭhāsi mane khalu bhavattayaṃ;

As he looked on thus, his mind stirred with urgency regarding existence, the triple world indeed appeared to his mind as if ablaze.

78. [O 0.0008]

78.

Dāvānala samāditta mahāraññāyathā gajo,

Tathevāsi narindassa gehato gamane matī;

Just as an elephant in a great forest set ablaze by a wildfire, so was the intention of the lord of men to go forth from his home.

79.

79.

Tato vuṭṭhāya sayanā karonto abhinikkhamaṃ,

Vitakkesi mahārājā passituṃ sakamatrajaṃ;

Then, rising from his bed and making the resolution to go forth, the great king thought to see his own son.

80.

80.

Pavisitvā tato gabbhaṃ sanikaṃ santamānaso,

Passitvā saha puttena niddāyantiṃ yasodharaṃ;

Then, gently entering the chamber with a serene mind, he saw Yasodharā sleeping with their son.

81.

81.

Cirāgataṃ mahāpemaṃ dhārayaṃ sakamānase,

Buddho hutvāpi’maṃ sakkā passituṃ iti cintiya;

Bearing in his own heart the great, long-held affection, he thought, 'It will be possible to see her even after I have become a Buddha.'

82.

82.

Gato nātho tato ṭhānā bodhiyā baddhamānaso

Tadahevāsi buddhoti kāmuko ko na cintaye;

The Protector departed from that place, his mind set on Enlightenment. Who that is desirous would not think, 'On that very day he was a Buddha'?

83.

83.

Sineru muddharantova pāduddhariya nikkhamaṃ,

Channamāhūya ānītaṃ kanthakaṃ turagādhipaṃ;

Lifting his foot, he went forth, as if from the summit of Sineru. Having summoned Channa, he had Kanthaka, the lord of steeds, brought to him.

84.

84.

Assarājaṃ tamāruyha saha channena rattiyaṃ,

Devehi vivaṭadvarā paṭipajji mahāpathaṃ;

Having mounted that king of horses with Channa in the night, he set out upon the great path, the gates having been opened by the devas.

85.

85.

Cakkavāḷesu nekesu devatā susamāgatā,

Dīpadhūpaddhajehe ca gandhamālehi pūjayuṃ;

In many world-systems, deities were well-assembled, and they paid homage with lamps, incense, flags, and fragrant garlands.

86.

86.

Purato saṭṭhīsahassāni daṇḍadīpāni dhārayuṃ,

Tathā dakkhiṇapassamhi vāmapasse ca pacchato;

Before him, sixty thousand deities carried torches on staves; likewise on his right side, on his left side, and behind him.

Thus was the victory of that glorious Perfectly Awakened One; in the final battle with Māra, Māra's was the defeat.

Thus, considering the Guide of Men to be of great power, always venerate and honor him—for he is your refuge always.

387.

Parivārayuṃ gatāgamma pure viya surādayo;

When the Buddha, having gained supreme victory, was seated on the Diamond Seat, gods and other beings surrounded him, coming and going as if in a city.

All those deities, having brought various kinds of offerings, then, being satisfied, performed a great victory celebration for the Sage.

Gentle breezes, arriving with fragrant nectar and moonlight, bring a cool season to the form of the Conqueror.

Developing meditation in the first watch, recollecting the continuity of the aggregates, the Lord of Men attained the knowledge of the recollection of past lives.

In the last watch of the night, contemplating aging and so forth, having gained insight into name-and-form, he applied the three characteristics.

Comprehending, having separated his mind from the defilements, he then attained the supreme fruit through the knowledge of the destruction of the taints.

Having reached the city of Nibbāna, the lord of the jewels of the enlightenment factors, having become the King of the True Dhamma, uttered words of joy.

Through the saṃsāra of many births I have run, not finding the house-builder I was seeking. Painful is birth again and again.

O house-builder, you are seen! You will not build a house again. All your rafters are broken, your ridgepole is shattered. My mind has reached the unconditioned; it has attained the destruction of craving.

Thus it was by the Conqueror—skilled in the method of giving the deathless path, with a heart of compassion and merit, greatly exalted, who traversed the boundless, endless ocean of suffering of becoming—that the supreme perfections were accumulated.

He who, having renounced all prosperity and kingship, having gone forth and reached the foot of the great tree with trembling leaves, sat there and conquered Māra with his army and might, and overcame all evil enemies—may he grant peace.

The Lord of the Three Worlds, the Sugata, then at that time, having uttered an inspired utterance, sitting cross-legged at the foot of the King of Trees, thus contemplated on the Diamond Seat:

"Having accumulated the perfections, beginning with generosity, and having spent countless aeons for the sake of this very excellent seat, that wandering and striving has today come to an end."

"So long as my aspirations are fulfilled, for that long I will remain here and not rise." Having resolved thus, the Victor there engaged in many thousands of meditative attainments.

The God beyond gods, the sole lord of the three existences, who had destroyed opportunity and conquered the five Māras, being honored by Brahmā and others, the Teacher spent seven days there.

Since he did not abandon that seat, but indeed remained seated, endowed with the three connections, therefore a doubt arose in the minds of the gods: 'Is there still something to be done by him?'

Then, near the king of trees, on the northwestern side of the earth, the lords of gods and asuras built a supreme dwelling adorned with various gems. There he sat upon a splendid throne.

The ocean of Dhamma—hard to see, profound, with no near or further shore, with waves of causal relations breaking on all sides, the summit of wisdom's Meru—he spent seven days contemplating it.

Then the Conqueror, having gone to the foot of the Ajapāla tree, enjoying the sweet fruit of liberation, spent a week there—the God beyond gods, foremost in the quality of compassion.

Having heard this news concerning the one with the makara banner, Craving, Discontent, and Passion went trembling in an instant to wherever Māra was lamenting.

Those who move in herds and flocks, such as horse-killers and others—various groups of quadrupeds—dwell there joyfully. Birds too: owls, pigeons, blue-necked peacocks, crows, francolins, cuckoos, and honey-bees.

744.

744.

Nijjivha dindiha cakoraka sāḷikā ca

Cakkavha kīra kurarā kulalā ca kaṅkā,

Citracchadā madhura kūjaka nekapakkhī

Saṅgamma yattha nivasanti manuññarūpā;

Nijjivha birds, ḍiṇḍibha birds, partridges, and mynahs; ruddy shelducks, parrots, ospreys, hawks, and herons; many birds of pleasing forms, with varied plumage and sweet songs, gather and dwell there.

745.

745.

Tesaṃ vanantamatha nāṭakamaṇḍalā’ca

Gītālayaṃ viya ahosi ca gāyakānaṃ,

Āpānabhumi sadisaṃ migapakkhikānaṃ

Niccussavaṃ ratikaraṃ nayanābhirāmaṃ;

Then that forest interior became for them like a stage for dancers and a hall of song for singers, like a drinking-ground for beasts and birds—ever festive, delight-giving, and pleasing to the eye.

746. [O 0.0078]

746.

Evaṃ vidho vipinarāji virājitehi

Kūṭehi nekasura sundari maṇḍitehi,

Atyucca nīlasikhigīva samāna vaṇṇo

Eso samantagiri me vasatī muninda;

Thus, with peaks resplendent with forest ranges and adorned with many celestial beauties, exceedingly lofty, with a hue like the neck of a blue peacock—this Samanta mountain is my abode, O Lord of Sages.

747.

747.

Evaṃ patīta manaso sumanābhidhāno

Vatvāna natvamasamaṃ gamanopayuttaṃ,

Kāsātha sopi muni tassa vacaṃ paṭicca

Sabbhikkhu nikkhami jino gaganāyanamhi;

Thus the one named Sumana, with a delighted mind, having spoken and bowed to the Incomparable One who was ready for departure; then that sage, the Conqueror, having heeded his word, departed into the sky with all the monks.

748.

748.

Niccetanāpi giripādapa ādayopi

Nāgā supaṇṇa miga pakkhika hetukāpi,

Vijjādharāmara’surā caturāṇanāpi

Saṅgamma’kaṃsu sumanā mahamabbhutaṃ te;

Even inanimate mountains, trees, and the like, Nāgas, Supaṇṇas, beasts, and birds, as well as Vidyādharas, gods, asuras, and four-faced Brahmās, having gathered together with joyful minds, performed a great and wonderful homage to you.

749.

749.

Muninde payante samiddhaṃ tilokaṃ

Girindābhinandā dumindā pabuddhā,

Migindā sutuṭṭhā khagindā sughuṭṭhā

Pavuṭṭho mahindo paṇaṭṭho nidāgho;

As the Lord of Sages proceeded, the three worlds prospered. Great mountains rejoiced, great trees awakened, lords of beasts were greatly delighted, lords of birds sweetly sang. Great Indra rained, and the scorching heat was dispelled.

750.

750.

Gacchante gaganāyanena sugate bhānu’si santo tadā

Vāresuṃ sūriyātapañca jaladā siñjiṃsu bhumyā jalaṃ,

Mandāmanda sugandha muddha pavano pāpeti sitaṃ sukhaṃ,

Devādi dhaja chatta cāmarakarā pūjenti mānenti ca;

As the Well-Gone One, peaceful like the sun, moved through the sky-path, clouds restrained the sun's heat and sprinkled water upon the ground. A soft, gentle, fragrant, and charming breeze brought cool comfort. Gods and others, holding banners, parasols, and yak-tail fans, worshipped and honored him.

751.

751.

Sannīra hintāla’ga sindi pūga

Tālambasālādi mahīruhitdā,

Tiṭṭhanti te cāmarahatthakā’va

Pupphehi channo gaganaṅganopi;

The hintāla and sindi trees, rich in water, the pūga, tālamba, sāla, and other great trees, stand like bearers of yak-tail fans, and even the courtyard of the sky is covered with flowers.

752.

752.

Anena vidhinā jagadeka nātho

Pavattamānesu mahāmahesu,

Disañca vidisaṃ paripūrayanto

Jabbaṇṇaraṃsīhi agā nagindaṃ;

In this manner, the sole lord of the world, while great festivals were proceeding, filling the directions and intermediate directions with six-colored rays, went to the king of mountains.

753. [O 0.0079]

753.

Tasmiṃ samantanagamuddhani lokanātho

Chabbaṇṇaraṃsi nikaraṃ disi pattharanto,

Bhikkhūhi so parivuto parasāgarantaṃ

Olokayaṃ ṭhitimakāsi aanomavaṇṇo;

On the supreme peak of Samanta mountain, the Lord of the world, of matchless hue, spreading a multitude of six-hued rays in every direction, surrounded by monks, stood gazing at the farther shore.

754.

754.

Laṅkāvadhū sumanakūṭa kirīṭa kūṭaṃ

Sajjesi’naggha jana rāja maṇī mahanto,

Icchatthadaṃ sivadamappaṭimaṃ tiloke

Taṃ’dāni bho bhajatha sevatha sabbakālaṃ;

Sumanakūṭa, the crown-peak of the bride of Lanka, is adorned with a great, priceless jewel for people and kings, which grants desires, bestows welfare, and is unequaled in the three worlds—now, O sirs, revere and serve it at all times.

755.

755.

Kāsuṃ tadā suravarā surasundarīhi

Laṅkāya selasikharesu mahāsamajjaṃ,

Vajjiṃsu bheri vikatī sayameva sabbā

Bhassiṃsu dibbakusumābharaṇā nabhamhā;

Then the noble gods, with celestial maidens, held a great assembly on the peaks of Lanka’s mountains. All the drums and lutes resounded of themselves, and divine flowers and ornaments rained down from the sky.

756.

756.

Laṅkambaraṃ nikhilamāsi ca chattachattaṃ

Nānā virāga dhaja ketu samākulañca,

Nānāsugandha kusumādi disantarālaṃ,

Nānagghikāvali virājita mantaḷikkhaṃ;

The entire sky of Lanka became a canopy of parasols, crowded with various colorful banners and flags. The intermediate spaces in every direction were filled with various fragrant flowers and the like, and the atmosphere was adorned with rows of priceless jewels.

757.

757.

Tasmiṃ dine’si ratanaṃ maṇitoraṇehi

Dīpāli puṇṇaghaṭapantīhi dassanīyaṃ,

Sambuddha dehaparitogata chappabhāhi

Rattaṃ nabhāvani carācara sabbadabbaṃ;

On that day, it was a precious sight, beautiful with jeweled arches, lines of lamps, and rows of full pots. The six rays issuing from around the body of the Perfectly Enlightened One suffused with red the sky and earth, and all moving and unmoving things.

758.

758.

Mālāvataṃsa samakā girayo samantā

Hutvā namanti ca bhamanti sacetanā’va,

Sabbepi tattha taravoca latādayo ca

Naccanti dibbanaṭakā viya onataggā;

The mountains all around, becoming like garlands and crest-ornaments, bow down and circle as if conscious. All the trees there, as well as creepers and the like, dance like divine dancers, with their tips bent low.

759.

759.

Evaṃ tadā mahata vimhaya pāṭihere

Buddhānubhāva janite idha vattamāne,

Tatvāna dhīracaraṇaṃ samano sudhāsī

Evaṃ vadī parama pitimano udaggo;

Then, while a great and astonishing marvel generated by the Buddha's power was occurring here, the monk of steadfast conduct and nectar-like virtue spoke thus, his mind uplifted with supreme joy:

760. [O 0.0080]

760.

Ye te mudu komala rattapādā

Suratta phullamburuhopamānā,

Vaṭṭānupubbāyata aṅgulikā

Sutambatuṅgagga nakhāvalīkā;

Those feet are soft, tender, and red, like a fully-bloomed red lotus; with gradually rounded, long toes; and with rows of very coppery, high-tipped nails.

761.

761.

Suvaṇṇakummu’nnata pādapiṭṭhi

Niguḷha gopphāyata paṇhibhāgā

Samacchamāyaṃ sakalaṃ patiṭṭhitā

Na limpate succhavitā rajādi;

His instep is arched like a golden tortoise; His ankles are hidden, His heels long; His feet are placed perfectly evenly; His pure skin is not smeared by dust or the like.

762.

762.

Sammatta hatthosabha haṃsa sīha

Samāna līlāya yahiṃ payāti,

Ninnunnatā bheritalā’va bhumi

Hotātha pupphādi sumaṇḍitā ca;

Wherever he goes with the graceful gait of a rutting elephant, a bull, a swan, or a lion, the earth, though uneven like the surface of a drum, becomes well-adorned with flowers and the like.

763.

763.

Apenti maggā sayameva khāṇu

Sakaṇṭamūlā kaṭhalā ca sabbe,

Gambhīra nīrāpaga paṅkaduggā

Hitvā sabhāvaṃ ramaṇīyamenti;

Stumps, thorny roots, and all hard clods of earth vanish from the paths by themselves. Deep waters, rivers, and muddy, impassable places, having abandoned their nature, become delightful.

764.

764.

Vajanti bhumiṃ girayo purattha

Pasārite pādavare jinassa,

Nibbāti aggī narakodarepi

Gaṇhanti pāde padumādayo ca;

The earth and mountains tremble when the Conqueror's excellent feet are extended. Even the fire in the depths of hell is extinguished, and lotuses and the like receive his feet.

765.

765.

Idañhi te pādatale yatīsa

Sanābhi nemi ghaṭikāvalīhi,

Susaṇṭhitaṃ cā’rasahassavantaṃ

Sandissate cakkavaraṃ mahantaṃ;

Indeed, on this sole of your foot, O Lord of Ascetics, a great and excellent wheel appears, well-formed, having a thousand spokes, complete with a nave, a rim, and rows of spokes.

766.

766.

Tameva cakkaṃ parivārayitvā

Sirivaccha sovatthi’vataṃsakā ca,

Pāsāda bhadrāsana puṇṇapāti

Sitātapattāsi mayūrahatthā;

Surrounding that very wheel are the Sirivaccha, the Sovatthi, and ear-ornaments; palaces, auspicious thrones, full bowls; white parasols, swords, and peacock-feather fans;

767. [O 0.0081]

767.

Nīlādibhedā kamaluppalā ca

Sameru sattaddi mahāsamuddā,

Sattāpagā satta mahāsarā ca

Himālayo cakkavāḷaddiko ca;

Blue and other kinds of lotuses and water lilies; Mount Meru, the seven great mountains, and the great oceans; the seven rivers, the seven great lakes; the Himalayas and the Cakkavāḷa mountain;

768.

768.

Candakkatārā ca chadevalokā

Pitāmahāvāsa manussalokaṃ,

Suvaṇṇa nāvā sivikā ca saṅkhaṃ

Kelāsaselaṃ dhajatoraṇā ca;

The moon, sun, and stars, and the six deva worlds; the abode of Brahmā and the human world; a golden ship, a palanquin, and a conch; Mount Kailasa, flags, and arches;

769.

769.

Cintāmaṇuṇhīsa savaccha dhenū

Mīnadvayaṃ cakkavatti saseno,

Sīha’ssa mātaṅga viyaggharājā

Haṃsosabho kimpuriso mayūro;

The wish-fulfilling gem, the uṇhīsa, the cow with her calf; the pair of fish, the wheel-turning monarch with his army; the lion, the horse, the elephant, the king of tigers; the swan, the bull, the kimpurisa, the peacock;

770.

770.

Koñcā ca erāvaṇa hatthirājā

Sacakkavākā makarādayo ca,

Nānā mahāmaṅgala lakkhaṇā te

Virocamānā vilasanti niccaṃ;

Cranes and Erāvaṇa, the king of elephants; with ruddy geese, makaras, and the like; these various great auspicious signs shine forth and gleam perpetually.

771.

771.

Jātakkhaṇe yassa mahiṃ pabhejja

Visāla sattuddaya paṅkajāni,

Paṭiggahesuṃ caraṇāni yāni

Te tānimānacchariyāni loke;

At the moment of his birth, wide lotuses with stamens arose, piercing the earth, and received his feet. Those are these wonders in the world.

772.

772.

Vandāpanatthāyu’panītakāle

Pitūhi te devala tāpasindaṃ,

Pādāni gantvāna jaṭāsu tassa

Āsuṃ tave’taṅghiyugaṃ aho bho;

At the time of being brought for homage by your father to Devala, the chief of ascetics, your pair of feet, O wonder, went and rested on his matted locks!

773.

773.

Suddhodanavhassa narādhipassa

Santosa toyehi papūritassa,

Siro visuddhambu ruhākarassa

Saroru hāsuṃ caraṇāni tuyhaṃ;

For the king named Suddhodana, who was filled with the waters of contentment, and whose head was a source of pure lotuses, your feet became lotuses.

774. [O 0.0082]

774.

Ye caṅkame caṅkamaṇāvasāne

Onamma merūdaya pabbaniṇdā,

Paṭiggahesuṃ caraṇāni yāni

Te tānimā’nacchariyāni loke;

Those lords of mountains on his walking path, like Meru and Udaya, bowing down at the end of his walking meditation, received his feet. Those are these wonders in the world.

775.

775.

Yaṃ vandamāno tidivādhipo so

Yassānubhāvena gatāyukopi,

Sakiyaṭhāne’sinapunāpi te’vaṃ

Pādambujaṃ dhīra!Mahānubhāvaṃ;

By whose power the lord of heaven, who was paying homage, though his life was spent, was restored to his own place—thus, O steadfast one, your lotus feet are of mighty power!

776.

776.

Dehīnamaggopi nisākarāri

Mānunnato so sayitassa tuyhaṃ,

Pādassa antampi na sakkhi daṭṭhuṃ

Acchera rūpaṃ idamaṅghikañjaṃ;

Even Rāhu, the enemy of the moon, though highly proud, could not see the end of your foot as you lay down. Astonishing is the form of this lotus-like foot!

777.

777.

Gaṅgāya gaṅgāpati sannidhāne

Tīre tadā nammada jimhagassa,

Pādassa lañchaṃ akarī muninda

Mayhaṃ’pi hotaṃ karuṇā tavesā;

On the bank of the Nammadā, in the presence of the river lord, O Lord of Sages, you then made a footprint for the crooked Nāga. May that compassion of yours be for me also.

778.

778.

Ārādhito saccaka tāpasena

Akā tuvaṃ saccaka baddhasele,

Pādassa lañchaṃ jagato hitāya

Mayhampi hotaṃ tamanuggahante;

Implored by the ascetic Saccaka, you placed a footprint on the firm rock for the welfare of the world. May that favor of yours be for me also.

779.

779.

Sutvāna nātho girametamassa

Passaṃ mahābhuti’manāgatesu,

Lokassa lokehi mahīya māno

Akāsi vāmena padena lañchaṃ;

The Lord, having heard this speech of his, seeing great prosperity in the future, and being honored by the world with worldly things, made an impression with his left foot.

780.

780.

Sambodhito aṭṭhama sāradasmiṃ

Vesākhamāse muṇi puṇṇamāyaṃ,

Pādassa’bhiññāṇamakā’paraṇhe

Sadevake sassamaṇe mahente;

In the eighth year after his enlightenment, in the month of Vesākha, on the full moon day, the Sage made a sign of his foot in the late afternoon, while the world with its gods and ascetics paid great honor.

781. [O 0.0083]

781.

Pataṅakgikā sitthaka matthakamhi

Yathaṅkitā khattiya muddikāya,

Āseva’mevaṃ jinapādalañchaṃ

Samantakūṭamhi namassanīyaṃ;

As a mark is impressed by a royal seal upon a surface of wax, even so is the Conqueror's footprint on Samantakūṭa, worthy of reverence.

782.

782.

Akālamegho ca tato pavassi

Vassiṃsu nānāratanāni khamhā,

Tathā parito kusumambarāni

Suvaṇṇacuṇṇāni jinekavaṇṇā;

Then an untimely cloud rained; various gems rained from the sky; likewise, all around, flower-garments and golden dust of the Conqueror's hue rained down.

783.

783.

Tato’pagantvā sugatebhagāmī

Tasmiṃ nitambe girigabbharāyaṃ,

Divāvihārāya nisīdi yattha

Supākaṭaṃ taṃ bhagavāguhā’ti;

Then the Sugata, the Blessed One, having approached that mountain slope, sat down for the day's abiding in a rock-cave, which is well-known as 'the Blessed One's Cave'.

784.

784.

Tatorahantā sugatorasā te

Gandhādinā sādhu mahetva sabbe,

Vanditva katvāna padakkhiṇantaṃ

Tahaṃ tahaṃ’kaṃsu divāvihāraṃ;

Then those Arahants, the spiritual sons of the Sugata, having all honored him well with perfumes and the like, and having paid homage and circumambulated him, made their day's abiding here and there.

785.

785.

Lataṅganāyo viṭapīdhavāna

Mālamba sākhāputhulaṃ’sapasse,

Suphulla namañjūkara mañjarīhi

Namassamānā’va sadonataggā;

The creeper-women, embracing the trees and hanging from their broad branch-shoulders, with fully bloomed and bowing flower clusters, seem to be worshipping, their tips ever bent.

786.

786.

Tiṭṭhanti rukkhā naṭakā’va tattha

Suphulla sākhākara’mukkhipitvā,

Namassamānā viya onataggā

Vattanti mānacchariyāni niccaṃ;

The trees stand there like actors, having lifted up their fully bloomed branch-hands; as if worshipping with their tips bent, they constantly display great wonders.

787.

787.

Tatheva uccāvaca pabbatā ca

Namassamānā viya pādalañchaṃ,

Tiṭṭhanti ninnagga sikhā samantā

Idampi niccababhutameva tattha;

Likewise, the high and low mountains, as if paying homage to the footprint, stand all around with lowered peaks; this too is a constant marvel there.

788. [O 0.0084]

788.

Tasmiṃ nage pādavaraṅkitasmiṃ

Khalamaṇḍalokāsa padesamatte,

Samosarante bahuke janepi

Hoteva okāsama,ho padaṅkaṃ;

On that mountain marked by the excellent foot, in a space merely the size of a threshing floor, even when many people gather, there is indeed room. Oh, what a wonder is the footprint!

789.

789.

Samosaritvāna mahetva satte

Nikkhantamatte jaladā samecca,

Sodhenti māla’mbuvahehi sādhu

Idampi niccabbhutameva tattha;

When the beings, having gathered and paid great honor, have just departed, clouds assemble and thoroughly cleanse the terrace with streams of water. This too is a constant marvel there.

790.

790.

Pādena phuṭṭhassa silātalassa

Etādisānacchariyāni honti,

Lokekanāthassa anāsavassa

Mahabbhutaṃ konu kathaṃ bhaṇeyya;

Such are the wonders of the stone slab touched by his foot. Of the sole Lord of the world, the Taintless One, who indeed could fully describe the great marvel?

791.

791.

Divāvihāraṃ bhagavā sasaṅgho

Katvāna tasmiṃ pana kiñcikālaṃ,

Mahīyamānesu sadevakesu

Tato gato rohaṇamambaramhā;

The Blessed One, with the Saṅgha, having spent some time there in the day's abiding, while the world with its gods was paying honor, then went to Rohaṇa through the sky.

792.

792.

Tasmiṃ sasaṅgho muni dīghavāpiyaṃ

Thūpassa ṭhāne paramāya bhumiyā,

Garuṃ karonto pana taṃ mahītalaṃ

Nirodhabhāvena nisīdi satrajo;

There, at Dīghavāpi, at the site of the Stūpa, on that most excellent ground, the Sage with his Saṅgha, bestowing honor upon that great place, sat down in the attainment of cessation, free from passion.

793.

793.

Tato’nurādhaṃ bhagavā nabhamhā

Gantvāna bodhiṭṭhitabhumiyā ca,

Ṭhāne mahāmaṅgalacetiyassa

Tatheva akkhantinahitassa ṭhāne;

Then the Blessed One, having gone from the sky to Anurādhapura, to the ground where the Bodhi tree stands, to the site of the Mahāmaṅgala Cetiya, and likewise to the site of the Akkhantinahita,

794.

794.

Nisīdi patvāna nirādhapītiṃ

Sasāvako pekkhama’nāgataddhaṃ,

Patiṭṭhitā me pana bodhidhātu

Karonti loke’ti janassa vuddhiṃ;

...sat down with his disciples, having attained the bliss of cessation, foreseeing the future time: 'My Bodhi-relic will be established here and will bring about the welfare of the people in the world.'

795. [O 0.0085]

795.

Vuṭṭhāya tuṭṭho bhagavā nirodhā

Gato silāthūpavarassa ṭhānaṃ,

Ṭhito tahiṃ dhammamathuddisitvā

Gato nabhā jetavanaṃ surammaṃ;

Having arisen from cessation, the pleased Blessed One went to the site of the excellent stone stūpa. Standing there and having taught the Dhamma, he went through the sky to the delightful Jetavana.

796.

796.

Evaṃ so dhammarājā janahitavihito vīta dosārivaggo

Laṅkārāmāya ramme sumanagirisire’kāsi yaṃ pādalañchaṃ

Taṃ vo saggā’pavaggaṃ dadati munisamaṃ citta matte pasanne

Tasmā bho!Bho! Pahaṭṭhā namatha mahatha taṃ sādhu sādhuppasatthaṃ;

Thus that King of Dhamma, devoted to the welfare of the people, free from the hostile host of defilements, made a footprint on the lovely summit of Mount Sumana in delightful Laṅkā. That footprint gives you heaven and liberation when your mind is merely serene like a sage's. Therefore, O sirs! Rejoicing, bow down and pay homage to that which is praised as 'good, good!'
